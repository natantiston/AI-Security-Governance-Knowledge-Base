# 14.01 — AI Privacy and Data Protection Foundations

## Part 1 — AI Privacy and Data Protection Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Privacy and Data Protection

AI privacy and data protection is the discipline of identifying, governing, controlling, and continuously assessing how artificial intelligence systems collect, access, process, generate, store, transfer, disclose, retain, and delete personal and sensitive information.

AI introduces additional privacy considerations because data may pass through prompts, context windows, models, retrieval systems, vector databases, memory systems, tools, agents, logs, monitoring platforms, and external AI providers.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Privacy and Data Protection

The purpose is to ensure that AI systems process information lawfully, fairly, transparently, securely, proportionately, and only for authorized purposes.

Privacy protection must cover the complete AI lifecycle:

```text id="aip14p1-01"
Collection
    |
    v
Preparation
    |
    v
Training / Fine-Tuning
    |
    v
Deployment
    |
    v
Prompt / Inference
    |
    v
Storage / Retrieval
    |
    v
Sharing / Transfer
    |
    v
Retention
    |
    v
Deletion
```

---

### 3. AI Privacy Risk

AI privacy risk arises when AI processing creates the possibility of unauthorized collection, use, disclosure, inference, retention, transfer, profiling, surveillance, identification, or exposure of information relating to individuals.

AI can amplify privacy risk because the same information may be replicated across multiple technical components.

---

### 4. AI Data Protection Lifecycle

An enterprise should govern personal data throughout its entire AI lifecycle.

```text id="aip14p1-02"
Identify
   ↓

   ↓

   ↓
Minimize
   ↓
Process
   ↓
Protect
   ↓
Monitor
   ↓
Retain
   ↓

```

The lifecycle should remain traceable and accountable.

---

### 5. Personal Data in AI Systems

Personal data may appear in:

* Prompts
* Uploaded documents
* Chat histories
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Retrieval repositories
* Vector databases
* Memory stores
* Logs
* Monitoring systems
* Error messages
* API payloads
* Model outputs
* Generated reports
* Agent workflows
* Enterprise applications

Privacy governance must therefore consider more than the primary database.

---

### 6. Sensitive and Special-Category Data

AI systems may process information requiring heightened protection, depending on applicable law and organizational policy.

Examples can include:

* Health information
* Biometric information
* Financial information
* Identification information
* Employment information
* Children's information
* Precise location information
* Authentication information
* Other legally protected categories

The AI architecture should identify such information before it reaches systems that are not authorized to process it.

---

### 7. AI Data Classification

AI privacy governance should integrate with enterprise data classification.

A practical classification model may distinguish:

| Classification     | Example                                |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Public             | Public documentation                   |
| Internal           | Internal business information          |
| Confidential       | Business-sensitive information         |
| Personal           | Information relating to individuals    |
| Sensitive Personal | Higher-risk personal information       |
| Restricted         | Highly sensitive regulated information |

Classification should determine where data can be processed and by which AI services.

### 9. Privacy by Design

Privacy should be incorporated into AI systems during design rather than added after deployment.

Design decisions should address:

* Data minimization
* Purpose limitation
* Access control
* Encryption
* Isolation
* Retention
* Deletion
* Transparency
* User rights
* Provider processing
* Logging
* Monitoring
* Privacy incident response

---

### 10. Privacy by Default

AI systems should use privacy-protective settings by default where practical and legally appropriate.

Examples include:

* Minimum necessary data collection
* Restricted data sharing
* Disabled unnecessary memory
* Limited retention
* Restricted model training on customer data
* Tenant isolation
* Least-privilege retrieval
* Restricted logging of sensitive content
* Default masking or redaction

The user should not have to understand complex AI architecture merely to prevent unnecessary disclosure of personal information.

---

### 11. Purpose Limitation

Personal data should be processed for defined and legitimate purposes.

An organization should avoid assuming:

> “Because the AI system can process the data, it may use the data for any purpose.”

A dataset collected for one purpose may not automatically be appropriate for:

* Model training
* Fine-tuning
* Prompt enrichment
* Retrieval
* Profiling
* Automated decision-making
* Product development
* Model evaluation
* Monitoring

---

### 12. Data Minimization

AI systems should process only the personal information reasonably necessary for the intended purpose.

For example, a customer-support model that needs to identify an order may not need unrestricted access to:

* Full payment information
* Unrelated medical information
* Complete identity documents
* Unrelated employee records
* Historical conversations unrelated to the request

Minimization should operate at the architecture, application, retrieval, prompt, and model layers.

---

### 13. AI Collection Controls

Collection controls should determine:

* What data is collected
* Why it is collected
* Who authorized the collection
* Which AI system receives it
* Whether collection is necessary
* How long it will be retained
* Whether it is transferred to another provider
* Whether individuals must be informed

AI interfaces should avoid silently collecting additional personal information merely because it is technically available.

---

### 14. Prompt Data Privacy

Prompts can become privacy-sensitive data-processing events.

A prompt may contain:

```text id="aip14p1-04"
Employee Name
      +
Medical Information
      +
Employment History
      +
Manager Comments
      |
      v
    PROMPT
      |
      v
 AI Service Provider
```

The organization must determine whether sending that information to the AI system is authorized and appropriate.

---

### 15. Context Window Privacy

AI systems may process information that is not explicitly typed by the user.

Context can originate from:

* Previous messages
* User profiles
* Documents
* RAG systems
* Memory
* Enterprise databases
* Application state
* System instructions
* Tool results

Therefore:

**The privacy boundary is the complete context supplied to the model, not merely the user's latest prompt.**

---

### 16. RAG Privacy

Retrieval-augmented generation creates a significant privacy boundary.

A user request may cause the system to retrieve personal information from enterprise repositories.

Controls should ensure that retrieval respects:

* User identity
* Tenant
* Role
* Purpose
* Authorization
* Data classification
* Document permissions
* Record-level access

A model must not receive information merely because the retrieval system can technically retrieve it.

---

### 17. Vector Database Privacy

Vector databases can contain embeddings derived from personal information.

Privacy governance must therefore consider:

* Source documents
* Embeddings
* Metadata
* Access permissions
* Search results
* Similarity queries
* Retention
* Deletion
* Backup copies
* Index rebuilding
* Cross-tenant isolation

Deleting the original document may not be sufficient if derived representations remain accessible.

---

### 18. AI Memory Privacy

Persistent AI memory may create additional privacy risk because information from previous interactions can influence future processing.

Memory controls should address:

* What is stored
* Why it is stored
* How long it is stored
* Who can access it
* Whether users can inspect it
* Whether users can correct it
* Whether users can delete it
* Whether it is shared across applications or agents

---

### 19. Training Data Privacy

Training data can contain personal information collected from:

* Internal systems
* Customer interactions
* Public sources
* Licensed datasets
* Third-party providers
* User submissions
* Synthetic data mixed with real data

Organizations must determine whether the intended training use is lawful, necessary, appropriately governed, and consistent with applicable obligations.

---

### 20. Fine-Tuning Data Privacy

Fine-tuning can introduce personal data into model-development workflows.

Privacy governance should establish:

* Approved fine-tuning datasets
* Data provenance
* Purpose
* Lawful basis where applicable
* Minimization
* Access restrictions
* Retention
* Dataset deletion
* Model artifact handling
* Provider restrictions
* Evaluation requirements

---

### 21. Model Memorization Risk

AI models may potentially memorize or reproduce portions of training data.

Privacy testing should therefore consider whether a model can reveal:

* Personal information
* Confidential records
* Identifiers
* Private conversations
* Sensitive attributes
* Training examples

The risk is not limited to direct database access.

---

### 22. Inference-Time Privacy

Privacy risk can occur during ordinary inference even when the model was not trained on personal information.

Examples include:

* User-provided personal data
* Retrieved customer records
* Enterprise documents
* Conversation history
* Personal memory
* Tool results

Inference-time controls are therefore as important as training-data controls.

---

### 23. Output Privacy

AI outputs may expose personal information to:

* The requesting user
* Other users
* Administrators
* Agents
* External recipients
* Logs
* Downstream applications

Output controls should evaluate whether the recipient is authorized to receive the generated information.

---

### 24. Cross-User Privacy

AI systems must prevent one user's private information from appearing in another user's interaction.

This requires isolation across:

* Sessions
* Conversations
* Memory
* Retrieval
* Caches
* Application state
* Logs
* Model context
* Tool results

```text id="aip14p1-05"
User A ──> Private Context ──> AI Session A
                                  X
User B ──> Private Context ──> AI Session B
```

Cross-user leakage should be treated as a security and privacy failure.

---

### 25. Cross-Tenant Privacy

Multi-tenant AI systems require tenant-level isolation.

A request from Tenant A must not retrieve, expose, or influence protected information belonging to Tenant B.

Tenant isolation should cover:

* Data stores
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Caches
* Logs
* APIs
* Model contexts
* Tool results
* Administrative interfaces

---

### 26. Identity and Privacy

Privacy controls depend on reliable identity.

The system should know:

* Who is making the request
* Which organization they belong to
* Which tenant they belong to
* Which role they have
* Which data they are permitted to access
* Which purpose they are acting under

Anonymous or ambiguous identity can weaken privacy enforcement.

---

### 27. Authorization and Privacy

Authorization must be enforced independently from model behavior.

```text id="aip14p1-06"
User Request
     |
     v
AI Model
     |
     v
Proposed Data Access
     |
     v
Authorization Layer
     |
   +---+---+
   |       |
 ALLOW    DENY
   |       |
   v       v
 Data     No Data
```

The model's decision to request information must never itself authorize access to personal data.

---

### 28. Privacy and Least Privilege

AI applications should receive the minimum data and permissions necessary to perform their approved function.

Least privilege should apply to:

* Users
* Applications
* Models
* Agents
* Retrieval services
* Tools
* APIs
* Databases
* Administrators

This reduces both security and privacy exposure.

---

### 29. Logging and Privacy

AI logging can create a secondary privacy repository.

Logs may contain:

* Prompts
* Outputs
* Personal information
* Documents
* Tool parameters
* User identifiers
* Conversation history
* Error messages

Logging therefore requires its own privacy controls rather than assuming that security logs are automatically exempt from privacy governance.

---

### 30. AI Monitoring Privacy

Monitoring systems should collect sufficient information to detect security and privacy events without unnecessarily reproducing sensitive content.

Organizations should consider:

* Data masking
* Tokenization
* Redaction
* Structured telemetry
* Limited content capture
* Access controls
* Retention limits
* Purpose limitation

---

External AI providers may process information on behalf of an organization or for their own purposes, depending on contractual and technical arrangements.

Provider governance should examine:

* Processing purposes

* Training use
* Retention
* Geographic processing
* Subprocessors
* Security controls
* Data deletion
* Contractual commitments
* Auditability

### 36. Data Subject Rights

AI systems may need to support applicable rights concerning personal data.

Depending on the legal context, these may include rights relating to:

* Access
* Rectification
* Erasure
* Restriction
* Objection
* Portability
* Automated decision-making

AI architecture should not make these rights impossible to exercise merely because information has been distributed across complex AI components.

### 37. AI Transparency

Individuals may need meaningful information about how their personal data is processed by AI systems.

Transparency should address, where applicable:

* Purpose
* Categories of data
* Processing activities
* Recipients
* Retention
* Relevant automated processing
* Rights
* Contact points

AI complexity should not become an excuse for incomprehensible privacy communication.

Examples include:

* Hiring
* Credit
* Insurance
* Benefits
* Fraud detection
* Customer eligibility
* Employee assessment

Where automated decision-making is involved, privacy and data protection governance must evaluate the applicable legal requirements and safeguards.

Profiling may use:

* Communications
* Location

* Preferences
* Demographic information

### 41. Privacy Security Controls

Privacy and security controls are closely connected but are not identical.

Security controls help protect data from unauthorized access.

Privacy controls additionally address:

* Why data is processed
* Whether processing is necessary
* Whether processing is proportionate
* Transparency
* Individual rights
* Purpose limitation
* Retention
* Lawful processing

Strong cybersecurity does not automatically establish privacy compliance.

### 46. AI Privacy Risk Assessment

Privacy risk assessment should consider:

**Data sensitivity × processing purpose × scale × exposure × affected individuals × AI capability × downstream impact.**

Higher-risk processing should receive greater scrutiny, stronger controls, and potentially additional assessment before deployment.

### 48. Privacy Failure Conditions

An AI privacy control should not be considered effective merely because it exists in documentation.

A privacy control should be considered effective only when there is reasonable evidence that it:

* Is implemented
* Applies to the relevant data
* Applies to the relevant processing
* Enforces the intended restriction
* Is monitored
* Can be tested
* Produces evidence
* Remains effective after relevant changes

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Privacy and Data Protection

**AI privacy is a continuous, lifecycle-based protection discipline covering personal data from collection through processing, inference, retrieval, memory, model interaction, output, transfer, retention, deletion, and downstream use.**

The enterprise must maintain visibility and control across the complete AI data-processing chain.

The fundamental separations are:

**Data availability ≠ authorization to process.**

**Model capability ≠ permission to access personal data.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Security protection ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Data deletion ≠ deletion of every derived artifact automatically.**

**Transparency ≠ meaningful transparency.**

**A privacy control ≠ effective privacy assurance.**

**Passing a privacy test ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

## Part 2 — AI Privacy and Data Protection Controls

The framework should connect:

### 52. AI Privacy Control Planning

AI privacy controls should be planned before personal data is introduced into an AI system.

Planning should identify:

* Processing purposes
* Data categories
* Individuals affected
* AI components involved
* Processing locations
* Providers and subprocessors
* Retention requirements
* Applicable privacy obligations
* Security requirements
* Data-subject requirements

---

### 53. AI Privacy Scope Control

The scope of privacy controls should cover the complete AI ecosystem rather than only the model.

Scope should include, where applicable:

* Applications
* Prompts
* Context
* Models
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* RAG repositories
* Vector databases
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* APIs
* Logs
* Monitoring
* Backups
* Cloud services
* Third-party AI providers

---

### 54. AI Privacy Classification Control

AI systems and datasets should be classified according to privacy risk and data sensitivity.

Classification should influence:

* Approved processing environments
* Access requirements
* Encryption
* Logging
* Provider eligibility
* Retention
* Testing
* Approval requirements
* Monitoring

---

### 55. Risk-Based AI Privacy Control Selection

Privacy controls should be proportional to risk.

Higher-risk systems should receive stronger controls when they involve:

* Sensitive personal data
* Large populations
* Vulnerable individuals
* Automated decisions
* Profiling
* Persistent memory
* Cross-border processing
* External AI providers
* High-impact decisions
* Extensive data access

---

Privacy requirements should be mapped to concrete AI controls.

### 59. AI Privacy Test-Case Control

Privacy test cases should be derived from identified risks and requirements.

* Sensitive prompt exposure
* Memory disclosure
* RAG leakage

Privacy testing should avoid unnecessary use of real personal data.

Where possible, organizations should use:

* Synthetic data
* Anonymized data
* Pseudonymized data
* Masked data
* Controlled test records

Use of real personal data for testing should require appropriate authorization and protection.

### 61. AI Privacy Environment Control

Privacy testing and development environments should be separated from production environments.

Production personal data should not be copied into development or testing environments merely for convenience.

Environment controls should address:

* Access
* Network isolation
* Data movement
* Credentials
* Logging
* Storage
* Backups
* Provider connections

---

### 62. AI Privacy Environment Integrity Control

The organization should verify that privacy controls are active in the environment being tested.

Testing should identify:

* Configuration changes
* Disabled controls
* Missing encryption
* Incorrect access policies
* Unexpected data sources
* Unauthorized integrations

### 67. Personal Data Collection Control

AI applications should collect only personal data necessary for the defined purpose.

Controls should prevent unnecessary collection through:

* Input restrictions
* Field minimization
* Data filtering
* Application validation
* DLP
* Redaction
* User guidance

---

### 68. AI Purpose-Limitation Control

The organization should prevent personal data collected for one purpose from being silently reused for unrelated AI processing.

Each material processing purpose should be documented and assessed.

---

### 69. AI Data-Minimization Control

The AI system should receive only the minimum information required.

```text id="aip14p2-03"
Full Customer Record
        |
        v
Data Filtering
        |
        v
Required Attributes
        |
        v
AI Context
```

Filtering should occur before unnecessary information reaches the model.

---

### 70. AI Personal-Data Access Control

Access to personal data should be based on verified identity, authorization, purpose, and applicable policy.

The model should not independently determine access.

---

### 71. AI Cross-User Isolation Control

AI sessions should be isolated so that information belonging to one user cannot become available to another user without authorization.

Isolation should include:

* Conversations
* Memory
* Context
* Caches
* Retrieval
* Tool results
* Session state

---

### 72. AI Cross-Tenant Isolation Control

Multi-tenant AI systems should enforce tenant boundaries at every relevant data layer.

Tenant isolation should cover:

* Databases
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Caches
* APIs
* Logs
* Retrieval
* Tool results

---

### 73. AI Session Isolation Control

Session data should be isolated according to defined privacy requirements.

A new session should not inherit private information from another session unless explicitly authorized.

---

### 74. AI Conversation Privacy Control

Conversation history should be treated as potentially personal information.

Controls should define:

* Who can access conversations
* How conversations are retained
* Whether conversations enter memory
* Whether conversations are used for training
* Whether administrators can inspect them
* How deletion operates

---

### 75. AI Memory Privacy Control

Persistent AI memory should use explicit privacy controls.

Memory should support:

* Defined storage purpose
* Access control
* Retention
* Review
* Correction
* Deletion
* Isolation
