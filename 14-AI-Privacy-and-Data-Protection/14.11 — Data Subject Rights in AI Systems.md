# 14.11 — Data Subject Rights in AI

## Part 1 — Data Subject Rights in AI Foundations

### 1. Definition of Data Subject Rights in AI

Data subject rights in AI are the rights available to individuals in relation to the processing of their personal data by AI systems.

AI can complicate the exercise of these rights because personal data may exist across:

* Prompts
* Conversations
* Context
* RAG repositories
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Model outputs
* Profiles
* Decision records
* Logs
* Backups
* Third-party providers

Rights governance must therefore address the **complete AI data lifecycle**.

---

### 2. Purpose of Data Subject Rights Governance

The purpose of rights governance is to ensure that individuals can effectively exercise applicable rights despite the complexity of AI architectures.

Governance should support appropriate mechanisms for:

* Access
* Rectification
* Erasure
* Restriction
* Objection
* Portability
* Human review
* Contesting applicable automated decisions

The organization should be able to locate relevant personal data and determine how AI processing affects it.

---

### 4. Right of Access in AI

The right of access generally concerns an individual's ability to obtain information about relevant personal-data processing and, where applicable, a copy of personal data.

In AI systems, relevant information may exist in:

* Source databases
* Prompts
* Conversations
* Profiles
* RAG repositories
* Memory
* Decision records
* Logs

The organization should determine which information falls within the applicable access requirements.

---

### 5. AI Access-Request Identification

The organization should be able to identify an individual's request reliably.

Identification controls should prevent:

* Unauthorized disclosure
* Identity confusion
* Account takeover
* Incorrect profile retrieval
* Disclosure to an impersonator

**Identity verification ≠ unrestricted data access.**

### 7. AI Data Lineage and Rights

Data lineage helps determine where personal information originated and where it was subsequently processed.

Relevant lineage may include:

**Source → Feature → Profile → Model Input → Output → Decision.**

Without lineage, rights requests may be difficult to execute reliably.

---

### 8. Right to Rectification in AI

Where applicable, individuals may have the right to have inaccurate personal data corrected.

AI creates additional challenges because inaccurate information may have propagated into:

* Features
* Profiles
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Decision records
* Derived datasets

Correction should therefore consider downstream dependencies.

### 10. Right to Erasure in AI

Where applicable, individuals may have the right to request deletion of personal data.

AI systems complicate erasure because information can exist in:

* Primary databases
* Prompts
* Conversation history
* Memory
* RAG stores
* Vector databases
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Logs
* Backups
* Derived datasets

---

### 11. AI Erasure versus Model Deletion

Deleting personal data from a dataset does not necessarily mean that information has been removed from an already-trained model.

```text id="dsr14-04"
Personal Data
     |
     v
Training Dataset
     |
     v
Model Training
     |
     v
Model Parameters
```

Therefore:

**Dataset deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

The appropriate response depends on the architecture, processing context, applicable requirements, and technical feasibility.

---

### 12. Right to Restriction in AI

Where applicable, restriction may require limiting how personal data is processed while preserving the information for permitted purposes.

AI architectures may need to prevent restricted information from being:

* Retrieved
* Included in context
* Used for profiling
* Used for decision-making
* Used for additional processing

---

### 14. Right to Object in AI

Where applicable, individuals may have the right to object to certain processing activities.

AI systems should be capable of associating applicable objections with relevant processing activities.

Potentially affected activities may include:

* Profiling
* Personalization
* Marketing
* Analytics
* Automated processing

### 16. Right to Data Portability in AI

Where applicable, data portability concerns personal data provided by an individual and the conditions established by applicable law.

AI systems should identify where relevant data exists in structured and commonly used formats.

Potential sources include:

* User accounts
* Transaction systems
* Profiles
* Interaction histories

---

### 17. AI Portability versus Model Extraction

Data portability should not automatically be interpreted as a requirement to provide:

* Model weights
* Proprietary algorithms
* Internal system prompts
* Security configurations
* Confidential information

The organization should distinguish personal-data portability from technical model extraction.

### 25. Rights and Model Memorization

A model may potentially reproduce information from training data.

Risks include:

* Memorization
* Extraction
* Membership inference
* Reconstruction

Therefore:

**Training-data deletion ≠ guaranteed removal of learned information.**

### 26. Rights and AI Inference

Examples include inferred:

* Risk
* Preference
* Behavior
* Eligibility
* Characteristics

Rights governance should distinguish source data from AI-derived information.

---

### 27. Rights and Profiles

AI-generated profiles can contain:

* Scores
* Predictions
* Categories
* Preferences
* Risk classifications
* Behavioral attributes

Profiles may need to be considered in applicable rights processes.

**Derived data ≠ automatically anonymous data.**

Data subject rights can intersect with automated decision-making.

Where applicable, individuals may have rights concerning:

* Information about automated processing
* Human intervention
* Contesting decisions
* Review
* Relevant personal data

### 31. Rights and Transparency

Individuals should receive appropriate information about relevant AI processing where required.

Transparency may address:

* Processing purpose
* Categories of data
* AI involvement
* Profiling
* Automated decision-making
* Rights

### 32. Rights and Explainability

Where explanations are required or appropriate, the organization should be capable of explaining relevant AI processing at an appropriate level.

The explanation should not falsely imply that the model performed a process it did not perform.

---

### 33. Rights and Data Minimization

Rights governance should not be used as a reason to retain unnecessary personal information.

The organization should minimize the data required to:

* Identify the individual
* Process the request
* Locate relevant information
* Fulfill applicable obligations

---

### 34. Rights and Identity

Identity resolution is fundamental to rights processing.

The organization should prevent:

* False matches
* Duplicate identities
* Incorrect profile association
* Unauthorized disclosure

---

### 35. Rights and Authorization

A rights request should be authenticated appropriately, but authorization boundaries must remain intact.

For example, an employee processing a request should not automatically receive unrestricted access to unrelated sensitive systems.

**Rights processing ≠ unrestricted administrative access.**

---

### 49. Enterprise AI Data Subject Rights Principle

Enterprise AI systems should be designed so that applicable data-subject rights remain **exercisable, traceable, secure, and operationally enforceable** despite distributed AI architectures.

The organization should be able to determine:

* Where personal data exists
* Where it has been transformed
* Which AI systems process it
* Which profiles or inferences depend on it
* Which providers receive it
* How it can be corrected
* How it can be deleted
* How applicable restrictions propagate

---

### 50. Core Principle of Data Subject Rights in AI

Data-subject rights in AI must be treated as a **lifecycle and architecture problem**, not merely as a request-handling workflow.

The complete rights chain should remain visible:

```text id="dsr14-12"
Data Subject
      |
      v
Identity Verification
      |
      v
Rights Request
      |
      v
Data Discovery
      |
      +------> Source Data
      +------> Prompts
      +------> Conversations
      +------> RAG
      +------> Vector Store
      +------> Memory
      +------> Profiles
      +------> Training Data
      +------> Logs
      +------> Provider Systems
      |
      v
Rights Assessment
      |
      v
Authorized Action
      |
      +--> Access
      +--> Rectification
      +--> Erasure
      +--> Restriction
      +--> Objection
      +--> Portability
      +--> Human Review
      |
      v
Propagation
      |
      v
Verification
      |
      v
Evidence
      |
      v
Monitoring / Reassessment
```

The following distinctions must remain explicit:

**Data-subject rights ≠ unrestricted access to enterprise systems.**

**Identity verification ≠ authorization for every data source.**

**Data discovery ≠ automatic disclosure.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

**Data access ≠ profile access.**

**Source-data correction ≠ automatic correction of every derived artifact.**

**Dataset deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

**Training-data deletion ≠ guaranteed removal of learned information.**

**Derived data ≠ automatically anonymous data.**

**Pseudonymization ≠ anonymization.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Provider capability ≠ permission to process personal data.**

**Model capability ≠ decision authority.**

**Model output ≠ authorized decision.**

**Profiling ≠ automatically automated decision-making.**

**Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**

**Security assurance ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Documentation ≠ technical enforcement.**

**Control implementation ≠ control effectiveness.**

**Rights-request completion ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

An effective AI rights architecture must therefore ensure that applicable rights can propagate across the **source data, AI prompts, context, RAG, vector stores, memory, training and fine-tuning datasets, models, profiles, outputs, decisions, logs, backups, and third-party providers** that participate in the relevant processing lifecycle.

The organization should be able to demonstrate not merely that a rights request was received and closed, but that the request was **correctly identified, securely authenticated, comprehensively assessed, appropriately authorized, technically propagated, verified, evidenced, and incorporated into ongoing AI privacy governance**.

## Part 2 — Data Subject Rights in AI Controls

### 51. AI Data Subject Rights Control Framework

The organization should establish a formal control framework for managing data-subject rights across AI systems.

The framework should address:

* Access
* Rectification
* Erasure
* Restriction
* Objection
* Portability
* Automated-decision rights
* Human intervention
* Contestability
* Rights-request security
* Data discovery
* Provider coordination
* Evidence
* Verification
* Continuous assurance

The control framework should cover both centralized enterprise systems and distributed AI components.

---

### 52. Data Subject Rights Planning Control

Rights requirements should be identified during AI system planning rather than after deployment.

Planning should determine:

* Applicable rights
* Relevant individuals
* Processing purposes
* Data locations
* AI components
* Providers
* Retention requirements
* Rights-request mechanisms
* Technical enforcement requirements

---

### 53. Data Subject Rights Scope Control

The organization should define the scope of each rights process.

Scope should consider:

* AI applications
* Models
* Databases
* RAG repositories
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Logs
* Backups
* External providers

A rights process should not assume that the primary application database represents the complete processing environment.

---

### 54. Data Subject Rights Classification Control

AI processing should be classified according to the types of personal data and rights implications involved.

Classification may consider:

* Personal data
* Sensitive information
* Special-category data
* Inferred information
* Profiles
* Decision data
* Behavioral information
* Identity information

Higher-risk processing should receive stronger controls and oversight.

---

### 55. Data Subject Rights Applicability Control

The organization should determine which rights apply to each processing activity.

Applicability should consider:

* Jurisdiction
* Individual status
* Processing purpose
* Legal basis
* Data category
* Processing method
* Automated decision-making
* Profiling
* Applicable exemptions or restrictions

**A rights request should be assessed, not automatically accepted or rejected.**

---

### 56. Data Subject Rights Requirement-Mapping Control

Applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and organizational requirements should be mapped to technical and procedural controls.

---

### 57. Data Subject Rights Risk-Classification Control

Rights-processing risk should be classified according to the potential impact of incorrect handling.

Risk factors may include:

* Sensitivity of data
* Number of individuals
* Number of systems
* AI autonomy
* Profiling
* Automated decisions
* Cross-border processing
* Provider involvement
* Security exposure
* Potential individual harm

---

### 58. Data Subject Rights Purpose Control

The purpose of processing should be documented for each AI system.

This allows the organization to determine:

* Why data is processed
* Whether the requested data belongs to that processing
* Whether additional processing is permitted
* Whether a rights request affects downstream purposes

**Purpose documentation ≠ authorization to use data for unrelated purposes.**

---

### 59. Data Subject Rights Data-Inventory Control

The organization should maintain an inventory of locations where personal data may exist.

The inventory should include, where relevant:

* Source systems
* Data warehouses
* Data lakes
* AI applications
* Prompt stores
* Conversation stores
* RAG repositories
* Vector databases
* Memory stores
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Logs
* Backups
* Provider platforms

---

### 60. AI Personal-Data Discovery Control

Rights workflows should provide reliable mechanisms for discovering relevant personal data.

Discovery should support:

* Identity matching
* Attribute matching
* Record linking
* Data lineage
* Repository search
* AI-specific artifact discovery

Discovery results should be traceable to their originating systems.

---

### 61. AI Personal-Data Identification Control

The organization should define how an individual is identified across systems.

Identifiers may include:

* Customer ID
* Employee ID
* Account ID
* Email address
* Transaction identifier
* Pseudonymous identifier
* Other authorized identity attributes

Identity matching should minimize false positives and false negatives.

---

### 62. AI Identity-Verification Control

Rights requests should use appropriate identity-verification mechanisms.

Controls may include:

* Authentication
* Account verification
* Identity-document verification where appropriate
* Step-up authentication
* Risk-based verification

Verification should collect only information necessary for the request.

---

### 63. AI Rights-Request Authorization Control

Authorization should determine whether the requester is entitled to initiate or receive the requested rights action.

The process should distinguish:

**Identity → Request → Entitlement → Authorized Action → Disclosure.**

A verified identity alone does not automatically authorize every possible disclosure.

---

### 64. AI Rights-Request Scope Control

The requested action should be explicitly defined.

Examples include:

* Access specific data
* Correct inaccurate information
* Delete applicable data
* Restrict processing
* Object to processing
* Obtain portable data
* Challenge an applicable automated decision

The organization should avoid interpreting a narrow request as authorization for unrelated processing.

---

### 65. AI Access-Request Control

Access-request workflows should provide a controlled mechanism for retrieving relevant personal data.

The workflow should support:

* Identity verification
* Scope determination
* Data discovery
* Applicability assessment
* Disclosure review
* Secure delivery
* Evidence retention

---

### 66. AI Access-Data Control

The organization should distinguish personal data from unrelated enterprise information.

Access fulfillment should not automatically expose:

* Other individuals' personal data
* Security credentials
* Secrets
* Proprietary information
* Internal security mechanisms
* Unrelated tenant information

**Data-subject access ≠ unrestricted system access.**

---

### 67. AI Processing-Information Access Control

Where applicable, rights workflows should provide relevant information about processing.

This may include:

* Processing purposes
* Categories of data
* Relevant recipients
* Retention information
* AI involvement
* Profiling
* Automated decision-making

---

### 68. AI-Generated Data Access Control

Where AI generates personal information about an individual, the organization should assess whether that information is relevant to the applicable access request.

The assessment should consider:

* Whether it constitutes personal data
* Its purpose
* Its relationship to the individual
* Applicable legal requirements
* Accuracy
* Disclosure restrictions

* Attributes

**Rectification ≠ automatic model retraining.**

**Erasure of source data ≠ automatic deletion from model parameters.**

**Deletion request ≠ automatic deletion from every derived artifact unless applicable.**

**Restriction ≠ deletion.**

**Objection ≠ automatic suspension of every processing activity.**

**Portability ≠ model extraction.**

**Derived data ≠ automatically anonymous data.**

**Inference ≠ factual truth.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Security assurance ≠ privacy compliance.**

The enterprise governance objective is therefore not simply to maintain a procedure for responding to individual requests.
