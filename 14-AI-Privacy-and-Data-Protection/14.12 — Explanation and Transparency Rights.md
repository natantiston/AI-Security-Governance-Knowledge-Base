# 14.12 — Explanation and Transparency Rights

## Part 1 — Explanation and Transparency Rights Foundations

### 1. Definition of Explanation and Transparency Rights in AI

Explanation and transparency rights in AI concern the ability of individuals and relevant stakeholders to receive understandable information about how personal data and AI systems are used.

In an AI environment, transparency may need to address:

* What data is processed
* Why it is processed
* How AI is involved
* Whether profiling occurs
* Whether automated decision-making occurs
* Who receives the data
* How long information is retained
* What rights are available
* How an individual can challenge applicable outcomes

Transparency should be meaningful rather than merely presenting extensive technical documentation.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Transparency Governance

The purpose of AI transparency governance is to ensure that individuals receive information necessary to understand relevant AI processing and exercise applicable rights.

Transparency should support:

* Awareness
* Understanding
* Accountability
* Contestability
* Trust
* Rights exercise
* Human oversight

**Transparency ≠ disclosure of everything in the AI system.**

---

---

### 4. Transparency versus Explanation

Transparency and explanation are related but distinct.

**Transparency** primarily concerns providing information about processing.

**Explanation** concerns making relevant processing, outcomes, or AI behavior understandable.

For example:

* A privacy notice may provide transparency.
* A decision explanation may explain why a particular outcome occurred.

**Transparency ≠ explanation.**

---

### 5. Transparency versus Explainability

Explainability concerns the ability to understand or communicate how an AI system produces or supports an outcome.

Transparency is broader and may include:

* Purpose
* Data categories
* Processing operations
* Recipients
* Retention
* AI involvement
* Rights

An explainable model can still operate within an inadequate transparency framework.

---

### 6. AI Transparency and Personal Data

Where AI processes personal data, transparency should address relevant processing in a manner appropriate to the individual and context.

Potentially relevant information includes:

* Source data
* Prompt data
* Retrieved information
* Memory
* Profiles
* Inferences
* Outputs
* Decisions

---

### 7. AI Processing Purpose Transparency

Individuals should receive appropriate information about why personal data is processed.

Purpose transparency should identify the actual processing purpose rather than use vague descriptions such as "AI improvement" when more specific information is appropriate.

**Technical capability ≠ processing purpose.**

---

### 8. AI Data-Category Transparency

Transparency should identify relevant categories of personal data being processed.

Categories may include:

* Identity information
* Contact information
* Behavioral data
* Transaction data
* Interaction history
* Inferred characteristics
* Profile information
* Sensitive information

---

### 9. AI Source Transparency

Where applicable, transparency should address the source of personal data.

Potential sources include:

* Individual-provided information
* Enterprise databases
* Public sources
* Third-party providers
* RAG repositories
* Operational systems
* AI-generated inferences

The organization should not imply that inferred information was directly supplied by the individual.

---

### 10. AI Derived-Data Transparency

AI can derive information from multiple data points.

Examples include:

* Risk scores
* Preferences
* Classifications
* Predictions
* Behavioral categories

Transparency should distinguish derived information from directly collected information.

**Derived data ≠ directly provided data.**

---

### 11. AI Inference Transparency

AI inference can create conclusions about an individual that were not explicitly recorded.

For example:

     +--> Risk
     +--> Classification
     +--> Prediction

Where relevant, individuals should receive appropriate information about such processing.

---

### 12. AI Profile Transparency

An AI-generated profile may contain:

* Attributes
* Scores
* Categories
* Predictions
* Risk classifications
* Behavioral characteristics

Transparency should make clear that a profile is an AI-generated or derived representation rather than necessarily an objective factual record.

---

### 13. Profiling Transparency

Where profiling occurs, transparency should identify relevant profiling activities where required.

Information may address:

* Existence of profiling
* Purpose
* Categories of data
* General logic or relevant factors
* Consequences
* Applicable rights

**Profiling ≠ automatically automated decision-making.**

---

### 14. Automated Decision Transparency

Where applicable automated decision-making occurs, transparency requirements may become more significant.

Individuals may need appropriate information about:

* The existence of automated decision-making
* Its significance
* Relevant logic or factors
* Expected consequences
* Available safeguards
* Human intervention
* Contestability

---

### 15. AI Recommendation versus AI Decision Transparency

AI recommendations and final decisions should be clearly distinguished.

```text id="atr14-03"

     |
     v
AI Model
     |
     v
Recommendation
     |
     v
Authorized Human / Decision System
     |
     v

```

The organization should not tell individuals that AI made the final decision when AI only provided a recommendation.

---

### 16. Human Involvement Transparency

Where humans participate in AI-assisted decisions, transparency should accurately describe their role.

The organization should distinguish between:

* Human review
* Human approval
* Human modification
* Human override
* Human rubber-stamping

**Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**

---

### 17. Meaningful Human Review

Human review should involve genuine evaluation rather than automatic confirmation of an AI output.

Relevant factors include:

* Reviewer competence
* Access to relevant information
* Authority to override
* Ability to challenge the model
* Sufficient time for review

---

### 18. AI Logic Transparency

Where applicable, individuals should receive meaningful information about the logic involved in AI processing.

This does not necessarily require disclosure of:

* Source code
* Model weights
* Proprietary algorithms
* Security mechanisms
* Trade secrets

The organization should provide meaningful information without unnecessarily exposing protected technical assets.

---

### 19. Explanation of AI Outcomes

An explanation should be relevant to the outcome being challenged.

For example, an explanation may identify:

* Relevant data categories
* Significant factors
* Applicable rules
* Model output
* Human intervention
* Decision pathway

The explanation should not falsely claim that a particular factor influenced an outcome if it did not.

---

### 20. Individual-Level Explanation

Aggregate model performance does not necessarily explain an individual's outcome.

```text id="atr14-04"
Aggregate Model
Performance
      |
      X
Does not automatically explain
      |
      v
Individual Outcome
```

An explanation should distinguish population-level statistics from individual-level decision logic.

**Aggregate accuracy ≠ individual-level explanation.**

---

### 21. AI Transparency and Accuracy

Transparency information should itself be accurate.

The organization should avoid statements that:

* Overstate human involvement
* Misrepresent data sources
* Hide provider processing
* Mischaracterize AI decisions
* Claim deletion capabilities that do not exist
* Claim that a system does not profile when it does

---

### 22. AI Transparency and Data Minimization

Transparency should provide sufficient information without unnecessarily exposing personal data.

The organization should avoid disclosing:

* Other individuals' information
* Confidential data
* Security secrets
* Unnecessary internal records

**Transparency ≠ unrestricted disclosure.**

---

### 23. AI Transparency and Security

Transparency mechanisms must be designed securely.

A transparency portal should not expose:

* Credentials
* Internal system prompts
* Security configurations
* Other users' data
* Confidential model information

Security controls should protect the transparency mechanism itself.

---

### 24. AI Transparency and Privacy

Transparency is a privacy requirement in many AI processing contexts, but transparency does not eliminate other privacy obligations.

An organization may provide an accurate notice while still violating:

* Data minimization
* Purpose limitation
* Access control
* Retention
* Security
* Rights requirements

**Transparency ≠ privacy compliance.**

---

### 25. AI Transparency and Data Subject Rights

Transparency enables individuals to understand and exercise applicable rights.

Information may help individuals determine whether they should request:

* Access
* Rectification
* Erasure
* Restriction
* Objection
* Portability
* Human review
* Contesting an applicable decision

---

### 26. AI Transparency Timing

Transparency should occur at an appropriate point in the processing lifecycle.

Depending on the processing context, relevant information may be provided:

* Before processing
* At collection
* When a new purpose begins
* When AI functionality changes
* When automated decisions become applicable
* When profiling is introduced

---

### 27. AI Transparency and Change Management

AI systems change frequently.

Changes may involve:

* Model
* Prompt
* RAG
* Memory
* Provider
* Data source
* Purpose
* Decision logic
* Automation level

Transparency information should be reassessed when such changes materially affect individuals.

---

### 28. AI Transparency and Model Changes

A model replacement can change:

* Outputs
* Risk
* Processing characteristics
* Inference behavior
* Decision recommendations
* Data handling

The organization should determine whether existing transparency information remains accurate.

---

### 29. AI Transparency and Prompt Changes

Prompt changes can modify system behavior without changing the underlying model.

For example, a prompt may cause the system to:

* Retrieve additional data
* Generate profiles
* Invoke tools
* Produce recommendations
* Apply new decision logic

Therefore:

**Prompt change ≠ merely a cosmetic change.**

---

### 30. AI Transparency and RAG

RAG introduces additional data sources into AI processing.

Transparency may need to account for:

* Retrieval sources
* Categories of retrieved information
* Purpose of retrieval
* Relevant data processing
* External knowledge sources

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

---

### 31. AI Transparency and Memory

Persistent memory can change the nature of AI interactions.

Transparency should address relevant:

* Memory existence
* Memory purpose
* Data categories
* Retention
* Use
* Applicable controls

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

### 32. AI Transparency and Tool Use

Examples include:

* Databases
* Search systems
* APIs

* Transaction systems

### 35. AI Transparency and Provider Data Use

Provider processing should be accurately represented.

If a provider uses data for an additional purpose, the organization should determine whether transparency information must reflect that processing.

**Provider capability ≠ permission to process.**

### 37. AI Transparency and Retention

Individuals should receive appropriate information about how long personal data is retained or the criteria used to determine retention where applicable.

### 38. AI Transparency and Deletion

Transparency should accurately describe applicable deletion mechanisms.

Organizations should avoid promising:

> "Deleting your data removes all traces from the AI model."

unless the organization can substantiate that statement.

**Source-data deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

---

### 39. AI Transparency and Model Memorization

Where personal data may be learned or memorized by models, governance should consider whether transparency information needs to account for the relevant processing.

The organization should understand:

* Training sources
* Model dependencies
* Memorization risks
* Extraction risks
* Available mitigation

---

### 40. AI Transparency and Explainability Boundaries

Explanations should be meaningful without exposing information that would create unacceptable risks.

Potential protected information includes:

* Security controls
* Credentials
* Detection logic
* Other individuals' data
* Confidential business information
* Proprietary model information

The organization should balance explainability with security and confidentiality.

---

### 41. AI Transparency and Contestability

Transparency should enable an individual to understand how to challenge applicable AI outcomes.

A contestability mechanism may provide:

* Decision information
* Relevant processing information
* Review mechanism
* Human contact
* Appeal pathway
* Correction mechanism

---

### 42. AI Transparency and Access

Transparency and access rights should be coordinated but not conflated.

**Transparency** provides information about processing.

**Access** may provide applicable personal data.

An individual may receive transparency information without receiving unrestricted access to the entire AI system.

---

### 43. AI Transparency and Documentation

Internal documentation should support external transparency.

Relevant internal documentation may include:

* AI system descriptions
* Data-flow diagrams
* Model documentation
* Processing records
* Provider inventories
* Decision logic
* Risk assessments

However:

**Internal documentation ≠ automatically appropriate external disclosure.**

### 44. AI Transparency and Evidence

Evidence may include:

* Model inventories

### 45. AI Transparency Testing

Transparency should be tested against the actual system.

Testing should verify that:

* Notices match actual processing
* AI involvement is accurately described
* Providers are correctly represented
* Profiling is disclosed where applicable
* Automated decisions are accurately described
* Human involvement is not overstated

### 46. AI Transparency Monitoring

Transparency should be continuously monitored for material divergence from system behavior.

Potential signals include:

* Undocumented model changes
* New data sources
* New providers
* New AI capabilities
* New profiling
* New automated decisions
* Changed retention
* Changed geographic processing

Responsibilities may include:

### 50. Core Principle of Explanation and Transparency Rights

The core principle is:

> **AI transparency must tell the truth about how the system actually processes data, uses AI, generates inferences, supports decisions, and affects individuals.**

The transparency chain should be:

```text id="atr14-07"
Individual
    |
    v
AI Processing Notice
    |
    +--> Purpose
    +--> Data
    +--> Source
    +--> AI Involvement
    +--> Profiling
    +--> Automated Decision
    +--> Human Involvement
    +--> Provider
    +--> Retention
    +--> Geography
    +--> Rights
    |
    v
Understanding
    |
    v
Explanation
    |
    v
Challenge / Review
    |
    v
Correction / Restriction / Other Applicable Right
```

The essential distinctions are:

**Transparency ≠ explanation.**

**Transparency ≠ explainability.**

**Transparency ≠ unrestricted disclosure.**

**Transparency ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Transparency ≠ source-code disclosure.**

**Transparency ≠ model-weight disclosure.**

**Transparency ≠ disclosure of security secrets.**

**AI recommendation ≠ final decision.**

**Model capability ≠ decision authority.**

**Model output ≠ authorized decision.**

**Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**

**Human review ≠ automatic independence.**

**Profiling ≠ automatically automated decision-making.**

**Inference ≠ factual truth.**

**Derived data ≠ directly provided data.**

**Aggregate accuracy ≠ individual-level explanation.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Provider capability ≠ permission to process personal data.**

**Source-data deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

**Documentation ≠ technical enforcement.**

**Control implementation ≠ control effectiveness.**

**Transparency completion ≠ permanent transparency assurance.**

A mature AI transparency architecture therefore ensures that the information communicated to individuals remains **accurate, synchronized with the actual AI system, understandable at the appropriate level, protected against unauthorized disclosure, connected to applicable rights, and reassessed whenever material changes alter how AI processes or affects individuals**.

## Part 2 — Explanation and Transparency Rights Controls

### 51. AI Explanation and Transparency Control Framework

The organization should establish a formal control framework governing transparency and explanation across AI systems.

The framework should address:

* Processing transparency
* AI involvement
* Data categories
* Data sources
* Profiling
* Automated decision-making
* Human involvement
* Explainability
* Provider processing
* Retention
* Cross-border processing
* Individual rights
* Contestability
* Evidence
* Testing
* Monitoring

The framework should apply throughout the AI lifecycle.

---

### 52. AI Transparency Planning Control

Transparency requirements should be identified during AI system planning.

Planning should determine:

* Intended audience
* Processing purpose
* Data categories
* AI capabilities
* Profiling
* Automated decisions
* Human involvement
* Providers
* Geographic processing
* Retention
* Applicable rights

Transparency should not be added only after deployment.

---

### 53. AI Transparency Scope Control

The organization should define which AI processing activities require transparency controls.

Scope should include relevant:

* Applications
* Models
* Data sources
* Prompts
* RAG repositories
* Vector stores
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* Profiles
* Decision systems
* Providers

---

### 54. AI Transparency Classification Control

AI processing should be classified according to its transparency and individual-impact requirements.

Higher-risk classifications may involve:

* Sensitive personal data
* Profiling
* Automated decision-making
* Significant individual effects
* High-impact use cases
* Extensive inference
* Agentic actions

Classification should determine the depth of transparency and explanation controls.

---

### 55. AI Transparency Applicability Control

The organization should determine which transparency and explanation requirements apply to each AI processing activity.

Assessment should consider:

* Jurisdiction
* Processing purpose
* Data subjects
* Personal-data processing
* Profiling
* Automated decisions
* Human involvement
* Provider processing
* Applicable exemptions

**Transparency applicability ≠ automatic disclosure of all system information.**

---

* AI autonomy
* Profiling
* Data sensitivity
* Model complexity
* Provider involvement
* Processing scale

* Understandable
* Consistent with actual processing

Methods may include:

* Feature analysis
* Decision factors
* Rule tracing
* Model documentation
* Local explanations

The selected method should match the use case and risk.

**Aggregate model performance ≠ individual-level explanation.**

* Omitting material factors
* Misrepresenting human involvement
