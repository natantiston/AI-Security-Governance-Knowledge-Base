# 14.06 — AI Impact Assessment versus DPIA

## Part 1 — AI Impact Assessment versus DPIA Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Impact Assessment

An AI Impact Assessment is a structured process used to identify, evaluate, and manage the broader impacts that an AI system may have on individuals, groups, organizations, society, and other affected stakeholders.

It can address multiple impact domains beyond privacy.

```text id="aia14p6-01"
                    AI System
                        |
        +---------------+---------------+
        |               |               |
     Privacy         Security         Ethics
        |               |               |
      DPIA          Security RA     AI Impact
        |               |               |
        +---------------+---------------+
                        |
                        v
                Enterprise AI Risk
```

---

### 2. Purpose of an AI Impact Assessment

The purpose of an AI Impact Assessment is to understand the broader consequences of deploying or operating an AI system and determine whether appropriate safeguards are required.

Potential impact domains include:

* Privacy
* Security
* Safety
* Fairness
* Discrimination
* Human rights
* Accessibility
* Transparency
* Employment
* Financial impact
* Social impact
* Operational impact

---

### 3. Definition of DPIA

A Data Protection Impact Assessment is a privacy-focused assessment concerning processing of personal data and risks to individuals' rights and freedoms.

A DPIA therefore has a narrower regulatory and privacy purpose than a general AI Impact Assessment.

**AI Impact Assessment ≠ DPIA.**

---

### 4. Relationship Between AI Impact Assessment and DPIA

An AI Impact Assessment and DPIA can operate together.

```text id="aia14p6-02"
                 AI Impact Assessment
                         |
       +-----------------+-----------------+
       |                 |                 |
    Privacy           Security          Safety
       |
      DPIA
       |
       v
Privacy Risks
```

The DPIA can form a specialized privacy assessment within a broader AI impact-management framework.

---

### 5. Scope of AI Impact Assessment

An AI Impact Assessment may examine the complete AI system and its consequences.

Its scope can include:

* Model
* Data
* Users
* Decisions
* Automation
* Human interaction
* Business processes
* External stakeholders
* Providers
* Deployment environment

---

### 6. Scope of DPIA

A DPIA focuses on processing of personal data and associated risks to individuals.

The scope should include relevant:

* Personal data
* Processing purposes
* Processing operations
* Data subjects
* Data flows
* Recipients
* Providers
* Retention
* Safeguards

---

AI impact assessment should begin early in the AI lifecycle.

---

### 8. DPIA Lifecycle

A DPIA should similarly operate across the relevant personal-data processing lifecycle.

---

### 9. Primary Objective Difference

The primary objective of an AI Impact Assessment is broader impact management.

The primary objective of a DPIA is assessing privacy risks arising from personal-data processing.

```text
AI Impact Assessment
        |
        +--> Privacy
        +--> Security
        +--> Safety
        +--> Fairness
        +--> Human Rights
        +--> Other Impacts

DPIA
        |
        +--> Personal Data
        +--> Privacy
        +--> Rights and Freedoms
```

---

### 10. Regulatory Role of DPIA

A DPIA can be a specific regulatory requirement under applicable data-protection law when processing is likely to result in a high risk to individuals.

An AI Impact Assessment may be required by other regulatory regimes, organizational policies, contractual requirements, or enterprise AI governance frameworks.

---

### 11. AI Impact Assessment versus Privacy Risk Assessment

Privacy risk assessment examines the potential consequences of processing personal data.

AI Impact Assessment can include privacy risk but may additionally evaluate:

* Bias
* Discrimination
* Safety
* Explainability
* Human oversight
* Accessibility
* Social consequences

Therefore:

**Privacy risk ≠ total AI impact.**

---

### 12. AI Impact Assessment versus Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

Cybersecurity risk assessment focuses primarily on threats to information, systems, services, and organizations.

AI Impact Assessment considers broader consequences of AI use.

```text id="aia14p6-05"
AI System
   |
   +--> Privacy Risk
   |
   +--> Cybersecurity Risk
   |
   +--> Safety Risk
   |
   +--> Fairness Risk
   |
   +--> Human-Rights Risk
   |
   +--> Operational Risk
```

A security assessment therefore does not replace either an AI Impact Assessment or a DPIA.

---

### 13. AI Impact Assessment versus AI Risk Assessment

AI risk assessment generally identifies and evaluates risks associated with AI.

AI Impact Assessment can place greater emphasis on consequences to affected stakeholders and society.

The terms may overlap operationally, but organizations should define their terminology clearly.

---

### 14. Privacy as One AI Impact Domain

Privacy should be treated as a distinct impact domain within broader AI governance.

```text id="aia14p6-06"
                 AI Impact
                    |
     +--------------+--------------+
     |              |              |
  Privacy        Safety         Fairness
     |              |              |
    DPIA       Safety Review    AI Review
```

The privacy assessment should not disappear into a generic AI risk score.

---

### 15. Personal Data as a DPIA Boundary

The presence of personal data is a fundamental boundary for DPIA analysis.

The assessment should determine:

* Whether personal data is processed
* What categories are involved
* Whose data is processed
* Why it is processed
* How it is processed
* Where it is processed

---

### 16. AI Capability versus Privacy Authorization

An AI system may technically be capable of processing personal information without being authorized to do so.

**AI capability ≠ privacy authorization.**

The distinction should be enforced architecturally and governed explicitly.

---

### 17. AI Impact versus AI Capability

The fact that an AI model can perform a particular function does not establish that the organization should deploy that capability.

For example, a model may technically be capable of:

* Profiling employees
* Predicting customer behavior
* Inferring sensitive attributes
* Recommending employment decisions

Capability must therefore be evaluated separately from impact and authorization.

---

### 18. DPIA versus AI Impact Assessment Evidence

The evidence requirements may overlap but are not necessarily identical.

AI Impact Assessment evidence may include:

* AI system description
* Stakeholder analysis
* Impact analysis
* Risk assessment
* Human oversight
* Testing
* Governance decisions

DPIA evidence additionally focuses on personal-data processing and privacy safeguards.

---

### 19. AI Stakeholder Impact Assessment

An AI Impact Assessment should identify stakeholders affected by the system.

Potential stakeholders include:

* Direct users
* Employees
* Customers
* Applicants
* Individuals evaluated by AI
* Communities
* Business partners
* Regulators
* The public

---

### 20. DPIA Data-Subject Analysis

A DPIA should identify the individuals whose personal data is processed and evaluate potential risks to them.

The analysis should consider:

* Vulnerability
* Data sensitivity
* Scale
* Consequences
* Exposure
* Ability to exercise rights

---

### 21. AI Impact Assessment Human-Rights Dimension

A broader AI Impact Assessment may evaluate whether AI creates risks involving:

* Equality
* Non-discrimination
* Autonomy
* Human dignity
* Freedom
* Access to services
* Employment opportunities

These considerations can complement, but do not automatically replace, DPIA analysis.

---

### 22. AI Impact Assessment Fairness Dimension

AI systems that classify, rank, recommend, or predict outcomes for individuals may require fairness analysis.

The assessment may examine:

* Protected characteristics
* Proxy variables
* Bias
* Error distribution
* Differential outcomes
* Disparate impacts

---

### 23. AI Impact Assessment Safety Dimension

AI systems operating in safety-sensitive environments should be assessed for potential physical or operational harm.

Examples include AI used in:

* Healthcare
* Industrial environments
* Transportation
* Critical infrastructure
* Security operations

Safety assessment is distinct from privacy assessment.

---

### 24. AI Impact Assessment Security Dimension

Security should be assessed as a separate risk domain.

Relevant threats include:

* Prompt injection
* Data leakage
* Unauthorized access
* Model manipulation
* Tool abuse
* Agent compromise
* Supply-chain attacks

Security findings may influence DPIA conclusions where they create privacy risks.

---

### 25. AI Impact Assessment Transparency Dimension

Transparency should be assessed according to the role and impact of the AI system.

Relevant considerations include:

* AI disclosure
* Processing information
* Decision logic
* Limitations
* Human involvement
* Communication to affected individuals

**Transparency ≠ meaningful transparency.**

---

### 26. AI Impact Assessment Human-Oversight Dimension

The assessment should determine whether meaningful human oversight is necessary.

Where human review is used as a safeguard, the human should have sufficient:

* Information
* Authority
* Competence
* Time
* Independence
* Ability to challenge AI output

---

### 27. AI Impact Assessment Automation Dimension

The assessment should identify how much authority is delegated to the AI system.

```text id="aia14p6-07"
Assistance
    |
Recommendation
    |
Prioritization
    |
Decision Support
    |
Automated Decision
    |
Autonomous Action
```

Increasing autonomy can increase the potential impact and may require stronger governance.

---

### 28. DPIA and Automated Decision-Making

Where AI processes personal data for automated decision-making, the DPIA should specifically evaluate the associated privacy and individual-rights risks.

The existence of an AI Impact Assessment does not eliminate the need for applicable DPIA analysis.

---

### 29. AI Impact Assessment Profiling

Profiling should be evaluated as a potential AI impact area.

The assessment should consider:

* Data inputs
* Predictions
* Inferences
* Profile creation
* Consequences
* Accuracy
* Individual safeguards

---

### 30. DPIA Profiling Analysis

Where profiling involves personal data, the DPIA should evaluate its privacy implications.

This may include:

* Data sources
* Processing purpose
* Inferences
* Accuracy
* Retention
* Access
* Individual impact

### 41. AI Impact Assessment Residual Impact

After safeguards are implemented, the organization should evaluate residual impact.

The question is not simply:

> “Do controls exist?”

It is:

> “What impact remains after the controls operate?”

---

### 42. DPIA Residual Privacy Risk

The DPIA should separately evaluate residual privacy risk after safeguards.

The broader assessment should be reassessed when material changes alter the system's impact profile.

Examples include:

* New model

* New users
* New purpose
* New autonomy
* New provider
* New geography
* New downstream integration

### 49. Enterprise AI Impact and DPIA Principle

An enterprise should treat the AI Impact Assessment and DPIA as **related but distinct governance instruments**.

```text id="aia14p6-13"
              Enterprise AI Assessment
                       |
          +------------+------------+
          |                         |
   Broader AI Impact            Privacy Impact
          |                         |
   +------+------+                  DPIA
   |      |      |
Safety Fairness Security
          |
          +----------+
                     |
                     v
             Enterprise Decision
```

The broader assessment can coordinate multiple impact domains, while the DPIA retains its specific privacy purpose and requirements.

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Impact Assessment versus DPIA

**An AI Impact Assessment and a DPIA should be integrated within the same AI governance ecosystem but should not be treated as interchangeable assessments.**

An **AI Impact Assessment** evaluates the broader consequences of an AI system, potentially covering privacy, security, safety, fairness, discrimination, human rights, transparency, autonomy, operational impact, and other relevant dimensions.

A **DPIA** specifically evaluates personal-data processing and the risks that processing may pose to individuals' rights and freedoms.

The fundamental distinctions are:

**AI Impact Assessment ≠ DPIA.**

**DPIA ≠ generic AI risk assessment.**

**DPIA ≠ cybersecurity risk assessment.**

**AI impact ≠ privacy risk.**

**Privacy risk ≠ cybersecurity risk.**

**AI capability ≠ authorization.**

**AI capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

**Technical feasibility ≠ necessity.**

**Model capability ≠ decision authority.**

**Model output ≠ authorized decision.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**

**Security control ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Risk acceptance ≠ lawful authorization.**

**DPIA completion ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

**AI Impact Assessment completion ≠ permanent AI assurance.**

An effective enterprise approach uses the AI Impact Assessment to understand the **broader impact landscape**, while using the DPIA to perform the **specific and deeper privacy assessment required for personal-data processing**. Neither assessment should be reduced to a checklist; both should remain connected to the actual AI architecture, processing activity, risks, safeguards, evidence, governance decisions, and material changes.
