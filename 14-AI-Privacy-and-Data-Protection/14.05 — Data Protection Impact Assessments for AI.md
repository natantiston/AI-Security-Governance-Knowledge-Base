# 14.05 — Data Protection Impact Assessments for AI

## Part 1 — Data Protection Impact Assessments for AI Foundations

### 1. Definition of a Data Protection Impact Assessment for AI

A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for AI is a structured process for identifying, assessing, and addressing risks that an AI processing activity may pose to the rights and freedoms of individuals.

For AI systems, the DPIA should consider not only the data-processing purpose but also the technical characteristics of the AI architecture.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI DPIA

The purpose of a DPIA is to determine whether an AI processing activity creates significant privacy risk and, where appropriate, identify measures to reduce that risk before and during processing.


Organizations should first determine whether a DPIA is required for the proposed AI processing.

Screening should consider:

* Nature of processing
* Scope
* Context
* Purpose
* Types of personal data
* Individuals affected
* Potential risks

### 7. Data Categories

The DPIA should identify the categories of personal data processed.

This may include:

* Identity data
* Contact information
* Employment information
* Financial information
* Location information
* Behavioral information
* Sensitive information

---

### 8. Special-Category Data

Where applicable, the DPIA should explicitly identify special-category personal data.

Additional safeguards may be necessary because of the increased potential impact on individuals.

---

### 9. Data Subjects

The assessment should identify the individuals affected by the AI processing.

Potential groups include:

* Customers
* Employees
* Applicants
* Patients
* Students
* Citizens
* Suppliers
* Members of the public

---

### 10. Scale of Processing

The DPIA should consider the scale of processing.

Relevant dimensions include:

* Number of individuals
* Volume of records
* Processing frequency
* Geographic scope
* Duration
* Number of AI interactions

---

### 11. Processing Purpose

The DPIA should clearly define why personal data is being processed.

Purpose should be specific enough to determine whether the proposed AI processing is necessary and proportionate.

---

### 12. Lawfulness of Processing

The DPIA should identify the applicable legal basis for processing.

The DPIA itself does not create a legal basis.

**DPIA ≠ lawful basis.**

---

### 13. Necessity Assessment

The organization should determine whether the proposed personal-data processing is necessary for the intended purpose.

Technical ability to process information does not establish necessity.

---

### 14. Proportionality Assessment

The DPIA should assess whether the processing is proportionate to the intended objective.

The organization should consider whether equivalent objectives can be achieved with:

* Less data
* Less retention
* Less intrusive processing
* Less automation
* Less extensive monitoring

---

### 15. AI Privacy Risk Identification

The DPIA should identify risks created by AI-specific processing.

Potential risks include:

* Data leakage
* Unauthorized inference
* Profiling
* Memorization
* Cross-user exposure
* Cross-tenant exposure
* Excessive retention
* Incorrect personal information
* Automated decision impacts

---

### 16. Risk to Rights and Freedoms

The assessment should focus on potential impacts to individuals rather than only technical vulnerabilities.

Potential impacts include:

* Discrimination
* Loss of privacy
* Financial harm
* Reputational harm
* Loss of opportunities
* Loss of autonomy
* Unauthorized disclosure

### 24. Model Memorization Risk

AI models may reproduce information contained in training or fine-tuning data.

* Behavioral characteristics
* Preferences
* Risk scores
* Predictions
* Sensitive attributes

### 50. Core Principle of Data Protection Impact Assessments for AI

**An AI DPIA is a risk-based privacy assessment that determines how an AI processing activity may affect individuals, whether the processing is necessary and proportionate, which safeguards are required, what residual risks remain, and whether those risks can be appropriately managed.**

The DPIA should evaluate the complete AI processing ecosystem rather than treating the model as an isolated component.

The fundamental principles are:

**DPIA ≠ generic AI risk assessment.**

**DPIA ≠ lawful basis.**

**DPIA ≠ security assessment.**

**DPIA ≠ automatic authorization.**

**Technical feasibility ≠ necessity.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

**Model capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Model output ≠ legal authorization.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Security control ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**

**DPIA completion ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

**A DPIA is effective only when it is based on the actual AI processing architecture, considers risks to individuals, identifies appropriate safeguards, evaluates residual risk, maintains evidence, and is revisited when the AI system, data, processing purpose, provider, geography, autonomy, or risk profile materially changes.**

### 52. AI DPIA Planning Control

The organization should establish a documented plan for conducting the DPIA.

The plan should define:

* Scope
* Objectives
* Stakeholders
* Assessment methodology
* Required evidence
* Review stages
* Approval requirements
* Reassessment triggers

### 53. AI DPIA Scope Control

The DPIA scope should identify the complete AI processing activity.

### 54. AI DPIA Applicability Control

A formal screening control should determine whether a DPIA is required.

The decision should be based on the characteristics and risks of the actual processing rather than simply the presence of AI.

Evidence should document:

* Screening criteria
* Facts considered
* Decision
* Decision owner
* Date
* Supporting evidence

---

### 55. AI DPIA Screening Control

Organizations should use standardized screening questions to identify potentially high-risk processing.

Screening should examine:

* Scale
* Sensitivity
* Systematic monitoring
* Profiling
* Automated decisions
* Vulnerable individuals
* New technologies
* Cross-border processing
* Potential severity of harm

---

### 56. AI DPIA Risk Classification Control

AI processing should be classified according to its privacy-risk characteristics.

A classification model may distinguish:

| Level    | Example                                                            |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Low      | Non-personal AI processing                                         |
| Moderate | Limited internal personal-data processing                          |
| High     | Extensive profiling or sensitive processing                        |
| Critical | Processing capable of producing significant effects on individuals |

The organization should define its own classification criteria consistently with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

---

Data-flow diagrams should be validated against the actual implementation.

Validation should identify discrepancies between:

* Documented architecture
* Deployed architecture
* Application configuration
* Provider configuration
* Actual network flows
* Data stores

A diagram that does not represent actual processing cannot provide reliable DPIA evidence.

---

### 60. AI DPIA Personal-Data Inventory Control

The organization should maintain an inventory of personal-data categories processed by the AI system.

The inventory should identify:

* Data category
* Source
* Purpose
* Processing component
* Recipient
* Retention
* Sensitivity
* Applicable controls

---

### 61. AI DPIA Processing-Purpose Control

Each processing activity should have a clearly defined purpose.

The control should prevent undefined or unnecessarily broad purposes such as:

> “Use AI to improve business operations.”

Instead, the purpose should be sufficiently specific to evaluate necessity and proportionality.

---

### 62. AI DPIA Data-Minimization Control

The DPIA should require assessment of whether the AI system processes more personal data than necessary.

The assessment should examine:

* Prompt fields
* Context
* RAG results
* Memory
* Training datasets
* Fine-tuning datasets
* Logs
* Outputs

---

### 63. AI DPIA Necessity Assessment Control

The organization should document why each significant category of personal-data processing is necessary.

The assessment should identify:

* Intended objective
* Data required
* Processing operation
* Alternative approaches
* Less intrusive alternatives considered
* Reason the selected approach is necessary

---

### 64. AI DPIA Proportionality Assessment Control

The DPIA should assess whether the benefits and objectives of processing are proportionate to the risks imposed on individuals.

Factors should include:

* Intrusiveness
* Scale
* Duration
* Sensitivity
* Automation
* Individual impact
* Available safeguards

---

### 65. AI DPIA Lawfulness Assessment Control

The DPIA process should require identification and evaluation of the applicable legal basis and other relevant legal requirements.

The control should explicitly prevent the DPIA from being treated as the legal basis itself.

**DPIA completion ≠ lawful authorization.**

---

* Necessity
* Safeguards

* Access

### 67. AI DPIA Automated-Decision Control

AI DPIA controls must establish an enforceable and traceable relationship between **processing activity, legal and privacy requirements, risk to individuals, necessity, proportionality, safeguards, residual risk, evidence, approval, monitoring, and reassessment**.

**DPIA ≠ generic AI risk assessment.**

**DPIA ≠ cybersecurity risk assessment.**

**DPIA ≠ lawful basis.**

**Technical feasibility ≠ necessity.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

**Model capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Model output ≠ authorization.**

**Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**

**Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**

**Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**

**Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Provider capability ≠ permission to process personal data.**

**Security control ≠ privacy compliance.**

**Documented safeguard ≠ effective safeguard.**

**Risk acceptance ≠ lawful authorization.**

**DPIA completion ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

**Privacy testing ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

**Increase monitoring.**

**Contain.**

**Suspend.**

**Rollback.**

**Revoke access.**

**Reauthorize.**
