# 14.20 — AI Privacy Governance and DPO Operating Model

## Part 1 — AI Privacy Governance and DPO Operating Model Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Privacy Governance

AI Privacy Governance is the organizational framework used to ensure that artificial intelligence systems process personal and privacy-sensitive information in a lawful, fair, transparent, secure, necessary, and accountable manner.

It connects:

* AI governance;
* privacy governance;
* data governance;
* information security;
* legal requirements;
* risk management;
* AI engineering;
* architecture;
* procurement;
* and operational oversight.

AI privacy governance should cover the complete lifecycle:

```text id="ai-privacy-gov-1"
Business Purpose
      |
      v
AI Use Case
      |
      v
Data Identification
      |
      v
Privacy Assessment
      |
      v
Architecture
      |
      v
Development
      |
      v
Testing
      |
      v

      |
      v
Deployment
      |
      v
Monitoring
      |
      v
Change
      |
      v
Reassessment
      |
      v
Retirement / Deletion
```

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Privacy Governance

The purpose of AI privacy governance is to ensure that AI capabilities do not expand an organization's authority to process personal information beyond what is justified and approved.

Governance should ensure that:

* processing purposes are defined;
* personal data is identified;
* processing is necessary;
* data is minimized;
* appropriate legal requirements are addressed;
* risks are assessed;
* controls are implemented;
* data-subject rights are supported;
* providers are governed;
* and ongoing compliance is demonstrated.

**AI capability ≠ privacy authorization.**

---

### 3. AI Privacy Governance Lifecycle

AI privacy governance should operate throughout the AI lifecycle.

The lifecycle should include:

1. use-case identification;
2. data discovery;
3. classification;
4. privacy assessment;
5. legal assessment;
6. risk assessment;
7. architecture;
8. control design;
9. testing;
10. approval;
11. deployment;
12. monitoring;
13. incident response;
14. change management;
15. reassessment;
16. reauthorization;
17. retirement;
18. deletion.

A privacy review performed only before deployment is insufficient for continuously changing AI systems.

---

### 4. DPO Role in AI Governance

The Data Protection Officer (DPO), where an organization is required to appoint one, provides independent privacy oversight and advice within the organization's AI governance ecosystem.

The DPO may contribute to:

* privacy assessments;
* DPIAs;
* data-protection advice;
* data-subject rights;
* regulatory engagement;
* monitoring compliance;
* awareness;
* privacy-risk escalation;
* and AI governance.

The DPO should not be treated as the operational owner of every privacy control.

**DPO oversight ≠ operational control ownership.**

---

### 5. DPO Independence

The DPO operating model should preserve appropriate independence.

The DPO should be able to:

* provide objective advice;
* identify compliance concerns;
* challenge decisions;
* escalate significant risks;
* communicate with senior management;
* and interact with supervisory authorities where applicable.

The DPO should not be placed in a position where they effectively approve their own operational decisions.

---

### 6. DPO versus AI Governance Function

The DPO and AI governance function have complementary responsibilities.

| Function        | Primary Focus                           |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| DPO             | Data-protection oversight and advice    |
| AI Governance   | AI lifecycle governance                 |
| Security        | Security and confidentiality            |
| Data Governance | Data quality, ownership, classification |
| Legal           | Legal interpretation                    |
| Risk            | Enterprise risk                         |
| Architecture    | Technical design                        |
| AI Engineering  | AI implementation                       |

The DPO should participate in AI governance without becoming the sole owner of AI risk.

---

### 7. DPO versus Data Owner

The data owner determines appropriate organizational control over specific information assets.

The DPO provides privacy oversight and advice.

For example:

```text id="ai-privacy-gov-7"

    +--> Business Purpose
    +--> Access Authorization
    |
    v

    |
    v
DPO / Privacy
    |

    +--> DPIA Where Applicable
    +--> Rights / Transparency
```

**Data ownership ≠ privacy oversight.**

### 13. AI Privacy Methodology

The organization should establish a standardized AI privacy assessment methodology.

The methodology should evaluate:

### 14. AI Privacy Risk Management

AI privacy risk management identifies, evaluates, treats, and monitors risks associated with AI processing.

Risks may include:

* unauthorized disclosure;
* excessive collection;
* inaccurate data;
* unlawful processing;
* re-identification;
* profiling;
* automated decisions;
* model memorization;
* data leakage;
* excessive retention;
* provider misuse;
* and inadequate rights handling.

The risk process should connect privacy risks to specific controls.

---

### 15. AI Privacy Risk Assessment

A privacy risk assessment should evaluate:

**Threat → Vulnerability → Processing Exposure → Individual Impact → Likelihood → Consequence → Control → Residual Risk.**

For AI, additional considerations may include:

* model behavior;
* inference;
* RAG;
* memory;
* tools;
* agents;
* training;
* fine-tuning;
* and model-generated personal information.

**AI risk assessment ≠ automatic DPIA.**

The appropriate assessment should depend on the nature and risk of the processing.

---

### 16. AI Privacy Impact Assessment

Where required, a Data Protection Impact Assessment should evaluate high-risk processing involving personal data.

An AI-related DPIA may consider:

* processing purpose;
* necessity;
* proportionality;
* data categories;
* affected individuals;
* processing operations;
* risks;
* safeguards;
* and residual risk.

The DPIA should be integrated into the AI lifecycle rather than treated as an isolated document.

---

### 17. DPIA versus AI Risk Assessment

These assessments should remain conceptually distinct.

| AI Risk Assessment     | DPIA                                |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Broad AI risk          | Data-protection risk                |
| May include security   | Focuses on personal-data processing |
| Model risks            | Individual privacy impacts          |
| Operational risks      | Data-protection requirements        |
| AI system risk         | Processing risk                     |
| Enterprise AI controls | Privacy safeguards                  |

Therefore:

**DPIA ≠ generic AI risk assessment.**

An organization may need both.

---

### 18. Privacy by Design Governance

Privacy should be incorporated into AI architecture from the beginning.

Privacy-by-design considerations should include:

* minimization;
* purpose limitation;
* access control;
* encryption;
* pseudonymization;
* privacy-enhancing technologies;
* retention;
* deletion;
* transparency;
* and rights support.

Privacy should not be added only after the model has been built.

---

### 19. Privacy by Default Governance

AI systems should use privacy-protective defaults where appropriate.

Examples include:

* minimum data collection;
* restricted retention;
* disabled unnecessary memory;
* limited logging;
* restricted provider training use;
* minimal tool permissions;
* restricted RAG access;
* and limited data sharing.

The default configuration should not expose more information than necessary.

### 21. Personal-Data Identification in AI

AI systems may process personal data in:

* prompts;
* uploaded documents;
* training datasets;
* fine-tuning datasets;
* RAG repositories;
* vector embeddings;
* memory;
* logs;
* outputs;
* and inferred attributes.

Therefore, privacy governance should identify personal data across both explicit and derived AI artifacts.

**Personal data ≠ only structured database fields.**

---

### 22. Sensitive-Data Governance

AI privacy governance should identify data requiring heightened protection.

Depending on the applicable legal and organizational framework, this may include:

* special-category personal data;
* financial information;
* health information;
* credentials;
* children's data;
* precise location;
* employment information;
* biometric information;
* and other highly sensitive information.

Sensitive data should trigger appropriate additional controls.

---

### 23. Data-Minimization Governance

The DPO operating model should promote data minimization across AI workflows.

Governance should ask:

* Is the data necessary?
* Can fewer fields be used?
* Can data be aggregated?
* Can data be pseudonymized?
* Can synthetic data be used?
* Can local processing reduce disclosure?
* Can the task be completed without persistent memory?

**Data availability ≠ permission to process.**

---

### 24. Purpose-Limitation Governance

Every significant AI processing activity should have a defined purpose.

Purpose governance should prevent unauthorized secondary use.

For example:

**Customer-service summarization**

does not automatically authorize:

**Employee behavioral profiling.**

Likewise:

**Fraud detection**

does not automatically authorize:

**General-purpose model training.**

**Purpose limitation ≠ technical capability limitation alone.**

---

### 25. Legal-Basis Governance

Where personal data is processed, the organization should identify the applicable legal basis and ensure that processing remains consistent with it.

Governance should evaluate:

* purpose;
* data category;
* individuals affected;
* processing operation;
* necessity;
* provider involvement;
* and changes in processing.

A legal basis for one processing activity does not automatically authorize another.

---

### 26. Special-Category Data Governance

Where AI processes special-category personal data, governance should determine whether additional legal and technical requirements apply.

Controls may include:

* strict access;
* minimization;
* encryption;
* pseudonymization;
* enhanced monitoring;
* restricted providers;
* and additional impact assessment.

The AI system should not process highly sensitive data merely because the model can technically accept it.

---

### 27. Automated-Decision Governance

Governance should determine:

* whether the decision is automated;
* the significance of the decision;
* human involvement;
* decision authority;
* transparency;
* contestability;
* and applicable safeguards.

**Model capability ≠ decision authority.**

### 49. Enterprise AI Privacy Governance Principle

Enterprise AI privacy governance should establish a continuous connection between:

**Business Purpose → AI Use Case → Data → Processing → Legal Requirements → Privacy Risk → Controls → Evidence → Monitoring → Change → Reassessment.**

The DPO operating model should be embedded into this lifecycle while preserving clear separation between:

* privacy oversight;
* business ownership;
* technical control ownership;
* legal interpretation;
* security;
* and AI engineering.

The following distinctions are fundamental:

* **AI capability ≠ privacy authorization.**
* **Data availability ≠ permission to process.**
* **Data relevance ≠ authorization.**
* **Technical feasibility ≠ necessity.**
* **DPIA ≠ generic AI risk assessment.**
* **DPIA ≠ lawful basis.**
* **DPO oversight ≠ operational control ownership.**
* **Human presence ≠ meaningful human involvement.**
* **Profiling capability ≠ profiling authorization.**
* **Model capability ≠ decision authority.**
* **Model output ≠ authorized decision.**
* **Provider capability ≠ permission to process personal data.**
* **Cloud hosting ≠ transfer of privacy accountability.**
* **Cloud region ≠ complete data-location visibility.**
* **Authentication ≠ authorization.**
* **Retrieval capability ≠ retrieval authorization.**
* **Memory capability ≠ permission to retain.**
* **Tool capability ≠ data-access authorization.**
* **Agent capability ≠ privacy authority.**
* **Security assurance ≠ privacy compliance.**
* **Documentation ≠ technical enforcement.**
* **Control implementation ≠ control effectiveness.**
* **Testing ≠ permanent assurance.**

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Privacy Governance and the DPO Operating Model

The fundamental principle is:

> **AI privacy governance must ensure that every AI processing activity involving personal information has a defined purpose, appropriate authorization, proportionate risk assessment, accountable ownership, effective safeguards, demonstrable evidence, and continuous oversight throughout its lifecycle.**

The DPO operating model should provide independent privacy oversight without becoming a substitute for business, technical, security, data, legal, or AI accountability.

The complete governance model is:

**Purpose → Data → Necessity → Legal Requirements → Privacy Risk → DPO/Privacy Oversight → Architecture → Controls → Approval → Deployment → Monitoring → Rights → Incident Response → Change → Reassessment → Reauthorization → Retirement.**

The central principle is:

**AI capability must never be allowed to become privacy authority by default.**

## Part 3 — AI Privacy Governance and DPO Operating Model Governance

### 118. AI Privacy Governance Operating Model

The AI Privacy Governance Operating Model defines how privacy responsibilities, decision rights, oversight, escalation, and accountability operate across the AI lifecycle.

The operating model should connect:

* Board and executive oversight;
* DPO;
* privacy function;
* AI governance;
* information security;
* legal;
* data governance;
* risk management;
* business owners;
* data owners;
* AI system owners;
* engineering;
* procurement;
* and internal audit.

A mature model separates **policy ownership, operational control ownership, independent oversight, and assurance**.

```text id="privacy-governance-operating-model"
Executive / Board Oversight
          |
          v
AI Governance ---- DPO / Privacy Oversight
      |                    |
      +---------+----------+
                |
        Risk / Legal / Security
                |
        Business & Data Owners
                |
       AI / Engineering Teams
                |
       AI Systems & Providers
```

The DPO should not become the operational owner of every privacy control simply because the DPO provides independent oversight.

Ownership should distinguish:

| Function          | Primary Responsibility                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Board / Executive | Strategic accountability and risk oversight |

| DPO / Privacy     | Independent privacy advice and oversight    |

### 177. Strategic Governance Principle

> **AI privacy governance should establish clear accountability while preserving the DPO's appropriate independence, integrating privacy into AI decision-making, and ensuring that every material AI processing activity has a documented purpose, lawful governance basis, accountable owner, enforceable controls, appropriate oversight, and continuous reassessment.**

The operating model should maintain a deliberate separation between:

**Operational Ownership → Governance → Independent Privacy Oversight → Independent Assurance**

The DPO should advise, monitor, challenge, and escalate where appropriate, while business and technology functions remain accountable for operating compliant AI systems.

The fundamental governance distinctions are:

* **DPO oversight ≠ operational control ownership.**
* **Privacy governance ≠ AI engineering.**
* **DPO independence ≠ lack of organizational accountability.**
* **AI governance ≠ privacy governance.**
* **Security governance ≠ privacy governance.**
* **Data ownership ≠ DPO ownership.**
* **Assessment ≠ approval.**
* **Approval ≠ permanent authorization.**
* **Documentation ≠ enforcement.**
* **Control implementation ≠ control effectiveness.**
* **Human review ≠ meaningful human involvement.**
* **Model capability ≠ decision authority.**
* **Provider capability ≠ processing authorization.**
* **Data availability ≠ permission to process.**
* **Risk acceptance ≠ lawful authorization.**
* **Testing ≠ permanent assurance.**
