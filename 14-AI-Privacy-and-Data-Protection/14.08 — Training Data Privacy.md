# 14.08 — Training Data Privacy

## Part 1 — Training Data Privacy Foundations

### 1. Definition of Training Data Privacy

Training data privacy refers to the governance and protection of personal data used to train, pre-train, fine-tune, adapt, evaluate, or otherwise improve AI models.

Training data may originate from:

* Public datasets
* Internal databases
* Customer records
* Employee information
* User interactions
* AI prompts
* Conversations
* Documents
* Logs
* RAG repositories
* Third-party datasets
* Synthetic-data generation processes

**Training data availability ≠ authorization to use the data for training.**

---

### 2. Purpose of Training Data Privacy

The purpose of training data privacy is to ensure that personal data used during AI development and model improvement is:

* Lawfully processed
* Necessary for the defined purpose
* Minimized
* Appropriately classified
* Protected
* Traceable
* Retained appropriately
* Subject to applicable individual rights
* Prevented from being used for unauthorized secondary purposes

---

### 5. Personal Data in Training Datasets

Training datasets may contain personal data even when the dataset was not originally designed for AI.

Examples include:

* Names
* Email addresses
* Customer identifiers
* Employee records
* Financial information
* Location information
* Communications
* Employment information
* Online activity

Free-text datasets present additional identification risk.

---

### 6. Directly Identifiable Training Data

Direct identifiers can identify individuals within training datasets.

Examples include:

* Full names
* Email addresses
* Telephone numbers
* Passport numbers
* Employee IDs
* Customer IDs

Organizations should determine whether such identifiers are necessary before including them in training data.

---

### 7. Indirectly Identifiable Training Data

Information can identify individuals when combined with other attributes.

Examples include:

* Age
* Occupation
* Location
* Employer
* Event dates
* Unique characteristics
* Behavioral patterns

Removing obvious identifiers does not necessarily eliminate identifiability.

---

### 8. Sensitive Personal Data in Training

Training datasets may contain sensitive personal information that requires stronger safeguards.

Potential examples include:

* Health information
* Financial information
* Biometric information
* Employment information
* Identity information
* Other restricted information

Sensitive data should not enter training pipelines simply because it exists in an available source.

---

### 9. Special-Category Data in Training

Where special-category personal data is present, the organization should assess the additional legal and governance requirements applicable to the processing.

Training pipelines should not treat special-category information as ordinary training material.

---

### 10. Training Purpose Limitation

Training data should be used only for defined and authorized purposes.

For example:

```text id="training14p8-03"
Customer Support Data
       |
       v
Approved Purpose
"Improve support classification"
       |
       X
Unauthorized Secondary Purpose
"General-purpose model training"
```

Authorization for one AI purpose does not automatically authorize another.

---

### 11. Training Data Minimization

Training datasets should contain only information necessary for the intended model objective.

The organization should ask:

> What data does the model actually need to learn the intended capability?

It should not assume:

> More data automatically means better or more authorized training.

---

### 12. Training Data Necessity

The organization should evaluate whether personal data is necessary at all.

Alternatives may include:

* Synthetic data
* Aggregated data
* Anonymized data
* Pseudonymized data
* Masked data
* Non-personal datasets

**Technical usefulness ≠ privacy necessity.**

---

* Data retained for analytics

### 37. Training Data Deletion versus Model Deletion

Training-data deletion and model deletion are separate concepts.

A dataset may be deleted while an already-trained model remains deployed.

This creates an important governance question:

> What happens when personal data incorporated into a model is later subject to deletion or restriction?

The answer may require additional technical and legal assessment.

---

### 38. Model Memorization Risk

Models can sometimes reproduce information encountered during training.

Potential risks include:

* Exact reproduction
* Partial reproduction
* Identifier disclosure
* Sensitive-information disclosure
* Membership inference

**Training-data protection ≠ protection against model memorization.**

---

### 39. Membership-Inference Risk

An attacker may attempt to determine whether information about an individual was included in training data.

This can reveal information even when the original dataset is no longer accessible.

---

### 40. Training Data Extraction Risk

Attackers may attempt to cause a model to reveal memorized training information.

Potential techniques include:

* Repeated prompting
* Extraction attacks
* Prompt manipulation
* Context manipulation
* Specialized probing

Training privacy should therefore extend into model testing.

---

* Benchmark data

* Regression datasets

### 43. Synthetic Data

* Re-identification
* Memorization
* Similarity to source data
* Leakage
* Generation methodology

**Synthetic ≠ automatically anonymous.**

### 50. Core Principle of Training Data Privacy

**Personal data must never enter an AI training pipeline merely because it is available, technically useful, publicly accessible, contained in an existing system, submitted through an AI prompt, retrieved through RAG, stored in memory, or accessible through an AI tool or agent.**

Training-data privacy requires an explicit relationship between:

**Data source → purpose → necessity → authorization → minimization → classification → transformation → training → model behavior → testing → retention → deletion.**

The fundamental distinctions are:

**Training capability ≠ training authorization.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to train.**

**Existing business data ≠ automatically authorized training data.**

**Public availability ≠ unrestricted training permission.**

**Prompt processing ≠ training authorization.**

**Conversation retention ≠ training permission.**

**RAG retrieval ≠ training authorization.**

**Retrieval authorization ≠ training authorization.**

**Memory retention ≠ training permission.**

**Tool access ≠ training authorization.**

**Agent access ≠ training authorization.**

**Provider capability ≠ permission to use personal data for training.**

**Pseudonymization ≠ anonymization.**

**Synthetic data ≠ automatically anonymous data.**

**Dataset deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

**Model training ≠ permanent authorization to retain personal information.**

**Model capability ≠ privacy authority.**

**Model output ≠ authorized disclosure.**

**Training-data security ≠ training-data privacy compliance.**

**Training-data documentation ≠ technical enforcement.**

**Training-data testing ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

A privacy-preserving AI training architecture must ensure that personal data is **purpose-bound, necessary, minimized, authorized, traceable, protected, tested for memorization and extraction risk, governed throughout model development, and subject to appropriate retention, deletion, reassessment, and accountability controls**.

### 117. Core Control Principle

Training-data privacy controls must ensure that personal data enters an AI training pipeline only when its **purpose, necessity, authorization, classification, provenance, minimization, protection, processing environment, retention, and downstream model implications** have been appropriately assessed.

The control framework must preserve the following distinctions:

**Training capability ≠ training authorization.**

**Data availability ≠ permission to train.**

**Existing business data ≠ automatically authorized training data.**

**Public availability ≠ unrestricted training permission.**

**Prompt processing ≠ training authorization.**

**Conversation retention ≠ training permission.**

**RAG retrieval ≠ training authorization.**

**Retrieval authorization ≠ training authorization.**

**Memory retention ≠ training permission.**

**Tool access ≠ training authorization.**

**Agent access ≠ training authorization.**

**Provider capability ≠ permission to use personal data for training.**

**Pseudonymization ≠ anonymization.**

**Synthetic data ≠ automatically anonymous data.**

**Dataset deletion ≠ automatic model deletion.**

**Dataset authorization ≠ authorization for every model trained from the dataset.**

**Training-data security ≠ training-data privacy compliance.**

**DLP detection ≠ complete privacy protection.**

**Control implementation ≠ control effectiveness.**

**Privacy testing ≠ permanent privacy assurance.**

**Risk acceptance ≠ lawful authorization.**

Effective training-data privacy requires controls to remain **technically enforceable, attributable, testable, observable, evidenced, and reassessable** throughout the lifecycle of the dataset and every model that depends upon it.
