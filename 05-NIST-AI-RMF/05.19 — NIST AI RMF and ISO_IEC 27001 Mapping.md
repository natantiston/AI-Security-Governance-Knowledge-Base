# 05.19 — NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 27001 Mapping

## Part 1 — Foundations of Mapping AI Risk Management to Information Security Management

### 1. Introduction to NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 27001 Mapping

AI governance and information security governance address different but highly complementary dimensions of organizational risk.

The **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)** provides a structured approach for managing risks associated with AI systems.

**ISO/IEC 27001** provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

The two frameworks can therefore be mapped to create a stronger governance structure for AI security.

---

### 2. Why AI Governance Needs Information Security

AI systems introduce security risks throughout their lifecycle.

Examples include:

* Unauthorized access.
* Data leakage.
* Model manipulation.
* Prompt injection.
* Adversarial attacks.
* Supply-chain compromise.
* Theft of models or intellectual property.
* Compromised AI infrastructure.
* Unauthorized use of AI services.

AI governance should therefore connect AI-specific risk management with established information-security controls.

---

### 3. Different Primary Objectives

The frameworks have different primary objectives.

**NIST AI RMF:**

> Manage risks associated with AI and promote trustworthy and responsible AI.

**ISO/IEC 27001:**

> Establish and operate a systematic information-security management system based on information-security risks.

The relationship can be represented as:

```text
NIST AI RMF
      ↓
AI Risk Management
      ↓
AI-Specific Risk Context
      ↓
ISO/IEC 27001
      ↓
Information Security Management
      ↓
Security Controls
```

---

### 4. AI Risk Management Versus Information Security Management

AI risk management is broader than AI cybersecurity alone.

AI risks can include:

* Security.
* Privacy.
* Safety.
* Fairness.
* Reliability.
* Transparency.
* Accountability.
* Legal and regulatory concerns.

ISO/IEC 27001 primarily addresses information-security risks involving:

* Confidentiality.
* Integrity.
* Availability.

Therefore, ISO/IEC 27001 can provide an important security foundation, but it should not be treated as a complete AI governance framework.

---

A practical governance architecture can be:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

```

This allows AI-specific risks to be connected to established organizational security processes.

```text

Govern provides the overarching governance structure across the other functions.

### 7. ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS Structure

ISO/IEC 27001 uses an Information Security Management System approach.

The ISMS establishes a structured process for:

* Understanding organizational context.
* Identifying information-security risks.
* Selecting risk treatments.
* Implementing controls.
* Monitoring performance.
* Conducting internal review.
* Continually improving the ISMS.

This makes the ISMS highly compatible with risk-based AI security governance.

### 45. Core Takeaway

The objective of mapping **NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 27001** is not to force one framework into the structure of the other.

The objective is to establish a practical relationship where:

> **NIST AI RMF provides the AI-risk perspective, while ISO/IEC 27001 provides a mature information-security management structure for addressing the security dimension of those AI risks.**

This creates a stronger governance model in which AI risks are identified in their AI context, translated into relevant security requirements, addressed through appropriate controls, measured through defined indicators, and continuously reviewed through the organization's established ISMS.

### 46. Mapping Is Not a One-to-One Exercise

NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 27001 should not be treated as if every requirement in one framework has an identical requirement in the other.

       ↓

```

The mapping should therefore focus on **functional alignment and complementary coverage**.

### 209. Interview-Level Explanation

If asked in an interview:

**"How would you map NIST AI RMF to ISO/IEC 27001?"**

A strong answer is:

> "I would not treat the frameworks as a one-to-one mapping. I would use NIST AI RMF to establish the AI-specific risk context through Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage, then identify which of those risks have information-security implications. I would map those security requirements into the organization's ISO 27001 ISMS, existing risk methodology, control library, and Statement of Applicability where appropriate. Where existing controls are insufficient, I would introduce AI-specific controls such as model integrity, prompt security, AI red teaming, output validation, and AI-specific monitoring. Finally, I would establish ownership, evidence, control testing, metrics, residual-risk assessment, and management review."

---

### 210. Short Executive Version

A shorter answer is:

> **"NIST AI RMF tells me how to think about AI risk; ISO 27001 gives me a mature management system for governing and treating the information-security portion of that risk. I would integrate them through a common risk register, control library, ownership model, evidence process, monitoring, and assurance framework rather than creating two separate programs."**

### 217. Final Principle
