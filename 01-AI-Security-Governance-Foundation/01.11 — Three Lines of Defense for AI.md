# 01.11 — Three Lines of Defense for AI

## Part 1 — Foundations of the Three Lines Model for AI Governance

### 1. Introduction to the Three Lines Model for AI

The **Three Lines Model** provides a structured approach for assigning responsibilities for governance, risk management, control, oversight, and assurance.

When applied to AI, the model helps distinguish between:

* Those who own and manage AI risks
* Those who provide specialized oversight and challenge
* Those who provide independent assurance

The model helps prevent a common governance problem in which the same function is expected to operate AI systems, oversee its own risks, and independently validate its own controls.

---

### 2. Purpose of the Three Lines Model for AI

The primary purpose of applying the Three Lines Model to AI is to establish clear organizational accountability.

It helps answer:

* Who owns AI risk?
* Who operates AI controls?
* Who provides risk oversight?
* Who challenges AI decisions?
* Who independently assesses governance effectiveness?
* Who reports significant weaknesses?

The model creates separation while maintaining coordination.

---

### 3. The Three Lines Model

The model consists broadly of:

**First Line**

→ Management and operational teams that own and manage AI risks.

**Second Line**

→ Functions that provide expertise, oversight, monitoring, challenge, and guidance.

**Third Line**

→ Internal Audit, which provides independent and objective assurance.

These lines work together but have different responsibilities.

---

### 4. First Line of AI Governance

The first line consists of the people and teams directly responsible for delivering and operating AI capabilities.

Examples include:

* Business owners
* AI product owners
* AI system owners
* Developers
* Data teams
* AI operations
* Technology teams
* AI platform teams

The first line owns the risks associated with the activities it performs.

---

### 5. First-Line AI Risk Ownership

First-line teams should identify and manage risks associated with their AI systems.

Responsibilities may include:

* Identifying risks
* Implementing controls
* Operating controls
* Monitoring systems
* Managing incidents
* Maintaining documentation
* Escalating issues

Risk ownership should remain with management rather than being transferred to the second line.

---

### 6. First-Line AI Control Responsibilities

The first line is generally responsible for implementing and operating controls.

Examples include:

* Access controls
* Secure development
* Data controls
* Model controls
* Change management
* Monitoring
* Incident response

The first line should be able to demonstrate that controls are operating effectively.

---

### 7. First-Line AI Business Ownership

Business leadership owns the business purpose and outcomes associated with AI use.

Responsibilities may include:

* Defining business objectives
* Approving business requirements
* Identifying business risks
* Ensuring appropriate use
* Monitoring business outcomes

The business should not delegate responsibility for AI outcomes simply because technology teams operate the system.

---

### 8. First-Line AI Technical Ownership

Technical teams are responsible for implementing and operating AI technology.

Responsibilities may include:

* Architecture
* Development
* Configuration
* Deployment
* Infrastructure
* Integration
* Technical monitoring
* Vulnerability remediation

Technical ownership operates within the organization's governance requirements.

---

### 9. First-Line AI System Ownership

The AI System Owner provides a central point of operational accountability.

The system owner may coordinate:

* Risk assessments
* Security requirements
* Privacy requirements
* Documentation
* Monitoring
* Changes
* Incidents
* Periodic reviews
* Retirement

The system owner should know the current status and risk profile of the AI system.

---

### 10. First-Line AI Data Responsibilities

Data teams and data owners are part of the first line when they directly operate or manage AI-related data processes.

Responsibilities may include:

* Data quality
* Data access
* Data classification
* Data lineage
* Data preparation
* Data protection

Data governance oversight may additionally be provided by second-line functions where applicable.

---

### 11. First-Line AI Security Responsibilities

Security engineering and operational security teams can be part of the first line when they directly implement and operate security controls.

Activities may include:

* Security configuration
* Identity management
* Access control
* Security monitoring
* Vulnerability remediation
* Incident response

This distinction is important because the security function can contain both first-line operational activities and second-line governance activities.

---

### 12. First-Line AI Operations

AI operations teams maintain systems in production.

Activities may include:

* Availability
* Performance
* Monitoring
* Deployment
* Backup
* Recovery
* Incident management
* Configuration

Operational teams are responsible for managing risks within their activities.

---

### 13. Second Line of AI Governance

The second line provides specialized oversight, guidance, monitoring, and challenge.

Second-line functions may include:

* AI governance
* Enterprise risk
* Information security governance
* Privacy
* Compliance
* Legal
* Responsible AI

The second line does not normally own the operational risks of the first line.

---

### 14. Second-Line AI Governance Function

An AI Governance function may establish:

* Policies
* Standards
* Risk methodologies
* Governance procedures
* Control expectations
* Reporting requirements
* Oversight mechanisms

It provides guidance and challenge to first-line teams.

---

### 15. Second-Line AI Risk Management

AI Risk Management may provide:

* Risk taxonomy
* Risk assessment methodology
* Risk appetite guidance
* Risk reporting
* Risk aggregation
* Risk escalation

The first line identifies and manages its risks, while the second line provides independent risk oversight.

---

### 16. Second-Line AI Security Governance

Security governance may establish enterprise-level AI security requirements.

These may cover:

* Security architecture
* Identity
* Access
* Threat management
* Secure development
* Monitoring
* Incident management

Security engineering may implement the controls as first line, while security governance provides second-line oversight.

---

### 17. Second-Line AI Privacy Governance

Privacy functions can provide second-line oversight of AI-related privacy risk.

Activities may include:

* Privacy assessments
* Privacy requirements
* Data-use oversight
* Privacy monitoring
* Privacy guidance
* Escalation

Operational teams remain responsible for implementing the required controls.

---

### 18. Second-Line AI Compliance

Compliance functions may identify and monitor applicable AI-related requirements.

Activities may include:

* Regulatory mapping
* Compliance assessments
* Control mapping
* Compliance monitoring
* Reporting

Compliance should challenge the first line when requirements are not appropriately addressed.

---

### 19. Second-Line AI Legal Oversight

Legal functions can provide specialized advice and challenge concerning:

* Regulatory obligations
* Contracts
* Liability
* Intellectual property
* Data rights
* Jurisdiction
* Third-party AI services

Legal involvement should be proportional to the nature and risk of the AI activity.

---

### 20. Second-Line Responsible AI Oversight

Responsible AI functions may provide oversight of:

* Fairness
* Transparency
* Human oversight
* Ethical considerations
* Impact assessment
* Responsible AI principles

Responsible AI oversight should be coordinated with security, privacy, legal, and risk functions.

---

### 21. Second-Line Monitoring

Second-line functions should monitor whether the first line is managing AI risks according to established requirements.

Monitoring may examine:

* Risk assessments
* Control implementation
* Policy compliance
* Exceptions
* Incidents
* Remediation
* Governance metrics

Monitoring provides visibility without assuming operational ownership.

---

### 22. Second-Line Challenge

Challenge is a fundamental second-line responsibility.

The second line may challenge:

* AI risk classifications
* Control adequacy
* Risk acceptance
* Exception requests
* Deployment decisions
* Monitoring arrangements

Challenge should be independent enough to provide meaningful oversight.

---

### 23. Third Line of AI Governance

The third line provides independent and objective assurance.

Internal Audit is the primary third-line function.

The third line evaluates whether:

* Governance is appropriately designed
* Risks are properly managed
* Controls are effective
* Responsibilities are clear
* Policies are followed
* Oversight is functioning

The third line should remain independent from management activities.

---

### 24. Internal Audit and AI Governance

Internal Audit may evaluate the organization's AI governance environment as part of its risk-based audit plan.

Audit areas may include:

* AI governance framework
* Operating model
* AI inventory
* Risk management
* Security
* Privacy
* Third-party governance
* Lifecycle management
* Incident management

Audit scope should reflect organizational risk.

---

### 25. Internal Audit Independence

Internal Audit should not become responsible for operating AI governance controls.

For example, Internal Audit should generally not:

* Approve AI systems
* Operate AI controls
* Own AI risks
* Manage remediation

Instead, it should independently evaluate management's governance and control environment.

---

### 26. Third-Line AI Assurance

Third-line assurance can evaluate whether the first and second lines are functioning as intended.

For example, Internal Audit may examine whether:

**First Line**

→ Properly identifies and manages AI risks.

**Second Line**

→ Provides effective oversight and challenge.

**Governance**

→ Provides appropriate decision-making.

**Controls**

→ Operate effectively.

---

### 27. External Assurance

External parties may provide additional assurance where appropriate.

Examples include:

* Independent assessors
* Certification bodies
* External auditors
* Specialized AI assurance providers
* Regulatory authorities

External assurance does not replace management responsibility or internal audit.

---

### 28. Three Lines and AI Accountability

The Three Lines Model should not be interpreted as dividing accountability equally among three groups.

The primary principle is:

**Management owns and manages risk.**

The second line provides oversight and challenge.

The third line provides independent assurance.

This distinction prevents accountability from becoming diluted.

---

### 29. Three Lines and AI Governance Committees

AI governance committees can contain representatives from multiple lines.

For example:

* Business leadership
* AI governance
* Risk
* Security
* Privacy
* Compliance
* Technology

However, committee membership does not automatically change the underlying line responsibilities.

Each participant should continue to operate according to their defined authority and role.

---

### 30. Three Lines and AI Decision Rights

Decision rights should specify which line has authority for different decisions.

For example:

**First Line**

→ Implements and operates.

**Second Line**

→ Challenges and provides oversight.

**Third Line**

→ Independently assesses.

This distinction should be reflected in RACI models and governance procedures.

---

### 31. Three Lines and Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance normally remains a management responsibility.

The second line may:

* Assess
* Challenge
* Recommend
* Escalate

The third line may later evaluate whether risk acceptance processes were appropriately designed and followed.

Internal Audit should not become the risk acceptance authority.

---

### 32. Three Lines and AI Exceptions

The same principle applies to AI governance exceptions.

**First Line**

→ Requests and manages the exception.

**Second Line**

→ Reviews and challenges the risk.

**Authorized Management**

→ Approves or rejects according to delegated authority.

**Third Line**

→ May independently assess whether the exception process is effective.

---

### 33. Three Lines and AI Control Ownership

Control ownership generally remains with management and operational functions.

For example:

**Access Control Owner**

→ Operates the access control.

**Security Governance**

→ Establishes requirements and monitors compliance.

**Internal Audit**

→ Independently assesses the control environment.

This prevents the assurance function from becoming the control operator.

---

### 34. Three Lines and AI Risk Escalation

AI risks should move through the organization according to defined escalation mechanisms.

A potential structure is:

**AI System Team**

↓

**Business Management**

↓

**Second-Line Governance**

↓

**Executive Management**

↓

**Board**

Internal Audit may independently review the effectiveness of the escalation process.

---

### 35. Three Lines and AI Incidents

AI incidents should also preserve line responsibilities.

**First Line**

→ Detects, contains, investigates, and remediates operational issues.

**Second Line**

→ Provides oversight, challenge, risk assessment, and regulatory coordination where applicable.

**Third Line**

→ Independently evaluates the effectiveness of incident governance.

This creates accountability throughout the incident lifecycle.

---

### 36. Three Lines and AI Lifecycle Governance

The Three Lines Model should apply throughout the AI lifecycle.

| Lifecycle Stage | First Line          | Second Line              | Third Line                           |
| --------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Concept         | Owns use case       | Provides guidance        | Independent review where planned     |
| Risk Assessment | Performs assessment | Challenges               | Audits methodology                   |
| Development     | Implements controls | Provides requirements    | Assesses governance                  |
| Testing         | Performs testing    | Reviews requirements     | Provides assurance                   |
| Approval        | Provides evidence   | Challenges risk          | Independent review where appropriate |
| Deployment      | Operates            | Oversees                 | Audits                               |
| Monitoring      | Monitors            | Reviews                  | Provides assurance                   |
| Change          | Implements          | Reviews material changes | Audits                               |
| Retirement      | Executes            | Oversees                 | Assesses process                     |

The precise allocation should reflect organizational structure and risk.

---

### 37. Three Lines and AI Governance Evidence

Each line should produce appropriate evidence.

**First Line Evidence**

→ Operational records, testing, monitoring, approvals, and remediation.

**Second Line Evidence**

→ Reviews, assessments, challenges, oversight reports, and escalations.

**Third Line Evidence**

→ Audit planning, workpapers, findings, conclusions, and assurance reports.

Evidence should support accountability and traceability.

---

### 38. Three Lines and AI Governance Reporting

Reporting should flow according to the responsibilities of each line.

First-line reporting may focus on:

* Operational performance
* Incidents
* Control status
* Risk treatment

Second-line reporting may focus on:

* Risk exposure
* Compliance
* Exceptions
* Governance maturity
* Control weaknesses

Third-line reporting may focus on:

* Independent findings
* Control effectiveness
* Governance weaknesses
* Assurance conclusions

---

### 39. Three Lines and AI Governance Effectiveness

An effective Three Lines structure should demonstrate:

* Clear ownership
* Independent challenge
* Effective escalation
* Appropriate separation
* Reliable reporting
* Independent assurance

The objective is not to create organizational bureaucracy but to establish confidence that AI risks are appropriately governed.

---

### 40. Three Lines as an AI Governance Foundation

The Three Lines Model provides a foundational organizational structure for AI governance by connecting:

**Management Ownership**

→ AI risks are owned and managed by those responsible for AI activities.

**Specialized Oversight**

→ Risk, security, privacy, compliance, and governance functions provide challenge and guidance.

**Independent Assurance**

→ Internal Audit independently evaluates governance and control effectiveness.

Together, these three perspectives create a structured system of **AI risk ownership, oversight, challenge, accountability, and independent assurance**.

## Part 2 — First Line Responsibilities for AI Governance

### 41. First Line as AI Risk Owner

The first line is responsible for **owning and managing AI risk within the activities it performs**.

This means that business and operational teams cannot transfer responsibility for AI risk simply because a second-line function provides governance oversight.

First-line ownership includes:

* Identifying AI risks
* Assessing operational exposure
* Implementing controls
* Operating controls
* Monitoring risks
* Addressing control weaknesses
* Escalating significant issues
* Maintaining evidence

The first line remains accountable for managing risk within its area of responsibility.

---

### 42. First-Line Management Accountability

Management is responsible for ensuring that AI activities operate within approved organizational requirements.

Management should ensure that:

* AI systems have appropriate owners
* Risks are identified
* Controls are implemented
* Employees understand their responsibilities
* Exceptions are controlled
* Significant issues are escalated
* AI systems remain aligned with business objectives

AI governance does not remove management accountability.

---

### 43. Business AI Use-Case Ownership

The business function requesting or using AI should own the business use case.

The business owner should understand:

* Why AI is being used
* What decision or activity AI supports
* Who is affected
* What could go wrong
* What business impact could occur
* What level of human oversight is required

Business ownership is particularly important when AI affects customers, employees, financial decisions, or critical operations.

---

### 44. First-Line AI Risk Identification

The first line should identify risks before and during AI deployment.

Risk identification may consider:

* Security risk
* Privacy risk
* Operational risk
* Compliance risk
* Model risk
* Data risk
* Third-party risk
* Business continuity risk
* Reputational risk

Risk identification should continue throughout the AI lifecycle.

---

### 45. First-Line Risk Assessment

The first line should participate directly in AI risk assessments.

The assessment should consider:

* Likelihood
* Impact
* Existing controls
* Residual risk
* Dependencies
* Vulnerabilities
* Business consequences

The second line may challenge the assessment, but operational knowledge generally comes from the first line.

---

### 46. First-Line Control Implementation

First-line teams are responsible for implementing required controls.

Controls may include:

* Identity and access management
* Secure configuration
* Data protection
* Logging
* Monitoring
* Model validation
* Change management
* Incident response

The first line should demonstrate that controls are operating as intended.

---

### 47. First-Line Control Operation

Implementing a control is different from operating it.

For example:

**Control Design**

→ Access approval process exists.

**Control Operation**

→ Access requests are actually reviewed and approved.

The first line should ensure that controls operate consistently and produce appropriate evidence.

---

### 48. First-Line Control Evidence

Evidence may demonstrate that controls are functioning.

Examples include:

* Access records
* Approval records
* Test results
* Monitoring logs
* Security reports
* Change records
* Incident records
* Review documentation

Evidence should be sufficient to support oversight and assurance.

---

### 49. First-Line AI Security Responsibilities

Where security teams directly operate AI security controls, they form part of the first line for those activities.

Responsibilities may include:

* Security architecture implementation
* Secure configuration
* Vulnerability remediation
* Access control
* Security monitoring
* Threat detection
* Incident response

The distinction between operational security and security governance should remain clear.

---

### 50. First-Line AI Data Responsibilities

First-line data teams should ensure that AI systems use data appropriately.

Responsibilities may include:

* Data preparation
* Data quality
* Data classification
* Data access
* Data lineage
* Data retention
* Data protection

Data use should remain consistent with approved business and governance requirements.

---

### 51. First-Line AI Model Responsibilities

Model-related responsibilities may include:

* Model development
* Model configuration
* Model testing
* Model deployment
* Model monitoring
* Model version management
* Model rollback

Teams responsible for models should understand their limitations and operational risks.

---

### 52. First-Line AI Application Responsibilities

AI applications may contain additional components beyond the underlying model.

First-line application teams may manage:

* Application logic
* APIs
* User interfaces
* Authentication
* Authorization
* Integrations
* Logging
* Error handling

Application-level controls should be coordinated with model-level controls.

---

### 53. First-Line AI Platform Responsibilities

AI platform teams may provide shared infrastructure.

Responsibilities may include:

* AI infrastructure
* Model hosting
* APIs
* Development environments
* Identity integration
* Logging
* Monitoring
* Deployment platforms

Platform teams should manage risks associated with shared AI infrastructure.

---

### 54. First-Line AI Operations

AI Operations teams are responsible for maintaining production services.

Operational responsibilities may include:

* Availability
* Performance
* Capacity
* Monitoring
* Backup
* Recovery
* Configuration
* Incident handling

Operational risk remains a first-line responsibility.

---

### 55. First-Line AI Monitoring

The first line should continuously or periodically monitor AI systems according to risk.

Monitoring may include:

* Performance
* Availability
* Security events
* Access
* Model behavior
* Data quality
* Drift
* Policy violations

Monitoring should provide early warning of emerging problems.

---

### 56. First-Line AI Incident Management

When an AI incident occurs, first-line teams should take operational action.

Activities may include:

* Detection
* Triage
* Containment
* Investigation
* Recovery
* Remediation
* Documentation

Material incidents should be escalated according to established governance requirements.

---

### 57. First-Line AI Change Management

First-line teams are responsible for managing changes to systems they operate.

Changes may involve:

* Models
* Prompts
* Data
* Code
* Infrastructure
* Configuration
* APIs
* Tools
* Agent permissions

Changes should follow approved change-management processes.

---

### 58. First-Line AI Testing

First-line teams should perform required testing before deployment and after significant changes.

Testing may include:

* Functional testing
* Security testing
* Performance testing
* Model evaluation
* Integration testing
* Resilience testing
* Regression testing

Testing depth should correspond to AI risk.

---

### 59. First-Line AI Validation Support

Where independent validation is required, first-line teams should provide the necessary evidence and access.

They may provide:

* Model documentation
* Test results
* Training information
* Configuration
* Data documentation
* Performance metrics

The first line should not control the independent validation conclusion.

---

### 60. First-Line AI Documentation

AI system owners should maintain accurate documentation.

Documentation may include:

* Purpose
* Architecture
* Data
* Model
* Dependencies
* Risks
* Controls
* Limitations
* Monitoring
* Ownership
* Approval status

Documentation should remain current throughout the lifecycle.

---

### 61. First-Line AI Inventory Responsibilities

The first line should provide accurate information for the enterprise AI inventory.

Information may include:

* System name
* Owner
* Business purpose
* Technology
* Data
* Risk classification
* Vendor
* Lifecycle status

The first line should notify governance functions when information changes.

---

### 62. First-Line AI Asset Management

AI assets may include:

* Models
* Datasets
* Prompts
* Applications
* APIs
* Agents
* Infrastructure
* AI services

First-line teams should know which assets they operate and how those assets relate to business services.

---

### 63. First-Line AI Dependency Management

AI systems may depend on:

* Cloud providers
* Foundation models
* Data providers
* APIs
* SaaS platforms
* Internal systems
* Human processes

First-line owners should understand these dependencies and their operational implications.

---

### 64. First-Line Third-Party AI Management

Where the business directly uses a third-party AI service, first-line teams should ensure that approved requirements are followed.

Responsibilities may include:

* Approved service usage
* Vendor configuration
* Data restrictions
* Access management
* Service monitoring
* Issue escalation

Second-line functions may provide independent oversight.

---

### 65. First-Line AI Vendor Monitoring

First-line owners should monitor material vendor dependencies.

Monitoring may consider:

* Service availability
* Security events
* Contractual performance
* Significant changes
* Model changes
* Data-handling changes
* Service termination risks

Significant vendor changes should trigger appropriate reassessment.

---

### 66. First-Line AI Policy Compliance

First-line teams are responsible for operating according to applicable AI policies.

They should understand:

* What is permitted
* What is prohibited
* What requires approval
* What requires escalation
* What evidence must be retained

Policy ownership may reside elsewhere, but policy compliance remains an operational responsibility.

---

### 67. First-Line AI Standard Implementation

Standards translate broad policy requirements into specific expectations.

First-line teams should implement applicable standards for:

* Security
* Data
* Development
* Model management
* Access
* Monitoring
* Documentation

Standards should be practical enough to support implementation.

---

### 68. First-Line AI Procedure Execution

Procedures provide operational instructions.

Examples include:

* AI onboarding
* Access requests
* Model deployment
* Incident handling
* Change management
* Periodic review

The first line executes these procedures within its operational environment.

---

### 69. First-Line AI Exception Requests

When a required control cannot be implemented, the first line should formally request an exception.

The request should explain:

* Requirement
* Reason for deviation
* Risk
* Business justification
* Compensating controls
* Proposed duration

The first line should not create informal exceptions.

---

### 70. First-Line AI Risk Treatment

The first line should determine and implement appropriate risk treatment options.

These may include:

**Avoid**

→ Do not use AI for the activity.

**Reduce**

→ Implement additional controls.

**Transfer**

→ Allocate defined risk through contractual or insurance mechanisms where appropriate.

**Accept**

→ Accept residual risk through authorized management.

Risk treatment should be documented.

---

### 71. First-Line AI Risk Acceptance Preparation

The first line may prepare a risk acceptance request.

The request should include:

* Risk description
* Potential impact
* Likelihood
* Existing controls
* Residual risk
* Business justification
* Alternatives
* Proposed duration

The final decision should be made by the appropriate authorized management authority.

---

### 72. First-Line AI Residual Risk Management

After controls are implemented, some risk will remain.

The first line should:

* Understand residual risk
* Monitor it
* Escalate changes
* Implement additional controls when necessary
* Support management decisions

Residual risk should not disappear simply because an AI system has passed an initial approval process.

---

### 73. First-Line AI Risk Appetite Alignment

First-line teams should understand the organization's AI risk appetite and apply it to their activities.

Risk appetite helps determine:

* Which uses are acceptable
* Which require additional controls
* Which require escalation
* Which should not proceed

Risk appetite should guide decisions rather than replace detailed risk assessment.

---

### 74. First-Line Human Oversight

Where human oversight is required, the first line should implement the operational mechanisms necessary to support it.

These may include:

* Human approval
* Escalation
* Review queues
* Override mechanisms
* Decision documentation
* Emergency intervention

Human oversight should be meaningful rather than purely procedural.

---

### 75. First-Line AI Autonomy Management

The first line should manage the level of autonomy assigned to an AI system.

Autonomy may range from:

**Assistive**

→ AI provides information.

**Advisory**

→ AI recommends actions.

**Human-Approved**

→ AI prepares an action requiring approval.

**Delegated**

→ AI performs predefined actions.

**Autonomous**

→ AI performs actions independently within defined boundaries.

Higher autonomy generally requires stronger operational controls.

---

### 76. First-Line AI Permission Management

AI systems should receive only the permissions required for authorized activities.

Permission management may cover:

* Data
* Applications
* APIs
* Tools
* Infrastructure
* Administrative functions

Permissions should be reviewed when the AI system's purpose or capabilities change.

---

### 77. First-Line AI Least Privilege

Least privilege should apply to AI identities and associated human users.

An AI system should not receive broad access merely because it might need that access in the future.

Access should be:

* Necessary
* Authorized
* Limited
* Monitored
* Reviewable

---

### 78. First-Line AI Segregation of Duties

Where practical, incompatible responsibilities should be separated.

For example:

**Development**

≠

**Production Approval**

and

**Control Operation**

≠

**Independent Assurance**

Segregation reduces the possibility of unauthorized or unchecked activity.

---

### 79. First-Line AI Secure Development

AI development teams should incorporate security into the development lifecycle.

Activities may include:

* Threat modeling
* Secure coding
* Dependency management
* Security testing
* Secrets management
* Access control
* Logging

Security should be incorporated before production deployment.

---

### 80. First-Line AI Threat Modeling

First-line technical teams should identify threats affecting the AI system.

Threat modeling may consider:

* Data
* Models
* Applications
* APIs
* Users
* Agents
* Tools
* External dependencies

Threats should be connected to appropriate security controls.

---

### 81. First-Line AI Security Testing

Security testing may address AI-specific and conventional threats.

Testing may include:

* Prompt injection
* Data leakage
* Unauthorized access
* Model manipulation
* Insecure integrations
* API abuse
* Privilege escalation
* Conventional application vulnerabilities

Testing should be appropriate to system risk.

---

### 82. First-Line AI Data Protection

First-line teams should implement approved data protection requirements.

Controls may include:

* Encryption
* Access restrictions
* Data minimization
* Data masking
* Data loss prevention
* Retention controls

Data protection should cover data used during development and production.

---

### 83. First-Line AI Logging

AI systems should generate appropriate logs.

Logs may record:

* User activity
* Authentication
* AI requests
* Tool execution
* Administrative changes
* Model changes
* Security events

Logging requirements should consider privacy, security, and regulatory obligations.

---

### 84. First-Line AI Monitoring and Alerting

Monitoring should identify conditions requiring action.

Alerts may be triggered by:

* Unauthorized access
* Abnormal activity
* Security events
* Performance degradation
* Model anomalies
* Excessive usage
* Policy violations

Alerts should have defined response procedures.

---

### 85. First-Line AI Performance Management

AI system owners should monitor whether systems continue to meet business requirements.

Performance may include:

* Accuracy
* Reliability
* Availability
* Latency
* Throughput
* Business outcomes

Performance deterioration may require investigation or model reassessment.

---

### 86. First-Line AI Drift Monitoring

Where applicable, teams should monitor for changes in:

* Data distribution
* Model behavior
* Input patterns
* Output quality
* Business conditions

Significant drift may require:

* Revalidation
* Retraining
* Model replacement
* Additional controls

---

### 87. First-Line AI Model Retirement

The first line should manage retirement when an AI system is no longer appropriate.

Retirement activities may include:

* Access removal
* Data handling
* Model decommissioning
* Contract termination
* Documentation
* Dependency removal
* Evidence retention

Retirement should be formally recorded.

---

### 88. First-Line AI Governance Culture

First-line teams should understand that AI governance is part of responsible operational management.

A strong culture encourages employees to:

* Ask questions
* Report concerns
* Escalate risks
* Follow approved processes
* Challenge unsafe practices
* Learn from incidents

Governance becomes more effective when it is embedded in everyday behavior.

### 89. First-Line AI Accountability

First-line accountability can be summarized as:

**Own**

→ Own the AI activity and associated risks.

**Implement**

→ Implement required controls.

**Operate**

→ Operate the controls and AI system.

**Monitor**

→ Monitor performance and risk.

**Escalate**

→ Escalate significant issues.

**Improve**

→ Correct weaknesses and improve controls.

The first line is therefore the primary operational owner of AI risk.

---

### 90. First Line as the Foundation of AI Risk Management

The Three Lines Model begins with effective first-line ownership.

Without strong first-line accountability, the second line can become responsible for operational risk that it does not control, while Internal Audit may become involved in activities that compromise its independence.

A mature AI governance environment therefore establishes the first line as the **owner, operator, and primary manager of AI risk**, with the second line providing specialized oversight and challenge and the third line providing independent assurance.

The third line provides **independent and objective assurance** over the effectiveness of AI governance, risk management, and internal controls.

### 182. Three Lines and AI Governance Culture

This distinction creates accountability without creating unnecessary organizational barriers.

### 190. Integrated Three Lines Model for AI

A mature AI governance environment brings all three lines together while preserving their independence.

**First Line**

**Own → Implement → Operate → Monitor → Escalate → Remediate**

**Second Line**

**Set Expectations → Advise → Monitor → Challenge → Escalate → Report**

**Third Line**

**Assess → Test → Evaluate → Report → Follow Up**

Together, these responsibilities establish a comprehensive AI governance assurance structure.

The effectiveness of the model ultimately depends not on the existence of three organizational groups, but on whether **risk ownership, oversight, challenge, escalation, and independent assurance are genuinely performed and remain appropriately separated**.
