# 01.07 — AI Governance Operating Models and Organizational Structures

## Part 1 — Introduction to AI Governance Operating Models and Organizational Structures

### 1. Introduction

Establishing AI Governance requires more than defining principles, policies, roles, and responsibilities.

An organization must also determine **how AI Governance will actually operate within the enterprise**.

This is the purpose of an **AI Governance Operating Model**.

An AI Governance Operating Model defines how:

* AI governance responsibilities are organized
* Decisions are made
* Risks are managed
* Policies are implemented
* Controls are operated
* Business units interact with governance functions
* Technical teams interact with risk and compliance functions
* Issues are escalated
* Governance performance is measured
* Independent assurance is performed

In simple terms:

> **The AI Governance Operating Model describes how the organization turns AI Governance requirements into day-to-day organizational activities.**

---

## 2. Governance Model Versus Operating Model

These concepts are related but should not be confused.

### Governance Model

Defines:

> **Who has authority and how decisions are governed.**

It establishes:

* Decision rights
* Accountability
* Oversight
* Risk ownership
* Escalation
* Committees
* Governance principles

### Operating Model

Defines:

> **How the governance system actually works.**

It establishes:

* Processes
* Organizational structures
* Workflows
* Interfaces
* Resources
* Technology
* Reporting
* Operational responsibilities

Therefore:

**Governance Model**

→ Defines authority and accountability.

**Operating Model**

→ Defines how those responsibilities are executed.

---

## 3. Why an AI Governance Operating Model Is Necessary

Organizations often begin AI adoption through individual business initiatives.

One department may use:

* ChatGPT
* Microsoft Copilot
* AI analytics
* Machine learning
* Generative AI
* AI agents
* Predictive models

Another department may independently adopt different AI technologies.

Without an operating model, the organization can quickly develop:

* Duplicate governance processes
* Inconsistent risk assessments
* Unclear ownership
* Shadow AI
* Conflicting policies
* Inconsistent vendor assessments
* Different security requirements
* Different approval processes

An operating model creates organizational consistency.

---

## 4. AI Governance as an Enterprise Capability

AI Governance should normally be treated as an **enterprise capability**, not simply an IT activity.

AI affects multiple organizational domains:

* Business
* Technology
* Cybersecurity
* Data
* Privacy
* Legal
* Compliance
* Risk
* Human Resources
* Procurement
* Finance
* Internal Audit

Therefore, an effective operating model must connect these functions.

A useful principle is:

> **AI Governance should follow the organization's risk and business structure rather than being isolated within a single technical department.**

## 6. Centralized AI Governance Model

A centralized model places AI Governance primarily within a central enterprise function.

For example:

**Chief AI Governance Office**

↓

**Enterprise AI Governance Team**

↓

Supports

* Business Units
* IT
* Security
* Privacy
* Data
* Risk
* Legal

The central team establishes common policies, standards, methodologies, and approval processes.

---

## 7. Characteristics of a Centralized Model

A centralized model typically provides:

* Strong consistency
* Central expertise
* Common standards
* Centralized reporting
* Consistent risk classification
* Centralized AI inventory
* Standardized approval processes

It can be particularly useful when:

* AI adoption is still developing
* Regulatory risk is significant
* AI use is concentrated
* Governance expertise is limited

---

## 8. Advantages of Centralized AI Governance

Advantages include:

### Consistency

The same governance requirements apply across the organization.

### Visibility

Leadership has a centralized view of AI.

### Expertise

Specialists can be concentrated in one function.

### Standardization

Processes and templates are easier to maintain.

### Strong Oversight

High-risk AI can be centrally reviewed.

---

## 9. Disadvantages of Centralized AI Governance

Centralization can also create challenges.

Potential disadvantages include:

* Governance bottlenecks
* Slower business decisions
* Excessive bureaucracy
* Limited business-unit ownership
* Central team becoming overloaded
* Perception that governance is a blocker

Therefore, centralization should not automatically mean that every AI decision requires central approval.

---

## 10. Decentralized AI Governance Model

In a decentralized model, individual business units manage much of their own AI governance.

For example:

**Business Unit A**

→ AI Governance Team

**Business Unit B**

→ AI Governance Team

**Business Unit C**

→ AI Governance Team

Each business unit may have its own:

* AI policies
* AI risk processes
* AI owners
* Governance committees
* Technical specialists

A central enterprise function may provide limited coordination.

---

## 11. Advantages of Decentralized AI Governance

Potential benefits include:

* Faster decisions
* Strong business ownership
* Local expertise
* Greater flexibility
* Better understanding of business processes

This model may be suitable for highly diversified organizations.

---

## 12. Disadvantages of Decentralized AI Governance

Potential weaknesses include:

* Inconsistent controls
* Different risk methodologies
* Duplicate systems
* Fragmented AI inventories
* Uneven regulatory compliance
* Inconsistent security
* Difficulty producing enterprise-wide reporting

Decentralization therefore requires strong coordination mechanisms.

---

## 13. Federated AI Governance Model

A federated model combines centralized governance with decentralized execution.

The central function establishes:

* Enterprise policy
* Standards
* Risk methodology
* Minimum controls
* AI taxonomy
* Reporting requirements
* Governance framework

Business units manage:

* Local AI systems
* Business-specific risks
* Operational processes
* Local implementation

This model is often useful for large enterprises.

---

## 14. Federated Governance Structure

A simplified model is:

**Enterprise AI Governance**

↓

**Enterprise Standards**

↓

**Business Unit AI Governance**

↓

**Local AI Systems**

At the same time, specialist functions provide horizontal oversight:

**Cybersecurity**

**Privacy**

**Data Governance**

**Legal**

**Risk**

**Compliance**

This creates both enterprise consistency and business flexibility.

---

## 15. Hybrid AI Governance Model

A hybrid model combines characteristics of centralized, decentralized, and federated governance.

For example:

**Centralized**

→ Policy, standards, AI inventory.

**Federated**

→ Business-unit governance.

**Decentralized**

→ Day-to-day operational decisions.

This allows the organization to tailor governance intensity to business needs.

## 16. Choosing the Appropriate Operating Model

There is no universal AI Governance operating model.

The appropriate model depends on factors such as:

* Organization size
* Geographic footprint
* Regulatory environment
* AI maturity
* Business complexity
* Number of AI systems
* AI risk profile
* Technology architecture
* Existing GRC structure
* Organizational culture

A multinational financial institution may require a different model from a small technology company.

## 36. Risk-Based Operating Model

Governance should be proportional to risk.

A low-risk AI assistant might require:

* Basic registration
* Acceptable-use requirements
* Security controls
* User training

A high-risk AI system may require:

* Formal risk assessment
* Security assessment
* Privacy assessment
* Model validation
* Human oversight
* Governance Committee approval
* Continuous monitoring
* Periodic reassessment

This prevents over-governance of low-risk AI while maintaining strong controls for high-risk systems.

## 50. Common Operating Model Failures

Organizations should avoid:

### Failure 1 — Governance Without Authority

The governance team can recommend but cannot influence decisions.

### Failure 2 — Central Bottleneck

Every AI decision requires central review.

### Failure 3 — Fragmentation

Every business unit creates its own governance.

### Failure 4 — Unclear Ownership

No accountable AI System Owner.

### Failure 5 — Duplicate Controls

AI Governance duplicates existing security, privacy, and risk processes.

### Failure 6 — Audit-Owned Governance

Internal Audit becomes responsible for operating controls.

### Failure 7 — Governance After Deployment

AI is reviewed only after implementation.

### Failure 8 — No Lifecycle Governance

Governance stops after initial approval.

## 01.07 — AI Governance Operating Models and Organizational Structures

A federated AI Governance model is often particularly useful for large enterprises because it balances **central governance consistency** with **business-unit flexibility**.

The central AI Governance function establishes the enterprise-wide framework, while business units retain responsibility for implementing governance within their operational environments.

---

### 58. Federated AI Governance Operating Model

A federated model can be represented as:

**Board / Executive Management**

↓

**Enterprise AI Governance Committee**

↓

**Enterprise AI Governance Office**

↓

**Global Policies, Standards, Taxonomy and Minimum Controls**

↓

**Regional / Business-Unit AI Governance**

↓

**AI System Owners**

↓

**AI Development and Operations**

The central governance function establishes the common framework.

Business units execute that framework according to their specific:

* Business requirements
* Risk profile
* Regulatory environment
* Technology architecture
* AI use cases

* Minimum control requirements
* Enterprise AI inventory
* Enterprise reporting
* Governance metrics
* AI risk appetite

* Performing business impact analysis

* Monitoring AI performance
* Managing operational risks
* Escalating material issues

### 63. Global Standard, Local Implementation

A useful principle is:

> **Global governance establishes the minimum standard; local governance determines how that standard is implemented within its environment.**

For example:

**Global Requirement**

> High-risk AI must undergo formal risk assessment.

↓

**Regional Implementation**

→ Apply local regulatory requirements.

↓

**Business Implementation**

→ Apply business-specific controls.

This prevents unnecessary duplication.

| Decision              | Business | Regional Governance | Enterprise Governance | Executive |

### 127. AI Governance and Residual Risk

**Control Effectiveness**

→ How well controls reduce risk.

**Residual Risk**

Residual risk should then be compared against:

If residual risk exceeds appetite, the organization should:

* Add controls
* Reduce the use case
* Transfer risk where appropriate
* Change the design
* Escalate
* Reject the use case

### 190. AI Governance Operating Model — Key Design Principles

A mature operating model should follow several principles:

3. **Integration** — AI Governance connects with existing GRC.
4. **Independence** — Assurance remains independent.
5. **Transparency** — Decisions and responsibilities are documented.
6. **Traceability** — Governance evidence can be reconstructed.
7. **Scalability** — The model works as AI adoption grows.
8. **Automation** — Repeatable governance activities should be automated.
9. **Human Oversight** — Material decisions retain appropriate human accountability.
10. **Continuous Improvement** — Governance evolves with technology and risk.

### 191. AI Governance Operating Model — Common Anti-Patterns

Organizations should avoid:

#### Governance Theater

Creating committees and policies without meaningful authority.

#### Governance Bottleneck

Requiring central approval for every low-risk AI activity.

#### Governance Fragmentation

Allowing every business unit to create incompatible governance.

#### Accountability Vacuum

Having many responsible participants but no accountable owner.

#### Audit as Operator

Using Internal Audit to perform management's governance responsibilities.

#### Policy-Only Governance

Assuming policies alone constitute an effective governance system.

#### Tool-First Governance

Purchasing a GRC platform before defining governance processes.

#### Compliance-Only Governance

Treating AI Governance purely as a regulatory exercise.
