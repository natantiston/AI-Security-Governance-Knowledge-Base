# 17.11 — AI Abuse and Misuse

## Part 1 — AI Abuse and Misuse Foundations, Misuse Concepts, Threat Boundaries, Impact and Governance

### 1. AI Abuse and Misuse

**AI Abuse and Misuse** describes situations in which an AI system, model, agent, capability, or associated service is intentionally or unintentionally used in ways that violate organizational policies, security boundaries, acceptable-use requirements, contractual restrictions, or applicable law.

AI abuse and misuse can involve:

- malicious use;
- unauthorized use;
- policy violations;
- harmful use;
- fraudulent use;
- excessive use;
- circumvention of security controls;
- inappropriate use of AI capabilities.

Abuse and misuse should be analyzed separately from ordinary model errors or technical failures.

### 2. Objective of AI Abuse and Misuse Management

The objective is to establish a structured capability to:

- identify abusive or unauthorized AI use;
- distinguish legitimate use from misuse;
- assess potential impact;
- detect harmful patterns;
- contain misuse;
- investigate causes;
- preserve evidence;
- coordinate response;
- reduce recurrence.

The objective is not to eliminate all unusual AI behavior. Legitimate experimentation, unusual workloads, and benign errors should not automatically be classified as abuse.

### 3. AI Abuse

**AI Abuse** refers to the use of an AI capability in a manner that causes, facilitates, or materially contributes to harmful, unauthorized, deceptive, fraudulent, or otherwise prohibited activity.

Examples may include:

- using an AI system to facilitate fraud;
- deliberately generating prohibited content;
- abusing privileged AI tools;
- using AI to circumvent organizational controls;
- exploiting AI services for unauthorized purposes.

The classification of conduct as illegal or legally prohibited requires assessment against applicable law and facts.

### 4. AI Misuse

**AI Misuse** refers to inappropriate, unauthorized, unsafe, or unintended use of an AI system.

Misuse may be:

- intentional;
- negligent;
- accidental;
- caused by misunderstanding;
- caused by inadequate controls.

Misuse does not necessarily imply malicious intent.

### 5. AI Abuse versus AI Misuse

The terms should be distinguished for governance purposes.

| Dimension | AI Abuse | AI Misuse |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Often deliberate | May be intentional or accidental |
| Objective | Frequently harmful or prohibited | May involve inappropriate use |
| Example | Deliberate fraud facilitation | Unauthorized use of an internal AI tool |
| Governance focus | Prevention, detection and response | Prevention, education and control |
| Legal classification | Fact- and jurisdiction-dependent | Fact- and jurisdiction-dependent |

These categories can overlap.

### 6. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Security Incident

Not every misuse event is a cybersecurity incident.

For example:

- an employee violating an AI acceptable-use policy may be misuse;
- stolen credentials used to operate an AI system may constitute a security incident;
- an attacker using an AI system for malicious activity may create both security and abuse concerns.

The applicable classification should depend on the facts.

### 7. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Model Failure

Model failure concerns unintended failure of the model or system.

Misuse concerns how the system is used.

```text id="r7m3qx"
MODEL FAILURE
      |
      v
UNINTENDED SYSTEM BEHAVIOR

AI MISUSE
      |
      v
INAPPROPRIATE SYSTEM USE

AI ABUSE
      |
      v
HARMFUL / PROHIBITED USE
```

A model can function correctly while being intentionally misused.

### 8. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Model Compromise

Model compromise involves unauthorized modification, control, or compromise of the model or its supporting environment.

Misuse may occur without compromising the model.

For example, an authorized user could misuse an otherwise uncompromised AI system.

### 9. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Prompt Injection

Prompt injection is an attack technique involving manipulation of model instructions or context.

It may be used to facilitate misuse, but prompt injection and misuse are not synonymous.

### 10. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Fraud

AI fraud involves deceptive or fraudulent activity in which AI may be used as a capability, instrument, or component.

AI misuse is broader and does not necessarily involve fraud.

### 11. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Safety Incident

An abuse or misuse event may produce safety consequences, but safety impact should be assessed separately.

For example:

- unauthorized use of an AI system may create safety risk;
- harmful output may create safety impact;
- deliberate misuse may intentionally target safety controls.

### 12. AI Abuse and Misuse versus AI Privacy Incident

Misuse may involve personal data without necessarily constituting a privacy incident.

A privacy assessment should separately consider:

- personal-data processing;
- authorization;
- purpose;
- disclosure;
- access;
- applicable privacy requirements.

### 13. AI Abuse and Misuse versus Policy Violation

A policy violation is an internal governance classification.

Not every policy violation is abuse, and not every abusive activity is necessarily captured by an existing internal policy.

Policies should therefore define relevant categories explicitly.

### 14. AI Abuse and Misuse versus Acceptable Use

**Acceptable Use** defines activities an organization permits for an AI system.

Misuse may occur when activity falls outside approved use conditions.

Acceptable-use requirements may address:

- prohibited activities;
- sensitive information;
- external AI services;
- automation;
- tool access;
- business use;
- personal use.

### 15. AI Abuse and Misuse Attack Surface

The AI abuse and misuse attack surface includes the points through which AI capabilities can be accessed or manipulated.

Examples include:

- public interfaces;
- APIs;
- enterprise applications;
- agent interfaces;
- model endpoints;
- plugins;
- tools;
- integrations;
- user accounts.

### 16. AI Abuse and Misuse Trust Boundary

A trust boundary exists where authority, data, identity, or control moves between different trust domains.

Examples include:

- employee to AI service;
- customer to AI application;
- AI model to external tool;
- enterprise environment to public model provider.

Misuse controls should identify where trust assumptions can be violated.

### 17. AI Abuse and Misuse Authorization Boundary

The authorization boundary defines what an identity or AI agent is permitted to do.

Authorization should be evaluated independently from authentication.

```text id="q5v8nt"
AUTHENTICATION
      |
      v
WHO ARE YOU?
      |
      v
AUTHORIZATION
      |
      v
WHAT MAY YOU DO?
      |
      v
ACTION
```

An authenticated user is not automatically authorized to perform every available action.

### 18. AI Abuse and Misuse Privilege

Privilege determines the scope of actions available to a user, agent, service, or application.

High-privilege AI capabilities create greater potential impact if misused.

Examples include access to:

- production systems;
- financial systems;
- sensitive data;
- administrative tools;
- external communication systems.

### 19. AI Abuse and Misuse Privilege Escalation

Privilege escalation occurs when an identity or AI agent obtains greater permissions than intended.

It may result from:

- compromised credentials;
- configuration errors;
- excessive permissions;
- application vulnerabilities;
- authorization failures.

Privilege escalation may represent both a security event and an abuse-enabling condition.

### 20. AI Abuse and Misuse Capability Exposure

Capability exposure occurs when an AI system provides capabilities that can be used beyond their intended purpose.

Examples include:

- unrestricted code execution;
- unrestricted web access;
- unrestricted file access;
- external communication;
- administrative actions.

Capability exposure is not automatically misuse; it becomes relevant when the capability is used or exposed contrary to defined controls.

### 21. AI Abuse and Misuse High-Risk Capability

Certain capabilities may create greater misuse consequences.

Examples can include:

- autonomous transactions;
- privileged administration;
- financial actions;
- access to sensitive information;
- automated communications;
- physical-system control.

The risk depends on context, authorization, safeguards, and potential impact.

### 22. AI Abuse and Misuse User

A **User** is an individual or organizational actor interacting with an AI capability.

Users may include:

- employees;
- contractors;
- customers;
- administrators;
- developers;
- external partners.

User status alone does not establish whether conduct is abusive or malicious.

### 23. AI Abuse and Misuse Actor

An **Actor** is any entity capable of initiating or influencing AI use.

Actors may include:

- human users;
- applications;
- agents;
- service accounts;
- automated workflows;
- external attackers.

### 24. AI Abuse and Misuse Insider

An insider is an actor with legitimate or authorized access to organizational resources who may misuse that access.

Insider misuse may be:

- malicious;
- negligent;
- accidental;
- coerced.

### 25. AI Abuse and Misuse External Actor

An external actor operates outside the organization's authorized trust domain.

External misuse may include:

- unauthorized access;
- fraudulent activity;
- service abuse;
- malicious automation;
- exploitation of public AI interfaces.

### 26. AI Abuse and Misuse Automated Actor

Automated actors include:

- AI agents;
- bots;
- scheduled workflows;
- service accounts;
- autonomous processes.

Automated actions should remain attributable to an accountable owner or controlling system.

### 27. AI Abuse and Misuse Agentic Actor

An **Agentic Actor** is an AI-enabled system capable of planning or executing actions with some degree of autonomy.

Governance should distinguish between:

- human instruction;
- model-generated plan;
- authorized action;
- actual execution.

### 28. AI Abuse and Misuse Intent

Intent describes whether the actor deliberately sought to perform the activity.

Intent may be:

- malicious;
- negligent;
- accidental;
- uncertain.

Intent should not be inferred solely from the outcome.

### 29. AI Abuse and Misuse Malicious Intent

Malicious intent indicates deliberate conduct intended to cause harm, obtain unauthorized benefit, evade controls, or perform prohibited activity.

Determining intent should be evidence-based.

### 30. AI Abuse and Misuse Negligent Use

Negligent use occurs when an actor fails to exercise required care or follow established controls.

Examples may include:

- uploading sensitive information to an unauthorized AI service;
- bypassing required approval;
- sharing credentials;
- disabling security controls.

### 31. AI Abuse and Misuse Accidental Use

Accidental misuse occurs without an intention to violate a control.

Examples include:

- selecting the wrong AI environment;
- unintentionally exposing information;
- triggering an automated action through incorrect configuration.

### 32. AI Abuse and Misuse Authorized Use

Authorized use occurs within the permissions and conditions established for the AI system.

Authorized use should not be assumed to be risk-free.

### 33. AI Abuse and Misuse Unauthorized Use

Unauthorized use occurs when an actor uses an AI capability without the required authorization.

Examples include:

- accessing an AI system without permission;
- using restricted models;
- invoking privileged tools without authorization.

### 34. AI Abuse and Misuse Excessive Use

Excessive use involves activity that materially exceeds defined operational or security limits.

Examples include:

- abnormal API consumption;
- excessive automated requests;
- resource exhaustion;
- unusually large-scale generation.

Excessive use is not necessarily malicious.

### 35. AI Abuse and Misuse Resource Abuse

Resource abuse occurs when AI resources are consumed in a manner inconsistent with authorized use.

Potential resources include:

- compute;
- storage;
- API quotas;
- cloud services;
- model inference capacity.

### 36. AI Abuse and Misuse Service Abuse

Service abuse occurs when an AI service is used in a manner inconsistent with its intended or authorized purpose.

Examples may include:

- automated account creation;
- excessive API activity;
- prohibited automated workflows;
- deliberate circumvention of usage restrictions.

### 37. AI Abuse and Misuse Security-Control Evasion

Security-control evasion occurs when an actor attempts to circumvent established security mechanisms.

Examples include attempts to bypass:

- authentication;
- authorization;
- content controls;
- monitoring;
- rate limits;
- approval mechanisms.

### 38. AI Abuse and Misuse Policy Evasion

Policy evasion involves deliberately attempting to avoid organizational AI governance requirements.

Examples include:

- using unapproved AI services;
- concealing AI usage;
- bypassing approval;
- disguising prohibited activity.

### 39. AI Abuse and Misuse Governance Evasion

Governance evasion occurs when an actor deliberately avoids controls designed to establish accountability or oversight.

### 40. AI Abuse and Misuse Shadow AI

**Shadow AI** refers to AI systems or services used without appropriate organizational visibility, approval, or governance.

Potential risks include:

- unknown data processing;
- uncontrolled suppliers;
- unreviewed security controls;
- unmanaged credentials;
- lack of auditability.

Shadow AI is a governance condition and does not automatically imply malicious intent.

### 41. AI Abuse and Misuse Unauthorized AI Service

An unauthorized AI service is an AI capability that has not been approved for the relevant organizational use.

Risk assessment may consider:

- provider;
- data handling;
- security;
- contractual terms;
- jurisdiction;
- business purpose.

### 42. AI Abuse and Misuse Unapproved Model

An unapproved model is a model that has not passed the organization's defined approval process for its intended use.

Use of an unapproved model may constitute policy non-compliance even when the model itself is technically secure.

### 43. AI Abuse and Misuse Unapproved Tool

An unapproved tool is an external or internal capability that has not been authorized for the relevant AI workflow.

Examples include:

- plugins;
- APIs;
- browser tools;
- code execution;
- external connectors.

### 44. AI Abuse and Misuse Data Misuse

Data misuse occurs when information is used in a manner inconsistent with:

- authorization;
- purpose;
- policy;
- contractual conditions;
- applicable requirements.

### 45. AI Abuse and Misuse Sensitive-Data Misuse

Sensitive-data misuse involves inappropriate handling of information requiring enhanced protection.

Potential categories depend on context and applicable requirements.

Examples may include:

- personal data;
- credentials;
- financial information;
- confidential business information;
- security information.

### 46. AI Abuse and Misuse AI Capability Misuse

Capability misuse occurs when an AI capability is used outside its authorized purpose or operating conditions.

Examples may include:

- using an AI coding system to access unauthorized infrastructure;
- using an autonomous agent to perform unapproved transactions;
- using an internal model to process data outside its approved purpose.

### 47. AI Abuse and Misuse Evidence

Evidence may include:

- authentication records;
- authorization logs;
- prompts;
- model outputs;
- tool calls;
- API records;
- network telemetry;
- application logs;
- user activity;
- configuration history.

Evidence should be handled according to applicable security, privacy, legal, and retention requirements.

A timeline should establish:

### 49. AI Abuse and Misuse Governance Principle

**AI abuse and misuse management should distinguish intentional abuse, unauthorized use, negligent use, accidental misuse, technical failure, security compromise, and legitimate activity before determining the appropriate response.**

Classification should be evidence-based and proportionate to:

- intent;
- authorization;
- capability;
- impact;
- persistence;
- reversibility;
- legal and contractual context.

### 50. Core Principle of AI Abuse and Misuse

**AI governance should control not only what an AI system can do, but also who can use those capabilities, under what authority, for which purposes, with what safeguards, and with what accountability.**

A mature AI abuse and misuse capability therefore requires clear boundaries between:

- authorized use and unauthorized use;
- legitimate activity and prohibited activity;
- misuse and model failure;
- misuse and model compromise;
- security incidents and policy violations;
- accidental behavior and intentional abuse;
- internal governance classifications and legal classifications.

AI acceptable-use policies, misuse taxonomies, monitoring controls, behavioral detection, user restrictions, agent permissions, abuse detection, investigation procedures, and response playbooks are generally **governance or implementation practices** unless a specific applicable law, regulation, contractual obligation, or authoritative requirement mandates a particular activity.

Internal `AI-IMR-*` identifiers are knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

## Part 2 — AI Abuse and Misuse Controls, Detection, Assessment, Containment, Investigation and Remediation

### 51. AI Abuse and Misuse Management Policy

An **AI Abuse and Misuse Management Policy** establishes organizational requirements for identifying, preventing, detecting, investigating, containing, and remediating inappropriate or prohibited use of AI capabilities.

The policy should define:

- scope;
- acceptable use;
- prohibited use;
- user responsibilities;
- monitoring boundaries;
- escalation;
- evidence requirements;
- disciplinary or contractual processes where applicable;
- legal and regulatory assessment.

The policy is an internal governance control unless a specific applicable requirement establishes a corresponding obligation.

### 52. AI Abuse and Misuse Scope

The scope should cover relevant AI systems, users, applications, agents, models, tools, APIs, and workflows.

It should consider:

- employee use;
- contractor use;
- customer-facing AI;
- autonomous agents;
- third-party AI services;
- development environments;
- production environments;
- shadow AI.

### 53. AI Abuse and Misuse Register

An **AI Abuse and Misuse Register** records identified cases and relevant supporting information.

Typical fields include:

- incident identifier;
- actor type;
- AI system;
- capability used;
- category;
- authorization status;
- impact;
- evidence;
- response;
- remediation;
- closure status.

### 54. AI Abuse and Misuse Intake

A defined intake mechanism should allow potential misuse to be reported or detected.

Sources may include:

- users;
- security operations;
- application monitoring;
- abuse detection;
- privacy teams;
- suppliers;
- customers;
- automated controls.

### 55. AI Abuse and Misuse Detection Control

Detection controls identify potentially abusive or unauthorized AI activity.

Potential signals include:

- unusual usage patterns;
- prohibited prompts;
- unauthorized tools;
- excessive API activity;
- suspicious automation;
- unusual data access.

A detection signal is not automatically proof of abuse.

### 56. AI Abuse and Misuse User-Behavior Monitoring

User-behavior monitoring evaluates patterns such as:

- unusual frequency;
- unusual access times;
- abnormal model usage;
- unusual tool invocation;
- unexpected data access.

Monitoring should be proportionate and subject to applicable privacy, employment, and other requirements.

### 57. AI Abuse and Misuse Usage-Pattern Monitoring

Usage-pattern monitoring can identify deviations from expected behavior.

Examples include:

- sudden increases in inference volume;
- unusual model switching;
- repeated attempts to access restricted capabilities;
- unusual automation.

### 58. AI Abuse and Misuse Prompt Monitoring

Prompt monitoring may identify patterns associated with:

- policy evasion;
- unauthorized requests;
- prohibited activities;
- attempts to bypass security controls.

Prompt monitoring should account for privacy and data-protection requirements where applicable.

### 59. AI Abuse and Misuse Output Monitoring

Output monitoring can identify potentially problematic behavior such as:

- prohibited content;
- unauthorized disclosure;
- suspicious instructions;
- unexpected operational commands.

Output detection should distinguish generated content from confirmed real-world activity.

### 60. AI Abuse and Misuse Tool-Use Monitoring

Tool-use monitoring should record relevant model or agent interactions with external capabilities.

Examples include:

- database access;
- file operations;
- web browsing;
- code execution;
- administrative APIs;
- financial systems.

### 61. AI Abuse and Misuse Agent-Action Monitoring

Agentic systems should be monitored for actions that exceed their intended authority.

Monitoring should compare:

```text id="y5k8pn"

       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

```

Differences should be investigated where material.

### 62. AI Abuse and Misuse API Monitoring

API monitoring should identify:

- excessive requests;
- unusual clients;
- unexpected destinations;
- unauthorized endpoints;
- abnormal request patterns.

### 63. AI Abuse and Misuse Rate-Limit Monitoring

Rate-limit monitoring identifies activity exceeding defined thresholds.

Rate limits may reduce:

- resource abuse;
- automated misuse;
- denial-of-service risk;
- uncontrolled API consumption.

A rate-limit violation does not necessarily establish malicious intent.

### 64. AI Abuse and Misuse Authentication Monitoring

Authentication monitoring identifies suspicious access to AI services.

Signals may include:

- unusual login locations;
- repeated failures;
- unexpected devices;
- abnormal service-account use.

### 65. AI Abuse and Misuse Authorization Monitoring

Authorization monitoring verifies whether users and agents remain within their approved permissions.

### 66. AI Abuse and Misuse Privilege Monitoring

Privilege monitoring identifies excessive or unexpected access to high-impact AI capabilities.

### 67. AI Abuse and Misuse Capability Inventory

Organizations should maintain an inventory of material AI capabilities, including:

- models;
- agents;
- tools;
- APIs;
- data access;
- external integrations;
- administrative functions.

### 68. AI Abuse and Misuse Capability Classification

Capabilities should be classified according to potential misuse consequences.

Relevant factors include:

- privilege;
- autonomy;
- data sensitivity;
- external connectivity;
- reversibility;
- business impact.

### 69. AI Abuse and Misuse Acceptable-Use Control

Acceptable-use controls define permitted AI activities.

They should communicate:

- approved uses;
- restricted uses;
- prohibited uses;
- escalation requirements.

### 70. AI Abuse and Misuse Prohibited-Use Control

Prohibited-use controls identify activities that organizational policy does not permit.

Examples may include:

- unauthorized access;
- deliberate security-control bypass;
- unauthorized data disclosure;
- prohibited automated actions.

The exact prohibition should be defined by the applicable policy and legal context.

### 71. AI Abuse and Misuse Restricted-Use Control

Restricted-use controls identify activities requiring additional approval or safeguards.

Examples may include:

- processing sensitive data;
- using highly autonomous agents;
- accessing production systems;
- performing external transactions.

### 72. AI Abuse and Misuse Approval Control

Approval controls establish authorization for higher-risk AI uses.

Approval may consider:

- user;
- purpose;
- data;
- model;
- capability;
- environment;
- duration.

### 73. AI Abuse and Misuse Purpose Validation

Purpose validation confirms that an AI capability is being used for an approved business or operational purpose.

### 74. AI Abuse and Misuse Data-Access Validation

Data-access validation confirms that the user, model, or agent is permitted to access the relevant information.

### 75. AI Abuse and Misuse Tool-Authorization Validation

Tool authorization should be evaluated separately from model authorization.

A model being approved does not automatically authorize every tool available to it.

### 76. AI Abuse and Misuse Agent-Permission Control

Agent permissions should follow least privilege.

Permissions should be scoped according to:

- task;
- environment;
- data;
- tool;
- duration;
- user authority.

### 77. AI Abuse and Misuse Human-Approval Gate

Human approval may be required before an AI agent performs high-impact or difficult-to-reverse actions.

Potential examples include:

- financial transactions;
- privileged administration;
- external communications;
- deletion;
- production changes.

### 78. AI Abuse and Misuse Action-Boundary Control

Action boundaries define what an AI system may execute automatically and what requires human authorization.

### 79. AI Abuse and Misuse Transaction Limit

Transaction limits can constrain the maximum impact of an automated AI action.

Limits may apply to:

- monetary value;
- number of records;
- number of actions;
- resource consumption;
- communication volume.

### 80. AI Abuse and Misuse Geographic Control

Where appropriate, geographic restrictions can limit use of AI capabilities from unauthorized locations or jurisdictions.

Such controls should account for legitimate remote access and applicable privacy or employment requirements.

### 81. AI Abuse and Misuse Session Control

Session controls can restrict:

- session duration;
- concurrent sessions;
- privileged sessions;
- inactive sessions.

### 82. AI Abuse and Misuse Context Isolation

Context isolation limits the ability of one interaction or workflow to influence another.

This is particularly relevant where AI agents retain:

- memory;
- retrieved information;
- session state;
- tool state.

### 83. AI Abuse and Misuse Tenant Isolation

Multi-tenant AI services should prevent one customer or organizational unit from accessing another tenant's information or capabilities.

### 84. AI Abuse and Misuse Data-Handling Control

AI data-handling controls should define what information may be:

- entered;
- processed;
- stored;
- retrieved;
- transmitted;
- exposed.

### 85. AI Abuse and Misuse Sensitive-Data Control

Sensitive information should receive appropriate restrictions when used with AI services.

Potential controls include:

- classification;
- masking;
- redaction;
- access control;
- DLP;
- approval.

### 86. AI Abuse and Misuse Data-Loss Prevention

DLP controls can identify or prevent inappropriate transmission of sensitive information through AI systems.

Potential channels include:

- prompts;
- outputs;
- APIs;
- file uploads;
- tool calls.

### 87. AI Abuse and Misuse External-Communication Control

AI systems capable of sending external communications should have appropriate authorization and safeguards.

### 88. AI Abuse and Misuse Code-Execution Control

Code-execution capabilities should be constrained according to:

- identity;
- environment;
- network access;
- filesystem access;
- execution duration;
- privilege.

### 89. AI Abuse and Misuse Browser-Access Control

AI browser capabilities should be controlled to reduce unauthorized:

- browsing;
- account access;
- transactions;
- downloads;
- uploads.

### 90. AI Abuse and Misuse Administrative-Action Control

Administrative actions initiated through AI systems should require appropriate authorization and monitoring.

### 91. AI Abuse and Misuse External-Service Control

External services connected to AI systems should be:

- inventoried;
- assessed;
- authorized;
- monitored.

### 92. AI Abuse and Misuse Shadow-AI Detection

Shadow-AI detection identifies unauthorized use of AI services.

Potential techniques include:

- network monitoring;
- application discovery;
- endpoint telemetry;
- expense analysis;
- identity monitoring.

### 93. AI Abuse and Misuse Shadow-AI Containment

Where unauthorized AI usage creates material risk, containment may include:

- access restriction;
- data controls;
- service blocking;
- user notification;
- migration to an approved service.

### 94. AI Abuse and Misuse Alert Validation

Potential misuse alerts should be validated before escalation.

Validation should consider:

- authorized business activity;
- user context;
- change records;
- approved exceptions;
- evidence quality.

### 95. AI Abuse and Misuse Triage

Triage determines the urgency and handling path for suspected misuse.

Relevant dimensions include:

- intent;
- authorization;
- capability;
- impact;
- persistence;
- affected population;
- reversibility.

### 96. AI Abuse and Misuse Classification

Cases may be classified as:

- authorized activity;
- policy violation;
- accidental misuse;
- negligent misuse;
- suspected abuse;
- security incident;
- privacy incident;
- safety incident;
- fraud-related incident.

Multiple classifications may apply.

### 97. AI Abuse and Misuse Severity Assessment

Severity should consider:

- actual impact;
- potential impact;
- affected systems;
- affected population;
- duration;
- reversibility;
- degree of control bypass.

Severity is an internal governance classification unless an applicable authority establishes a specific legal classification.

### 98. AI Abuse and Misuse Intent Assessment

Intent should be assessed from available evidence.

Possible classifications include:

- confirmed intentional;
- likely intentional;
- accidental;
- negligent;
- unknown.

Intent should not be inferred solely from suspicious-looking activity.

### 99. AI Abuse and Misuse Authorization Assessment

The investigation should establish:

- what authority existed;
- what authority was actually exercised;
- whether authorization was exceeded;
- whether authorization was revoked or expired.

### 100. AI Abuse and Misuse Impact Assessment

Impact assessment should consider:

- confidentiality;
- integrity;
- availability;
- privacy;
- safety;
- financial loss;
- operational disruption;
- customer impact;
- reputational consequences.

### 101. AI Abuse and Misuse Blast-Radius Assessment

Blast radius determines how broadly misuse could affect:

- users;
- systems;
- data;
- business processes;
- customers;
- external parties.

### 102. AI Abuse and Misuse Persistence Assessment

Persistence evaluates whether misuse can continue after the immediate activity stops.

Potential persistence mechanisms include:

- scheduled workflows;
- agent memory;
- stored credentials;
- persistent integrations;
- automated processes.

### 103. AI Abuse and Misuse Reversibility Assessment

Reversibility evaluates whether the consequences of misuse can be reliably reversed.

Actions involving:

- deletion;
- financial transactions;
- external publication;
- privileged changes

may have limited reversibility.

### 104. AI Abuse and Misuse Containment Criteria

Containment criteria should define when action is required to prevent continued misuse.

Possible triggers include:

- active unauthorized access;
- ongoing data exposure;
- active fraud;
- uncontrolled agent execution;
- material security-control bypass.

### 105. AI Abuse and Misuse Account Suspension

Where justified and authorized, accounts may be suspended to prevent continued misuse.

Suspension should consider:

- business continuity;
- evidence preservation;
- legal requirements;
- proportionality.

### 106. AI Abuse and Misuse Credential Revocation

Credentials associated with confirmed or suspected unauthorized activity may be revoked or rotated.

### 107. AI Abuse and Misuse Token Revocation

API tokens, session tokens, or service credentials may be revoked when they could enable continued misuse.

### 108. AI Abuse and Misuse Agent Suspension

An AI agent may be suspended when its continued operation creates unacceptable risk.

### 109. AI Abuse and Misuse Tool Disablement

Individual tools may be disabled without completely shutting down the AI system.

This can provide a more targeted containment mechanism.

### 110. AI Abuse and Misuse Network Isolation

Network isolation may be appropriate when misuse involves:

- unauthorized external communication;
- compromised credentials;
- malicious automation;
- uncontrolled data transfer.

### 111. AI Abuse and Misuse Data-Access Revocation

Access to sensitive datasets may be revoked while preserving other legitimate AI functionality.

### 112. AI Abuse and Misuse Transaction Blocking

High-risk automated transactions may be blocked while the underlying activity is investigated.

### 113. AI Abuse and Misuse Containment Validation

Containment should be verified rather than assumed.

Validation should confirm:

- unauthorized activity has stopped;
- credentials are controlled;
- agents are contained;
- external communication is restricted;
- sensitive access has been removed.

### 114. AI Abuse and Misuse Evidence Preservation

Evidence should be preserved before remediation where practical.

Where an AI abuse or misuse requirement is legally mandatory, it should be separately mapped to the authoritative source, applicable jurisdiction, organizational role, affected activity, effective date, and specific requirement.
