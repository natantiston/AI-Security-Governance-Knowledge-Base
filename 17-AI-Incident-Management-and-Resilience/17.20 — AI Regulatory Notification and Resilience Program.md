# 17.20 — AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience Program

## Part 1 — AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience Foundations, Notification Concepts, Regulatory Assessment, Resilience Governance and Accountability

### 1. AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience Program

An AI regulatory notification and resilience program is an organizational framework for assessing regulatory notification obligations arising from AI-related incidents, disruptions, failures, or other regulated events while maintaining resilience and recovery capability.

The program should connect:

- AI incident management;
- regulatory assessment;
- notification decision-making;
- business continuity;
- disaster recovery;
- crisis management;
- regulatory communications;
- operational resilience.

A regulatory notification program should distinguish legal obligations from internal governance practices.

### 2. Objective of AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience

The objective is to establish a controlled capability for:

- identifying potentially reportable events;
- determining applicable notification requirements;
- preserving evidence;
- coordinating regulatory communications;
- maintaining critical services;
- recovering from disruption;
- demonstrating compliance where required.

### 3. AI Regulatory Notification

AI regulatory notification is communication to a competent authority where an applicable legal or regulatory framework requires, permits, or otherwise provides for notification concerning an AI-related event.

Notification obligations depend on:

- jurisdiction;
- organizational role;
- regulated activity;
- affected system;
- event type;
- applicable legal framework;
- applicable thresholds;
- effective date.

An internal incident classification does not by itself create a regulatory notification obligation.

### 4. AI Regulatory Notification versus Internal Escalation

Internal escalation moves information or decision authority within an organization.

Regulatory notification communicates with an external competent authority.

The two processes may be related but are not equivalent.

### 5. AI Regulatory Notification versus Regulatory Reporting

Regulatory notification may concern an event requiring prompt communication to an authority.

Regulatory reporting may involve:

- periodic reports;
- structured submissions;
- compliance reports;
- supervisory reporting;
- incident reports.

The applicable legal framework determines the distinction.

### 6. AI Regulatory Notification versus Regulatory Disclosure

Regulatory notification is communication to an authority.

Regulatory disclosure may involve communication to:

- customers;
- investors;
- the public;
- affected individuals;
- business partners.

A regulatory notification obligation does not automatically establish a public-disclosure obligation.

### 7. AI Regulatory Notification Trigger

A regulatory notification trigger is a fact or condition that requires the organization to assess whether notification obligations have arisen.

Potential triggers may include:

- a qualifying incident;
- a personal-data breach;
- a significant service disruption;
- a material cybersecurity event;
- a regulated AI-system incident;
- another event defined by applicable law.

The specific trigger must be established from the applicable legal or regulatory source.

### 8. AI Regulatory Notification Threshold

A notification threshold is the legal, regulatory, or contractual condition that determines whether a notification obligation applies.

Thresholds may depend on:

- severity;
- materiality;
- affected persons;
- duration;
- service impact;
- security impact;
- safety impact;
- financial impact.

An organization's internal severity level should not automatically be treated as a statutory notification threshold.

### 9. AI Regulatory Notification Applicability

Regulatory applicability should be assessed before concluding that an event is reportable.

The assessment should consider:

- jurisdiction;
- organizational role;
- AI-system classification;
- regulated activity;
- affected population;
- applicable authority;
- effective date.

### 10. AI Regulatory Authority

A regulatory authority is a competent public body with powers or responsibilities established by applicable law.

Depending on jurisdiction and sector, authorities may have powers concerning:

- supervision;
- investigation;
- enforcement;
- information requests;
- incident notification;
- corrective measures.

The identity and powers of the authority must be established from the applicable legal framework.

### 11. AI Regulatory Competent Authority

The competent authority is the authority legally responsible for the relevant regulated activity or obligation.

Organizations should identify the competent authority before an incident occurs where practical.

### 12. AI Regulatory Notification Ownership

Notification ownership defines who is responsible for coordinating the assessment and submission of a regulatory notification.

Possible functions include:

- legal;
- compliance;
- privacy;
- cybersecurity;
- AI governance;
- regulatory affairs.

The responsible function should not be inferred solely from the technical ownership of the AI system.

### 13. AI Regulatory Notification Decision Authority

The organization should establish who has authority to approve a regulatory notification.

The decision structure may involve:

- incident management;
- legal;
- compliance;
- privacy;
- security;
- business leadership;
- executive management.

The required approval structure depends on organizational governance and applicable law.

### 14. AI Regulatory Notification Evidence

A regulatory notification decision should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Evidence may include:

- incident facts;
- timeline;
- affected systems;
- affected populations;
- impact assessment;
- applicable legal requirement;
- notification deadline;
- decision rationale.

### 15. AI Regulatory Notification Decision Record

The organization should maintain a record of material notification decisions.

The record may document:

- event;
- applicable jurisdiction;
- authority;
- legal basis;
- threshold assessment;
- decision;
- decision maker;
- date and time;
- evidence.

A decision not to notify may also require documentation where appropriate.

### 16. AI Regulatory Notification Timeline

Where law or regulation establishes a notification deadline, the organization should maintain a mechanism for tracking:

- event discovery;
- qualification;
- assessment;
- approval;
- submission;
- follow-up.

The legally applicable deadline should be taken from the relevant authority rather than inferred from an internal incident policy.

### 17. AI Regulatory Notification Clock

The notification clock is the legally or regulatorily relevant period that begins from the event or condition specified by the applicable framework.

The organization should establish precisely:

- when the clock begins;
- what event starts it;
- what information is required;
- whether updates are permitted or required.

### 18. AI Regulatory Notification Content

Where notification is legally required, the submission should contain the information required by the applicable authority.

Depending on the framework, this may include:

- nature of the event;
- affected systems;
- impact;
- mitigation;
- corrective actions;
- contact information;
- timeline.

Organizations should not assume that one notification format applies across jurisdictions.

### 19. AI Regulatory Notification Completeness

Notification completeness should be assessed against the applicable legal or regulatory requirements.

A technically detailed incident report may still be incomplete from a regulatory perspective.

### 20. AI Regulatory Notification Accuracy

Regulatory communications should distinguish:

- confirmed facts;
- preliminary findings;
- estimates;
- assumptions;
- unresolved questions.

Organizations should avoid presenting hypotheses as established facts.

### 21. AI Regulatory Notification Updates

Where applicable law requires or permits supplementary notifications, organizations should maintain a mechanism for:

- initial notification;
- material updates;
- corrective information;
- final reporting.

The requirement for updates depends on the applicable framework.

### 22. AI Regulatory Notification Correction

Material errors in regulatory communications should be assessed for correction according to applicable requirements and organizational procedures.

### 23. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Liaison

A regulatory liaison coordinates communication between the organization and the competent authority.

The role may include:

- identifying the authority;
- coordinating submissions;
- tracking requests;
- maintaining records;
- coordinating internal responses.

### 24. AI Regulatory Notification Authority Register

Organizations should maintain a register of relevant authorities where appropriate.

The register may contain:

- authority;
- jurisdiction;
- regulated activity;
- notification mechanism;
- contact channel;
- submission procedure;
- escalation route.

### 25. AI Regulatory Notification Requirement Register

A regulatory requirement register should identify relevant notification requirements.

Possible fields include:

- jurisdiction;
- authority;
- legal source;
- regulated activity;
- trigger;
- threshold;
- deadline;
- required content;
- owner;
- evidence.

### 26. AI Regulatory Notification Legal Mapping

Notification requirements should be mapped to authoritative legal or regulatory sources.

The mapping should identify:

- source;
- provision;
- applicability;
- obligation;
- effective date;
- responsible organizational function.

Internal taxonomy identifiers should not be presented as legal provisions.

### 27. AI Regulatory Notification Jurisdiction Mapping

Organizations operating across jurisdictions should map applicable notification requirements by:

- country;
- regulatory regime;
- sector;
- organizational role;
- affected population;
- AI activity.

### 28. AI Regulatory Notification Cross-Border Assessment

Cross-border incidents may create multiple regulatory considerations.

Assessment should determine:

- affected jurisdictions;
- relevant authorities;
- applicable laws;
- potentially different notification thresholds;
- different deadlines.

One jurisdiction's notification obligation should not automatically be assumed to apply elsewhere.

### 29. AI Regulatory Notification Conflict Management

Different jurisdictions may establish different:

- thresholds;
- deadlines;
- content requirements;
- confidentiality rules;
- notification channels.

The organization should maintain a documented method for resolving operational conflicts while complying with applicable requirements.

### 30. AI Regulatory Notification Legal-Privilege Consideration

Where applicable, organizations should consider legal-privilege and confidentiality requirements when conducting notification assessments and preparing regulatory communications.

The existence and scope of privilege depend on applicable law and circumstances.

### 31. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Inquiry

A regulatory inquiry is a request or communication from a competent authority concerning an AI-related matter.

It should be distinguished from:

- an internal investigation;
- a supplier inquiry;
- a customer complaint;
- a regulatory notification initiated by the organization.

### 32. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Request Management

Organizations should maintain a controlled process for responding to regulatory requests.

The process may include:

- request intake;
- authority verification;
- legal assessment;
- ownership;
- response coordination;
- evidence preservation;
- deadline management.

### 33. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Communication Channel

Regulatory submissions should use the communication mechanism specified by the relevant authority where applicable.

Examples may include:

- regulatory portals;
- designated email;
- formal correspondence;
- prescribed reporting systems.

### 34. AI Regulatory Notification Submission Evidence

Evidence of regulatory submission should be retained where appropriate.

Examples include:

- submission receipt;
- reference number;
- timestamp;
- submitted content;
- acknowledgment;
- subsequent correspondence.

### 35. AI Regulatory Notification Notification Failure

Notification failure may occur when an organization:

- misses an applicable deadline;
- submits to the wrong authority;
- provides materially incomplete information;
- fails to follow a required submission method.

The consequences depend on the applicable framework.

### 36. AI Regulatory Notification Notification Readiness

Notification readiness is the organization's ability to identify, assess, approve, and submit a required notification within the applicable timeframe.

Readiness may depend on:

- authority mapping;
- legal requirements;
- evidence availability;
- decision authority;
- communication procedures.

### 37. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Notification Exercise

Organizations may conduct exercises to test the ability to assess and prepare regulatory notifications.

Exercises may test:

- authority identification;
- legal applicability;
- evidence collection;
- decision-making;
- drafting;
- approval;
- submission workflow.

Such exercises are generally governance or implementation practices unless specifically required by an applicable authority.

### 38. AI Regulatory Notification Tabletop Exercise

A tabletop exercise may simulate an AI incident requiring regulatory assessment.

Participants may include:

- legal;
- compliance;
- privacy;
- security;
- AI governance;
- business leadership.

### 40. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Resilience

Regulatory resilience is the organization's ability to continue meeting applicable regulatory obligations during significant operational disruption.

It may require:

- alternate personnel;
- alternate communication channels;
- accessible records;
- regulatory contact continuity;
- emergency decision authority.

### 41. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Continuity

Regulatory continuity ensures that regulatory obligations can still be assessed and managed when normal personnel, systems, or processes are unavailable.

### 42. AI Regulatory Notification Evidence Resilience

Regulatory evidence should remain available during disruption.

Critical evidence may include:

- incident records;
- system logs;
- decision records;
- legal mappings;
- authority information;
- submission history.

### 43. AI Regulatory Notification Communication Resilience

Regulatory communication capability should have appropriate fallback mechanisms where notification deadlines could otherwise be affected by:

- system outage;
- personnel absence;
- network failure;
- supplier failure.

### 44. AI Regulatory Notification Decision Resilience

Notification decision-making should remain possible during:

- executive unavailability;
- legal-team unavailability;
- system disruption;
- communications failure;
- prolonged incident response.

### 45. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Escalation

Regulatory escalation is the controlled elevation of an issue requiring regulatory or legal attention.

It should be distinguished from:

- internal executive escalation;
- incident escalation;
- mandatory notification;
- enforcement.

### 46. AI Regulatory Notification Regulatory Readiness

Regulatory readiness should include:

- current requirement mappings;
- current authority information;
- assigned owners;
- defined decision procedures;
- accessible evidence;
- tested communication mechanisms.

### 47. AI Regulatory Notification Resilience Governance Principle

**AI regulatory notification capability should combine accurate legal applicability assessment, clear ownership, evidence-based decision-making, reliable regulatory communication, and resilient processes that remain functional during significant disruption.**

### 48. AI Regulatory Notification Program Evidence

Program evidence may include:

- regulatory requirement mappings;
- authority registers;
- notification decision records;
- regulatory submissions;
- acknowledgments;
- exercises;
- corrective actions;
- assurance findings.

Documentation alone does not demonstrate that the organization can meet a notification deadline during a real disruption.

### 49. AI Regulatory Notification Program Governance Principle

**Regulatory notification governance should maintain a clear separation between legal obligations, regulatory powers, contractual requirements, and internal governance controls while providing traceable evidence for material notification decisions.**

### 50. Core Principle of AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience

**An effective AI regulatory notification and resilience program enables the organization to determine applicable notification obligations accurately, communicate with the appropriate authority when required, preserve decision evidence, and maintain regulatory capability during operational disruption without treating internal incident classifications or governance thresholds as substitutes for applicable law.**

The following distinctions are fundamental:

- regulatory notification ≠ internal escalation;
- regulatory notification ≠ regulatory reporting in every context;
- regulatory notification ≠ public disclosure;
- regulatory notification trigger ≠ internal incident trigger;
- internal severity ≠ statutory notification threshold;
- regulatory relevance ≠ regulatory violation;
- regulatory assessment ≠ notification obligation;
- notification assessment ≠ notification decision;
- notification decision ≠ notification submission;
- notification submission ≠ regulatory acceptance;
- regulatory inquiry ≠ regulatory notification;
- regulatory authority ≠ internal governance committee;
- supplier notification ≠ fulfillment of the organization's own regulatory obligation;
- contractual notification ≠ statutory notification;
- internal notification procedure ≠ legal requirement;
- regulatory exercise ≠ proof of regulatory compliance;
- documentation ≠ demonstrated notification capability;
- regulatory resilience ≠ business continuity alone;
- regulatory continuity ≠ disaster recovery;
- evidence availability ≠ evidence sufficiency;
- risk acceptance ≠ elimination of regulatory obligation.

Internal `AI-IMR-*` identifiers remain knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

## Part 2 — AI Regulatory Notification and Resilience Controls, Assessment, Decision-Making, Submission and Governance

### 51. AI Regulatory Notification Policy

The organization should maintain a regulatory notification policy defining:

- scope;
- applicable regulatory regimes;
- notification assessment;
- decision authority;
- notification procedures;
- evidence requirements;
- submission controls;
- resilience requirements;
- exception management.

The policy should distinguish legal requirements from internal governance practices.

### 52. AI Regulatory Notification Scope Control

The scope should identify AI systems, business activities, jurisdictions, and regulatory regimes potentially subject to notification obligations.

### 53. AI Regulatory Notification Requirement Inventory

The organization should maintain an inventory of potentially applicable notification requirements.

Relevant fields may include:

- jurisdiction;
- authority;
- legal source;
- organizational role;
- trigger;
- threshold;
- deadline;
- required content;
- owner.

### 54. AI Regulatory Notification Authority Register

The authority register should identify relevant competent authorities and their applicable communication mechanisms.

It should be periodically reviewed for changes.

### 55. AI Regulatory Notification Jurisdiction Control

For multinational operations, notification requirements should be mapped by jurisdiction.

The organization should avoid assuming that one jurisdiction's requirements apply universally.

### 56. AI Regulatory Notification Legal-Applicability Assessment

Each potentially reportable event should undergo an applicability assessment based on:

- jurisdiction;
- organizational role;
- regulated activity;
- AI system;
- event type;
- affected population;
- applicable law.

### 57. AI Regulatory Notification Trigger Assessment

The organization should determine whether the facts of an event satisfy a legally relevant notification trigger.

Internal incident categories should be used as supporting information rather than as substitutes for legal analysis.

### 58. AI Regulatory Notification Threshold Assessment

The applicable threshold should be assessed using the terminology and criteria established by the relevant legal or regulatory framework.

Potential criteria may include:

- materiality;
- severity;
- duration;
- affected persons;
- service disruption;
- security impact;
- safety impact.

### 59. AI Regulatory Notification Deadline Control

Where a notification deadline applies, the organization should establish a mechanism for tracking the legally relevant deadline.

The control should identify:

- triggering event;
- clock start;
- deadline;
- responsible owner;
- escalation point.

### 60. AI Regulatory Notification Clock Management

The organization should record when the relevant notification clock begins.

This is important because different frameworks may define the triggering point differently.

### 61. AI Regulatory Notification Preliminary Assessment

A preliminary assessment should establish whether an event potentially falls within a notification regime.

The assessment may initially rely on incomplete information, provided that uncertainty is explicitly recorded.

### 62. AI Regulatory Notification Detailed Assessment

A detailed assessment should be performed as sufficient evidence becomes available.

It may address:

- affected systems;
- affected individuals;
- business impact;
- technical facts;
- regulatory scope;
- threshold;
- deadline.

### 63. AI Regulatory Notification Evidence Collection

Evidence supporting the assessment should be collected and preserved.

Potential evidence includes:

- incident timeline;
- logs;
- affected-system inventory;
- data-flow information;
- impact analysis;
- system configuration;
- relevant legal mapping.

### 64. AI Regulatory Notification Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency should be assessed before a material notification decision is finalized.

Incomplete evidence should be distinguished from evidence demonstrating that a notification obligation does not exist.

### 65. AI Regulatory Notification Uncertainty Register

Material uncertainties should be documented.

Examples include uncertainty concerning:

- number of affected persons;
- incident duration;
- root cause;
- regulatory applicability;
- impact;
- geographic scope.

### 66. AI Regulatory Notification Assumption Register

Assumptions used in the notification assessment should be documented where they materially influence the decision.

### 67. AI Regulatory Notification Legal Review

Where appropriate, legal or qualified regulatory review should assess:

- applicability;
- threshold;
- deadline;
- authority;
- notification content;
- confidentiality considerations.

### 68. AI Regulatory Notification Compliance Review

Compliance functions may review whether the notification assessment aligns with applicable regulatory obligations and internal governance requirements.

### 69. AI Regulatory Notification Privacy Review

Where personal data is involved, privacy functions should assess potentially applicable privacy notification obligations separately from other regulatory regimes.

A single incident may create multiple notification assessments.

### 70. AI Regulatory Notification Security Review

Security teams should provide technical information relevant to regulatory assessment, including:

- attack scope;
- affected systems;
- compromise status;
- containment;
- recovery.

Technical findings should not automatically be treated as legal conclusions.

### 71. AI Regulatory Notification Safety Review

Where AI affects safety-relevant activities, appropriate safety functions should contribute to assessment of:

- affected systems;
- consequences;
- safeguards;
- mitigation;
- residual risk.

### 72. AI Regulatory Notification Business Review

Business owners should provide information concerning:

- affected services;
- customer impact;
- operational disruption;
- financial consequences;
- continuity measures.

### 73. AI Regulatory Notification Cross-Functional Assessment

Material notification assessments should coordinate relevant functions.

A typical structure may be:
