
# 17.15 — AI Incident Escalation

## Part 1 — AI Incident Escalation Foundations, Escalation Concepts, Authority, Thresholds and Governance

### 1. AI Incident Escalation

AI incident escalation is the controlled process of transferring an AI incident to a higher level of authority, expertise, response capability, or governance when defined conditions are met.

Escalation may occur because of:

- increasing severity;
- expanding scope;
- safety implications;
- privacy implications;
- security compromise;
- financial impact;
- regulatory significance;
- inability to contain the incident;
- uncertainty requiring specialized expertise.

### 2. Objective of AI Incident Escalation

The objective is to ensure that incidents receive an appropriate level of:

- authority;
- expertise;
- resources;
- decision-making;
- oversight;
- communication;
- response coordination.

Escalation should occur early enough to reduce avoidable impact while avoiding unnecessary escalation of routine incidents.

### 3. AI Incident Escalation versus AI Incident Response

Incident response describes the activities performed to manage an incident.

Escalation describes when responsibility, authority, expertise, or governance is elevated.

An incident may require response without escalation.

Conversely, an incident may require immediate escalation while technical investigation is still underway.

### 4. AI Incident Escalation Event

An escalation event occurs when a defined condition causes an incident to be transferred or elevated to another:

- team;
- authority;
- management level;
- specialist;
- governance function;
- external stakeholder.

### 5. AI Incident Escalation Trigger

An escalation trigger is a condition that requires or recommends escalation.

Examples include:

- severity threshold exceeded;
- safety impact identified;
- material customer impact;
- active compromise;
- rapid propagation;
- containment failure;
- significant uncertainty.

Escalation triggers should be documented.

### 6. AI Incident Escalation Threshold

An escalation threshold defines the point at which an incident must be elevated.

Thresholds may be based on:

- severity;
- financial impact;
- number of affected users;
- duration;
- criticality;
- safety;
- privacy;
- regulatory relevance;
- operational disruption.

Internal thresholds are governance mechanisms unless an external requirement establishes a mandatory threshold.

### 7. AI Incident Escalation Authority

Escalation authority defines who may initiate or approve escalation.

Potential authorities include:

- incident commander;
- security manager;
- AI system owner;
- risk owner;
- privacy officer;
- compliance function;
- executive management.

### 8. AI Incident Escalation Owner

An escalation owner is responsible for ensuring that an incident reaches the appropriate authority when escalation criteria are met.

The owner should ensure:

- criteria are applied;
- escalation is documented;
- receiving parties are informed;
- decisions are tracked.

### 50. Core Principle of AI Incident Escalation Foundations

**AI incident escalation ensures that incidents receive the appropriate level of technical expertise, operational authority, management oversight, and governance attention when defined thresholds relating to severity, impact, uncertainty, criticality, propagation, or response failure are reached.**

The following distinctions are fundamental:

- escalation ≠ incident response;
- escalation ≠ notification;
- escalation ≠ regulatory reporting;
- functional escalation ≠ hierarchical escalation;
- technical escalation ≠ executive escalation;
- supplier escalation ≠ transfer of organizational responsibility;
- severity ≠ escalation by itself;
- impact ≠ severity;
- uncertainty ≠ confirmed severity;
- criticality ≠ incident severity;
- containment failure ≠ automatic regulatory breach;
- regulatory relevance ≠ notification obligation;
- internal escalation threshold ≠ statutory threshold;
- internal governance control ≠ legal requirement.

Internal `AI-IMR-*` identifiers are knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

- unnecessary escalation;
- escalation volume;
- acknowledgment time;
- de-escalation rate.

Metrics should be interpreted alongside incident volume and detection coverage.

### 124. AI Incident Escalation Governance Review

The escalation framework should be reviewed periodically based on:

- incident experience;
- exercises;
- audit findings;
- organizational changes;
- AI-system changes;
- emerging threats.

### 125. AI Incident Escalation Control Principle

**AI incident escalation controls should provide clearly defined, evidence-based pathways for transferring incidents to the appropriate technical, operational, management, legal, regulatory, or crisis authority when established thresholds are reached.**

The following distinctions remain essential:

- escalation ≠ notification;
- escalation ≠ regulatory reporting;
- escalation ≠ incident response;
- escalation threshold ≠ statutory threshold;
- severity-based escalation ≠ automatic escalation in every case;
- regulatory relevance ≠ regulatory violation;
- legal review ≠ legal determination;
- supplier escalation ≠ transfer of organizational responsibility;
- acknowledgment ≠ agreement with classification;
- de-escalation ≠ incident closure;
- re-escalation ≠ new incident;
- escalation evidence ≠ proof of legal liability;
- internal governance control ≠ statutory requirement;
- recommended practice ≠ legal obligation.

Internal `AI-IMR-*` identifiers remain knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.
