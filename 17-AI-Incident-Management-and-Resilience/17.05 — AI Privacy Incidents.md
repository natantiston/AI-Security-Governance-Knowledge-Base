# 17.05 — AI Privacy Incidents

## Part 1 — AI Privacy Incident Foundations, Privacy Harm, Personal Data, Incident Boundaries and Governance

### 1. AI Privacy Incident

An AI privacy incident is an event involving an AI system that results in, or creates a material risk of, unauthorized access to, disclosure, alteration, loss, misuse, or other inappropriate processing of personal data.

AI privacy incidents can arise from:

* unauthorized disclosure;
* excessive data collection;
* inappropriate data use;
* data leakage through model outputs;
* unauthorized access;
* retention failures;
* incorrect data deletion;
* insecure integrations;
* supplier failures;
* model or application behavior;
* human error.

An AI privacy incident may occur without a cybersecurity compromise.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-001`

### 2. Objective of AI Privacy Incident Management

The objective of AI privacy incident management is to identify, contain, investigate, assess, remediate, and learn from AI-related events that affect privacy or personal-data protection.

The capability should protect:

* individuals;
* personal data;
* confidentiality;
* data-use restrictions;
* privacy rights;
* organizational compliance;
* trust in AI-enabled processing.

Privacy incident management should integrate with security incident response while maintaining a distinct privacy perspective.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-002`

### 3. AI Privacy Event versus AI Privacy Incident

An AI privacy event is an observable occurrence involving personal data or privacy controls.

An AI privacy incident is an event that meets defined organizational criteria for incident treatment because it involves actual or potential material privacy consequences.

For example:

```text id="p6r2mx"
Privacy-Relevant Event
        |
        v
Personal Data Involved?
        |
    +---+---+
    |       |
   No      Yes
    |       |
Record    Assess
            |
            v
     Unauthorized / Risky
         Processing?
          /       \
        No         Yes
        |           |
      Monitor     Incident
```

Not every privacy-relevant event is necessarily a reportable or material privacy incident.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-003`

### 4. AI Privacy Incident versus AI Security Incident

An AI security incident concerns compromise or violation of security properties.

An AI privacy incident concerns consequences involving personal data or privacy.

The two can overlap.

For example, compromised credentials may allow unauthorized access to an AI application's personal-data repository. The event may therefore represent both:

* a security incident; and
* a privacy incident.

Classification should preserve both dimensions where applicable.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-004`

### 5. AI Privacy Incident versus Data Breach

A data breach is a specific type of personal-data security incident involving a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data.

An AI privacy incident can be broader than a data breach.

For example, an AI system may process personal data for an unauthorized purpose without a conventional security breach.

The organization should therefore avoid treating every AI privacy incident as synonymous with a security breach.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-005`

### 6. AI Privacy Harm

AI privacy harm is a negative consequence to an individual or other protected interest arising from inappropriate AI-related processing of personal data.

Potential consequences may include:

* loss of confidentiality;
* exposure of sensitive information;
* identity-related harm;
* discrimination;
* financial harm;
* reputational harm;
* loss of autonomy;
* unwanted profiling;
* loss of control over personal information.

Actual harm should be distinguished from potential harm.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-006`

### 7. AI Privacy Risk

AI privacy risk is the possibility that AI-related processing of personal data could produce adverse consequences for individuals or create non-compliance with applicable privacy requirements.

Risk analysis may consider:

* type of data;
* purpose;
* scale;
* affected population;
* processing activity;
* likelihood;
* severity;
* safeguards;
* uncertainty.

Privacy risk should be assessed throughout the AI lifecycle.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-007`

### 8. Personal Data in AI Systems

Personal data may exist throughout an AI system's lifecycle.

Examples include:

* prompts;
* uploaded documents;
* training data;
* fine-tuning datasets;
* retrieval indexes;
* embeddings;
* logs;
* telemetry;
* user profiles;
* model outputs;
* feedback;
* support records.

The presence of personal data should be established based on the applicable legal definition rather than assumptions about the data format.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-008`

### 9. AI Personal-Data Processing

AI processing may include:

* collection;
* recording;
* organization;
* storage;
* retrieval;
* consultation;
* use;
* disclosure;
* combination;
* modification;
* deletion.

An AI architecture should therefore be evaluated across the complete personal-data lifecycle rather than only at the model-training stage.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-009`

### 10. AI Privacy Boundary

An AI privacy boundary defines the conditions within which personal-data processing is authorized and controlled.

The boundary may specify:

* permitted data;
* permitted purpose;
* authorized users;
* approved systems;
* geographic restrictions;
* retention;
* disclosure;
* downstream processing.

A privacy incident may occur when processing crosses an established privacy boundary.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-010`

### 11. AI Privacy Control

An AI privacy control is a measure designed to prevent, detect, reduce, or remediate privacy risk associated with AI processing.

Controls may include:

* access control;
* data minimization;
* purpose restrictions;
* retention controls;
* privacy testing;
* output filtering;
* deletion mechanisms;
* monitoring;
* contractual controls.

A documented privacy control should not automatically be considered effective without evidence.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-011`

### 12. AI Privacy Control Failure

A privacy control failure occurs when a privacy control does not operate as designed or does not adequately reduce the intended risk.

Examples include:

* access restrictions failing;
* retention controls failing;
* deletion not occurring;
* unauthorized data being processed;
* output filtering failing;
* monitoring failing to identify exposure.

Control failure should be distinguished from the resulting privacy impact.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-012`

### 13. AI Privacy Exposure

Privacy exposure represents the extent to which personal data may have been accessible, processed, disclosed, retained, or otherwise affected.

Exposure assessment may consider:

* number of individuals;
* data categories;
* duration;
* recipients;
* systems;
* geographic scope;
* accessibility.

Exposure does not necessarily establish actual access or harm.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-013`

### 14. AI Privacy Data Exposure

Data exposure occurs when personal data becomes accessible or available outside the intended privacy boundary.

Examples include:

* unauthorized model output;
* exposed prompt data;
* accessible logs;
* incorrect retrieval results;
* unintended API responses;
* misconfigured storage.

The organization should distinguish exposure from confirmed disclosure.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-014`

### 15. AI Privacy Disclosure

Privacy disclosure occurs when personal data is communicated or made available to a recipient who was not appropriately authorized to receive it.

Disclosure may occur through:

* model output;
* API response;
* generated document;
* email;
* user interface;
* logging system;
* third-party provider.

The recipient and circumstances should be established through evidence where possible.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-015`

### 16. AI Privacy Unauthorized Access

Unauthorized access occurs when a person, system, model, agent, or other entity accesses personal data without appropriate authorization.

Unauthorized access may result from:

* compromised credentials;
* excessive permissions;
* configuration errors;
* application flaws;
* model behavior;
* supplier access.

Authorization should be evaluated according to the applicable access model.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-016`

### 17. AI Privacy Unauthorized Processing

Unauthorized processing occurs when personal data is processed outside the applicable authorization, purpose, policy, contract, or legal basis.

Examples may include:

* using data for a new purpose without appropriate authorization;
* processing data outside approved systems;
* using personal data in an unapproved model;
* retaining data beyond authorized requirements.

The precise legal assessment depends on the applicable jurisdiction and processing context.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-017`

### 18. AI Privacy Purpose Violation

A purpose violation occurs when personal data is used for a purpose that is not appropriately authorized under the applicable governance or legal framework.

AI systems can increase this risk because the same data may be reused across:

* training;
* evaluation;
* retrieval;
* analytics;
* personalization;
* monitoring.

Purpose should therefore be explicitly governed.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-018`

### 19. AI Privacy Data Minimization Failure

A data-minimization failure occurs when an AI system processes more personal data than is necessary for its defined purpose.

Examples include:

* unnecessary prompt information;
* excessive training data;
* unnecessary logging;
* excessive retrieval context;
* unnecessary retention.

Data minimization should be evaluated against the specific processing purpose.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-019`

### 20. AI Privacy Retention Failure

A retention failure occurs when personal data is retained beyond an applicable retention requirement, policy, purpose, or justified operational need.

AI-specific retention challenges may involve:

* prompts;
* conversation histories;
* logs;
* embeddings;
* vector databases;
* training datasets;
* backups.

Deletion from one system does not necessarily mean all relevant copies have been removed.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-020`

### 21. AI Privacy Deletion Failure

A deletion failure occurs when personal data that should have been deleted remains accessible or continues to be processed.

AI environments may complicate deletion because data can exist in:

* source systems;
* caches;
* logs;
* indexes;
* embeddings;
* training datasets;
* backups.

Deletion requirements should therefore be mapped to the actual data architecture.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-021`

### 22. AI Privacy Correction Failure

A correction failure occurs when inaccurate personal data is not appropriately corrected within systems where correction is required.

AI systems may introduce additional complexity where personal data has propagated into:

* model inputs;
* retrieval systems;
* generated outputs;
* derived records;
* downstream applications.

The organization should determine which components actually contain or reproduce the affected information.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-022`

### 23. AI Privacy Data Integrity

Privacy data integrity concerns whether personal data remains accurate, complete, and protected against unauthorized alteration where those characteristics matter to the processing purpose.

Integrity failures may cause:

* incorrect decisions;
* incorrect profiles;
* inaccurate outputs;
* inappropriate actions.

Data integrity and confidentiality should be assessed separately.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-023`

### 24. AI Privacy Confidentiality

Privacy confidentiality concerns protection of personal data from unauthorized disclosure or access.

AI-specific confidentiality risks may arise through:

* prompts;
* outputs;
* retrieval;
* model interactions;
* logs;
* APIs;
* third-party services.

Confidentiality should be assessed across the complete AI processing chain.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-024`

### 25. AI Privacy Availability

Privacy-related availability concerns whether individuals or authorized personnel can access personal data or privacy-management functions when appropriately required.

Examples may include:

* inability to process access requests;
* loss of privacy records;
* unavailable consent records;
* unavailable deletion mechanisms.

Availability is therefore relevant to privacy governance as well as cybersecurity.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-025`

### 26. AI Privacy Data Subject

A data subject is an identified or identifiable natural person whose personal data is processed.

AI privacy incident management should identify affected data subjects where practical and legally appropriate.

Potential populations may include:

* customers;
* employees;
* applicants;
* students;
* patients;
* citizens;
* website users.

The applicable legal definition depends on the jurisdiction.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-026`

### 27. AI Privacy Affected Individual

An affected individual is a person whose personal data, privacy interests, or rights may have been affected by an AI privacy incident.

The affected population should be determined using evidence rather than assumptions.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-027`

### 28. AI Privacy Sensitive Data

Some categories of personal data may create heightened privacy risk because of their nature or the applicable legal framework.

Examples can include:

* health information;
* biometric information;
* financial information;
* precise location;
* authentication information;
* information concerning vulnerable individuals.

The exact legal classification depends on the applicable jurisdiction.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-028`

### 29. AI Privacy Special-Category Data

Under frameworks such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation, certain categories of personal data receive heightened protection.

Examples include data revealing:

* racial or ethnic origin;
* political opinions;
* religious or philosophical beliefs;
* trade-union membership;
* genetic data;
* biometric data used for uniquely identifying a natural person;
* health data;
* sex life or sexual orientation.

The applicable legal conditions and exceptions should be assessed separately from internal AI privacy classifications.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-029`

### 30. AI Privacy Data Classification

Personal data used by AI systems should be classified according to relevant organizational and legal requirements.

Classification may consider:

* sensitivity;
* identifiability;
* regulatory requirements;
* business impact;
* access requirements;
* retention requirements.

Classification should support appropriate controls.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-030`

### 31. AI Privacy Data Flow

A data flow describes how personal data moves through the AI architecture.

Example:

```text id="w4m8pk"
Individual
    ↓
Application
    ↓
Prompt / Input
    ↓
AI Model
    ↓
Retrieval / Tools
    ↓
Output
    ↓
Downstream System
    ↓
Storage / Logging
```

Privacy incident analysis should consider the complete flow rather than only the model itself.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-031`

### 32. AI Privacy Data Lineage

Data lineage identifies the origin, transformation, movement, and use of personal data.

Lineage may cover:

* source;
* collection;
* transformation;
* training;
* retrieval;
* inference;
* output;
* retention.

Incomplete lineage can create uncertainty during incident investigation.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-032`

### 33. AI Privacy Processing Context

Privacy risk depends heavily on processing context.

Relevant context includes:

* purpose;
* data subject;
* data category;
* model;
* recipient;
* location;
* retention;
* decision use.

The same dataset may present different privacy risks under different processing contexts.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-033`

### 34. AI Privacy Processing Purpose

The processing purpose identifies why personal data is being processed.

Purpose should be sufficiently defined to support assessment of:

* necessity;
* proportionality;
* authorization;
* retention;
* disclosure;
* secondary use.

AI systems should not be assumed to have unrestricted permission to reuse data merely because data is technically available.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-034`

### 35. AI Privacy Legal Basis

Where a legal framework requires a lawful basis for processing, the organization should identify the applicable basis.

Under the GDPR, possible lawful bases include:

* consent;
* contract;
* legal obligation;
* vital interests;
* public task;
* legitimate interests, subject to applicable conditions.

The correct basis depends on the actual processing activity and circumstances.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-035`

### 36. AI Privacy Consent Failure

A consent-related privacy incident may occur when processing relies on consent but the relevant consent conditions are not appropriately satisfied.

Potential issues include:

* missing consent;
* invalid consent;
* consent withdrawn but processing continues;
* inability to demonstrate consent;
* consent not covering the relevant purpose.

Consent should not be assumed to be the lawful basis for every AI processing activity.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-036`

### 37. AI Privacy Legitimate-Interest Assessment

Where processing relies on legitimate interests under an applicable framework, the organization should maintain appropriate assessment and documentation.

An AI privacy incident may arise when actual processing materially exceeds the scope supported by the assessment.

The precise legal requirements depend on the applicable jurisdiction.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-037`

### 38. AI Privacy Contractual Processing

AI systems may process personal data on behalf of another organization under contractual arrangements.

Privacy incidents should therefore distinguish:

* controller responsibilities;
* processor responsibilities;
* other relevant roles;
* contractual requirements.

Supplier involvement does not automatically determine legal responsibility.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-038`

### 39. AI Privacy Controller

Where applicable, a controller determines the purposes and means of processing personal data.

Incident governance should identify the relevant organizational role because privacy responsibilities can differ according to that role.

The precise legal definition depends on the applicable framework.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-039`

### 40. AI Privacy Processor

Where applicable, a processor processes personal data on behalf of a controller.

AI suppliers may act in different legal capacities depending on the processing arrangement.

The organization should not infer the legal role solely from the supplier's technical function.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-040`

### 41. AI Privacy Joint Responsibility

Some processing arrangements may involve multiple organizations sharing responsibility for determining purposes or means.

Where such arrangements exist, incident governance should establish:

* roles;
* responsibilities;
* evidence ownership;
* communication;
* notification responsibilities.

The legal assessment should be based on the actual processing arrangement.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-041`

### 42. AI Privacy Third-Party Processing

Third-party AI services may process personal data through:

* APIs;
* hosted models;
* SaaS applications;
* cloud platforms;
* analytics services;
* model providers.

Third-party processing should be included in privacy incident analysis.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-042`

### 43. AI Privacy Cross-Border Processing

AI services may cause personal data to move across jurisdictions.

Incident analysis should consider:

* data location;
* provider location;
* processing location;
* recipients;
* transfer mechanism;
* applicable restrictions.

Cross-border processing should be evaluated according to the applicable legal framework.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-043`

### 44. AI Privacy Transfer Incident

A transfer-related privacy incident may occur when personal data is transferred or made accessible across jurisdictions without appropriate authorization or safeguards.

AI-specific transfer paths may include:

* model APIs;
* cloud inference;
* support access;
* telemetry;
* logging;
* remote administration.

The actual legal assessment depends on the jurisdictions and transfer arrangement.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-044`

### 45. AI Privacy Model Memorization

Model memorization occurs when information from training or other model-development data may be retained in a manner that allows it to influence future outputs.

Memorization can create privacy risk where personal data is involved.

The existence of memorization does not automatically establish unlawful processing or a privacy incident; the specific circumstances must be assessed.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-045`

### 46. AI Privacy Training-Data Exposure

Training-data exposure occurs when personal information contained in training or fine-tuning data becomes accessible outside the intended processing boundary.

Potential mechanisms include:

* model outputs;
* debugging interfaces;
* training artifacts;
* datasets;
* logs;
* unauthorized access.

Exposure should be established through appropriate evidence where possible.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-046`

### 47. AI Privacy Inference Exposure

Inference exposure occurs when an AI system produces information about an individual that reveals or allows inference of personal information.

Examples may include:

* sensitive characteristics;
* private relationships;
* identity information;
* behavioral information.

The legal and privacy significance depends on the data, context, and applicable framework.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-047`

### 48. AI Privacy Membership-Inference Risk

Membership inference concerns attempts to determine whether particular information or an individual was included in a model's training data.

Where personal data is involved, successful membership inference may create privacy risk.

Membership-inference risk is distinct from confirmed disclosure of the underlying training record.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-048`

### 49. AI Privacy Incident Governance Principle

AI privacy incident governance should ensure that privacy-relevant events are:

* identified;
* assessed;
* contained;
* investigated;
* documented;
* remediated;
* appropriately escalated.

The organization should maintain clear distinctions between:

* privacy event and privacy incident;
* privacy incident and security incident;
* privacy incident and data breach;
* exposure and confirmed disclosure;
* access and unauthorized access;
* processing and unauthorized processing;
* potential harm and actual harm;
* personal data and non-personal data;
* data minimization and data deletion;
* model memorization and confirmed disclosure;
* privacy risk and confirmed legal violation;
* supplier involvement and legal responsibility;
* internal classification and regulatory classification.

Control ID: `AI-IMR-PRI-049`

### 50. Core Principle of AI Privacy Incident Foundations

AI privacy incident management should protect individuals and personal data throughout the AI lifecycle while ensuring that privacy incidents are investigated according to both the technical architecture and the applicable legal context.

A mature AI privacy incident model should recognize that privacy exposure can occur at many layers:

```text id="z3k8nt"
Data Collection
      ↓
Training / Fine-Tuning
      ↓
Model / Embedding
      ↓
Prompt / Input
      ↓
Retrieval
      ↓
Inference
      ↓
Output
      ↓
Tool / API
      ↓
Logging
      ↓
Storage
      ↓
Third Party
      ↓
Deletion / Retention
```

The central governance principle is:

**An AI privacy incident should be assessed across the complete personal-data lifecycle, not only at the model layer.**

The organization should distinguish internal governance controls from legal obligations. Requirements concerning personal-data breaches, individual rights, lawful processing, international transfers, notification, retention, and other privacy matters depend on the applicable jurisdiction, role, processing activity, and facts of the incident.

Controls represented by `AI-IMR-PRI-*` are internal knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers. They do not themselves constitute statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, or legal obligations.
