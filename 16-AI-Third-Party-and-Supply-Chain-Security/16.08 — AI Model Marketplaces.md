# 16.08 — AI Model Marketplaces

## Part 1 — Foundations, Marketplace Risk, Model Acquisition, Trust, Provenance, Distribution and Governance

### 1. AI Model Marketplace Risk

AI model marketplace risk is the risk arising from acquiring, discovering, evaluating, downloading, integrating, deploying, or operating AI models obtained through platforms that aggregate, host, distribute, or facilitate access to models from multiple publishers or contributors.

A marketplace may provide convenience and discovery, but it also introduces additional trust and supply-chain considerations.

Relevant risks include:

* model provenance;
* publisher identity;
* artifact authenticity;
* malicious or compromised models;
* licensing;
* model versions;
* dependencies;
* marketplace security;
* distribution integrity;
* incomplete documentation;
* abandoned models;
* vulnerable components;
* unauthorized models;
* model substitution;
* concentration in a marketplace.

**Control ID: AI-MKT-001**

---

### 2. Objective of AI Model Marketplace Governance

The objective is to ensure that models acquired through marketplaces are subject to appropriate:

* identification;
* provenance verification;
* licensing assessment;
* security evaluation;
* artifact integrity verification;
* dependency assessment;
* approval;
* deployment control;
* monitoring;
* lifecycle management.

Marketplace availability should not be treated as equivalent to organizational approval.

**Control ID: AI-MKT-002**

---

### 3. AI Model Marketplace

An AI model marketplace is a platform through which users can discover, evaluate, access, download, deploy, or otherwise obtain AI models from one or more publishers.

Marketplace functionality may include:

* model catalogs;
* model cards;
* downloads;
* hosted inference;
* APIs;
* deployment integrations;
* evaluation information;
* ratings;
* usage statistics;
* licensing information.

The marketplace may act as a distribution platform without being the original model developer.

**Control ID: AI-MKT-003**

---

### 49. Marketplace Governance Principle

An AI model marketplace should be treated as a **model-distribution and supply-chain environment**, not merely as a catalog of downloadable AI models.

Governance should establish visibility into:

* who published the model;
* where the model originated;
* which artifact was acquired;
* whether the artifact is authentic;
* what license applies;
* what dependencies exist;
* what modifications occurred;
* what security evidence exists;
* what risks arise from marketplace dependence.

**Control ID: AI-MKT-049**

### 50. Marketplace Trust Principle

Trust in a marketplace model should be based on evidence rather than solely on:

* marketplace presence;
* download volume;
* ratings;
* popularity;
* publisher claims;
* benchmark performance.

The organization should establish its own trust decision based on provenance, integrity, licensing, security evaluation, deployment context, and business risk.

**Control ID: AI-MKT-050**

### Core Principles

1. **Marketplace-Dependency Principle** — A marketplace should be considered a material third-party dependency where organizational operations rely on it.
2. **Publisher-Identity Principle** — The identity of the model publisher should be established to the extent reasonably possible.
3. **Provenance Principle** — Material model artifacts should have traceable origin and acquisition history.
4. **Authenticity Principle** — Marketplace artifacts should be authenticated using appropriate technical and organizational evidence.
5. **Integrity Principle** — Model artifacts should be protected from unauthorized modification.
6. **Version Principle** — The exact model version or artifact used in production should be identifiable.
7. **Lineage Principle** — Material modifications should remain traceable to the original marketplace artifact.
8. **Licensing Principle** — Model and associated component licenses should be assessed according to their applicable terms.
9. **Documentation Principle** — Marketplace documentation should be evaluated for scope, currency, and relevance.
10. **Evaluation Principle** — Marketplace benchmark information should not replace organization-specific evaluation.
11. **Security Principle** — Marketplace models should undergo proportionate security assessment before material deployment.
12. **Supply-Chain Principle** — Model governance should include material runtime, library, container, and infrastructure dependencies.
13. **Quarantine Principle** — Untrusted or newly acquired artifacts should be controlled before production use where appropriate.
14. **Registry Principle** — Approved marketplace artifacts should be maintained in controlled organizational repositories where feasible.
15. **Popularity Principle** — Popularity and community adoption should not be treated as independent proof of security.
16. **Trust-Boundary Principle** — The marketplace-to-organization boundary should be explicitly governed.
17. **Modification Principle** — Fine-tuning, quantization, conversion, and adapters should be governed as material model changes where applicable.
18. **Accountability Principle** — Marketplace availability does not transfer organizational responsibility for secure deployment.
19. **Evidence Principle** — Material marketplace governance decisions should be supported by traceable evidence.
20. **Governance Principle** — Marketplace model acquisition should remain lifecycle-based, risk-based, and evidence-driven.


## Part 2 — Marketplace Due Diligence, Security Controls, Model Assurance, Licensing, Data Governance and Supply-Chain Security

* marketplace identity;
* publisher identity;
* model provenance;
* artifact integrity;
* licensing;
* model documentation;
* evaluation evidence;
* dependencies;
* known vulnerabilities;
* maintenance;
* marketplace security;
* intended use;
* organizational criticality.

The depth of due diligence should be proportionate to the model's risk.

### Core Principles

1. **Due-Diligence Principle** — Material marketplace models should undergo proportionate due diligence.
2. **Marketplace-Assurance Principle** — The marketplace itself should be assessed when it represents a material dependency.
3. **Publisher Principle** — Publisher identity, maintenance, and security evidence should be assessed.
4. **Artifact Principle** — The exact acquired artifact should be identifiable and controlled.
5. **Integrity Principle** — Material artifacts should be authenticated and protected against unauthorized modification.
6. **Version Principle** — Production deployments should use explicitly approved versions or immutable artifacts.
7. **Evaluation Principle** — Marketplace benchmark information should not replace organization-specific evaluation.
8. **Behavioral-Assurance Principle** — Model behavior should be tested for the intended use case.
9. **License Principle** — Model and associated component licensing should be assessed separately where applicable.
10. **Data-Governance Principle** — Marketplace data processing should remain subject to appropriate organizational governance.
11. **Privacy Principle** — Applicable privacy requirements should be assessed according to the actual processing arrangement.
12. **Supply-Chain Principle** — Security should cover marketplace, model, software, runtime, and infrastructure dependencies.
13. **Runtime-Security Principle** — A trusted marketplace artifact does not guarantee a secure runtime.
14. **Identity Principle** — Marketplace and model identities should be appropriately controlled.
15. **Least-Privilege Principle** — Marketplace models and associated workloads should receive only necessary access.
16. **Resilience Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should have appropriate recovery and fallback arrangements.
17. **Incident Principle** — Marketplace and model supply-chain incidents should have defined response procedures.
18. **Regulatory-Traceability Principle** — Legal obligations should be distinguished from internal governance practices.
19. **Evidence Principle** — Material decisions should be supported by traceable and current evidence.
20. **Assurance Principle** — Marketplace assurance should be evaluated separately from assurance over the organization's actual implementation.

### Core Principles

1. **Continuous-Monitoring Principle** — Marketplace dependencies should be monitored throughout their lifecycle.
2. **Version-Monitoring Principle** — Production model versions should remain identifiable and controlled.
3. **Integrity-Monitoring Principle** — Material artifacts should be monitored for unauthorized change.
4. **Publisher-Monitoring Principle** — Material changes to publishers and maintainers should be assessed.
5. **Dependency-Monitoring Principle** — Model and software dependencies should be monitored for material changes and vulnerabilities.
6. **Behavioral-Assurance Principle** — Material behavioral changes should be identified and evaluated.
7. **Regression Principle** — Significant changes should be evaluated against established behavioral and security baselines.
8. **License-Monitoring Principle** — Material licensing changes should be identified and assessed.
9. **Incident Principle** — Marketplace and model supply-chain incidents should have defined detection, containment, recovery, and evidence procedures.
10. **Evidence Principle** — Monitoring and incident decisions should produce traceable evidence.
11. **Regulatory-Applicability Principle** — Regulatory applicability should be reassessed when relevant circumstances change.
12. **Concentration Principle** — Common marketplace, publisher, model, and infrastructure dependencies should be identified.
13. **Resilience Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should have appropriate recovery arrangements.
14. **Fallback Principle** — Material workloads should have appropriate fallback capabilities where justified by risk.
15. **Portability Principle** — Marketplace dependence should be evaluated for practical portability, not merely artifact availability.
16. **Exit-Readiness Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should have credible exit arrangements.
17. **Assurance Principle** — Assurance should be refreshed when material risk, model, publisher, or marketplace conditions change.
18. **Automation Principle** — Automated marketplace controls should be governed, auditable, and appropriately bounded.
19. **Human-Accountability Principle** — Significant automated control decisions should retain appropriate human accountability.
20. **Continuous-Assurance Principle** — Marketplace governance should continuously incorporate monitoring, testing, incidents, assurance findings, and risk reassessment.

### Core Principles

1. **Enterprise-Governance Principle** — Marketplace dependencies should be governed within the organization's broader AI, cybersecurity, third-party-risk, and enterprise-risk frameworks.
2. **Accountability Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should have identifiable accountable owners.
3. **Lifecycle Principle** — Marketplace governance should cover discovery, acquisition, verification, deployment, monitoring, change, and retirement.
4. **Architecture Principle** — Marketplace integrations should operate within defined enterprise security and trust boundaries.
5. **Threat-Modeling Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should be assessed for realistic attack paths and supply-chain threats.
6. **Least-Privilege Principle** — Marketplace models and applications should receive only the permissions necessary for approved purposes.
7. **Data-Boundary Principle** — Marketplace models should have controlled access to enterprise data according to classification and business need.
8. **Supply-Chain Principle** — Marketplace governance should include model artifacts, software dependencies, datasets, runtimes, and supporting infrastructure.
9. **Concentration Principle** — Marketplace diversification should be evaluated for genuine dependency independence.
10. **Strategic-Dependency Principle** — Material marketplace dependencies should be visible within enterprise strategic-risk management.
11. **Resilience Principle** — Critical marketplace-dependent capabilities should have recovery arrangements appropriate to business requirements.
12. **Portability Principle** — Portability should be assessed operationally rather than inferred from the ability to download an artifact.
13. **Exit Principle** — Material dependencies should have credible and, where appropriate, tested exit arrangements.
14. **Assurance Principle** — Marketplace assurance should consider evidence quality, scope, recency, and independence.
15. **Evidence Principle** — Material marketplace decisions should be supported by traceable evidence.
16. **Automation Principle** — Automated marketplace controls should be governed, auditable, and resilient to automation failure.
17. **Human-Accountability Principle** — Automation should not obscure responsibility for significant risk decisions.
18. **Continuous-Improvement Principle** — Incidents, assessments, testing, audits, and changes should improve marketplace governance.
19. **Legal-Distinction Principle** — Legal requirements, regulatory powers, contractual obligations, internal controls, and recommended practices should be explicitly distinguished.
20. **Enterprise-Assurance Principle** — Effective marketplace governance requires evidence that controls operate across the complete dependency lifecycle rather than relying solely on marketplace claims or documentation.
