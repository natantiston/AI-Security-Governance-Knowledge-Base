# 16.14 — AI Software Supply-Chain Assurance

## Part 1 — Foundations, Assurance Scope, Software Supply-Chain Trust, Assurance Objectives, Evidence and Risk Architecture

### 1. AI Software Supply-Chain Assurance Definition

AI software supply-chain assurance is the structured process of obtaining, evaluating, validating, and maintaining evidence that software used to build, deploy, operate, secure, and govern AI systems is sufficiently trustworthy for its intended use.

AI software supply-chain assurance extends conventional software assurance to account for AI-specific dependencies such as:

* Machine-learning frameworks
* Inference engines
* Model-serving runtimes
* Tokenizers
* Embedding libraries
* Vector-database clients
* AI SDKs
* Agent frameworks
* Tool-integration libraries
* Model-conversion utilities
* Evaluation frameworks
* AI security tooling
* Data-processing libraries

---

### 2. Assurance Objective

The primary objective is to provide reasonable confidence that material AI software dependencies:

* Are correctly identified
* Originate from sufficiently trusted sources
* Have appropriate provenance
* Have not been materially tampered with
* Are appropriately secured
* Are evaluated for vulnerabilities
* Are subject to appropriate lifecycle management
* Remain consistent with approved architecture and risk decisions

---

### 3. Assurance Is Not Absolute Trust

Assurance does not establish that a software component is universally safe.

It establishes a level of confidence based on:

* Scope
* Evidence
* Testing
* Validation
* Risk
* Context
* Time

**Assurance ≠ absolute security.**

---

* Artifact tampering
* Dependency confusion
* Typosquatting
* Unsafe software behavior

### 197. Assurance Limitation Statement

Material assurance conclusions should identify significant limitations.

* Incomplete source visibility

* Limited supplier transparency

* Incomplete runtime visibility

### 198. Residual Assurance Risk

Even after assurance activities, uncertainty may remain.

Residual uncertainty should be considered in risk decisions.

### 199. Assurance Governance Principle

AI software supply-chain assurance should provide evidence-based confidence that material software dependencies are appropriately identified, evaluated, protected, monitored, and governed throughout their lifecycle.

---

### 200. Software Supply-Chain Trust Principle

Trust in an AI software dependency should be based on evidence and controls rather than reputation, popularity, licensing model, or supplier claims alone.

---

### 201. Assurance Scope Principle

The scope of assurance should correspond to the actual dependency and trust boundary rather than only the immediately visible software package.

---

### 202. Assurance Proportionality Principle

Assurance depth should be proportionate to:

* Criticality
* Exposure
* Privilege
* Data access
* AI autonomy
* Dependency concentration
* Replacement difficulty

---

### 203. Assurance Evidence Principle

Material assurance conclusions should be supported by evidence that is sufficiently relevant, reliable, current, complete, and traceable.

---

### 204. Continuous-Assurance Principle

Material software dependencies should remain subject to assurance throughout their operational lifecycle rather than only at initial adoption.

---

### 205. Runtime-Assurance Principle

Where risk warrants, assurance should consider the difference between approved software composition and actual runtime composition.

---

### 206. Dependency-Assurance Principle

Assurance should consider direct, transitive, hidden, shared, and strategically important dependencies.

---

### 207. AI-Specific Assurance Principle

AI software assurance should account for dependencies that can influence model execution, data processing, retrieval, tool use, agent behavior, security controls, and AI decision workflows.

---

### 208. Independent-Assurance Principle

Higher-risk dependencies should receive an appropriate degree of independent validation rather than relying exclusively on the component supplier or development team.

---

### 209. Evidence-Traceability Principle

Assurance should maintain traceability from the applicable requirement through controls and evidence to the resulting risk decision.

---

### 210. AI Software Supply-Chain Assurance Risk Decision Principle

The organization should make software supply-chain assurance decisions based on the combination of dependency criticality, trust, provenance, integrity, vulnerability exposure, runtime behavior, lifecycle status, resilience, evidence quality, and residual risk.

Internal `AI-SSA-*` identifiers used in this knowledge base are organizational taxonomy identifiers and are not statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, or mandatory identifiers from external legislation or standards.
