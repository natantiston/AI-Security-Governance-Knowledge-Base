# 16.10 — Third-Party Dataset Risk

## Part 1 — Foundations, Dataset Provider Classification, Data Provenance, Trust Boundaries and Risk Architecture

### 1 — Third-Party Dataset Risk Definition

Third-party dataset risk is the risk that data obtained from an external party introduces security, privacy, legal, quality, integrity, operational, ethical, regulatory, or model-performance risks into an organization's AI systems.

### 2 — Third-Party Dataset Governance Objective

Third-party datasets should be governed so that the organization can establish, proportionate to risk:

* where the data originated;
* who supplied it;
* what it contains;
* how it was obtained;
* what rights apply;
* how trustworthy it is;
* how it may be used;
* how it was transformed;
* where it is stored;
* who can access it;
* how it affects downstream AI systems.

### 3 — Dataset Definition

A dataset is a structured or unstructured collection of data used for one or more purposes such as:

* training;
* fine-tuning;
* validation;
* testing;
* evaluation;
* benchmarking;
* retrieval;
* analytics;
* model development.

### 4 — Third-Party Dataset Definition

A third-party dataset is data obtained from an external organization, individual, marketplace, repository, partner, supplier, public source, commercial provider, or other external origin rather than being generated exclusively within the organization.

### 5 — Dataset Provider

A dataset provider is the external party that supplies or makes the dataset available to the organization.

The provider may be:

* a commercial data vendor;
* research organization;
* government entity;
* academic institution;
* nonprofit organization;
* marketplace;
* repository operator;
* software or technology company;
* business partner;
* individual publisher.

### 6 — Dataset Source

The dataset source is the underlying origin from which the data was collected, generated, aggregated, or derived.

The source and provider may be different entities.

### 7 — Provider Versus Source

Organizations should distinguish between:

**Source → Collector/Aggregator → Dataset Provider → Organization**

because each stage may introduce different risks and responsibilities.

### 8 — Dataset Consumer

The dataset consumer is the organizational function or system that obtains and uses the third-party dataset.

### 9 — Dataset Owner

An accountable internal owner should be identified for material third-party datasets.

Ownership should include responsibility for appropriate:

* business use;
* governance;
* risk assessment;
* access;
* lifecycle management.

### 10 — Dataset Custodian

A technical or operational custodian may be responsible for:

* storage;
* access;
* transfer;
* processing;
* backup;
* security controls.

### 11 — Dataset Governance Roles

Material datasets may involve:

* business owner;
* data owner;
* data steward;
* data custodian;
* AI/model owner;
* cybersecurity;
* privacy;
* legal;
* procurement;
* compliance;
* internal audit.

### 12 — Dataset Role Separation

Organizations should distinguish responsibility for:

* acquiring data;
* approving data use;
* securing data;
* determining legal requirements;
* using data in AI systems;
* independently assuring controls.

### 13 — Dataset Risk Boundary

Third-party dataset risk extends beyond the dataset file itself to include:

* source;
* provider;
* collection method;
* licensing;
* processing;
* transformation;
* storage;
* transmission;
* downstream AI use.

### 14 — Dataset Trust Boundary

The boundary between organizational data environments and third-party datasets should be explicitly identified.

### 15 — External Data Trust Principle

Externally sourced data should not automatically be treated as trustworthy merely because it is publicly available, commercially sold, widely used, or supplied by a reputable organization.

### 16 — Dataset Acquisition Principle

Third-party datasets should be acquired through controlled processes proportionate to their intended use and risk.

### 17 — Dataset Classification

Organizations should classify third-party datasets according to relevant characteristics such as:

* sensitivity;
* intended AI use;
* business criticality;
* regulatory relevance;
* intellectual-property implications;
* privacy exposure;
* security risk.

### 18 — Dataset Criticality

Dataset criticality should reflect the potential impact of:

* loss;
* corruption;
* unauthorized disclosure;
* unavailability;
* incorrect data;
* malicious manipulation.

### 19 — Dataset Risk Dimensions

Third-party dataset risk assessment should consider at least:

* provenance;
* integrity;
* quality;
* privacy;
* security;
* licensing;
* legal rights;
* representativeness;
* bias;
* supply-chain risk;
* operational dependency.

### 20 — Dataset Use Classification

The same dataset may require different controls depending on whether it is used for:

* experimentation;
* research;
* training;
* fine-tuning;
* evaluation;
* production inference;
* retrieval;
* decision support.

### 21 — Training Dataset

A training dataset contains information used to develop model parameters or otherwise train an AI model.

### 22 — Fine-Tuning Dataset

A fine-tuning dataset contains examples used to adapt an existing model to a particular task, domain, behavior, or organization.

### 23 — Evaluation Dataset

An evaluation dataset is used to measure model or system performance against defined criteria.

### 24 — Validation Dataset

A validation dataset may be used during model-development processes to tune or assess system behavior without serving as the primary training dataset.

### 25 — Test Dataset

A test dataset is used to assess system performance after development activities under defined testing conditions.

### 26 — Benchmark Dataset

A benchmark dataset provides a standardized or comparative basis for evaluating AI-system performance.

### 27 — Retrieval Dataset

A retrieval dataset contains information made available to an AI system for retrieval-augmented generation or other retrieval-based processing.

### 28 — Reference Dataset

A reference dataset may provide authoritative or organizational information against which AI outputs are compared or grounded.

### 29 — Production Dataset

A production dataset is used by an operational AI system or directly supports operational AI functionality.

### 30 — Dataset Purpose Limitation

The organization should document the intended purpose of each material third-party dataset.

### 31 — Dataset Secondary Use

Potential secondary uses should be assessed where contractual, legal, privacy, licensing, or governance restrictions may apply.

### 32 — Dataset Purpose Drift

Changes in how a dataset is used should trigger appropriate reassessment where the new use materially changes risk.

### 33 — Dataset Provider Classification

Dataset providers should be classified according to characteristics such as:

* commercial versus noncommercial;
* public versus private;
* direct versus intermediary;
* licensed versus freely available;
* proprietary versus open;
* static versus continuously updated.

### 34 — Commercial Dataset

A commercial dataset is supplied under commercial terms, licensing arrangements, subscriptions, usage restrictions, or other commercial conditions.

### 35 — Public Dataset

A public dataset is made available to the public but should not automatically be assumed to have unrestricted:

* use rights;
* redistribution rights;
* modification rights;
* commercial-use rights.

### 36 — Open Dataset

An open dataset is made available under terms intended to permit specified forms of reuse.

The actual license and applicable restrictions should be evaluated.

### 37 — Proprietary Dataset

A proprietary dataset is controlled by an entity that restricts access or use through ownership, contract, license, confidentiality, or other controls.

### 38 — Licensed Dataset

A licensed dataset is made available under defined legal or contractual permissions.

### 39 — Subscription Dataset

Subscription-based datasets should be assessed for:

* access duration;
* permitted use;
* update rights;
* archival rights;
* termination;
* continued use after subscription expiry.

### 40 — API-Provided Dataset

Data accessed through an API should be governed according to both:

* dataset risks;
* API/service risks.

### 41 — Streaming Dataset

Continuously or periodically streamed data should be assessed for:

* source consistency;
* integrity;
* availability;
* change frequency;
* version traceability.

### 42 — Marketplace Dataset

Datasets acquired through a marketplace should be assessed for risks associated with:

* marketplace operator;
* publisher;
* source;
* licensing;
* distribution;
* integrity.

### 43 — Repository Dataset

Datasets obtained from repositories should be evaluated for:

* repository ownership;
* publisher identity;
* version;
* provenance;
* integrity;
* licensing.

### 44 — Scraped Dataset

Scraped datasets are collections derived from automated extraction of information from websites, platforms, documents, or other sources.

Their use should be assessed for:

* provenance;
* collection method;
* terms;
* privacy;
* intellectual property;
* data quality.

### 45 — Web-Sourced Dataset

Data collected from publicly accessible websites should not automatically be considered unrestricted for AI training or other processing.

### 46 — Synthetic Dataset

Synthetic datasets are generated artificially rather than collected directly from real-world observations.

Synthetic data may reduce some direct data-exposure risks but can introduce:

* generation bias;
* synthetic artifacts;
* privacy leakage;
* distributional distortion;
* quality problems.

### 47 — Generated Dataset

Datasets generated by another AI system should be assessed for risks associated with the generating system and source data.

### 48 — Augmented Dataset

Datasets enhanced through transformations, generated records, enrichment, or external information should maintain appropriate lineage.

### 49 — Aggregated Dataset

Aggregated datasets combine information from multiple sources.

Source-level provenance should be retained where feasible.

### 50 — Composite Dataset

Composite datasets should identify significant constituent datasets where practical.

### 51 — Dataset Provenance

Dataset provenance is information establishing the origin, acquisition, ownership, processing history, and lineage of data.

### 52 — Provenance Requirement

Material third-party datasets should have sufficient provenance information to support risk assessment and appropriate downstream use.

### 53 — Provenance Record

A provenance record may include:

* source;
* provider;
* acquisition date;
* acquisition method;
* license;
* version;
* transformation history;
* geographic origin;
* processing activities.

### 54 — Source Authenticity

Organizations should assess whether the claimed source of material dataset content can be reasonably substantiated.

### 55 — Provider Authenticity

The identity and authority of a material dataset provider should be verified proportionately to risk.

### 56 — Dataset Ownership

Organizations should determine who claims ownership or control of the dataset and what evidence supports that claim where material.

### 57 — Dataset Custody

The chain through which the dataset passed before organizational acquisition should be considered where relevant.

### 58 — Dataset Chain of Custody

For high-impact or high-risk datasets, organizations should maintain an appropriate chain of custody covering material acquisition and transformation events.

### 59 — Dataset Lineage

Dataset lineage should describe how data moves from source through processing and transformation to its current organizational form.

### 60 — Source-to-Model Lineage

Where material, organizations should be able to trace:

**Source → Dataset → Transformation → Model Development → AI System**

### 61 — Dataset-to-Model Traceability

Material AI systems should maintain sufficient traceability to identify datasets materially contributing to model development where technically feasible.

### 62 — Dataset Versioning

Third-party datasets should be versioned where changes could materially affect:

* model behavior;
* evaluation;
* legal rights;
* security;
* reproducibility.

### 63 — Dataset Snapshot

Material datasets should be preserved as controlled snapshots where reproducibility or investigation requires them.

### 64 — Dataset Version Identifier

Each material dataset version should have an identifiable version or equivalent reference.

### 65 — Dataset Change Detection

Organizations should detect material changes to datasets where feasible.

### 66 — Dataset Integrity

Dataset integrity is the assurance that data has not been improperly altered, corrupted, replaced, or tampered with.

### 67 — Dataset Integrity Verification

Integrity should be verified using proportionate technical or procedural mechanisms.

### 68 — Dataset Checksum

Checksums or cryptographic hashes may be used to detect unauthorized or unintended dataset modification.

### 69 — Dataset Digital Signature

Digital signatures may provide additional assurance regarding dataset authenticity and integrity where supported.

### 70 — Dataset Hash Record

Material dataset acquisition records may retain:

* hash;
* algorithm;
* acquisition date;
* source;
* version.

### 71 — Dataset Mirror Risk

Mirrors may introduce uncertainty regarding:

* authenticity;
* completeness;
* version;
* modification;
* provenance.

### 72 — Dataset Distribution Chain

Organizations should consider the complete distribution chain between original source and organizational acquisition.

### 73 — Dataset Repository Trust

Repository reputation should be considered but should not substitute for verification of dataset integrity and provenance.

### 74 — Repository Account Security

Where datasets are distributed through repositories, repository account compromise should be considered as a potential supply-chain risk.

### 75 — Publisher Account Compromise

Compromise of a dataset publisher's account could result in malicious or unauthorized dataset modification.

### 76 — Dataset Tampering

Organizations should consider the possibility of deliberate or accidental dataset modification during:

* collection;
* transformation;
* storage;
* distribution;
* acquisition.

### 77 — Malicious Dataset

A malicious dataset may intentionally contain information designed to influence, compromise, manipulate, or degrade downstream AI systems.

### 78 — Dataset Poisoning

Dataset poisoning is the deliberate manipulation of data to influence model behavior, training outcomes, classification, or other downstream AI characteristics.

### 79 — Targeted Dataset Poisoning

Targeted poisoning may attempt to affect:

* particular classes;
* prompts;
* entities;
* conditions;
* model behaviors.

### 80 — Availability Poisoning

Dataset manipulation may also seek to degrade model performance broadly rather than create a specific behavior.

### 81 — Backdoor-Oriented Data

Data may be constructed or manipulated to introduce hidden conditional behavior into downstream models.

### 82 — Trigger Patterns

Potential poisoning analysis should consider unusual patterns that could function as model-training triggers where appropriate.

### 83 — Data Integrity Versus Data Quality

Integrity and quality are distinct:

* integrity concerns whether data was improperly altered;
* quality concerns whether data is accurate, complete, consistent, relevant, and fit for purpose.

### 84 — Dataset Quality

Material datasets should be assessed for relevant quality dimensions.

### 85 — Dataset Accuracy

Accuracy should be evaluated where incorrect information could affect model or business outcomes.

### 86 — Dataset Completeness

Material missing data should be identified where incompleteness could distort model behavior or analysis.

### 87 — Dataset Consistency

Consistency should be assessed across records, fields, sources, and versions where relevant.

### 88 — Dataset Timeliness

Dataset currency should be evaluated against its intended purpose.

### 89 — Dataset Freshness

Data freshness requirements should reflect the use case rather than assuming newer data is always preferable.

### 90 — Dataset Relevance

Dataset content should be relevant to the intended:

* model;
* domain;
* task;
* population;
* operating environment.

### 91 — Dataset Fitness for Purpose

A dataset should be assessed for suitability to the intended AI use case.

### 92 — Dataset Representativeness

Organizations should assess whether material datasets appropriately represent the populations, environments, conditions, or classes relevant to the intended use.

### 93 — Sampling Bias

Sampling methods may introduce systematic differences between dataset populations and real-world populations.

### 94 — Selection Bias

Dataset selection processes may cause particular groups, conditions, or outcomes to be overrepresented or underrepresented.

### 95 — Coverage Bias

Important populations, geographic areas, languages, operating conditions, or edge cases may be missing from a dataset.

### 96 — Dataset Imbalance

Material class imbalance should be assessed where it could affect AI-system performance.

### 97 — Dataset Distribution Shift

Organizations should consider whether dataset characteristics differ materially from the environment in which the resulting AI system will operate.

### 98 — Dataset Bias Assessment

Potential dataset bias should be assessed according to the intended use and materiality of consequences.

### 99 — Label Quality

Where datasets contain labels, organizations should assess:

* label accuracy;
* consistency;
* completeness;
* methodology;
* provenance.

### 100 — Annotation Provenance

Material annotation activities should be traceable to the responsible annotation process or provider where practical.

### 101 — Annotation Provider Risk

Third-party annotation providers may introduce:

* quality risk;
* confidentiality risk;
* privacy risk;
* security risk;
* labor/process risk.

### 102 — Annotation Subcontractor

Where annotation is outsourced, relevant subcontractors should be considered within the data supply chain.

### 103 — Label Manipulation

Organizations should consider the possibility of intentional or accidental manipulation of labels.

### 104 — Data Transformation

Transformations applied before organizational use should be documented where they materially affect dataset characteristics.

### 105 — Transformation Lineage

Material transformations should be traceable to:

* transformation method;
* date;
* responsible process;
* resulting version.

### 106 — Preprocessing Governance

Preprocessing should be controlled to prevent unintended introduction of:

* bias;
* leakage;
* corruption;
* information loss.

### 107 — Data Cleaning

Data-cleaning processes should be documented where material to model outcomes.

### 108 — Deduplication

Duplicate records should be identified and managed where duplication could distort training or evaluation.

### 109 — Data Leakage Risk

Organizations should assess whether information improperly crosses boundaries between:

* training;
* validation;
* testing;
* evaluation.

### 110 — Training-Test Contamination

Training data should not improperly contain test or evaluation information where such contamination could invalidate performance assessment.

### 111 — Benchmark Contamination

Benchmark datasets should be assessed for potential contamination when benchmark results materially influence model decisions.

### 112 — Evaluation Integrity

Evaluation data should be protected from inappropriate modification or disclosure where this could undermine evaluation validity.

### 113 — Dataset Security Classification

Datasets should receive an appropriate security classification based on:

* content;
* sensitivity;
* business impact;
* regulatory requirements.

### 114 — Sensitive Dataset

Sensitive datasets may contain information whose compromise could cause significant:

* privacy;
* security;
* legal;
* commercial;
* operational;

harm.

### 115 — Personal Data

Where third-party datasets contain personal data, applicable privacy and data-protection requirements should be identified and addressed.

### 116 — Special-Category or Highly Sensitive Data

Where applicable, datasets containing highly sensitive categories of personal information require enhanced legal, privacy, security, and governance assessment.

### 117 — Privacy Risk Classification

Privacy risk should consider:

* data subjects;
* data categories;
* purpose;
* scale;
* processing operations;
* downstream AI use.

### 118 — Data Minimization

Third-party datasets should contain no more information than is appropriate for the intended purpose, subject to applicable legal and contractual requirements.

### 119 — Data Necessity

The organization should establish why material data elements are necessary for the intended AI use.

### 120 — Data Purpose Compatibility

The intended AI use should be evaluated against the purposes and restrictions associated with the data source and acquisition arrangement.

### 121 — Lawful Basis Assessment

Where applicable, organizations should determine the relevant lawful basis for processing personal data.

### 122 — Consent Assessment

Where consent is relied upon, the organization should assess whether the relevant consent conditions and scope support the intended processing.

### 123 — Consent Scope

Consent, where applicable, should not automatically be assumed to cover:

* AI training;
* model development;
* profiling;
* secondary use;
* downstream sharing.

### 124 — Privacy Notice Alignment

Where required, applicable transparency obligations should be assessed in relation to the processing of personal data.

### 125 — Data Subject Rights

Where applicable, third-party dataset governance should consider relevant rights of data subjects.

### 126 — Data Subject Rights Traceability

Organizations should assess whether acquired datasets can support appropriate handling of applicable data-subject rights.

### 127 — Re-identification Risk

Datasets that have been anonymized, pseudonymized, aggregated, or de-identified should be assessed for residual re-identification risk where relevant.

### 128 — Pseudonymized Dataset

Pseudonymization does not necessarily eliminate the applicability of privacy requirements.

### 129 — Anonymized Dataset

Anonymization should be assessed based on the applicable legal and technical standard rather than assumed from a provider's label.

### 130 — Linkage Risk

Combining third-party datasets with internal or other external information may increase re-identification or sensitivity risk.

### 131 — Dataset Enrichment Risk

Additional enrichment may change the risk classification of a dataset.

### 132 — Cross-Dataset Correlation

Organizations should consider whether combining datasets creates information that was not apparent in individual datasets.

### 133 — Data Confidentiality

Third-party datasets should be protected against unauthorized disclosure proportionate to sensitivity.

### 134 — Dataset Access Control

Access should be restricted according to:

* business need;
* data sensitivity;
* role;
* environment;
* purpose.

### 135 — Dataset Least Privilege

Users and systems should receive only the dataset access necessary for authorized activities.

### 136 — Dataset Segregation

Sensitive third-party datasets should be segregated from lower-trust data environments where appropriate.

### 137 — Dataset Environment Separation

Development, testing, and production environments should apply appropriate dataset separation.

### 138 — Dataset Encryption

Material datasets should be protected using appropriate encryption for:

* storage;
* transmission;
* backup.

### 139 — Dataset Key Management

Encryption keys protecting material datasets should be managed according to organizational cryptographic controls.

### 140 — Dataset Secret Management

Credentials and secrets used to access third-party datasets should not be embedded insecurely in:

* source code;
* notebooks;
* scripts;
* configuration files.

### 141 — Dataset API Authentication

API-based dataset access should use appropriate authentication mechanisms.

### 142 — Dataset API Authorization

Dataset APIs should enforce authorization appropriate to the sensitivity and permitted use of the data.

### 143 — Dataset Download Security

Downloaded datasets should be subject to appropriate security controls before entering trusted environments.

### 144 — Dataset File Scanning

Where technically appropriate, dataset files and archives should be scanned for:

* malware;
* malicious content;
* unexpected executables;
* unsafe file structures.

### 145 — Dataset Archive Security

Compressed or archived datasets should be inspected for potentially malicious or unexpected contents.

### 146 — Unsafe Dataset Serialization

Serialized data formats capable of executing code or invoking unsafe behavior should receive enhanced security scrutiny.

### 147 — Dataset Parser Security

Data-processing tools and parsers should be maintained and protected against known vulnerabilities relevant to dataset processing.

### 148 — Dataset Processing Isolation

Untrusted third-party datasets should be processed in appropriately isolated environments when risk warrants.

### 149 — Dataset Sandbox

High-risk or untrusted datasets may require sandboxed processing before being introduced into trusted AI environments.

### 150 — Dataset Network Isolation

Dataset-processing environments should restrict unnecessary network connectivity where appropriate.

### 151 — Dataset Egress Control

Outbound transfers from dataset-processing environments should be controlled according to data sensitivity and business need.

### 152 — Dataset Exfiltration Risk

Organizations should consider whether malicious or compromised processing could exfiltrate third-party dataset contents or organizational information.

### 153 — Dataset DLP

Data-loss-prevention controls may be applied to material third-party datasets according to sensitivity and organizational requirements.

### 154 — Dataset Logging

Material dataset access and processing activities should be logged where required by risk and governance requirements.

### 155 — Dataset Auditability

Material dataset operations should be sufficiently traceable to support:

* investigation;
* accountability;
* compliance;
* reproducibility.

### 156 — Dataset Access Review

Access to material datasets should be periodically reviewed according to risk.

### 157 — Dataset Privileged Access

Privileged access to sensitive datasets should receive enhanced controls.

### 158 — Dataset Administrative Separation

Administrative capabilities should be separated from ordinary dataset-use privileges where appropriate.

### 159 — Dataset Segregation of Duties

Conflicting dataset responsibilities should be separated where necessary to reduce unauthorized or inappropriate activity.

### 160 — Dataset Retention

Third-party datasets should be retained only for as long as required by:

* legitimate business purpose;
* contractual requirements;
* legal requirements;
* operational requirements.

### 161 — Dataset Deletion

Dataset deletion requirements should be established for material datasets.

### 162 — Provider Deletion Requirement

Where contractual or legal requirements require provider deletion, organizations should establish appropriate evidence or assurance mechanisms.

### 163 — Dataset Backup

Backups of material third-party datasets should be governed consistently with applicable retention, security, and deletion requirements.

### 164 — Dataset Backup Deletion

Deletion processes should consider retained backup copies where applicable.

### 165 — Dataset Geographic Origin

The geographic origin of third-party datasets should be recorded where relevant to:

* privacy;
* regulatory;
* contractual;
* security;
* sovereignty;

requirements.

### 166 — Dataset Storage Location

The locations in which material datasets are stored or processed should be understood where relevant.

### 167 — Dataset Residency

Where residency restrictions apply, organizations should verify that dataset storage and processing arrangements satisfy applicable requirements.

### 168 — Cross-Border Dataset Transfer

Cross-border transfer of personal or otherwise regulated data should be assessed against applicable legal and contractual requirements.

### 169 — Dataset Transfer Mechanism

Where applicable, the legal or contractual mechanism supporting a cross-border transfer should be identified.

### 170 — Subprocessor Dataset Risk

Where providers use subprocessors to collect, process, annotate, host, or distribute datasets, relevant subprocessor risks should be considered.

### 171 — Fourth-Party Dataset Risk

Material fourth-party dependencies should be identified where they can materially affect:

* provenance;
* privacy;
* security;
* availability;
* integrity.

### 172 — Dataset Supply Chain

The dataset supply chain may include:

**Source → Collector → Aggregator → Annotator → Publisher → Marketplace/Repository → Organization → AI System**

### 173 — Dataset Supply-Chain Visibility

Organizations should maintain sufficient visibility into material dataset supply chains to support proportionate risk assessment.

### 174 — Dataset Supply-Chain Integrity

Controls should address the possibility of compromise at any material stage of the dataset supply chain.

### 175 — Dataset Dependency

Critical AI systems should identify material dependencies on third-party datasets.

### 176 — Dataset Dependency Mapping

Dataset dependencies should be mapped to:

* models;
* applications;
* business processes;
* critical services.

### 177 — Dataset Concentration

Organizations should assess concentration on a single:

* dataset provider;
* dataset;
* repository;
* marketplace;
* source;
* geographic region.

### 178 — Dataset Common-Mode Risk

Multiple AI systems may share the same third-party dataset or source, creating correlated risk.

### 179 — Dataset Systemic Dependency

A single compromised or materially incorrect dataset may affect multiple models and applications simultaneously.

### 180 — Dataset Replacement Risk

Organizations should determine whether material third-party datasets can be replaced without unacceptable operational or model-performance impact.

### 181 — Dataset Portability

Where feasible, material dataset dependencies should preserve sufficient information to support migration to alternative sources.

### 182 — Dataset Provider Lock-In

Organizations should assess lock-in caused by:

* proprietary formats;

* unique data;
* provider-specific APIs;
* unavailable alternatives.

### 183 — Dataset Exit Planning

Material dataset dependencies should have proportionate replacement or exit considerations.

### 184 — Dataset Alternative Source

Organizations should identify alternative sources where continued availability of a third-party dataset is critical.

### 185 — Dataset Availability Risk

Dataset availability should be assessed where interruption could affect:

* model development;
* model operation;
* evaluation;
* regulatory obligations;
* business continuity.

### 186 — Dataset Continuity

Critical dataset dependencies should have proportionate continuity arrangements.

### 187 — Dataset Version Recovery

Organizations should be able to recover material dataset versions required for investigation, reproducibility, or continuity where appropriate.

### 188 — Dataset Provider Due Diligence

Material third-party dataset providers should undergo proportionate due diligence.

### 189 — Dataset Provider Identity Verification

Provider identity should be verified using appropriate evidence.

### 190 — Dataset Provider Ownership Verification

Material ownership or control information should be evaluated where relevant.

### 191 — Dataset Provider Reputation

Provider reputation may inform risk assessment but should not substitute for objective evidence.

### 192 — Dataset Provider Security Assessment

Where risk warrants, provider security capabilities should be assessed.

### 193 — Dataset Provider Privacy Assessment

Where personal data is involved, relevant provider privacy practices should be assessed.

### 194 — Dataset Provider Licensing Assessment

* use rights;

* legality;
* privacy;

should be validated against appropriate evidence.

### 199 — Dataset Evidence Quality

Evidence supporting dataset approval should be evaluated for:

* authenticity;
* relevance;
* completeness;
* currency;
* independence.

### 200 — Dataset Evidence Gap

Material gaps in provenance, licensing, privacy, security, or quality evidence should be documented.

### 201 — Dataset Risk Assessment

Third-party dataset risk assessments should integrate:

* source risk;
* provider risk;
* data risk;
* processing risk;
* downstream AI risk.

### 202 — Dataset Inherent Risk

Inherent dataset risk should be assessed before considering mitigating controls.

### 203 — Dataset Residual Risk

Residual dataset risk should be assessed after applicable controls are considered.

### 204 — Dataset Risk Acceptance

Material residual dataset risks should be accepted only by appropriately authorized decision-makers.

### 205 — Dataset Risk Acceptance Limitation

Risk acceptance should not be used to override mandatory legal, regulatory, or contractual requirements.

### 206 — Dataset Approval Decision

Material dataset approval should consider:

* intended use;
* data sensitivity;
* provenance;
* quality;
* integrity;
* legal rights;
* privacy;
* security;
* downstream impact.

### 207 — Dataset Admission Principle

Third-party datasets should satisfy defined organizational admission requirements before being introduced into material AI development or production environments.

### 208 — Dataset Trust Principle

Trust in a third-party dataset should be based on evidence concerning its **origin, integrity, quality, rights, security, processing history, and suitability for the intended use**, rather than provider reputation alone.

### 209 — Dataset Provenance Principle

Material third-party datasets should maintain sufficient provenance and lineage to support appropriate risk assessment, reproducibility, investigation, and downstream governance.

### 210 — Dataset Risk Decision Principle

Material third-party dataset decisions should be based on the **specific dataset, source, provider, acquisition method, data characteristics, intended use, legal and contractual environment, security exposure, downstream AI dependency, and available evidence**.

Internal `AI-DATA-*` identifiers used within this knowledge base are organizational taxonomy identifiers and should not be represented as provisions of external law or regulation unless an authoritative source explicitly establishes the corresponding requirement.
