# 16.10 — Third-Party Dataset Risk

## Part 1 — Foundations, Dataset Dependency, Provenance, Data Rights, Integrity, Security and Governance

### 1. Third-Party Dataset Risk

Third-party dataset risk is the risk arising when an organization acquires, accesses, licenses, receives, or otherwise relies on datasets supplied or maintained by an external party for AI development, training, fine-tuning, evaluation, retrieval, analytics, or operational decision-making.

Third-party datasets may introduce risks involving:

* provenance;
* data rights;
* accuracy;
* integrity;
* poisoning;
* privacy;
* security;
* licensing;
* bias;
* availability;
* supplier dependency;
* regulatory exposure.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-001**

---

### 2. Objective of Third-Party Dataset Governance

The objective of third-party dataset governance is to establish reasonable assurance that externally sourced datasets are:

* appropriately identified;
* obtained through legitimate channels;
* suitable for their intended purpose;
* sufficiently understood;
* protected against unauthorized alteration;
* governed according to applicable requirements;
* monitored throughout their lifecycle.

Dataset governance should cover both the dataset itself and the external party providing or maintaining it.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-002**

---

### 3. Third-Party Dataset

A third-party dataset is a collection of data obtained from an external organization, individual, platform, repository, service, data broker, research organization, partner, or other external source.

Examples include:

* commercial datasets;
* public datasets;
* licensed datasets;
* partner-provided datasets;
* government datasets;
* research datasets;
* benchmark datasets;
* synthetic datasets obtained externally;
* externally maintained knowledge bases;
* data accessed through APIs.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-003**

---

### 4. Dataset Provider

The dataset provider is the external entity that supplies, publishes, licenses, hosts, maintains, or otherwise makes the dataset available.

The provider may be:

* the original data collector;
* a data aggregator;
* a marketplace;
* a research institution;
* a cloud service;
* an open-data publisher;
* a commercial data vendor;
* an intermediary.

The party supplying the dataset is not necessarily the original source of all information contained within it.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-004**

---

### 5. Dataset Dependency

Dataset dependency exists when an AI system relies materially on an externally supplied dataset.

Dependency may occur during:

* model training;
* fine-tuning;
* retrieval;
* evaluation;
* validation;
* benchmarking;
* inference;
* analytics;
* monitoring.

A dataset can therefore remain operationally important even when it is not directly used to train the underlying model.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-005**

---

### 6. Dataset Trust Boundary

A third-party dataset creates a trust boundary between the external data source and the organization's controlled environment.

```text id="b7m2qx"
External Dataset Provider
          |
          | Data Transfer
          v
   Enterprise Boundary
          |
          v
 Data Ingestion Layer
          |
          v
 Validation / Quarantine
          |
          v
 Approved Dataset
          |
          v

```

Controls should prevent unvalidated external data from being automatically treated as trusted enterprise information.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-006**

---

### 7. Dataset Acquisition

Dataset acquisition is the process through which an organization obtains an external dataset.

Acquisition mechanisms may include:

* direct download;
* API access;
* secure file transfer;
* cloud storage;
* data marketplace;
* partner exchange;
* database connection;
* streaming service.

The acquisition method should be documented for material datasets.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-007**

---

### 8. Dataset Inventory

Material third-party datasets should be recorded in an appropriate inventory.

The inventory may identify:

* dataset name;
* provider;
* source;
* version;
* acquisition method;
* owner;
* intended purpose;
* classification;
* sensitivity;
* license;
* geographic scope;
* update frequency;
* retention requirement.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-008**

---

### 9. Dataset Identity

A dataset should have a sufficiently unique identity to distinguish it from other datasets or versions.

Identity may include:

* dataset name;
* provider;
* identifier;
* version;
* release date;
* hash;
* repository;
* acquisition timestamp.

Weak dataset identification makes provenance and change management difficult.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-009**

---

### 10. Dataset Version

Third-party datasets may change over time.

Version information should be captured where available.

Changes may involve:

* added records;
* removed records;
* modified records;
* schema changes;
* labeling changes;
* source changes;
* preprocessing changes.

A dataset version should not be assumed to remain unchanged merely because its name is unchanged.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-010**

---

### 11. Dataset Release

A dataset release is a particular publication or delivery of a dataset by its provider.

Release information should be retained where material to:

* reproducibility;
* model development;
* investigation;
* validation;
* audit;
* incident response.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-011**

---

### 12. Dataset Provenance

Dataset provenance describes the origin and history of the data.

Provenance may include:

* original source;
* collection method;
* intermediary;
* transformations;
* aggregation;
* labeling;
* filtering;
* enrichment;
* transfer;
* storage.

Strong provenance supports assessment of data quality, rights, integrity, and suitability.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-012**

---

**Control ID: AI-DATA-013**

---

### 14. Source Attribution

The organization should understand, where reasonably available, the sources from which a third-party dataset was derived.

Source attribution may support:

* rights assessment;
* provenance;
* quality assessment;
* correction;
* deletion;
* investigation.

A provider's statement that a dataset is "proprietary" does not by itself explain the underlying source chain.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-014**

---

### 15. Dataset Provenance Evidence

Provenance claims should be supported by evidence appropriate to the risk.

Evidence may include:

* provider documentation;
* collection records;
* metadata;
* source references;
* transformation records;
* contractual representations;
* technical lineage.

Provider assertions should be distinguished from independently verified evidence.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-015**

---

### 17. Dataset Transformation

Third-party datasets may undergo transformation before enterprise use.

Transformations may include:

* normalization;
* deduplication;
* translation;
* tokenization;
* filtering;
* anonymization;
* pseudonymization;
* labeling;
* feature extraction.

Transformations should be documented where they can affect security, quality, rights, or model behavior.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-017**

---

### 18. Dataset Integrity

Dataset integrity is the property that data has not been altered, corrupted, or manipulated in an unauthorized or unintended manner.

Integrity controls may include:

* hashes;
* signatures;
* authenticated transfer;
* access controls;
* immutable storage;
* version control;
* integrity monitoring.

Dataset integrity does not establish that the underlying data is accurate or lawful.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-018**

---

### 19. Dataset Hash

A cryptographic hash can provide an integrity reference for a particular dataset artifact.

Hash verification can help determine whether the received artifact matches the expected artifact.

However:

> **Hash verification confirms artifact consistency; it does not prove dataset trustworthiness, accuracy, legality, or absence of malicious content.**

**Control ID: AI-DATA-019**

---

### 20. Dataset Signature

Digital signatures may be used to establish authenticity and integrity of dataset artifacts where supported.

Signature verification can help establish that an artifact was signed by an expected signing identity and has not been modified since signing.

The trustworthiness of the signing identity must still be assessed.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-020**

---

### 21. Dataset Authenticity

Dataset authenticity concerns whether the received dataset originated from the expected source.

Controls may include:

* authenticated provider channels;
* digital signatures;
* trusted repositories;
* secure credentials;
* certificate validation;
* controlled acquisition processes.

Authenticity should be distinguished from data quality.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-021**

---

### 22. Dataset Tampering Risk

Third-party datasets may be altered intentionally or accidentally before or during acquisition.

Potential attack points include:

* provider systems;
* repositories;
* distribution channels;
* mirrors;
* APIs;
* storage;
* transfer mechanisms;
* enterprise ingestion pipelines.

Material datasets should therefore have appropriate integrity protections.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-022**

---

### 23. Dataset Poisoning Risk

Dataset poisoning occurs when malicious or inappropriate data is introduced with the intention or effect of influencing AI behavior.

Potential objectives include:

* degrading model performance;
* creating targeted misclassification;
* influencing model outputs;
* introducing unsafe behavior;
* creating backdoors;
* manipulating evaluation results.

Poisoning risk should be assessed according to the dataset's role in the AI lifecycle.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-023**

---

### 24. Targeted Data Poisoning

Targeted poisoning attempts to influence specific model behaviors rather than simply degrading overall performance.

Examples may involve:

* particular classes;
* specific prompts;
* selected entities;
* specific environments;
* targeted outputs.

Testing should consider whether unusual or strategically placed data can produce disproportionate behavioral effects.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-024**

---

### 25. Dataset Anomaly Detection

Organizations may use anomaly detection to identify unusual dataset characteristics.

Indicators may include:

* unexpected volume changes;
* unusual distributions;
* duplicate spikes;
* abnormal labels;
* unexpected source domains;
* unusual timestamps;
* anomalous metadata.

Anomaly detection should complement, not replace, provenance and integrity controls.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-025**

---

### 26. Dataset Quality

Dataset quality concerns whether data is sufficiently accurate, complete, consistent, relevant, and suitable for its intended use.

Quality dimensions may include:

* accuracy;
* completeness;
* consistency;
* timeliness;
* relevance;
* uniqueness;
* validity.

Security assurance should not be confused with data-quality assurance.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-026**

---

### 27. Dataset Accuracy

Accuracy concerns whether dataset values correctly represent the underlying subject matter they are intended to describe.

Accuracy requirements depend on the intended use.

A dataset may be technically intact while containing inaccurate information.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-027**

---

### 28. Dataset Completeness

Completeness concerns whether the dataset contains the information required for its intended purpose.

Incomplete datasets may cause:

* model performance degradation;
* missing populations;
* incomplete evaluations;
* incorrect decisions;
* distorted analysis.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-028**

---

### 29. Dataset Timeliness

Timeliness concerns whether the dataset is sufficiently current for its intended use.

Timeliness requirements vary by application.

For example:

* historical research may tolerate older data;
* fraud detection may require near-current information;
* regulatory reporting may have defined reporting periods.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-029**

---

### 30. Dataset Relevance

A dataset should be assessed for relevance to the intended AI use case.

A dataset can be:

* technically accurate;
* well documented;
* legally licensed;

and still be inappropriate for a particular application.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-030**

---

### 31. Dataset Representativeness

Representativeness concerns whether the dataset sufficiently reflects the population, conditions, or scenarios relevant to its intended use.

Assessment may consider:

* demographic coverage;
* geographic coverage;
* temporal coverage;
* operational conditions;
* edge cases;
* rare events.

Representativeness requirements depend on the use case.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-031**

---

### 32. Dataset Bias Risk

Third-party datasets may contain biases resulting from:

* collection methods;
* source selection;
* historical practices;
* labeling;
* sampling;
* missing data;
* aggregation.

Bias should be assessed in relation to the intended AI use rather than assumed solely from the fact that the dataset is third-party.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-032**

---

### 33. Dataset Label Quality

Where datasets contain labels, the organization should assess label quality when labels materially influence AI behavior.

Assessment may consider:

* labeling methodology;
* label consistency;
* annotator qualifications;
* disagreement;
* quality assurance;
* label provenance.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-033**

---

### 34. Dataset Label Provenance

Label provenance identifies how labels were created and modified.

Relevant information may include:

* labeling organization;
* labeling methodology;
* annotation instructions;
* quality controls;
* version;
* correction history.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-034**

---

### 35. Dataset Suitability

Dataset suitability is the degree to which a dataset is appropriate for its intended technical, business, security, privacy, and governance purpose.

Suitability should consider the complete context rather than dataset quality alone.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-035**

---

### 36. Dataset Intended Use

The intended use of a third-party dataset should be documented.

Possible uses include:

* training;
* fine-tuning;
* validation;
* testing;
* benchmarking;
* retrieval;
* analytics;
* operational inference.

Rights and risk assumptions may differ between these uses.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-036**

---

### 37. Dataset Use Restrictions

Third-party datasets may contain restrictions on how they may be used.

Restrictions may concern:

* commercial use;
* redistribution;
* modification;
* publication;
* training;
* profiling;
* geographic use;
* downstream sharing.

Restrictions should be assessed before material use.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-037**

---

### 38. Dataset License

A dataset license defines contractual or licensing terms governing use where applicable.

The organization should identify:

* applicable license;
* permitted uses;
* restrictions;
* attribution;
* redistribution;
* derivative-use conditions;
* termination provisions.

A dataset license should not automatically be assumed to grant every desired AI use.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-038**

---

### 39. Dataset Rights

Organizations should assess whether they have the necessary rights to use the dataset for the intended purpose.

Rights analysis may involve:

* copyright;
* database rights;
* contractual rights;
* confidentiality;
* privacy;
* intellectual-property considerations;
* sector-specific restrictions.

The applicable legal analysis depends on jurisdiction and circumstances.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-039**

---

### 40. Dataset Redistribution Risk

Redistribution of third-party datasets may create additional obligations or restrictions.

Before redistribution, the organization should determine whether:

* redistribution is permitted;
* derived datasets are covered;
* attribution is required;
* downstream recipients are restricted;
* personal or confidential information is involved.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-040**

---

### 41. Dataset Intellectual-Property Risk

Third-party datasets may contain information subject to intellectual-property rights.

Risk assessment should consider:

* ownership;
* licensing;
* permitted use;
* derivative works;
* copying;
* extraction;
* redistribution.

Technical access to data does not itself establish unrestricted intellectual-property rights.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-041**

---

### 42. Dataset Privacy Risk

Third-party datasets may contain:

* personal data;
* sensitive information;
* identifiers;
* location information;
* behavioral information;
* inferred information.

Privacy assessment should determine whether the intended processing is appropriate under applicable law and organizational policy.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-042**

---

### 43. Dataset Personal-Data Classification

Where personal data is present, the organization should determine the applicable classification and handling requirements.

Classification may influence:

* access;
* retention;
* encryption;
* transfer;
* deletion;
* monitoring;
* contractual requirements.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-043**

---

### 44. Dataset Sensitive-Data Risk

Datasets may contain sensitive or highly restricted information even when the provider does not classify it as such.

The organization should perform appropriate validation before importing material datasets into AI environments.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-044**

---

### 45. Dataset Privacy Provenance

For personal-data datasets, organizations should understand, where reasonably available:

* source;
* collection context;
* processing purpose;
* relevant permissions or legal basis;
* transformations;
* recipients;
* retention.

The organization's legal obligations depend on its role and applicable law.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-045**

---

### 46. Dataset Data Minimization

Only data reasonably necessary for the intended AI purpose should be acquired or processed where applicable.

Data minimization can reduce:

* privacy exposure;
* security exposure;
* storage requirements;
* breach impact;
* governance complexity.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-046**

---

### 47. Dataset Retention

Retention requirements should be established for material third-party datasets.

Retention should consider:

* business purpose;
* legal requirements;
* contractual restrictions;
* privacy requirements;
* model lifecycle;
* reproducibility;
* incident investigation.

Retention should not be determined solely by storage convenience.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-047**

---

### 48. Dataset Deletion

Where deletion is required or appropriate, the organization should define how third-party datasets and relevant copies are removed.

Deletion scope may include:

* primary storage;
* backups;
* caches;
* derived copies;
* staging environments;
* data pipelines.

Specific deletion obligations depend on applicable law, contractual terms, and the organization's role.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-048**

---

### 49. Dataset Security Classification

Material third-party datasets should receive an appropriate security classification.

Classification should consider:

* confidentiality;
* integrity;
* availability;
* privacy;
* business criticality;
* regulatory sensitivity.

The classification should drive corresponding security controls.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-049**

---

### 50. Third-Party Dataset Governance Principle

Third-party datasets should never be treated as inherently trustworthy merely because they originate from a recognized provider, public repository, commercial marketplace, research institution, or established partner.

Governance should establish a controlled path:

```text id="v3n7qm"
External Dataset
       |
Provider Assessment
       |
Provenance Assessment
       |
Rights / Privacy Review
       |
Integrity Verification
       |
Quality Assessment
       |
Security Assessment
       |
Quarantine / Validation
       |
Approved Dataset
       |
Controlled AI Use
       |
Continuous Monitoring
```

The central governance principle is that **dataset provenance, integrity, quality, legal rights, privacy status, and security must be assessed separately**. A dataset may have strong provenance but poor quality, valid licensing but unacceptable privacy risk, verified integrity but malicious content, or excellent technical quality but insufficient rights for the intended use.

Internal `AI-DATA-*` identifiers represent the knowledge-base control taxonomy and are not statutory provisions or regulatory requirements unless a separate legal or regulatory mapping explicitly establishes that relationship.


## Part 2 — Third-Party Dataset Due Diligence, Security Controls, Data Quality, Privacy, Licensing and Supply-Chain Security

### 51. Third-Party Dataset Due Diligence

Material third-party datasets should undergo due diligence proportionate to their intended use and risk.

Due diligence should consider:

* provider identity;
* dataset provenance;
* collection methodology;
* data rights;
* privacy;
* security;
* quality;
* integrity;
* update practices;
* downstream dependencies.

Due diligence should be completed before material production use where practicable.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-051**

---

### 52. Dataset Provider Assessment

The organization should assess the entity providing the dataset.

Assessment may consider:

* organizational identity;
* reputation;
* data governance;
* security practices;
* collection practices;
* privacy practices;
* contractual commitments;
* incident history;
* operational resilience.

Provider reputation should not substitute for evidence.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-052**

---

### 53. Dataset Provider Ownership

The organization should understand who owns or controls the dataset and the relevant provider relationship.

Ownership questions may include:

* Who created the dataset?
* Who collected the underlying data?
* Who currently controls it?
* Who can modify it?
* Who can redistribute it?
* Who is responsible for corrections?

**Control ID: AI-DATA-053**

---

### 54. Dataset Provider Data-Governance Assessment

Where material, the provider's data-governance practices should be assessed.

Relevant areas may include:

* data classification;
* access control;
* retention;
* deletion;
* provenance;
* quality management;
* privacy;
* incident response.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-054**

---

### 55. Dataset Collection-Method Assessment

The organization should understand how the dataset was collected where the collection method materially affects risk.

Collection methods may include:

* direct observation;
* user submission;
* web collection;
* sensors;
* surveys;
* transactional systems;
* public records;
* aggregation.

Collection methodology can materially affect quality, rights, privacy, and representativeness.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-055**

---

### 56. Dataset Source Verification

Material claims about dataset sources should be verified where practical.

Verification may include:

* source references;
* metadata;
* provider documentation;
* sample validation;
* provenance records;
* independent evidence.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-056**

---

### 57. Dataset Acquisition Channel

Organizations should use controlled acquisition channels for material third-party datasets.

Approved channels may include:

* authenticated provider portals;
* approved APIs;
* secure transfer;
* trusted repositories;
* approved marketplaces.

Uncontrolled downloads should be treated as higher-risk acquisition paths.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-057**

---

### 58. Dataset Quarantine

Externally acquired datasets should be quarantined before production use when justified by risk.

```text id="j4m8qx"

       |

 Quarantine
       |

       |

       |

       |

       |

       |

```

Quarantine reduces the possibility that unvalidated data immediately enters production AI pipelines.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-058**

---

### 59. Dataset Malware Scanning

Dataset files and associated artifacts should be scanned for malicious content where technically applicable.

This may be particularly relevant to:

* archives;
* notebooks;
* scripts;
* serialized objects;
* executable components;
* containerized datasets.

Scanning should consider the actual file formats involved.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-059**

---

### 60. Dataset Serialization Security

Dataset formats capable of containing executable or unsafe serialized objects should receive additional scrutiny.

Examples may include:

* serialized machine-learning objects;
* executable notebooks;
* embedded scripts;
* unsafe deserialization formats.

Data files should not automatically be treated as inert content.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-060**

---

### 61. Dataset Artifact Integrity

Material dataset artifacts should have an integrity mechanism appropriate to risk.

Mechanisms may include:

* cryptographic hashes;
* digital signatures;
* immutable storage;
* version control;
* authenticated downloads.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-061**

---

### 62. Dataset Hash Verification

Where a trusted reference hash exists, the organization should verify the received dataset against that reference.

Hash verification should occur:

* at acquisition;
* after transfer where appropriate;
* before promotion;
* after controlled replication where required.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-062**

---

### 63. Dataset Signature Verification

Where datasets are digitally signed, signature verification should be performed according to the organization's trust model.

Verification should confirm:

* signature validity;
* signing identity;
* certificate or key status;
* expected artifact association.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-063**

---

### 64. Dataset Immutable Storage

Material approved dataset versions may be stored in immutable or write-protected repositories where appropriate.

This can support:

* reproducibility;
* forensic investigation;
* rollback;
* auditability;
* integrity.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-064**

---

### 65. Dataset Version Pinning

AI pipelines should reference an explicit dataset version where reproducibility or risk requires it.

A production system should not unexpectedly switch to a materially different dataset merely because a provider updated the default release.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-065**

---

### 66. Dataset Reproducibility

Material AI workflows should be capable of identifying the dataset version used to produce significant results.

Reproducibility may require recording:

* dataset version;
* preprocessing;
* filtering;
* code;
* configuration;
* model version;
* evaluation environment.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-066**

---

### 67. Dataset Change Assessment

Changes to material third-party datasets should undergo impact assessment proportionate to risk.

Changes may affect:

* model behavior;
* evaluation results;
* privacy;
* rights;
* security;
* regulatory analysis.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-067**

---

### 68. Dataset Behavioral Impact

Changes to training or evaluation datasets may alter AI system behavior even when the underlying model code remains unchanged.

Organizations should therefore assess material dataset changes as potential model-behavior changes.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-068**

---

### 69. Dataset Regression Testing

Where dataset changes can materially affect an AI system, regression testing should be performed.

Testing may assess:

* accuracy;
* security;
* safety;
* bias;
* robustness;
* business-specific behavior.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-069**

---

### 70. Dataset Quality Testing

Quality testing should evaluate dimensions relevant to the intended use.

Potential tests include:

* completeness;
* duplication;
* validity;
* consistency;
* distribution;
* missingness;
* label quality.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-070**

---

### 71. Dataset Distribution Analysis

Organizations should assess material changes in dataset distributions.

Significant distribution changes may indicate:

* source changes;
* collection changes;
* errors;
* manipulation;
* changing populations.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-071**

---

### 72. Dataset Duplicate Detection

Duplicate records should be assessed where duplication can distort:

* training;
* validation;
* evaluation;
* analytics.

Duplicate detection should use methods appropriate to the dataset type.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-072**

---

### 73. Dataset Outlier Analysis

Material datasets should be evaluated for unusual observations where outliers could affect AI behavior or indicate data-quality or security problems.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-073**

---

### 74. Dataset Missing-Data Analysis

Missing-data patterns should be assessed where missing information can influence AI outcomes.

Assessment should consider whether missingness is:

* random;
* systematic;
* source-specific;
* time-dependent;
* associated with particular populations.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-074**

---

### 75. Dataset Label Validation

Where labels are used, the organization should validate label quality according to risk.

Validation may include:

* sampling;
* expert review;
* inter-annotator agreement;
* automated consistency checks;
* ground-truth comparison.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-075**

---

### 76. Dataset Evaluation Integrity

Evaluation datasets should be protected from inappropriate manipulation.

Where evaluation data is externally sourced, controls should address:

* provenance;
* integrity;
* access;
* versioning;
* contamination;
* change.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-076**

---

### 77. Dataset Benchmark Integrity

Benchmark datasets should be evaluated for relevance and integrity.

A high benchmark score does not necessarily demonstrate acceptable performance in the organization's actual operating environment.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-077**

---

### 78. Dataset Contamination Risk

Dataset contamination occurs when evaluation or test information is included in training or otherwise becomes available to the model in a manner that compromises evaluation validity.

Controls should reduce inappropriate overlap between:

* training;
* validation;
* test;
* benchmark datasets.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-078**

---

### 79. Dataset Split Governance

Where datasets are divided into training, validation, and test sets, the splitting methodology should be documented and appropriate to the use case.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-079**

---

### 80. Dataset Security Testing

Security testing should consider whether third-party data can introduce malicious or unexpected behavior.

Testing may include:

* poisoning analysis;
* anomaly detection;
* integrity testing;
* adversarial testing;
* malicious-content scanning.

**Control ID: AI-DATA-080**

---

### 81. Dataset Poisoning Detection

Poisoning detection mechanisms may assess:

* anomalous records;
* suspicious labels;
* unusual clusters;
* targeted examples;
* source anomalies;
* temporal anomalies.

Detection should be proportionate to the AI system's risk.

### 400. Third-Party Dataset Governance Principle

Third-party dataset governance should operate as an integrated enterprise capability connecting:

```text id="z8p5qm"
External Source
      |
Dataset Provider
      |
Dataset
      |
Provenance / Rights / Privacy
      |
Integrity / Quality / Security
      |
AI Data Pipeline
      |
Model / Retrieval System
      |
AI Application
      |
Business Service
      |
Enterprise Risk
```

Effective governance requires the organization to understand where data originated, how it was collected and transformed, what rights govern its use, what privacy obligations apply, whether the artifact is authentic and intact, whether the data is suitable for the intended purpose, and how changes or failures can affect downstream AI systems.

The organization should distinguish between:

* **dataset provenance and dataset trust**;
* **dataset integrity and dataset accuracy**;
* **dataset quality and dataset security**;
* **dataset licensing and unrestricted usage rights**;
* **provider assurance and independent validation**;
* **multiple datasets and genuine diversification**;
* **dataset availability and business resilience**;
* **backup capability and tested recovery**;
* **technical portability and behavioral portability**;
* **documented exit plans and tested exit capability**.

Third-party dataset governance should also distinguish **legal requirements**, **regulatory obligations or powers**, **contractual requirements**, **internal governance controls**, **implementation practices**, and **recommended practices**. Internal `AI-DATA-*` identifiers are organizational control-taxonomy identifiers and are not themselves statutory or regulatory provisions.

### Core Principles

1. **Enterprise-Governance Principle** — Third-party datasets should be governed within the organization's broader AI, data, cybersecurity, privacy, third-party-risk, and enterprise-risk frameworks.
2. **Accountability Principle** — Material datasets should have identifiable accountable owners.
3. **Lifecycle Principle** — Dataset governance should cover discovery, assessment, acquisition, validation, use, monitoring, change, and retirement.
4. **Provenance Principle** — Organizations should understand the origin and transformation history of material third-party datasets to the extent reasonably necessary for their risk.
5. **Integrity Principle** — Dataset artifacts should be protected against unauthorized or unintended modification.
6. **Quality Principle** — Dataset quality should be assessed separately from security and integrity.
7. **Rights Principle** — The organization should assess whether it has the necessary rights for the intended use.
8. **Privacy Principle** — Where personal or otherwise regulated information is involved, applicable privacy and data-protection requirements should be assessed.
9. **Least-Privilege Principle** — Access to datasets and processing pipelines should be limited according to business need.
10. **Supply-Chain Principle** — Dataset governance should consider providers, aggregators, original sources, software dependencies, infrastructure, and other downstream parties.
11. **Concentration Principle** — Dependence on common providers or sources should be identified and assessed.
12. **Resilience Principle** — Material dataset dependencies should have appropriate backup, recovery, and continuity arrangements.
13. **Portability Principle** — Dataset portability should be assessed practically where material.
14. **Exit Principle** — Material dataset dependencies should have credible and, where appropriate, tested exit arrangements.
15. **Assurance Principle** — Assurance should consider scope, relevance, recency, independence, and evidence quality.
16. **Monitoring Principle** — Material datasets should be monitored for changes in provenance, integrity, quality, rights, privacy, security, and availability.
17. **Automation Principle** — Automated dataset controls should be auditable, resilient, and subject to appropriate human oversight.
18. **Evidence Principle** — Material dataset decisions should be supported by traceable evidence throughout the lifecycle.
19. **Legal-Distinction Principle** — Legal requirements, regulatory obligations, contractual requirements, internal controls, implementation practices, and recommendations should not be conflated.
20. **Continuous-Assurance Principle** — Effective third-party dataset governance requires continuous reassessment of dependencies, evidence, controls, incidents, changes, resilience, and exit capability rather than reliance solely on the provider's original representations.
