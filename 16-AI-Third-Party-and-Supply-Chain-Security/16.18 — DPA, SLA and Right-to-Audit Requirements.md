# 16.18 — DPA, SLA and Right-to-Audit Requirements

## Part 1 — Foundations, DPA Scope, SLA Architecture, Audit Rights, Accountability, Security and Privacy Requirements

### 1. DPA, SLA and Right-to-Audit Requirements Definition

DPA, SLA and right-to-audit requirements establish the contractual mechanisms through which an organization governs how an AI provider processes data, delivers contracted services, maintains security and resilience, demonstrates compliance, and provides evidence or access necessary for assurance.

These mechanisms address different but interconnected dimensions:

* **DPA** — governs applicable data-processing relationships and obligations.
* **SLA** — establishes measurable service-performance commitments.
* **Right to audit** — establishes mechanisms through which the customer can obtain assurance concerning contractual obligations.

They should be governed as complementary components rather than treated as interchangeable documents.

### 2. DPA Definition

A Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is a contractual instrument governing specified processing activities where applicable data-protection law requires or supports contractual allocation of responsibilities between the relevant parties.

The DPA should reflect:

* actual processing activities;
* roles of the parties;
* categories of data;
* purposes;
* security measures;
* subprocessors;
* data-subject rights support;
* breach cooperation;
* deletion or return;
* applicable transfer requirements.

### 3. SLA Definition

A Service Level Agreement (SLA) establishes measurable commitments concerning service performance.

Depending on the AI service, an SLA may address:

* availability;
* response time;
* support;
* incident response;
* restoration;
* service requests;
* security notifications;
* maintenance;
* service credits.

An SLA should not automatically be treated as a comprehensive security agreement.

### 4. Right-to-Audit Definition

A right-to-audit clause establishes the contractual ability to obtain assurance concerning the provider's compliance with specified contractual requirements.

Depending on the arrangement, assurance may involve:

* document review;
* questionnaires;
* independent reports;
* certifications;
* remote assessment;
* technical evidence;
* on-site audit;
* targeted testing.

### 6. Integrated Contractual Assurance

* evidence;

* findings;
* remediation;
* risk decisions.

### 7. Legal Requirement Versus Contractual Requirement

A DPA, SLA or audit clause may implement or support a legal or regulatory obligation, but the contractual clause itself should not automatically be described as legislation.

The knowledge base should distinguish:

* **law**;
* **regulation**;
* **regulatory guidance**;
* **contractual obligation**;
* **organizational policy**;
* **internal control**;
* **recommended practice**.

```text id="t6n2p8"
Reassessment
```

The existence of a DPA, SLA, audit clause, certification, assessment report, or provider assertion should not by itself be treated as evidence that all relevant AI security, privacy, resilience, or regulatory risks have been eliminated.

Internal `AI-DPA-SLA-*` identifiers remain organizational taxonomy identifiers and should not be represented as statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, or mandatory identifiers from external standards.
