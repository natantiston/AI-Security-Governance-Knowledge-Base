# 16.17 — Contractual AI Security Requirements

## Part 1 — Foundations, Contract Scope, Security Obligations, Accountability, Risk Allocation and AI Contract Architecture

### 1. Contractual AI Security Requirements Definition

Contractual AI security requirements are the security, privacy, governance, resilience, assurance, compliance, and accountability obligations established between an organization and an AI provider, vendor, developer, platform operator, dataset provider, model provider, cloud provider, managed service provider, or other third party through legally binding contractual arrangements.

A contractual requirement may establish obligations that are more specific than general organizational policy or regulatory expectations. It can define what a provider must do, what evidence it must provide, how incidents must be handled, what changes require notification, what audit rights exist, and what remedies apply when security obligations are not satisfied.

### 2. Contractual Requirement Versus Legal Requirement

A contractual AI security requirement is not automatically a statutory or regulatory requirement.

The distinction should be maintained explicitly:

* **Law or regulation** — an external legal obligation imposed by an applicable jurisdiction.
* **Regulatory requirement** — an obligation established by a competent regulatory authority or applicable regulatory instrument.
* **Contractual requirement** — an obligation created by agreement between contracting parties.
* **Organizational policy** — an internally approved governance requirement.
* **Security control** — a mechanism implemented to reduce identified risk.
* **Recommended practice** — a practice adopted because it improves assurance or risk management.

A contract may incorporate legal, regulatory, standards-based, or internal requirements, but incorporation does not change the underlying source of the requirement.

### 4. AI Contract Risk Objective

The objective of contractual AI security requirements is to ensure that material security and governance expectations are explicitly allocated, measurable, enforceable, auditable, and operationally achievable throughout the AI service lifecycle.

The contract should reduce ambiguity concerning:

* who is responsible;
* what must be protected;
* which controls apply;
* what evidence is required;
* when notification is required;
* who may access information;
* what happens during incidents;
* how changes are governed;
* how the relationship terminates.

### 6. Use-Case Dependency

Contractual requirements should be proportionate to the organization's actual AI use case.

The same provider may present substantially different contractual risk depending on whether the service is used for:

* regulated decision support;
* processing confidential information;
* processing personal data;
* handling sensitive business information;
* autonomous actions;
* high-impact business processes.

Contractual requirements should therefore be linked to the approved use case rather than evaluated solely against the provider's general service description.

### 12. Mandatory Versus Negotiable Terms

Contractual requirements should distinguish between:

* mandatory organizational requirements;
* mandatory regulatory or legal requirements;
* preferred contractual terms;
* negotiable commercial terms;
* compensating-control scenarios.

This prevents material security requirements from being treated as ordinary commercial negotiation points without risk assessment.

* incident-response requirements;

Any exception should follow formal risk acceptance rather than informal procurement approval.

### 210. Contractual AI Security Risk Decision Principle

A contractual AI security decision should consider the complete relationship between the AI use case, provider, data, architecture, contractual obligations, operational controls, evidence, residual risk, and exit capability.

The decision should distinguish clearly between:

* what regulation requires;
* what the contract requires;
* what organizational policy requires;
* what security controls implement;
* what evidence demonstrates;
* what risk remains.

The framework should maintain a precise distinction between **law, regulation, contractual commitment, organizational policy, internal control, standard, and recommended practice**.
