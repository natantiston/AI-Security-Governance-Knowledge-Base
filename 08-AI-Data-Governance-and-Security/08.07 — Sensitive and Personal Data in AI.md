# 08.07 — Sensitive and Personal Data in AI

## Part 1

### 1. Introduction to Sensitive and Personal Data in AI

AI systems increasingly process information that can identify individuals, describe their behavior, reveal confidential circumstances, or expose sensitive organizational information.

Examples include:

* Names and contact details.
* Identification numbers.
* Financial information.
* Employment records.
* Customer information.
* Authentication information.
* Location information.
* Communications.
* Confidential business information.

The central governance principle is:

> **AI does not change the sensitivity of data. Instead, AI can increase the scale, speed, and potential impact of how sensitive data is processed.**

---

### 2. What Is Personal Data?

Personal data is information relating to an identified or identifiable individual.

Examples include:

```text
Name
Email Address
Telephone Number
Customer ID
Employee ID
IP Address
Location Information
Online Identifier
```

Whether a specific data element qualifies as personal data depends on the applicable legal and regulatory framework.

---

### 3. Direct Identifiers

A direct identifier can identify an individual by itself or with little additional information.

Examples include:

* Full name.
* Passport number.
* National identification number.
* Email address.
* Telephone number.
* Customer account number.

Example:

```text
John Morales
Passport: X1234567
Email: john@example.com
```

These values require appropriate protection when processed by AI systems.

---

### 4. Indirect Identifiers

Some information may not identify an individual by itself but may contribute to identification when combined with other information.

Examples:

* Age.
* Location.
* Job role.
* Department.
* Date of birth.
* Device information.
* Unique behavioral patterns.

```text
Age
+
Location
+
Employer
+
Job Role
        ↓
Potential Identification
```

---

### 5. Sensitive Data

Some categories of information require stronger safeguards because unauthorized disclosure or misuse could create significant harm.

Depending on the applicable framework, this can include information relating to:

* Health.
* Biometrics.
* Financial circumstances.
* Precise location.
* Authentication.
* Children's information.
* Other specially protected categories.

Organizations should define sensitive-data categories in their own data-classification framework.

---

### 6. Personal Data vs Sensitive Data

These concepts should not automatically be treated as identical.

```text
Personal Data
       │
       ├── Ordinary Personal Data
       │
       └── Sensitive / Specially Protected Data
```

For example:

```text
Name
→ Personal Data

Medical Information
→ Personal + Potentially Specially Protected Data
```

The applicable legal definition determines the precise classification.

---

### 7. Confidential Business Data

Not all sensitive information is personal data.

An AI system may process confidential corporate information such as:

* Business strategies.
* Source code.
* Intellectual property.
* Security architecture.
* Pricing information.
* Contracts.
* Internal investigations.

```text
Sensitive Data
      │
      ├── Personal
      ├── Security
      └── Business Confidential
```

AI governance should address all relevant categories.

---

### 8. Why AI Creates Additional Risk

AI can process very large quantities of information rapidly.

Traditional processing might involve:

```text
100 Records
   ↓
Manual Review
```

AI may process:

```text
1,000,000 Records
       ↓
Automated Analysis
```

The scale of processing can therefore amplify existing data risks.

---

### 9. AI Data Aggregation

AI systems can combine information from multiple sources.

For example:

```text
CRM
+
HR
+
Customer Support
+
Location
+
Transaction Data
       ↓
AI Context
```

The combined dataset may reveal information that was not obvious from each individual source.

---

### 10. Profiling

AI can analyze personal information to create profiles.

Examples include:

* Customer preferences.
* Employee behavior.
* Fraud risk.
* Purchasing patterns.
* Service usage.
* User interests.

Profiling may create additional governance obligations depending on the use case and applicable law.

---

### 11. Automated Decision-Making

AI may be used to support or make decisions affecting individuals.

Examples:

* Credit decisions.
* Recruitment.
* Fraud detection.
* Insurance assessment.
* Customer eligibility.
* Access decisions.

Higher-impact decisions require stronger governance.

---

### 12. Human Oversight

Where AI contributes to significant decisions about individuals, organizations should determine whether meaningful human oversight is required.

A simplified model is:

```text
Personal Data
      ↓
AI Analysis
      ↓
Recommendation
      ↓
Human Review
      ↓
Decision
```

Human oversight should be meaningful rather than merely a formal approval step.

---

### 13. Purpose Limitation

Personal data should be processed for defined and legitimate purposes.

Example:

```text
Purpose:
Customer Support

Required:
Customer support history

Not Automatically Required:
Entire customer profile
```

The fact that information is technically accessible does not mean it should be submitted to an AI system.

---

### 14. Data Minimization

AI systems should receive only the information necessary for the intended task.

Example:

```text
Task:
Summarize customer complaint

Poor:
Entire customer database

Better:
Specific complaint + necessary context
```

Data minimization reduces exposure and limits potential impact.

---

### 15. Necessity Assessment

Before processing personal data through AI, ask:

> **Is this data actually necessary for the AI task?**

A useful decision model is:

```text
Data Element
     ↓
Required for Purpose?
   ↙          ↘
 Yes           No
 ↓              ↓
Process       Remove
```

---

### 16. Data Classification Before AI Processing

Personal and sensitive data should be identified before entering an AI workflow.

```text
Data
 ↓
Classification
 ↓
Personal?
 ↓
Sensitive?
 ↓
AI Policy
 ↓
Allowed / Restricted / Blocked
```

Classification allows technical controls to make policy decisions.

---

### 17. Sensitive Data Discovery

Organizations can use automated tools to identify sensitive information.

Potential technologies include:

* DLP.
* Data discovery.
* Data classification.
* Sensitive-data scanners.
* Database discovery.
* Content inspection.

---

### 18. Personally Identifiable Information Detection

AI gateways can inspect prompts and documents for potential personal information.

Example:

```text
Prompt
  ↓
PII Detection
  ↓
Email / Phone / ID Found
  ↓
Policy Evaluation
```

The system can then:

* Allow.
* Warn.
* Mask.
* Redact.
* Block.
* Escalate.

---

### 19. Data Redaction

Redaction removes unnecessary sensitive information before AI processing.

Example:

```text
Original:
Customer Maria Santos, account 938271,
requested a refund.

AI Input:
Customer [REDACTED], account [REDACTED],
requested a refund.
```

Redaction can reduce unnecessary exposure while preserving task context.

---

### 20. Data Masking

Masking replaces sensitive information with a controlled representation.

Example:

```text
Original:
maria.santos@example.com

Masked:
customer001@example.test
```

Masking is useful when the AI needs the structure or role of the information but does not need the actual identifier.

---

### 21. Pseudonymization

Pseudonymization replaces identifying information with pseudonyms while maintaining the ability to associate records through separately protected information.

```text
Real Identity
      ↓
Pseudonymous Identifier
      ↓
AI Processing
```

The mapping information must itself be appropriately protected.

---

### 22. Anonymization

Anonymization attempts to prevent individuals from being identified from the resulting information.

However:

> **Removing obvious identifiers does not automatically make a dataset anonymous.**

Re-identification risks should be considered when multiple attributes remain.

---

### 23. Re-Identification Risk

AI systems can sometimes combine seemingly harmless information.

Example:

```text
Age
+
Location
+
Employer
+
Rare Job Role
       ↓
Potential Re-Identification
```

Therefore, organizations should evaluate the combined information rather than relying only on removal of names.

---

### 24. Data Subject Rights

Where applicable, organizations processing personal data through AI should consider relevant data-subject rights.

Depending on the applicable framework, these may include:

* Right of access.
* Right to rectification.
* Right to erasure.
* Right to restriction.
* Right to object.
* Other applicable rights.

AI architecture should be capable of supporting relevant obligations.

---

### 25. Data Subject Access

If an individual requests access to their personal information, the organization should understand where AI-related copies may exist.

Potential locations include:

```text
Source System
     ↓
AI Application
     ↓
Inference Logs
     ↓
Temporary Storage
     ↓
Provider
     ↓
Backups
```

Data mapping is therefore essential.

---

### 26. Data Correction

If inaccurate personal information enters an AI system, the organization should understand how corrections propagate.

Example:

```text
Source Record Corrected
        ↓
AI Data Store Updated
        ↓
Cached Data Updated
        ↓
Future Inference Uses Correct Data
```

Historical AI outputs may require separate consideration depending on the use case.

---

### 27. Data Deletion

Deletion requirements can become more complicated when personal information exists in multiple AI-related locations.

Potential locations include:

* Original datasets.
* Prompt histories.
* Application databases.
* Vector databases.
* Caches.
* Logs.
* Backups.
* Provider systems.

A deletion process should identify the relevant copies.

---

### 28. Personal Data in Prompts

Users may unintentionally place personal data directly into prompts.

Example:

```text
"Review this employee's performance:
Name: John Smith
Employee ID: 12345
Salary: €72,000
Performance history: ..."
```

The organization should determine whether this processing is authorized and necessary.

---

### 29. Personal Data in Documents

AI applications may process entire documents containing personal information.

Examples include:

* Resumes.
* Contracts.
* Customer complaints.
* Employee records.
* Invoices.
* Support tickets.

Before processing, the application should determine whether the complete document is actually necessary.

---

### 30. Core GRC Principle

Sensitive and personal data in AI requires governance across the entire processing lifecycle:

```text
              PERSONAL / SENSITIVE DATA
                         │
                         ▼
                    IDENTIFICATION
                         │
                         ▼
                    CLASSIFICATION
                         │
                         ▼
                      PURPOSE
                         │
                         ▼
                     NECESSITY
                         │
                         ▼
                    MINIMIZATION
                         │
                         ▼
              REDACTION / MASKING
                         │
                         ▼
                    AUTHORIZATION
                         │
                         ▼
                    AI PROCESSING
                         │
                         ▼
                 MONITORING / LOGGING
                         │
                         ▼
                  RETENTION / DELETION
                         │
                         ▼
                      ASSURANCE
```

The fundamental principle is:

> **Personal and sensitive data should enter an AI system only when the processing is justified, authorized, necessary, appropriately protected, and governed throughout its lifecycle.**

AI governance must therefore extend beyond simply asking **"Can the model process this data?"**

The more important GRC questions are:

**Should the organization process this data?**

**Does the AI system need this data?**

**Is the user authorized to provide it?**

**Is the AI provider authorized to process it?**

**Can the data be minimized or de-identified?**

**Can the organization control retention and deletion?**

**Can the organization demonstrate compliance and accountability?**
