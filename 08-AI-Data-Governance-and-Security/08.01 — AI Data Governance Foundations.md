# 08.01 — AI Data Governance Foundations

## Part 1

### 1. Introduction to AI Data Governance

AI systems depend fundamentally on data.

Data may be used to:

* Train models.
* Fine-tune models.
* Validate models.
* Test models.
* Provide context during inference.
* Support RAG systems.
* Enable AI agents.
* Evaluate AI performance.
* Monitor AI behavior.

Because AI decisions and outputs can be influenced directly by data, poor data governance can become a direct **AI security, privacy, compliance, and business-risk issue**.

The fundamental principle is:

> **AI governance is only as reliable as the governance of the data that feeds, supports, and surrounds the AI system.**

---

### 2. What Is AI Data Governance?

AI data governance is the framework of **policies, processes, roles, standards, controls, and accountability mechanisms** used to manage data throughout its use in AI systems.

It establishes how organizations:

* Identify data.
* Classify data.
* Acquire data.
* Validate data.
* Protect data.
* Control access.
* Track provenance.
* Maintain quality.
* Preserve integrity.
* Manage retention.
* Monitor usage.
* Dispose of data.

---

### 3. Why AI Requires Specialized Data Governance

Traditional data governance remains important, but AI introduces additional considerations.

AI systems may:

* Process enormous datasets.
* Combine multiple data sources.
* Learn patterns from historical information.
* Generate new information from existing data.
* Retrieve information dynamically.
* Use unstructured data.
* Use third-party datasets.
* Process data through external AI providers.

Therefore, organizations need to understand not only **where data is stored**, but also **how data influences AI behavior**.

---

### 4. Traditional Data Governance vs AI Data Governance

Traditional data governance often focuses on:

* Data ownership.
* Data quality.
* Classification.
* Access.
* Retention.
* Privacy.

AI data governance additionally considers:

* Training datasets.
* Model-development data.
* Evaluation datasets.
* Prompt data.
* Inference data.
* Embeddings.
* Vector databases.
* Retrieval data.
* Synthetic data.
* AI-generated data.
* Data poisoning.
* Model memorization.
* AI-specific leakage.

---

### 5. The AI Data Governance Objective

The objective is to ensure that AI uses data that is:


The organization should be able to demonstrate **why the data is being used, where it came from, who owns it, who can access it, and how it is protected**.

### 7. Data Ownership

Every important AI dataset should have an accountable owner.

The data owner may be responsible for:

* Approving use.
* Defining classification.
* Determining access.
* Establishing retention.
* Defining acceptable uses.
* Reviewing risks.


Data stewardship is the operational management of data according to governance requirements.

A data steward may help ensure:

* Data quality is monitored.

* Data issues are addressed.

### 15. Data Classification

Data classification determines how strongly information must be protected.

A simple model could be:

```text id="j6q9r3"
Public
   ↓
Internal
   ↓
Confidential
   ↓
Restricted
```

The classification should influence:

* Access.
* Encryption.
* Storage.
* Sharing.
* Retention.
* Monitoring.

---

### 16. Purpose Limitation

Data should be used only for approved purposes.

For example:

> Customer support data approved for customer-service assistance should not automatically be reused to train another AI model for an unrelated purpose.

The new purpose should be evaluated according to organizational, contractual, privacy, and legal requirements.

---

### 17. Data Minimization

AI systems should use only the data necessary for the intended purpose.

Instead of:

```text id="m4q8v2"
Entire Enterprise Database
          ↓
          AI
```

Prefer:

```text id="x7p3m9"
Required Data
     ↓
Filtered
     ↓
AI
```

Data minimization reduces exposure and potential impact.

---

### 18. Data Quality

AI systems can be strongly affected by poor-quality data.

Quality dimensions may include:

* Accuracy.
* Completeness.
* Consistency.
* Timeliness.
* Validity.
* Uniqueness.

Poor data quality can produce unreliable AI outputs.

---

### 19. Data Integrity

Data integrity means maintaining the correctness and trustworthiness of data throughout its lifecycle.

AI data integrity can be affected by:

* Unauthorized modification.
* Corruption.
* Accidental changes.
* Malicious manipulation.
* Data poisoning.

---

### 20. Data Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that AI data is accessible only to authorized entities.

Potential controls include:

* IAM.
* Encryption.
* DLP.
* Network controls.
* Access reviews.
* Data masking.

---

### 21. Data Availability

AI systems may depend on continuous access to critical data.

Governance should therefore consider:

* Backups.
* Replication.
* Recovery.
* Availability requirements.
* Disaster recovery.

---

### 22. Data Provenance

Provenance answers:

> **Where did this data come from?**

For an AI dataset, provenance can include:

* Original source.
* Provider.
* Collection method.
* Acquisition date.
* Transformations.
* Processing history.
* Ownership.

---

### 23. Data Lineage

Lineage answers:

> **How did the data move and change from source to AI system?**

Example:

```text id="k8m4q6"
Source
  ↓
Collection
  ↓
Transformation
  ↓
Cleaning
  ↓
Storage
  ↓
Training / Retrieval
  ↓
AI System
```

---

### 24. Provenance vs Lineage

These concepts are related but different.

**Provenance** focuses primarily on **origin and history**.

**Lineage** focuses primarily on **movement and transformation**.

A useful distinction is:

> Provenance tells you **where data came from**; lineage tells you **how it got where it is**.

### 29. Test Data Governance

Test data should not automatically be assumed safe.

Production data used for testing may contain:

* Personal information.
* Confidential business information.
* Credentials.
* Customer information.
* Sensitive operational data.

Appropriate controls should therefore be applied.

---

### 30. Inference Data Governance

Inference data is the information provided to an AI system during runtime.

Examples include:

* User prompts.
* Uploaded files.
* API requests.
* Retrieved documents.
* Images.
* Audio.
* Agent context.

This data may be sensitive even if it never becomes part of model training.

---

### 31. Prompt Data as Enterprise Data

A prompt should not automatically be treated as harmless text.

A prompt may contain:

```text id="m8q3v7"

Financial Information
Credentials
Internal Documents
Personal Information
Business Secrets
```

Therefore, prompt data should be governed according to its actual content and sensitivity.

### 33. Embedding Governance

Embeddings should not automatically be considered harmless because they are mathematical representations.

Organizations should evaluate:

* What source data produced the embedding.
* Who can access it.
* Where it is stored.
* Whether it can be linked back to sensitive information.
* How it is deleted.

---

### 34. Vector Database Governance

Vector databases should be governed like other enterprise data stores.

Controls may include:

* Encryption.

* Logging.
* Retention.
* Backup controls.

### 35. Data Access Governance

AI data access should follow least privilege.

The key principle is:

> **Users and AI components should access only the data required for their approved purpose.**

This applies to:

* Humans.
* Applications.
* Agents.
* Service accounts.
* AI pipelines.

---

### 36. Human Data Access

Human users should be granted access based on:

* Role.
* Business need.
* Data sensitivity.
* Organizational policy.

For example:

A shared administrative credential should not be used simply because it is convenient.

### 38. Agent Data Access

Agents introduce additional complexity because their actions may be dynamic.

Agent permissions should therefore be:

* Explicit.
* Limited.
* Monitored.
* Reviewable.
* Revocable.

### 51. AI Data Governance Framework

A mature AI data governance program should provide a structured framework for managing data throughout its AI lifecycle.

### 52. Governance Policies

AI data governance begins with formal policies.

Policies should establish requirements for:

* Approved data usage.
* Data ownership.
* Data classification.
* Data protection.
* Privacy.
* Access control.
* Data retention.
* Data sharing.
* Training data.
* Inference data.
* Third-party data.

Policies provide the governance foundation, but they must be translated into enforceable standards and controls.

---

### 53. AI Data Governance Standards

Standards convert high-level policy requirements into measurable expectations.

Example:

**Policy:**

> Sensitive information must be protected when used by AI systems.

**Standard:**

> Confidential and restricted datasets must use approved encryption, access control, monitoring, and retention mechanisms.

This distinction is important in GRC because standards provide more concrete assessment criteria.

---

### 54. AI Data Procedures

Procedures describe how employees and technical teams implement governance requirements.

Examples include:

* Dataset onboarding.
* Dataset approval.
* Data classification.
* Access provisioning.
* Data-quality validation.
* Dataset retirement.
* Data deletion.
* Third-party data review.

```text id="q8m3r7"
Policy
  ↓
Standard
  ↓
Procedure
  ↓
Control
  ↓
Evidence
```

---

### 55. Data Governance Control Objectives

A control objective defines what the organization wants to achieve.

Examples:

**Objective 1:**

> Ensure only authorized datasets are used by approved AI systems.

**Objective 2:**

> Ensure sensitive AI data is protected from unauthorized disclosure.

**Objective 3:**

> Ensure important AI datasets have traceable provenance.

**Objective 4:**

> Ensure data quality is sufficient for its intended AI purpose.

---

A control framework can organize controls into domains.

| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |

| Provenance     | Establish data origin                    |

| Retention      | Prevent unnecessary retention            |
| Security       | Protect confidentiality and availability |

### 58. Data Owner Responsibilities

The data owner should generally determine:

* Who may use the data.
* For what purposes.
* How sensitive the data is.
* Whether AI usage is permitted.
* What risks are acceptable.

The data owner is therefore an important governance decision-maker.

---

### 59. Data Steward Responsibilities

The data steward typically supports the operational governance of the dataset.

Responsibilities can include:

* Maintaining metadata.
* Monitoring data quality.
* Coordinating classification.
* Supporting access reviews.
* Identifying data issues.
* Maintaining governance documentation.

---

### 60. AI System Owner Responsibilities

The AI system owner should understand:

* What data the AI consumes.
* Why the data is required.
* How the data is processed.
* Which AI components use it.
* What risks result from the data usage.

The AI owner connects business requirements with AI implementation.

### 61. Security Responsibilities

Security teams may establish and validate controls for:

* Access.
* Encryption.
* DLP.
* Monitoring.
* Data leakage prevention.
* Infrastructure protection.
* Incident response.

### 62. Privacy Responsibilities

* Personal-data processing.

This distinction is important when assessing AI data environments.

### 195. Governance Anti-Pattern: “The Data Is Internal, So It Is Safe”

Internal classification does not automatically mean low risk.

Internal data may contain:

* Customer information.
* Employee information.
* Intellectual property.
* Credentials.
* Security information.
* Confidential business information.

Security requirements should therefore reflect the actual sensitivity of the data.

---

### 196. Governance Anti-Pattern: “The Model Provider Handles Security”

An AI provider may implement strong security controls, but the organization remains responsible for governing what it sends to the provider and whether that processing is authorized.

The organization must understand:

* What data is transferred.
* Why it is transferred.
* Where it is processed.
* How it is protected.
* How long it is retained.

---

### 197. Governance Anti-Pattern: “Only Training Data Matters”

Training data is important, but AI systems process many other forms of data.

Governance should also cover:

* Validation data.
* Test data.
* Prompt data.
* Inference data.
* RAG documents.
* Embeddings.
* Logs.
* AI-generated data.

---

### 198. Governance Anti-Pattern: “Compliance Equals Governance”

Passing a compliance assessment does not necessarily mean an organization has mature AI data governance.

Compliance generally demonstrates alignment with defined requirements.

Governance additionally requires:

* Accountability.
* Decision-making.
* Risk management.
* Continuous oversight.
* Continuous improvement.

---

### 199. Governance Anti-Pattern: “One Policy Fits Everything”

AI systems have different:

* Risk profiles.
* Data types.
* Business purposes.
* Users.
* Architectures.
* Regulatory requirements.

A mature governance program therefore uses **risk-based requirements** rather than identical controls for every AI system.

### 200. AI Data Governance Foundations — Final Framework

The complete foundation can be summarized as:

```text id="v6q9m3"
                 BUSINESS PURPOSE
                        │
                        ▼
                    AI USE CASE
                        │
                        ▼
                  DATA REQUIREMENTS
                        │
                        ▼
                 DATA IDENTIFICATION
                        │
                        ▼
                    OWNERSHIP
                        │
                        ▼
                  CLASSIFICATION
                        │
                        ▼
                 QUALITY & INTEGRITY
                        │
                        ▼
             PROVENANCE & LINEAGE
                        │
                        ▼
               SECURITY & PRIVACY
                        │
                        ▼
                  ACCESS CONTROL
                        │
                        ▼
                  RETENTION / DLP
                        │
                        ▼
                 MONITORING
                        │
                        ▼
                   ASSURANCE
                        │
                        ▼
               CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
```

The fundamental GRC principle is:

> **AI data governance establishes the accountability, controls, and assurance necessary to ensure that data used by AI is appropriate for its intended purpose, properly owned and classified, sufficiently accurate and trustworthy, securely accessed and processed, traceable throughout its lifecycle, retained only as required, and continuously governed according to risk.**

This foundation is essential because every subsequent AI data-security topic—whether training data, sensitive information, provenance, lineage, data quality, poisoning, leakage, retention, access control, or DLP—depends on the organization first knowing **what data it has, who owns it, why AI is using it, and what controls are required to protect it**.
