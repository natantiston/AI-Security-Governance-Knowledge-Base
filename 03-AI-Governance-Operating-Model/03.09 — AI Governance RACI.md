# 03.09 — AI Governance RACI

## Introduction

An AI Governance RACI establishes a structured method for assigning accountability and responsibility across AI governance activities.

AI governance involves numerous stakeholders, including business owners, AI governance teams, cybersecurity, risk management, privacy, legal, data governance, technology, engineering, compliance, internal audit, and executive leadership. Without a clearly defined responsibility model, organizations may encounter duplicated activities, unclear decision authority, delayed approvals, or governance gaps.

The RACI model provides a practical mechanism for translating the organization's AI governance roles and responsibilities into an operational structure.

RACI stands for:

* **R — Responsible**
* **A — Accountable**
* **C — Consulted**
* **I — Informed**

A well-designed AI Governance RACI establishes **who performs an activity, who owns the outcome, who provides input, and who needs visibility**.

---

# Purpose of an AI Governance RACI

The primary purpose of an AI Governance RACI is to clarify accountability across the AI lifecycle.

It can be used to define responsibilities for:

* AI strategy.
* AI governance.
* AI policy.
* AI risk assessment.
* AI security.
* AI privacy.
* AI data governance.
* AI development.
* AI validation.
* AI approval.
* AI deployment.
* AI monitoring.
* AI incident management.
* AI change management.
* AI retirement.
* AI third-party governance.
* AI risk acceptance.

The RACI should provide an operational representation of the organization's governance model.

---

# 1. Understanding the RACI Model

The four RACI designations have distinct meanings.

## Responsible — R

The person or function that performs the work.

Responsible parties may:

* Execute an activity.
* Prepare documentation.
* Perform assessments.
* Implement controls.
* Produce evidence.

There may be more than one Responsible party when multiple functions perform different portions of the activity.

---

## Accountable — A

The person who ultimately owns the outcome and is answerable for the activity.

The Accountable role:

* Owns the final outcome.
* Ensures the activity is completed.
* Has appropriate authority.
* Approves the result where required.
* Accepts accountability for the decision.

As a governance principle, each significant activity should normally have **one clearly identifiable Accountable role**.

---

## Consulted — C

The person or function that provides expertise or input before a decision or activity is completed.

Consulted parties may include:

* Cybersecurity.
* Privacy.
* Legal.
* Risk.
* Data Governance.
* Architecture.
* Compliance.

Consultation should occur where the expertise is materially relevant.

---

## Informed — I

The person or function that needs to receive information about the activity or decision but does not actively participate in making it.

Examples may include:

* Executive management.
* Governance committees.
* Business stakeholders.
* Audit.
* Affected operational teams.

Being Informed does not imply approval authority.

---

# 2. RACI and AI Governance Accountability

RACI should translate organizational governance requirements into operational accountability.

For example:

**AI Security Assessment**

could be structured as:

| Role            | RACI |
| --------------- | ---- |
| AI System Owner | A    |
| Security        | R    |
| Risk            | C    |
| Privacy         | C    |
| AI Governance   | C    |
| Business Owner  | I    |

This establishes that Security performs the assessment, while the System Owner remains accountable for ensuring that the assessment is completed.

---

# 3. RACI Is Not an Organizational Chart

A RACI does not describe the organization's hierarchy.

An organizational chart shows:

* Reporting relationships.
* Management hierarchy.
* Organizational structure.

A RACI shows:

* Activity ownership.
* Responsibility.
* Consultation.
* Information flow.

A single role may therefore appear across multiple organizational areas without changing the reporting structure.

---

# 4. RACI Is Not a Risk Register

A RACI should not replace the AI risk register.

The:

**Risk Register**

identifies and manages risks.

The:

**RACI**

identifies who performs and owns governance activities.

The two should work together.

For example:

```text
AI Risk
   ↓
Risk Treatment
   ↓
Control
   ↓
Control Owner
   ↓
RACI
```

---

# 5. RACI Is Not a Policy

A policy establishes mandatory organizational requirements.

A RACI translates those requirements into responsibility assignments.

For example:

**Policy**

> High-risk AI systems must undergo formal security assessment before production deployment.

**RACI**

identifies:

* Who performs the assessment.
* Who is accountable.
* Who must be consulted.
* Who must be informed.

---

# 6. Establish a Single Accountable Role

One of the most important RACI principles is establishing clear accountability.

A poorly designed matrix may contain:

| Activity    | Business | Security | Risk | Governance |
| ----------- | -------- | -------- | ---- | ---------- |
| AI approval | A        | A        | A    | A          |

This creates ambiguity.

A better model identifies one primary Accountable role while assigning supporting responsibilities appropriately.

For example:

| Activity    | Business | Security | Risk | Governance |
| ----------- | -------- | -------- | ---- | ---------- |
| AI approval | A        | C        | C    | R          |

The exact assignment should reflect the organization's actual decision authority.

---

# 7. Avoid Activities Without Accountability

Every material AI governance activity should have an Accountable role.

Examples include:

* AI risk assessment.
* AI security assessment.
* AI system registration.
* AI deployment approval.
* AI risk acceptance.
* AI incident escalation.
* AI retirement.

A RACI matrix should be reviewed specifically for activities that have no **A**.

An activity without accountability represents a governance weakness.

---

# 8. Avoid Excessive Accountability

Assigning multiple Accountable roles to every activity can make governance ineffective.

For example:

```text
AI Governance = A
Risk = A
Security = A
Business = A
Legal = A
```

may appear comprehensive but often results in unclear decision authority.

Where multiple stakeholders have significant interests, the organization should still define who has final accountability.

---

# 9. Establish Appropriate Responsibility

Responsible assignments should identify the party performing the actual work.

For example:

**AI Risk Assessment**

Risk may be **R**.

**AI Security Assessment**

Security may be **R**.

**AI Privacy Assessment**

Privacy may be **R**.

**AI System Registration**

AI Governance may be **R**.

The Accountable role should ensure that the activity is completed appropriately.

---

# 10. Establish Appropriate Consultation

Consultation should be meaningful rather than excessive.

For example, an AI system processing personal information may require consultation with:

* Privacy.
* Legal.
* Data Governance.
* Security.
* Risk.

However, involving every governance function in every AI activity can create unnecessary delays.

Consultation should therefore be **risk-based and relevant**.

---

# 11. Establish Appropriate Information Flow

The **I** designation should be used for stakeholders who need visibility but do not need to participate in the activity.

Examples include:

* Executive management.
* Governance committees.
* Business stakeholders.
* Affected users.
* Internal Audit where appropriate.

Information requirements should reflect materiality.

---

# 12. Build RACI Around AI Lifecycle Activities

A comprehensive AI Governance RACI should cover the entire lifecycle.

For example:

```text
AI Strategy
     ↓
AI Use Case
     ↓
Risk Classification
     ↓
Design
     ↓
Development
     ↓
Testing
     ↓
Validation
     ↓
Approval
     ↓
Deployment
     ↓
Operation
     ↓
Monitoring
     ↓
Change
     ↓
Incident Management
     ↓
Retirement
```

Each stage should have defined RACI assignments.

---

# 13. Establish RACI for AI Strategy

AI strategy may involve:

* Board.
* Executive Management.
* AI Governance.
* Business Leadership.
* Technology Leadership.
* Risk.

The RACI should distinguish between:

**Strategic approval**

and:

**Strategic execution**.

Executive management may be accountable for the strategy while AI Governance is responsible for coordinating its implementation.

---

# 14. Establish RACI for AI Policy Management

A typical structure may be:

| Role                 | RACI |
| -------------------- | ---- |
| AI Governance        | R    |
| Governance Executive | A    |
| Risk                 | C    |
| Security             | C    |
| Privacy              | C    |
| Legal                | C    |
| Business             | C    |
| Employees            | I    |

The exact assignment depends on the organization's policy authority.

---

# 15. Establish RACI for AI Inventory

AI system inventory management may involve:

* AI Governance.
* System Owners.
* Business Owners.
* Technology.
* Security.

A common model is:

**AI Governance — R**

for maintaining the enterprise inventory.

**System Owner — A**

for ensuring their AI system is accurately registered.

---

# 16. Establish RACI for AI Risk Assessment

AI risk assessment may involve:

* System Owner.
* Risk.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Compliance.
* Legal.

The RACI should distinguish between:

**Performing the assessment**

and:

**Owning the risk identified by the assessment**.

These are not necessarily the same role.

---

# 17. Establish RACI for AI Security Assessment

Security should normally be responsible for performing or coordinating security assessments.

The System Owner should remain accountable for ensuring security requirements are addressed.

Other functions may be consulted depending on the risk.

---

# 18. Establish RACI for AI Privacy Assessment

Privacy may be responsible for performing or reviewing privacy assessments.

The relevant Business or System Owner may remain accountable for ensuring that privacy requirements are satisfied.

Legal, Data Governance, Security, and Risk may be consulted.

---

# 19. Establish RACI for AI Data Governance

Data governance may involve:

* Data Owner.
* Data Steward.
* System Owner.
* Privacy.
* Security.
* AI Governance.

The Data Owner should remain accountable for appropriate data governance decisions.

---

# 20. Establish RACI for AI Model Governance

Model governance may involve:

* Model Owner.
* AI Engineering.
* Validation.
* Risk.
* Business Owner.
* AI Governance.

The Model Owner may be accountable for the model lifecycle while independent validation provides appropriate challenge.

---

# 21. Establish RACI for AI Approval

AI approval should depend on the system's risk level.

For lower-risk systems:

**Business Owner**

may have approval authority.

For higher-risk systems:

approval may require:

* Business Owner.
* Risk.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Governance Committee.

The RACI should identify who has the final decision authority.

---

# 22. Establish RACI for AI Deployment

Deployment responsibilities may involve:

* AI Engineering.
* Platform Operations.
* System Owner.
* Security.
* Change Management.
* AI Governance.

The deployment process should verify that required governance gates have been completed before production deployment.

---

# 23. Establish RACI for AI Monitoring

AI monitoring is multidimensional.

| Monitoring Area       | Typical Responsible Function |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Business performance  | Business / System Owner      |
| Model performance     | Model Owner                  |
| Security              | Security                     |
| Data quality          | Data Owner                   |
| Privacy               | Privacy                      |
| Risk                  | Risk Owner                   |
| Governance compliance | AI Governance                |
| Platform availability | Technology / Operations      |

The RACI should avoid treating "AI monitoring" as a single undifferentiated activity.

---

# 24. Establish RACI for AI Incidents

AI incidents may involve multiple response functions.

A typical structure may include:

* System Owner.
* Security Incident Response.
* Privacy.
* Risk.
* Legal.
* Communications.
* Executive Management.

The RACI should identify who:

* Detects.
* Coordinates.
* Investigates.
* Escalates.
* Makes decisions.
* Communicates.
* Closes the incident.

---

# 25. Establish RACI for AI Change Management

Material AI changes should have defined responsibility.

Examples include:

* Model changes.
* Data changes.
* Architecture changes.
* Integration changes.
* Autonomy changes.
* Provider changes.

The System Owner may be accountable for initiating reassessment, while technical teams perform implementation activities.

---

# 26. Establish RACI for AI Retirement

AI retirement should have explicit ownership.

Possible responsibilities include:

* Business Owner.
* System Owner.
* Data Owner.
* Security.
* Technology.
* Procurement.
* Legal.
* AI Governance.

The RACI should ensure that retirement is fully controlled and documented.

---

# 27. Establish RACI for Third-Party AI

Third-party AI governance may involve:

* Business Owner.
* Procurement.
* Third-Party Risk.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Legal.
* System Owner.
* AI Governance.

The RACI should identify who is accountable for the organization's use of the third-party AI service.

---

# 28. Establish RACI for Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance requires special attention.

The RACI should distinguish:

**Risk Assessment**

from:

**Risk Recommendation**

from:

**Risk Acceptance**

from:

**Risk Monitoring**.

The person performing the assessment should not automatically be the person authorized to accept the risk.

---

# 29. Establish RACI for Governance Exceptions

The exception process should identify:

* Who requests the exception.
* Who evaluates it.
* Who owns the associated risk.
* Who approves the exception.
* Who tracks its expiration.
* Who receives reporting.

This prevents temporary exceptions from becoming permanent undocumented practices.

---

# 30. Establish RACI as an Operational Governance Mechanism

A mature AI Governance RACI should connect:

```text
AI Governance Policy
        ↓
Governance Process
        ↓
Activity
        ↓
RACI Assignment
        ↓
Decision Authority
        ↓
Control
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Assurance
```

This creates traceability between governance requirements and actual execution.

# RACI Design Principles

An effective AI Governance RACI should follow several principles:

### 1. Every material activity should have an Accountable role.

### 2. Accountability should normally be singular.

### 3. Responsible roles should perform the actual work.

### 4. Consultation should be risk-based.

### 5. Information should reach the appropriate stakeholders.

### 6. Decision authority should align with accountability.

### 7. Independent assurance should remain independent.

### 8. RACI assignments should cover the complete AI lifecycle.

### 9. RACI should be connected to policies, controls, and evidence.

### 10. RACI should be reviewed when the operating model changes.

---

# Final Governance Principle

An AI Governance RACI should transform organizational role definitions into an **operational accountability mechanism**.

Its fundamental purpose is to eliminate ambiguity by making clear:

> **Who performs the activity, who owns the outcome, who provides expert input, and who needs visibility.**

A well-designed RACI enables AI governance to move from conceptual responsibility statements to **measurable execution, controlled decision-making, effective escalation, and demonstrable accountability throughout the AI lifecycle**.
