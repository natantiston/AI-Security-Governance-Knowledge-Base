# 03.05 — AI Security Council

## Introduction

An AI Security Council is a specialized governance body responsible for providing enterprise-level oversight and strategic direction for the security of artificial intelligence systems, AI-enabled applications, AI infrastructure, AI data, models, agents, and supporting technologies.

As organizations increasingly integrate AI into business processes, traditional cybersecurity governance alone may not adequately address the unique security characteristics of AI.

AI introduces security considerations involving:

* Models.
* Training and inference data.
* Prompts and instructions.
* Model interfaces.
* AI agents.
* Tool integrations.
* Machine-to-machine interactions.
* Third-party AI services.
* Foundation models.
* AI supply chains.
* Autonomous actions.
* Model outputs.

The AI Security Council provides a mechanism for bringing together cybersecurity, AI engineering, architecture, risk, data, privacy, legal, and business stakeholders to establish a coordinated approach to AI security.

Its primary objective is to ensure that AI is **secure by design, securely deployed, continuously monitored, and appropriately governed throughout its lifecycle**.

---

## Purpose of the AI Security Council

The purpose of the AI Security Council is to establish and maintain enterprise-level oversight of AI security.

The council may be responsible for:

* Establishing AI security strategy.
* Defining AI security principles.
* Reviewing significant AI security risks.
* Establishing security requirements for AI systems.
* Coordinating AI security capabilities.
* Reviewing major AI security incidents.
* Addressing emerging AI-specific threats.
* Overseeing AI security architecture.
* Supporting secure AI adoption.
* Coordinating security requirements for third-party AI services.
* Escalating material AI security risks.

The council should provide strategic security oversight rather than replacing the operational responsibilities of cybersecurity teams.

---

## AI Security as a Specialized Governance Domain

AI security is related to cybersecurity but has characteristics that require specialized consideration.

Traditional cybersecurity primarily protects:

* Networks.
* Systems.
* Applications.
* Endpoints.
* Identity.
* Infrastructure.
* Data.

AI security must additionally consider:

* Model behavior.
* Prompt manipulation.
* Training-data integrity.
* Model integrity.
* Model extraction.
* Adversarial inputs.
* AI-specific supply-chain risks.
* Insecure tool use.
* Excessive AI permissions.
* Autonomous actions.
* Manipulated outputs.

Therefore:

**Cybersecurity Governance + AI-Specific Security Governance = Enterprise AI Security Governance**

The AI Security Council should help ensure that AI-specific security risks are integrated into the organization's broader cybersecurity framework.

# AI Security Council Versus AI Governance Committee

The AI Security Council and AI Governance Committee may have overlapping stakeholders, but their primary responsibilities should remain distinct.

### AI Governance Committee

Focuses broadly on:

* AI governance.
* AI risk.
* Compliance.
* Responsible AI.
* Policy.
* Accountability.
* Oversight.
* Exceptions.
* Enterprise governance.

### AI Security Council

Focuses specifically on:

* AI cybersecurity.
* AI security architecture.
* AI threats.
* Security controls.
* AI vulnerabilities.
* AI incidents.
* AI security testing.
* AI supply-chain security.
* Security monitoring.

The relationship can be represented as:

**AI Governance → Enterprise AI Oversight**

**AI Security Council → AI Security Oversight**

The AI Security Council should operate within the broader AI governance framework.

---

# Strategic Role of the AI Security Council

The council should provide strategic direction for protecting AI capabilities throughout their lifecycle.

This includes security considerations during:

**Design → Development → Training → Testing → Deployment → Operation → Monitoring → Change → Retirement**

Security should therefore not be treated as a final approval step.

Instead, it should be incorporated throughout the AI lifecycle.

# AI Security Principles

The AI Security Council should establish principles that guide AI security across the organization.

Important principles may include:

### Secure by Design

Security should be incorporated into AI systems from the beginning.

### Least Privilege

AI systems and agents should receive only the permissions required to perform their intended functions.

### Defense in Depth

AI security should rely on multiple complementary controls.

### Zero Trust

AI components should not automatically be trusted based on network location or system identity alone.

### Data Protection

Sensitive data should be appropriately protected throughout the AI lifecycle.

### Model Integrity

Models and associated artifacts should be protected against unauthorized modification.

### Continuous Monitoring

AI systems should be monitored for security threats and abnormal behavior.

### Human Oversight

High-impact or high-risk AI activities should have appropriate human intervention mechanisms.

### Accountability

AI security responsibilities should be clearly assigned.

### Resilience

Critical AI capabilities should have appropriate recovery and fallback mechanisms.

# AI Security Lifecycle

AI security should be incorporated throughout the AI lifecycle.

### Design

Identify security requirements and threats.

### Development

Implement secure development practices.

### Training

Protect training data, models, infrastructure, and processes.

### Testing

Conduct security and adversarial testing.

### Deployment

Ensure security controls are implemented before production use.

### Operation

Monitor security events and AI behavior.

### Change

Reassess security when models, prompts, data, tools, or architectures change.

### Retirement

Securely decommission AI systems and associated data and credentials.

This lifecycle perspective helps prevent security controls from becoming disconnected from AI development and operations.

# AI Security Risk

The council should work closely with enterprise risk and AI governance functions to understand AI security risk.

Examples include:

* Unauthorized access.
* Data exposure.
* Model compromise.
* AI service disruption.
* Malicious manipulation.
* Unauthorized autonomous actions.
* Third-party compromise.
* AI supply-chain attacks.

The council should determine whether security risks are:

* Controlled.
* Accepted by the appropriate authority.
* Remediated.
* Escalated.

The council should not automatically assume authority to accept risks that belong to another formally designated risk owner.

---

# Risk-Based AI Security

Security requirements should be proportional to AI risk.

A simple model may be:

**Low Risk → Standard Security Controls**

**Moderate Risk → Enhanced Security Assessment**

**High Risk → Formal Security Review and Additional Controls**

**Critical Risk → Executive-Level Security Oversight**

This prevents organizations from applying identical security requirements to every AI system regardless of risk.

# AI Security and the CIA Triad

Traditional confidentiality, integrity, and availability principles remain relevant to AI.

### Confidentiality

Protect:

* Prompts.
* Training data.
* Customer data.
* Model parameters.
* Credentials.
* Proprietary AI information.

### Integrity

Protect against:

* Model tampering.
* Data poisoning.
* Unauthorized changes.
* Manipulated prompts.
* Compromised AI components.

### Availability

Protect AI services against:

* Denial-of-service attacks.
* Infrastructure failures.
* Provider outages.
* Resource exhaustion.
* Dependency failures.

AI security should therefore extend traditional cybersecurity principles into AI-specific environments.

# AI Security and Identity

Identity becomes particularly important when AI systems can access enterprise resources.

The council should ensure that AI identities and service accounts are appropriately managed.

Considerations include:

* Unique identities.
* Authentication.
* Authorization.
* Least privilege.
* Credential protection.
* Token management.
* Privileged access.
* Service-account governance.
* Credential rotation.

An AI agent should not receive broad enterprise access simply because it is an approved AI system.

---

# AI Agent Security

AI agents require special attention because they may independently perform actions.

Security controls may include:

* Restricted tool access.
* Action allowlists.
* Transaction limits.
* Human approval.
* Context isolation.
* Session controls.
* Monitoring.
* Logging.
* Emergency shutdown.
* Credential restrictions.

The greater the autonomy, the greater the importance of preventive and detective controls.

# AI Supply Chain Security

AI supply chains may include:

**Data → Code → Libraries → Models → APIs → Infrastructure → Applications → Users**

A compromise at any point can affect the resulting AI capability.

The AI Security Council should therefore promote visibility and security across the AI supply chain.

This may include:

* Software supply-chain security.
* Model provenance.
* Dataset provenance.
* Dependency management.
* Artifact integrity.
* Vendor assessment.
* Secure repositories.

# AI Security Council Authority

The council's authority should be formally defined.

Potential authority may include:

* Establishing AI security requirements.
* Reviewing material AI security risks.
* Recommending security standards.
* Requiring security assessments.
* Reviewing significant security exceptions.

* Recommending suspension of unsafe AI capabilities.

The council should not override legally or formally assigned authorities without an established governance mechanism.

---

# Key Takeaways

The AI Security Council provides a specialized mechanism for enterprise oversight of AI cybersecurity.

Its role is to ensure that AI systems are:

* Securely designed.
* Properly protected.
* Appropriately authorized.
* Continuously monitored.
* Resilient.
* Tested against relevant threats.
* Protected throughout their lifecycle.
* Governed according to risk.

The council should connect **AI strategy, cybersecurity, AI engineering, enterprise architecture, data protection, risk management, and governance** without replacing the operational responsibilities of those functions.

Ultimately, the AI Security Council should ensure that the organization's adoption of AI does not create unmanaged cybersecurity exposure and that security remains an integral component of **AI design, deployment, operation, transformation, and enterprise governance**.
