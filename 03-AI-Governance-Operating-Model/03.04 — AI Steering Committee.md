# 03.04 — AI Steering Committee

## Introduction

An AI Steering Committee is a senior governance body responsible for providing strategic direction, prioritization, coordination, and executive oversight of an organization's AI portfolio and transformation initiatives.

While an AI Governance Committee primarily focuses on governance requirements, risk oversight, controls, and accountability, an AI Steering Committee typically focuses more heavily on **AI strategy, investment, prioritization, business alignment, organizational adoption, and strategic execution**.

The distinction is important because AI governance and AI strategy are closely related but serve different purposes.

AI governance establishes the boundaries within which AI should operate.

AI steering establishes how the organization should prioritize and direct its AI ambitions within those boundaries.

An effective AI Steering Committee helps ensure that AI initiatives are not developed as isolated technology projects. Instead, they are coordinated as an enterprise portfolio aligned with strategic objectives, business value, risk appetite, organizational capabilities, and long-term transformation goals.

## Purpose of the AI Steering Committee

The primary purpose of an AI Steering Committee is to provide strategic leadership and executive coordination for enterprise AI initiatives.

The committee may be responsible for:

* Aligning AI initiatives with business strategy.
* Establishing AI strategic priorities.
* Reviewing the enterprise AI roadmap.
* Prioritizing AI investments.
* Resolving cross-business dependencies.
* Allocating or recommending resources.
* Monitoring strategic AI programs.
* Supporting enterprise AI adoption.
* Reviewing business value and outcomes.
* Coordinating major AI transformation initiatives.
* Ensuring strategic initiatives operate within approved governance boundaries.

The committee should provide direction without taking over the operational responsibilities of AI program teams or the control responsibilities of specialized governance functions.

## AI Steering Committee Versus AI Governance Committee

The two committees may coexist, particularly in larger organizations, but their responsibilities should be clearly differentiated.

### AI Steering Committee

Primarily focuses on:

* Strategy.
* Portfolio.
* Investment.
* Prioritization.
* Business value.
* Transformation.
* Organizational adoption.
* Executive coordination.

### AI Governance Committee

Primarily focuses on:

* Governance requirements.
* Risk.
* Compliance.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Controls.
* Exceptions.
* Governance decisions.
* Assurance.

The relationship can be represented as:

**AI Strategy → AI Steering Committee → Strategic Execution**

and:

**AI Governance → AI Governance Committee → Risk and Control Oversight**

The two governance bodies should coordinate closely but should not unnecessarily duplicate one another.

## Strategic Role

The AI Steering Committee should help translate enterprise strategy into an actionable AI portfolio.

This may involve identifying:

* Strategic AI opportunities.
* Priority business capabilities.
* Transformation opportunities.
* AI investment areas.
* Critical AI dependencies.
* Required organizational capabilities.
* Workforce requirements.
* Technology priorities.

The committee should ensure that AI investments support measurable business objectives rather than simply increasing the number of AI initiatives.

## Enterprise AI Portfolio Oversight

Organizations may have numerous AI initiatives operating simultaneously.

Without centralized strategic oversight, this can result in:

* Duplicate investments.
* Conflicting technology choices.
* Fragmented architectures.
* Competing priorities.
* Inconsistent business objectives.
* Excessive vendor concentration.
* Insufficient resources.
* Unmanaged dependencies.

The AI Steering Committee should maintain visibility across the enterprise AI portfolio.

Portfolio oversight may consider:

* Number of initiatives.
* Business owners.
* Strategic objectives.
* Investment levels.
* Expected benefits.
* Delivery status.
* Dependencies.
* Risks.
* Resource requirements.
* Governance status.
* Business outcomes.

## Strategic Prioritization

Not every AI initiative should receive equal organizational attention or investment.

The committee should establish prioritization criteria.

Potential criteria include:

* Strategic alignment.
* Business value.
* Customer impact.
* Operational efficiency.
* Revenue potential.
* Risk reduction.
* Regulatory requirements.
* Competitive advantage.
* Implementation feasibility.
* Technology readiness.
* Data availability.
* Resource requirements.

A structured prioritization process helps ensure that limited organizational resources are directed toward the most valuable and strategically important AI initiatives.

## AI Investment Oversight

AI initiatives may require significant investment in:

* Infrastructure.
* Cloud services.
* AI platforms.
* Foundation models.
* Data.
* Cybersecurity.
* Talent.
* Integration.
* Training.
* Governance.
* Monitoring.

The AI Steering Committee may review major AI investment proposals to determine whether they support strategic objectives and provide sufficient expected value.

Financial approval authority should remain consistent with the organization's broader financial governance framework.

## Business Value

The committee should ensure that AI initiatives have clearly defined business outcomes.

Potential outcomes may include:

* Revenue growth.
* Cost reduction.
* Productivity improvement.
* Customer experience improvement.
* Risk reduction.
* Operational resilience.
* Faster decision-making.
* Improved service quality.
* New products or services.

AI adoption should not be considered successful merely because a technology has been deployed.

The committee should ask whether the AI initiative actually produces the intended business outcome.

## AI Roadmap Oversight

The AI Steering Committee should oversee the enterprise AI roadmap.

The roadmap may identify:

* Strategic initiatives.
* Major milestones.
* Technology dependencies.
* Data requirements.
* Organizational capabilities.
* Investment phases.
* Target outcomes.
* Governance milestones.
* Deployment priorities.

The roadmap should be reviewed periodically as business priorities, technology capabilities, and external conditions change.

## Strategic Dependencies

AI initiatives frequently depend on capabilities owned by different parts of the organization.

Dependencies may include:

* Data platforms.
* Cloud infrastructure.
* Cybersecurity.
* Identity management.
* Enterprise architecture.
* Legal and compliance.
* Procurement.
* Business process transformation.
* Talent.
* Vendor contracts.

The steering committee can help resolve dependencies that cannot be effectively addressed within an individual project.

## Organizational Alignment

AI transformation frequently affects multiple business functions.

The AI Steering Committee should promote alignment between:

* Executive leadership.
* Business units.
* Technology.
* Cybersecurity.
* Data.
* Risk.
* Legal.
* Compliance.
* Human resources.
* Finance.
* Procurement.

This cross-functional perspective is particularly important when AI initiatives affect enterprise-wide processes.

## AI Adoption and Change Management

AI transformation is not solely a technology initiative.

Successful adoption may require changes to:

* Business processes.
* Organizational roles.
* Workforce skills.
* Operating procedures.
* Performance measures.
* Management practices.
* Employee responsibilities.

The AI Steering Committee should therefore provide strategic oversight of organizational change associated with AI adoption.

This may include:

* Workforce readiness.
* Training.
* Communication.
* Stakeholder engagement.
* Adoption measurement.
* Change resistance.
* Process redesign.

## Relationship With AI Governance

The AI Steering Committee should operate within the organization's AI Governance Framework.

It should not bypass governance requirements in order to accelerate strategic initiatives.

For example, an AI initiative may be strategically important but still require:

* Security assessment.
* Privacy assessment.
* AI risk assessment.
* Regulatory review.
* Model validation.
* Appropriate approval.

Strategic importance does not eliminate governance obligations.

Instead, the steering committee should work with the AI Governance Committee and relevant control functions to ensure that strategic initiatives can proceed within acceptable risk boundaries.

## Relationship With AI Governance Committee

Where both committees exist, their relationship should be explicitly defined.

The AI Steering Committee may:

* Set strategic priorities.
* Prioritize investments.
* Review portfolio performance.
* Resolve business dependencies.
* Sponsor strategic AI initiatives.

The AI Governance Committee may:

* Establish governance requirements.
* Review AI risk.
* Oversee controls.
* Review exceptions.
* Monitor compliance.
* Escalate material governance concerns.

For significant initiatives, the two committees may interact through formal reporting and escalation mechanisms.

## Executive Sponsorship

The AI Steering Committee should have sufficient executive sponsorship to influence enterprise priorities.

Executive sponsorship provides:

* Strategic authority.
* Cross-functional coordination.
* Resource prioritization.
* Organizational visibility.
* Decision-making capability.

Without appropriate executive sponsorship, the committee may struggle to resolve cross-business conflicts or secure resources for strategic initiatives.

## Committee Chair

The chair should have sufficient organizational authority to coordinate strategic AI decisions.

Potential chair roles may include:

* Chief Executive Officer.
* Chief Operating Officer.
* Chief Information Officer.
* Chief Digital Officer.
* Chief Technology Officer.
* Chief AI Officer.

The appropriate role depends on the organization's structure and AI strategy.

The chair should not necessarily be the person with the deepest technical AI expertise.

The role requires the ability to align stakeholders, prioritize enterprise interests, and make or facilitate strategic decisions.

## Committee Membership

Membership should reflect the strategic and cross-functional nature of AI.

Potential permanent members may include representatives from:

* Executive management.
* Business leadership.
* Technology.
* AI or digital transformation.
* Data.
* Cybersecurity.
* Risk.
* Legal.
* Compliance.
* Finance.
* Human resources.
* Operations.

Specialist participants can be invited when specific initiatives require additional expertise.

## Committee Responsibilities

Typical responsibilities may include:

### Strategy

* Review AI strategy.
* Establish strategic priorities.
* Align AI with enterprise objectives.

### Portfolio

* Review AI initiatives.
* Prioritize programs.
* Resolve dependencies.

### Investment

* Review major investments.
* Evaluate expected business value.
* Recommend resource allocation.

### Transformation

* Oversee strategic AI transformation.
* Monitor adoption.
* Support organizational change.

### Performance

* Review AI portfolio performance.
* Monitor benefits realization.
* Assess strategic outcomes.

### Coordination

* Resolve cross-functional issues.
* Coordinate enterprise dependencies.
* Align major initiatives.

### Escalation

* Escalate material strategic issues.
* Refer governance risks to appropriate authorities.
* Escalate decisions beyond delegated authority.

## Decision Rights

The committee should have clearly defined decision rights.

These may include authority to:

* Prioritize AI initiatives.
* Recommend investment decisions.
* Approve strategic AI programs within delegated authority.
* Resolve portfolio conflicts.
* Establish strategic priorities.
* Request changes to AI roadmaps.
* Require business cases.
* Escalate significant strategic risks.

The committee should not assume authority over areas that belong to other governance bodies.

For example, it should not independently override cybersecurity, privacy, legal, or regulatory requirements simply because an AI initiative is strategically important.

## Strategic Governance Principle

The AI Steering Committee should provide **strategic direction and enterprise coordination**, while ensuring that AI initiatives remain subject to appropriate governance and risk controls.

Its central question is:

> **Are we investing in and scaling the right AI capabilities, in the right way, at the right level of risk, to achieve the organization's strategic objectives?**

A mature AI Steering Committee therefore connects **enterprise strategy, AI investment, portfolio prioritization, organizational transformation, business value, and governance alignment** into a coordinated strategic decision-making mechanism.
