# 03.06 — AI Risk Committee

## Introduction

An AI Risk Committee is a specialized governance body responsible for providing enterprise-level oversight of risks arising from the development, deployment, operation, and use of artificial intelligence.

As AI becomes integrated into business processes, products, decision-making, customer interactions, and autonomous workflows, organizations must manage a broader range of risks than traditional technology risk alone.

AI-related risks may include:

* Strategic risk.
* Operational risk.
* Cybersecurity risk.
* Privacy risk.
* Legal and regulatory risk.
* Model risk.
* Data risk.
* Third-party risk.
* Financial risk.
* Reputational risk.
* Ethical and responsible AI risk.
* Human and workforce risk.
* Business continuity risk.

The AI Risk Committee provides a structured mechanism for identifying, evaluating, prioritizing, treating, monitoring, and escalating material AI risks across the enterprise.

Its fundamental purpose is to ensure that **AI-related risk remains visible, owned, measured, and managed according to the organization's risk appetite and governance requirements**.

---

# Purpose of the AI Risk Committee

The primary purpose of the AI Risk Committee is to establish effective oversight of AI risk across the enterprise.

The committee may be responsible for:

* Establishing AI risk governance principles.
* Reviewing material AI risks.
* Monitoring the AI risk profile.
* Reviewing risk assessments.
* Challenging risk treatment decisions.
* Monitoring risk acceptance.
* Reviewing significant AI risk exceptions.
* Escalating material risks.
* Coordinating AI risk management across functions.
* Reviewing emerging AI risks.
* Providing risk reporting to executive leadership.

The committee should focus on **risk oversight and decision-making**, rather than taking over the day-to-day responsibilities of business, technology, security, compliance, or risk teams.

---

# AI Risk as an Enterprise Governance Domain

AI risk should not be treated as a single category of technology risk.

An AI system can simultaneously create multiple risk dimensions.

For example, an AI customer-service system may create:

**Operational Risk**

The system may provide incorrect information.

**Cybersecurity Risk**

Attackers may manipulate prompts or exploit the underlying infrastructure.

**Privacy Risk**

Sensitive customer information may be exposed.

**Legal Risk**

AI-generated decisions or communications may create legal exposure.

**Reputational Risk**

Customers may lose trust in the organization.

**Third-Party Risk**

The organization may depend on an external model provider.

Therefore:

**AI Risk Management = Multiple Risk Domains Applied to AI**

The AI Risk Committee should help ensure that these risk dimensions are considered collectively.

---

# AI Risk Committee Versus AI Governance Committee

The AI Risk Committee and AI Governance Committee may involve many of the same stakeholders, but their primary objectives should remain distinct.

### AI Governance Committee

Focuses on:

* AI governance.
* Enterprise policy.
* Accountability.
* Responsible AI.
* Governance standards.
* Oversight structures.
* Decision mechanisms.

### AI Risk Committee

Focuses on:

* AI risk.
* Risk assessment.
* Risk treatment.
* Risk acceptance.
* Risk appetite.
* Risk escalation.
* Risk monitoring.

The relationship can be represented as:

**AI Governance → Establishes the governance environment**

**AI Risk Committee → Oversees AI risk within that environment**

---

# AI Risk Committee Versus AI Security Council

The AI Risk Committee and AI Security Council should also have clearly differentiated responsibilities.

### AI Risk Committee

Focuses broadly on:

* Enterprise AI risk.
* Operational risk.
* Model risk.
* Strategic risk.
* Compliance risk.
* Third-party risk.
* Reputational risk.
* Risk acceptance.

### AI Security Council

Focuses specifically on:

* AI cybersecurity.
* AI threats.
* Security architecture.
* Security controls.
* Security testing.
* AI security incidents.

Security risk is therefore one component of the broader AI risk landscape.

# Risk-Based Governance Principle

The AI Risk Committee should promote a fundamental principle:

**Higher AI Risk → Greater Governance Oversight**

Not every AI system requires the same level of governance.

A simple model may be:

### Low Risk

Standard governance and monitoring.

### Moderate Risk

Enhanced assessment and management oversight.

### High Risk

Formal risk assessment, enhanced controls, and defined approval authority.

### Critical Risk

Executive-level oversight and potentially board-level involvement.

Risk classification should determine the depth of governance required.

---

# AI Risk Lifecycle

AI risk should be managed throughout the AI lifecycle:

**Identify → Assess → Treat → Approve → Monitor → Review → Escalate → Retire**

### Identify

Identify potential AI risks.

### Assess

Evaluate likelihood, impact, and exposure.

### Treat

Implement controls or other risk responses.

### Approve

Obtain appropriate authorization where required.

### Monitor

Track risk indicators and changes.

### Review

Reassess risks when circumstances change.

### Escalate

Escalate material or deteriorating risks.

### Retire

Ensure risks are appropriately closed when AI systems are retired.

This lifecycle prevents AI risk management from becoming a one-time assessment.

# AI Risk Categories

The AI Risk Committee should maintain visibility across major AI risk categories.

AI initiatives may fail to support organizational strategy.

Examples include:

* Poor investment decisions.
* Misaligned AI initiatives.
* Excessive AI investment.
* Failure to adopt strategically important AI capabilities.
* Competitive disadvantage.

## 2. Operational Risk

Examples include:

* Incorrect outputs.
* System failures.
* Automation errors.
* Inadequate human intervention.
* Poor integration.

## 3. Model Risk

Examples include:

* Poor model performance.
* Model drift.
* Inappropriate assumptions.
* Inaccurate predictions.
* Unexpected behavior.

---

## 4. Data Risk

AI systems may depend on inaccurate, incomplete, manipulated, or inappropriate data.

Examples include:

* Poor data quality.
* Data poisoning.
* Unauthorized data use.
* Data leakage.
* Incomplete datasets.

## 5. Cybersecurity Risk

AI systems may introduce new attack surfaces.

Examples include:

* Prompt injection.
* Model theft.
* Data exfiltration.

* API attacks.

## 6. Privacy Risk

AI systems may improperly process personal or sensitive information.

Examples include:

* Unauthorized processing.
* Data leakage.
* Excessive data collection.

* Cross-border data issues.

## 7. Legal and Regulatory Risk

AI systems may create exposure to applicable laws and regulations.

* Regulatory non-compliance.

## 8. Third-Party Risk

AI systems may depend on external providers.

Examples include:

* Vendor failure.

* Data-processing concerns.
* Provider concentration.
* Contractual limitations.

---

## 9. Reputational Risk

AI failures may damage stakeholder trust.

Examples include:

* Public AI failures.
* Biased outputs.
* Data exposure.
* Unsafe AI behavior.
* Misleading AI-generated content.

---

## 10. Financial Risk

AI initiatives may create financial losses.

Examples include:

* Cost overruns.
* Poor investment returns.
* Unexpected infrastructure costs.
* AI service consumption.
* Regulatory penalties.

# AI Risk Appetite

The AI Risk Committee should operate within the organization's defined risk appetite.

Risk appetite describes the amount and type of risk the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.

AI risk appetite may address:

* Acceptable levels of AI autonomy.
* Acceptable residual risk.
* Sensitive data usage.
* Third-party dependencies.
* Model uncertainty.
* AI service availability.
* Security exposure.
* Regulatory uncertainty.

The committee should help translate enterprise risk appetite into practical AI risk requirements.

---

# AI Risk Ownership

Every material AI risk should have an identifiable owner.

The risk owner should have:

* Authority.
* Accountability.
* Resources.
* Understanding of the risk.
* Responsibility for treatment decisions.

The AI Risk Committee should provide oversight but should not automatically become the owner of every AI risk.

A useful distinction is:

**Committee → Oversees**

**Risk Owner → Manages**

**Control Owner → Implements Controls**

**Executive Authority → Accepts Material Risk Where Required**

---

# AI Risk Assessment

AI risk assessments should evaluate both the probability and potential consequences of AI-related events.

Assessment may consider:

* Business impact.
* Financial impact.
* Customer impact.
* Security impact.
* Privacy impact.
* Regulatory impact.
* Reputational impact.
* Operational impact.
* Strategic impact.

The assessment should consider the **inherent risk** before controls and the **residual risk** after controls.

---

# Inherent and Residual AI Risk

### Inherent Risk

The level of risk that exists before considering controls.

### Residual Risk

The remaining risk after controls are implemented.

The relationship can be expressed as:

**Inherent Risk → Controls → Residual Risk**

The committee should pay particular attention to material residual risks.

A strong control environment does not mean that all AI risk has been eliminated.

---

# AI Risk Treatment

Risk treatment options may include:

### Avoid

Do not implement or discontinue the AI activity.

### Reduce

Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.

### Transfer

Transfer some risk through contracts, insurance, or third-party arrangements where appropriate.

### Accept

Formally accept the residual risk through the appropriate authority.

### Share

Share risk with another party through structured arrangements.

Risk treatment should be proportionate to the organization's risk appetite.

---

# AI Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance should be governed by defined authority levels.

For example:

**Low Risk → Business Management**

**Moderate Risk → Functional Management**

**High Risk → Executive Authority**

**Critical Risk → Executive or Board-Level Authority**

The exact thresholds should be defined by the organization's governance framework.

The AI Risk Committee may review or recommend risk acceptance but should not automatically assume risk acceptance authority.

---

# AI Risk Escalation

Material AI risks should be escalated when they exceed defined thresholds.

Escalation may be triggered by:

* High residual risk.
* Risk appetite breach.
* Significant customer impact.
* Regulatory exposure.
* Major financial impact.
* Material security risk.
* Significant reputational risk.
* Failure of critical controls.
* Rapidly increasing risk.

Escalation should occur before the risk becomes an actual incident whenever possible.

---

# AI Risk Committee's Strategic Objective

The ultimate objective of the AI Risk Committee is not to eliminate every AI risk.

AI adoption inherently involves uncertainty.

The objective is to ensure that AI risks are:

* Identified.
* Understood.
* Owned.
* Assessed.
* Treated.
* Monitored.
* Accepted by the appropriate authority.
* Escalated when necessary.
* Reassessed as circumstances change.

This allows the organization to pursue AI opportunities while maintaining disciplined risk management.

---

# Key Principle

The AI Risk Committee should ensure that the organization does not ask only:

**"Can we deploy this AI system?"**

It should also ask:

**"What could go wrong, how significant would the consequences be, who owns the risk, what controls are required, and who has the authority to accept the remaining risk?"**

This risk-based perspective forms the foundation for effective AI risk governance.

# AI Risk Committee — Part 2

## AI Risk Governance Responsibilities

The AI Risk Committee should translate the organization's AI risk principles into a structured governance process that enables consistent identification, assessment, treatment, monitoring, and escalation of AI-related risks.

The committee should provide enterprise-level challenge and oversight while preserving accountability within the business and functional areas that own the underlying risks.

---

## 1. Establish an AI Risk Governance Framework

The AI Risk Committee should operate within a formally defined AI risk governance framework.

The framework should establish:

* AI risk categories.
* Risk assessment methodology.
* Risk classification.
* Risk appetite.
* Risk treatment requirements.
* Risk acceptance authorities.
* Escalation thresholds.
* Monitoring requirements.
* Reporting requirements.
* Exception management.
* Review frequency.

The framework should integrate with the organization's broader enterprise risk management framework.

---

## 2. Define AI Risk Taxonomy

A standardized AI risk taxonomy enables risks to be consistently classified across the enterprise.

Potential categories include:

* Strategic risk.
* Business risk.
* Operational risk.
* Model risk.
* Data risk.
* Cybersecurity risk.
* Privacy risk.
* Legal risk.
* Regulatory risk.
* Third-party risk.
* Financial risk.
* Reputational risk.
* Human and workforce risk.
* Resilience risk.
* Responsible AI risk.

The taxonomy should provide enough structure for consistent reporting without becoming unnecessarily complex.

---

## 3. Establish AI Risk Assessment Criteria

The organization should establish consistent criteria for evaluating AI risks.

Assessment factors may include:

* Likelihood.
* Impact.
* Exposure.
* Control effectiveness.
* Business criticality.
* Data sensitivity.
* Degree of autonomy.
* User population.
* Regulatory significance.
* Third-party dependency.

The committee should ensure that assessments consider both quantitative and qualitative information where appropriate.

---

## 4. Establish Inherent Risk Assessment

AI systems should first be evaluated based on their inherent risk.

Inherent risk considers the exposure that exists before security, governance, or other mitigating controls are taken into account.

Factors may include:

* Nature of the AI use case.
* Business criticality.
* Data sensitivity.
* Potential impact.
* Degree of automation.
* External exposure.
* Number of affected individuals.

This establishes a baseline for determining the level of controls required.

---

## 5. Establish Residual Risk Assessment

After controls are implemented, residual risk should be assessed.

The assessment should determine whether:

* Controls are appropriately designed.
* Controls are operating effectively.
* Remaining risk is within appetite.
* Additional treatment is required.
* Risk acceptance is necessary.

The committee should focus particularly on material residual risks.

---

## 6. Establish Risk Rating Methodology

The organization should establish a consistent approach for rating AI risks.

A simplified model may use:

**Risk Rating = Likelihood × Impact**

However, more sophisticated models may incorporate:

* Control effectiveness.
* Velocity.
* Exposure.
* Detectability.
* Regulatory sensitivity.
* Business criticality.

The methodology should be consistent with the enterprise risk framework where possible.

---

## 7. Establish AI Risk Tiers

AI systems may be classified into risk tiers.

For example:

### Tier 1 — Low Risk

Limited business impact and low sensitivity.

### Tier 2 — Moderate Risk

Meaningful business impact or moderate data and operational exposure.

### Tier 3 — High Risk

Significant business, customer, regulatory, security, or operational consequences.

### Tier 4 — Critical Risk

Potential for severe enterprise-wide consequences.

The tier should determine the level of governance, assessment, approval, and monitoring required.

---

## 8. Establish Risk-Based Control Requirements

Controls should correspond to the risk level of the AI system.

A low-risk system may require:

* Standard security.
* Basic documentation.
* Basic monitoring.

A high-risk system may require:

* Formal risk assessment.
* Enhanced security testing.
* Independent review.
* Human oversight.
* Enhanced monitoring.
* Formal approval.
* Periodic reassessment.

This prevents both under-governance and unnecessary bureaucracy.

---

## 9. Review AI Risk Assessments

The committee should periodically review material AI risk assessments.

Review should consider:

* Risk classification.
* Risk assumptions.
* Control effectiveness.
* Residual risk.
* Risk treatment.
* Risk acceptance.
* Changes in the AI environment.

The committee should challenge assessments when the evidence does not support the stated risk level.

---

## 10. Establish Risk Challenge

An important responsibility of the committee is independent challenge.

The committee should ask questions such as:

* Is the risk assessment sufficiently supported?
* Are important risks missing?
* Are controls actually effective?
* Is residual risk within appetite?
* Is the risk owner appropriate?
* Is the proposed treatment sufficient?
* Has third-party dependency been considered?
* Has the potential impact been underestimated?

Challenge should improve risk quality rather than simply delay AI initiatives.

---

## 11. Establish AI Risk Treatment Plans

Material risks should have documented treatment plans.

A treatment plan may contain:

* Risk description.
* Risk owner.
* Treatment strategy.
* Required controls.
* Responsible control owners.
* Target completion date.
* Required investment.
* Residual risk target.
* Monitoring requirements.

Treatment plans should be tracked until completion or formal acceptance.

---

## 12. Monitor Risk Treatment Progress

The committee should monitor whether agreed risk treatments are being implemented.

Important indicators may include:

* Open high-risk findings.
* Overdue remediation.
* Control implementation progress.
* Repeated findings.
* Risk aging.
* Risk reduction achieved.

A risk that remains open for a prolonged period should receive increased management attention.

---

## 13. Establish Risk Acceptance Governance

The organization should establish formal criteria for risk acceptance.

A risk acceptance record may include:

* Risk description.
* Business justification.
* Residual risk.
* Risk appetite comparison.
* Compensating controls.
* Risk owner.
* Approval authority.
* Acceptance date.
* Expiration or review date.

Risk acceptance should be an informed management decision rather than an informal acknowledgement.

---

## 14. Establish Risk Acceptance Thresholds

The committee should support clear thresholds defining who can accept different levels of AI risk.

For example:

| Risk Level | Potential Authority                |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Low        | Business or functional management  |
| Moderate   | Senior functional management       |
| High       | Executive risk authority           |
| Critical   | Executive or board-level authority |

Actual authorities should be defined by the organization's enterprise risk framework.

---

## 15. Establish AI Risk Escalation Criteria

The committee should define when AI risks must be escalated.

Potential triggers include:

* Risk exceeds appetite.
* Risk exceeds delegated authority.
* Critical controls fail.
* Risk rapidly increases.
* Risk affects multiple business units.
* Significant regulatory exposure exists.
* Major customer impact is possible.
* Material financial exposure exists.
* Risk cannot be adequately mitigated.

Escalation should be timely and documented.

---

## 16. Establish AI Risk Exceptions

Organizations may occasionally need to operate outside established AI risk requirements.

Exceptions should be:

* Documented.
* Justified.
* Risk assessed.
* Approved.
* Time-bound where appropriate.
* Monitored.

Repeated requests for the same exception may indicate that the underlying governance requirement needs to be reconsidered.

---

## 17. Establish AI Risk Register Governance

The AI Risk Committee should maintain visibility of the enterprise AI risk register.

Important fields may include:

| Field            | Purpose                |
| ---------------- | ---------------------- |
| Risk ID          | Unique identifier      |
| AI System        | Affected system        |
| Risk Category    | Type of risk           |
| Risk Description | What could happen      |
| Risk Owner       | Accountable risk owner |
| Inherent Risk    | Risk before controls   |
| Controls         | Existing mitigation    |
| Residual Risk    | Remaining exposure     |
| Treatment        | Planned response       |
| Due Date         | Target remediation     |
| Status           | Current state          |
| Escalation       | Governance status      |

The register should integrate with enterprise risk management where appropriate.

---

## 18. Establish AI Risk Appetite Monitoring

The committee should monitor whether the organization's AI risk profile remains within approved risk appetite.

Monitoring may include:

* Number of high-risk AI systems.
* Residual risk exposure.
* Risk appetite breaches.
* Number of accepted risks.
* Aging of accepted risks.
* Critical risk concentrations.

The objective is to understand the **aggregate AI risk profile**, not merely individual risks.

* Infrastructure.
* Security control.
* Business process.

A common dependency may create concentration risk.

The committee should identify material concentrations and determine whether mitigation is required.

---

## 20. Monitor Emerging AI Risks

AI technology evolves rapidly, creating new forms of risk.

Emerging risks may include:

* New attack techniques.
* Increasing model autonomy.
* Agentic AI.
* AI-to-AI interaction.
* New regulatory requirements.
* New vendor dependencies.
* Rapidly changing model capabilities.
* Unexpected model behavior.

The committee should maintain mechanisms for identifying and assessing emerging risks before they become material incidents.

---

## 21. Establish AI Risk Horizon Scanning

Horizon scanning should consider developments that may affect the organization's future AI risk profile.

Sources may include:

* Threat intelligence.
* Regulatory developments.
* Technology developments.
* Industry events.
* Internal incidents.
* Audit findings.
* Vendor changes.

The objective is to move from reactive risk management toward anticipatory risk management.

---

## 30. Establish Continuous AI Risk Review

AI risk should be reassessed whenever significant changes occur.

Triggers may include:

* New AI capability.

# AI Risk Governance Operating Principle

The AI Risk Committee should establish a continuous cycle:

**Identify → Assess → Challenge → Treat → Accept → Monitor → Escalate → Reassess**

### Identify

Determine what could go wrong.

### Assess

Determine likelihood, impact, and exposure.

### Challenge

Validate the quality of the risk assessment.

### Treat

Implement appropriate mitigation.

### Accept

Obtain formal acceptance where residual risk remains.

### Monitor

Track changes in risk.

### Escalate

Raise material risks to the appropriate authority.

### Reassess

Update the assessment when circumstances change.

This operating cycle ensures that AI risk remains an active management responsibility rather than a static governance document.

# AI Risk Committee — Part 3

## AI Risk Committee Operating Model

The AI Risk Committee requires a formal operating model that defines how AI risks are brought into governance, evaluated, challenged, treated, accepted, monitored, and escalated.

The committee should operate as a **risk oversight and challenge mechanism**, not as the operational owner of every AI risk. Business and functional owners remain accountable for managing risks within their areas, while the committee provides enterprise-level visibility and governance.

---

## 1. Establish the AI Risk Committee Charter

The committee should operate under a formally approved charter.

The charter should define:

* Purpose.
* Scope.
* Mandate.
* Authority.
* Membership.
* Chair.
* Secretariat.
* Meeting frequency.
* Quorum.
* Decision rights.
* Escalation mechanisms.
* Reporting requirements.
* Record-keeping.
* Review frequency.

The charter should clearly define the relationship between the AI Risk Committee and other governance bodies.

---

## 2. Define Committee Authority

The committee should have sufficient authority to perform effective risk oversight.

Its authority may include:

* Reviewing material AI risks.
* Challenging risk assessments.
* Requiring additional risk analysis.
* Reviewing risk treatment plans.
* Reviewing significant exceptions.
* Recommending risk escalation.
* Monitoring risk appetite.
* Requesting additional assurance.
* Recommending suspension of activities where material risks are not adequately controlled.

The committee should not assume authority that belongs to designated risk owners or executive risk-acceptance authorities.

---

## 3. Establish Committee Membership

Membership should reflect the enterprise-wide nature of AI risk.

Potential members may include:

* Chief Risk Officer or delegate.
* AI governance leader.
* CISO or security representative.
* AI security representative.
* Enterprise architecture.
* AI engineering.
* Data governance.
* Privacy.
* Legal.
* Compliance.
* Internal control.
* Business representatives.
* Third-party risk management.

Membership should be appropriate to the organization's size and risk profile.

---

## 4. Establish the Committee Chair

The chair should have sufficient authority to coordinate enterprise AI risk discussions.

Possible chairs include:

* Chief Risk Officer.
* Chief AI Officer.
* AI Governance Executive.
* Senior Risk Executive.

The chair should ensure that material risks receive appropriate attention and that decisions and escalations are properly documented.

---

## 5. Establish Committee Secretariat

The secretariat should provide administrative and governance support.

Responsibilities may include:

* Preparing agendas.
* Collecting risk submissions.
* Preparing meeting materials.
* Recording decisions.
* Maintaining action registers.
* Maintaining risk registers.
* Tracking remediation.
* Preparing reports.

The secretariat creates continuity across committee meetings.

---

## 6. Establish Meeting Frequency

The committee should meet at a frequency appropriate to the organization's AI risk exposure.

Possible structure:

* Monthly operational risk meetings.
* Quarterly strategic AI risk reviews.
* Ad hoc meetings for material risks.
* Emergency meetings for major AI incidents.

Meeting frequency should increase when the AI portfolio or risk environment changes significantly.

---

## 7. Establish a Standard Committee Agenda

A standard agenda may include:

1. Previous decisions.
2. Open actions.
3. AI risk dashboard.
4. Top AI risks.
5. Risk appetite status.
6. New high-risk AI systems.
7. Risk assessments.
8. Risk treatment progress.

11. Material incidents.
12. Emerging risks.
13. Third-party risks.
14. Decisions required.
15. Escalations.
16. Closing actions.

The agenda should focus on matters requiring risk oversight and decision-making.

---

## 8. Establish Risk Submission Criteria

Not every AI risk needs to be presented to the committee.

Submission criteria may include:

* High residual risk.
* Risk appetite breach.
* Critical business impact.
* Significant regulatory exposure.
* Significant customer impact.
* Material third-party dependency.
* Major security exposure.
* Cross-business-unit impact.
* Risk exceeding delegated authority.

This prevents the committee from becoming overloaded with routine matters.

---

## 9. Establish Risk Decision Papers

Material risk decisions should be supported by structured documentation.

A risk decision paper may contain:

### Risk

What could happen?

### Context

Which AI system or business process is affected?

### Impact

What would be the consequence?

### Likelihood

How likely is the event?

### Existing Controls

What protections currently exist?

### Residual Risk

What risk remains?

### Treatment Options

What options are available?

### Recommendation

What action is proposed?

### Decision Required

What does the committee or delegated authority need to decide?

This structure promotes consistent decision-making.

---

## 10. Establish Risk Challenge Questions

The committee should actively challenge risk submissions.

Typical questions may include:

* Is the risk complete?
* Are assumptions supported by evidence?
* Has the worst credible impact been considered?
* Are dependencies understood?
* Are controls actually effective?
* Has residual risk been calculated correctly?
* Is the risk owner appropriate?
* Is the proposed treatment sufficient?

# AI Risk Committee — Part 4

## Advanced AI Risk Governance and Enterprise Risk Integration

A mature AI Risk Committee must operate beyond the review of individual AI systems. As AI becomes embedded across business processes, products, technology platforms, and decision-making, AI risk must be managed as an **enterprise portfolio of interconnected risks**.

The committee should therefore focus on aggregate exposure, systemic dependencies, emerging risks, risk concentration, resilience, risk appetite, executive decision-making, and continuous improvement.

---

## 1. Govern AI Risk as an Enterprise Portfolio

The organization should maintain an enterprise-wide view of AI risk rather than managing each AI system independently.

The portfolio view should consider:

* Number of AI systems.
* Risk classifications.
* Business criticality.
* Aggregate residual risk.
* Common technology dependencies.
* Common data dependencies.
* Common vendors.
* Shared control weaknesses.
* Geographic exposure.
* Regulatory exposure.

A portfolio perspective can reveal risks that may not be visible within individual AI assessments.

## 2. Identify Systemic AI Risk

Systemic risk occurs when a common weakness can affect multiple AI systems simultaneously.

Examples include:

* A compromised AI platform used by multiple applications.
* A common model provider suffering a major incident.
* A shared data repository becoming compromised.
* A common identity platform being misconfigured.
* A widespread vulnerability affecting AI infrastructure.

The AI Risk Committee should identify such dependencies and determine whether enterprise-wide mitigation is required.

## 3. Govern AI Risk Concentration

AI risk can become concentrated around a small number of providers, platforms, technologies, or capabilities.

Examples include:

**Multiple AI Applications → One Model Provider**

**Multiple Business Units → One AI Platform**

**Multiple AI Systems → One Critical Dataset**

**Multiple Processes → One AI Agent**

Concentration may increase the potential impact of a single failure.

The committee should monitor material concentration and consider diversification or resilience strategies.

Critical AI systems may depend on:

* Foundation models.

# Final Principle

The AI Risk Committee should ensure that **AI adoption and AI risk management develop together**.

Its role is not to eliminate uncertainty or prevent the organization from using AI. Its role is to ensure that AI-related risks are understood, appropriately controlled, consciously accepted where necessary, and continuously monitored.

A mature AI Risk Committee therefore creates a disciplined relationship between:

**AI Opportunity → AI Risk → Risk Treatment → Risk Acceptance → AI Decision**

The fundamental principle is:

> **The organization should not pursue AI merely because it is technically possible, nor reject AI merely because it introduces risk. It should make informed AI decisions based on clearly understood risk, appropriate controls, defined accountability, and authorized risk acceptance.**
