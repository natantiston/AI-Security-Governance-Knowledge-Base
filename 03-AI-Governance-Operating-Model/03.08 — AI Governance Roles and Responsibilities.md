# 03.08 — AI Governance Roles and Responsibilities

## Introduction

AI governance requires clearly defined roles and responsibilities to ensure that accountability is established across the entire AI lifecycle.

AI systems typically involve multiple stakeholders, including business leaders, AI developers, data owners, cybersecurity teams, risk functions, privacy specialists, legal teams, compliance functions, technology teams, and third-party providers. Without clearly defined responsibilities, critical governance activities can be overlooked, duplicated, or assumed to be someone else's responsibility.

AI governance roles and responsibilities establish **who is accountable, who performs activities, who provides oversight, who provides expertise, and who has authority to make decisions**.

The objective is not to create unnecessary organizational complexity. It is to ensure that every material AI governance responsibility has a clearly identified owner.

---

# Purpose of AI Governance Roles and Responsibilities

The purpose of defining AI governance roles is to establish clear accountability for:

* AI strategy.
* AI governance.
* AI risk.
* AI security.
* AI systems.
* AI models.
* AI data.
* AI privacy.
* AI compliance.
* AI development.
* AI deployment.
* AI monitoring.
* AI incidents.
* AI third-party relationships.
* AI risk acceptance.
* AI governance decisions.

A mature operating model should ensure that responsibilities exist from **AI conception through retirement**.

---

# Why Clear Accountability Is Necessary

AI governance often crosses organizational boundaries.

For example, an AI system may involve:

**Business Owner**

who determines why the AI system is needed.

**AI Engineering**

which develops or integrates the system.

**Data Owner**

who governs the data used by the system.

**Cybersecurity**

which manages security risks.

**Privacy**

which assesses personal-data implications.

**Risk**

which evaluates enterprise risk.

**Legal**

which evaluates legal obligations.

**AI Governance**

which coordinates governance requirements.

No single function necessarily owns all aspects of the AI system.

Therefore, AI governance must establish **distributed accountability with clearly defined boundaries**.

---

# Core AI Governance Accountability Model

A useful model separates responsibilities into several categories:

### Strategic Accountability

Determines the organization's AI direction and risk posture.

### Governance Accountability

Defines governance requirements and oversees their implementation.

### Business Accountability

Owns the business purpose and outcomes of AI systems.

### Technical Accountability

Owns the technical design, development, operation, and maintenance.

### Risk Accountability

Identifies, evaluates, and monitors AI-related risk.

### Control Accountability

Owns the operation of specific governance and security controls.

### Assurance Accountability

Provides independent assessment of governance and control effectiveness.

This separation helps prevent conflicts and unclear ownership.

---

# 1. Board of Directors

The Board provides the highest level of organizational oversight for material AI governance and risk.

Responsibilities may include:

* Understanding material AI risks.
* Approving or overseeing AI risk appetite where applicable.
* Providing strategic oversight.
* Challenging management on significant AI risks.
* Reviewing material AI incidents.
* Understanding regulatory and reputational exposure.
* Ensuring management has appropriate AI governance structures.

The Board generally should not manage operational AI activities.

Its role is strategic oversight and accountability.

---

# 2. Executive Management

Executive management translates organizational strategy into AI direction and governance expectations.

Responsibilities may include:

* Approving AI strategy.
* Establishing organizational priorities.
* Allocating resources.
* Supporting governance structures.
* Reviewing material AI risks.
* Making significant risk decisions.
* Ensuring accountability across business functions.

Executive management should ensure that AI adoption remains aligned with organizational objectives and risk appetite.

---

# 3. AI Governance Executive

The AI Governance Executive provides leadership for the enterprise AI governance program.

Depending on organizational structure, this role may be performed by:

* Chief AI Officer.
* Chief Risk Officer.
* Chief Information Officer.
* Chief Digital Officer.
* AI Governance Executive.

Responsibilities may include:

* Establishing AI governance strategy.
* Coordinating governance functions.
* Overseeing AI governance policies.
* Reporting governance status.
* Escalating material issues.
* Coordinating governance committees.
* Supporting executive decision-making.

The role should have sufficient authority to coordinate cross-functional governance.

---

# 4. AI Governance Function

The AI Governance function provides the central coordination mechanism for AI governance.

Responsibilities may include:

* Maintaining the AI governance framework.
* Maintaining AI governance policies.
* Coordinating governance committees.
* Managing the AI governance lifecycle.
* Maintaining governance requirements.
* Coordinating risk classification.
* Monitoring governance compliance.
* Maintaining governance metrics.
* Coordinating reporting.

The AI Governance function should not automatically become the owner of every AI risk.

Its role is primarily **governance coordination, oversight, and enablement**.

---

# 5. AI Governance Committee

The AI Governance Committee provides enterprise-level governance oversight.

Responsibilities may include:

* Reviewing AI governance strategy.
* Approving governance requirements.
* Reviewing material AI risks.
* Reviewing governance performance.
* Resolving cross-functional governance issues.
* Reviewing major AI initiatives.
* Escalating significant matters.

The committee provides collective governance rather than replacing individual accountability.

---

# 6. AI Steering Committee

The AI Steering Committee typically focuses on strategic AI investment, prioritization, and execution.

Responsibilities may include:

* AI portfolio prioritization.
* Investment decisions.
* Strategic AI initiatives.
* Business alignment.
* Resource allocation.
* Major implementation decisions.

The Steering Committee should coordinate with risk and governance bodies where strategic initiatives create material risk.

---

# 7. AI Security Council

The AI Security Council provides specialized oversight of AI cybersecurity.

Responsibilities may include:

* AI security strategy.
* AI threat management.
* AI security architecture.
* AI security standards.
* AI security assessments.
* AI security incidents.
* AI security monitoring.
* AI security exceptions.

The AI Security Council should work closely with cybersecurity leadership and broader AI risk governance.

---

# 8. AI Risk Committee

The AI Risk Committee provides specialized oversight of AI-related enterprise risk.

Responsibilities may include:

* Reviewing material AI risks.
* Challenging risk assessments.
* Monitoring risk appetite.
* Reviewing risk treatment.
* Reviewing risk acceptance.
* Monitoring emerging risks.
* Escalating material risks.

Risk ownership should remain with the appropriate business or functional owner.

---

# 9. Business / AI System Owner

The AI System Owner is accountable for the business use and lifecycle of a specific AI system.

Responsibilities may include:

* Defining the business purpose.
* Ensuring appropriate authorization.
* Ensuring required assessments are completed.
* Ensuring risks are managed.
* Ensuring controls are implemented.
* Monitoring system performance.
* Managing business impact.
* Coordinating system changes.
* Supporting retirement.

The AI System Owner should have sufficient authority and knowledge to manage the system responsibly.

---

# 10. AI Business Owner

The AI Business Owner is accountable for the business outcomes associated with an AI capability.

Responsibilities may include:

* Defining business objectives.
* Approving business use.
* Determining business criticality.
* Managing business risk.
* Ensuring resources are available.
* Monitoring business performance.
* Making business decisions regarding continued use.

The Business Owner may be different from the technical System Owner.

---

# 11. AI Product Owner

Where AI is embedded within a product, the AI Product Owner may be responsible for managing AI capabilities within that product.

Responsibilities may include:

* Product requirements.
* AI feature prioritization.
* User requirements.
* Product risk.
* Product performance.
* Change management.
* Customer impact.

The Product Owner should coordinate with AI governance and risk functions where required.

---

# 12. AI Model Owner

The Model Owner is accountable for the governance and lifecycle of a specific AI model.

Responsibilities may include:

* Model documentation.
* Model performance.
* Model validation.
* Model monitoring.
* Model changes.
* Model retirement.
* Model risk management.

Model ownership may be separate from business ownership.

---

# 13. Data Owner

The Data Owner is accountable for the appropriate governance of data used by AI systems.

Responsibilities may include:

* Data classification.
* Data quality.
* Data access.
* Data usage authorization.
* Data retention.
* Data lineage.
* Data protection requirements.

The Data Owner should ensure that AI use is consistent with applicable data governance requirements.

---

# 14. AI Security Owner

The AI Security Owner is responsible for ensuring that security requirements applicable to an AI system are appropriately addressed.

Responsibilities may include:

* Security architecture.
* Threat assessment.
* Security controls.
* Vulnerability management.
* Security testing.
* Identity and access controls.
* Security monitoring.
* AI security incident coordination.

The AI Security Owner should work with enterprise cybersecurity functions.

---

# 15. AI Risk Owner

The Risk Owner is accountable for managing a specific AI risk.

Responsibilities may include:

* Understanding the risk.
* Assessing the risk.
* Implementing treatment.
* Monitoring residual risk.
* Escalating changes.
* Requesting risk acceptance where required.

Risk ownership should be assigned to a person with sufficient authority to manage the underlying exposure.

---

# 16. Control Owner

The Control Owner is responsible for ensuring that a specific control is properly designed and operating.

Responsibilities may include:

* Control implementation.
* Control operation.
* Control monitoring.
* Evidence collection.
* Control testing.
* Remediation.

A Control Owner may be different from the Risk Owner.

---

# 17. AI Developer / Engineer

AI developers and engineers are responsible for implementing AI systems according to approved requirements.

Responsibilities may include:

* Secure development.
* Model integration.
* Testing.
* Documentation.
* Configuration management.
* Vulnerability remediation.
* Logging.
* Monitoring support.

Developers should not independently approve their own high-risk deployments where segregation of duties is required.

---

# 18. AI Architect

The AI Architect is responsible for ensuring that AI solutions align with approved technical and governance requirements.

Responsibilities may include:

* AI architecture.
* Integration design.
* Security architecture.
* Data flows.
* Technology dependencies.
* Resilience.
* Scalability.
* Technical risk identification.

Architecture should incorporate governance requirements early in the AI lifecycle.

---

# 19. AI Operations / Platform Owner

The AI Operations or Platform Owner manages the operational environment supporting AI capabilities.

Responsibilities may include:

* Infrastructure.
* Platform availability.
* Configuration.
* Monitoring.
* Access management.
* Operational resilience.
* Backup and recovery.
* Incident support.

Operational responsibilities should be clearly separated from risk acceptance authority.

---

# 20. AI Validation / Model Validation Function

An independent validation function may be required for higher-risk AI systems.

Responsibilities may include:

* Model validation.
* Performance assessment.
* Assumption review.
* Testing.
* Bias or performance analysis where applicable.
* Validation findings.
* Independent challenge.

Independence should be proportionate to the risk.

---

# 21. Privacy Function

The Privacy function provides specialist oversight of privacy-related AI risks.

Responsibilities may include:

* Privacy assessments.
* Personal-data requirements.
* Data protection obligations.
* Privacy risk assessment.
* Privacy controls.
* Regulatory interpretation.
* Privacy incident support.

Privacy responsibilities should remain with appropriately qualified specialists.

---

# 22. Legal Function

Legal provides specialist advice regarding legal implications of AI.

Responsibilities may include:

* Contractual requirements.
* Intellectual property.
* Regulatory interpretation.
* Liability.
* Legal restrictions.
* AI provider contracts.
* Dispute considerations.

Legal should be engaged when AI activities create material legal exposure.

---

# 23. Compliance Function

Compliance supports monitoring against applicable regulatory and internal requirements.

Responsibilities may include:

* Regulatory monitoring.
* Compliance assessments.
* Compliance reporting.
* Control monitoring.
* Regulatory change management.
* Escalation of material compliance gaps.

---

# 24. Internal Audit

Internal Audit provides independent assurance over AI governance, risk management, and controls.

Responsibilities may include:

* Independent audits.
* Control effectiveness assessments.
* Governance reviews.
* Risk management assurance.
* Reporting findings.

Internal Audit should maintain appropriate independence from management and operational ownership.

---

# 25. Third-Party AI Provider

External AI providers may have contractual responsibilities relating to:

* Security.
* Availability.
* Data protection.
* Service management.
* Incident notification.
* Compliance.
* Model or service changes.

Third-party responsibilities should be explicitly defined through contracts and governance processes.

---

# 26. Employees and AI Users

Employees and users of AI systems also have governance responsibilities.

These may include:

* Following AI policies.
* Using approved AI systems.
* Protecting sensitive information.
* Reporting incidents.
* Following acceptable-use requirements.
* Completing required training.
* Escalating unexpected AI behavior.

AI governance cannot be effective if users bypass established controls.

---

# 27. Establish Clear Accountability Boundaries

Each role should have defined boundaries.

For example:

**Business Owner**

owns the business outcome.

**System Owner**

owns the system lifecycle.

**Model Owner**

owns model governance.

**Data Owner**

owns data governance.

**Risk Owner**

owns the risk.

**Control Owner**

owns the control.

**AI Governance**

owns governance coordination.

**Security**

owns security expertise and controls.

This separation prevents accountability gaps.

---

# 28. Establish the Three-Lines Perspective

AI governance can be aligned with the Three Lines Model.

### First Line — Management and Operations

Own and manage AI risks.

Examples:

* Business Owners.
* System Owners.
* AI Engineers.
* Product Owners.

### Second Line — Risk and Governance

Provide oversight, challenge, policies, and monitoring.

Examples:

* AI Governance.
* Enterprise Risk.
* Cybersecurity Governance.
* Compliance.
* Privacy.

### Third Line — Internal Audit

Provides independent assurance.

This model reinforces the principle that **risk ownership should remain with management rather than being transferred to the risk or governance function**.

---

# 29. Establish Role Documentation

Every material AI governance role should have documented:

* Role name.
* Purpose.
* Authority.
* Responsibilities.
* Decision rights.
* Required competencies.
* Escalation responsibilities.
* Reporting responsibilities.
* Relationships with other roles.

This documentation creates a formal accountability structure.

---

# 30. Establish the Principle of Explicit Accountability

The most important principle of AI governance roles is:

> **Every material AI governance responsibility should have a clearly identified accountable role, even when multiple functions participate in the activity.**

Participation does not automatically equal accountability.

An AI governance operating model should therefore distinguish between:

**Who performs the work.**

**Who owns the outcome.**

**Who provides oversight.**

**Who provides expertise.**

**Who approves the decision.**

**Who provides independent assurance.**

This distinction forms the foundation for effective AI governance accountability.
