# 19.02 — AI Internal Audit

## Part 1 — AI Internal Audit Foundations, Governance, Independence, Scope and Core Concepts

### 1. Definition of AI Internal Audit

AI Internal Audit is an independent and objective assurance and advisory activity that evaluates whether an organization's AI governance, risk management, and control processes are appropriately designed and operating according to defined criteria.

AI Internal Audit may examine:

* AI governance
* AI risk management
* AI security
* AI privacy
* AI lifecycle controls
* AI model governance
* AI third-party dependencies
* AI regulatory compliance processes
* AI operational resilience
* AI control effectiveness

AI Internal Audit is an organizational assurance function. It should not automatically be treated as a statutory audit unless a specific legal or regulatory requirement establishes such an obligation.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Internal Audit

The purpose of AI Internal Audit is to provide independent evaluation and assurance concerning defined AI governance, risk, and control objectives.

Its purposes may include:

* Evaluating control design
* Testing control operation
* Identifying control weaknesses
* Assessing risk-management processes
* Evaluating governance structures
* Assessing compliance processes
* Identifying recurring weaknesses
* Supporting accountability
* Reporting material issues to appropriate governance bodies

**AI Internal Audit ≠ Management of AI Risk.**

Management owns AI risks and controls; Internal Audit evaluates them independently.

---

### 3. AI Internal Audit vs AI External Audit

AI Internal Audit is performed within or on behalf of the organization's internal assurance function.

External audit is performed by an independent external organization under its own engagement terms and applicable professional requirements.

| Dimension | AI Internal Audit | AI External Audit |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational relationship | Internal assurance function | External provider |
| Primary purpose | Internal assurance and improvement | Defined external assurance objective |
| Scope | Organization-defined | Engagement-defined |
| Reporting | Management / board / audit committee | Intended external users |
| Independence | Organizational independence required | External independence requirements may apply |
| Methodology | Internal audit methodology | Applicable external methodology |

The exact distinction depends on the engagement and applicable professional framework.

---

### 4. AI Internal Audit vs AI Assessment

An AI assessment evaluates a defined condition against specified criteria.

AI Internal Audit is broader when it includes:

* Formal audit planning
* Independent evidence evaluation
* Testing
* Findings
* Audit conclusions
* Reporting
* Follow-up

An assessment can be one input to an internal audit without itself constituting an internal audit.

**Assessment ≠ Internal Audit Automatically.**

---

### 5. AI Internal Audit vs AI Compliance Review

A compliance review may evaluate whether defined legal, regulatory, contractual, policy, or framework requirements are addressed.

AI Internal Audit may evaluate compliance-related controls while also examining:

* Governance
* Risk management
* Control effectiveness
* Operational processes
* Evidence
* Accountability

**Compliance Review ≠ Complete Internal Audit Automatically.**

---

### 6. AI Internal Audit vs AI Security Audit

An AI security audit focuses specifically on security-related criteria and controls.

Potential areas include:

* Identity
* Access control
* Data protection
* Model security
* Application security
* Infrastructure security
* Monitoring
* Incident response
* Supply-chain security

AI Internal Audit may include these areas when they are within the approved audit scope.

**AI Security Audit ⊂ Possible AI Internal Audit Scope.**

---

### 7. AI Internal Audit vs AI Governance Review

An AI governance review evaluates governance arrangements such as:

* Roles
* Accountability
* Policies
* Committees
* Decision rights
* Risk ownership
* Escalation
* Oversight

AI Internal Audit may audit governance controls but should maintain an independent position rather than becoming the governance owner.

---

### 8. AI Internal Audit vs AI Control Testing

Control testing evaluates whether a defined control is designed, implemented, or operating effectively.

AI Internal Audit may perform control testing as part of an audit engagement.

However:

**Control Testing ≠ Internal Audit Automatically.**

A control test may be performed by management, compliance, security, or another assurance function without constituting an internal audit.

---

### 9. AI Internal Audit Scope

Audit scope defines the boundaries of the engagement.

Scope may specify:

* AI systems
* Models
* Business units
* Processes
* Controls
* Suppliers
* Geographic areas
* Regulatory domains
* Audit period
* Technology environments

Scope should be documented before substantive testing begins, subject to appropriate change procedures.

### 11. AI Internal Audit Objective

The audit objective defines what the engagement is intended to determine.

Examples:

* Whether AI governance controls are appropriately designed
* Whether AI risk assessments are performed
* Whether model approvals operate effectively
* Whether AI security controls operate as defined
* Whether required evidence is maintained

The objective should be specific enough to guide audit procedures.

Potential factors include:

* Data sensitivity
* Security exposure
* Regulatory exposure

* Model complexity
* Supplier dependency
* Incident history
* Control maturity
* Rate of change

Risk-based planning should use defined and documented criteria.

### 16. AI Internal Audit Materiality

Materiality determines whether a matter is sufficiently significant to influence audit attention, reporting, or escalation.

AI-related materiality may involve:

* Safety
* Privacy
* Security
* Regulatory exposure
* Operational impact
* Financial impact
* Strategic impact
* Human impact
* Reputational impact

Materiality should be defined according to the organization's audit methodology and engagement context.

---

### 17. AI Internal Audit Independence

Internal Audit should maintain sufficient organizational independence from the activities it audits.

Independence can be supported through:

* Appropriate reporting lines
* Access to the audit committee
* Freedom from operational responsibility
* Authority to determine audit procedures
* Protection from management interference

**Independence ≠ Physical Separation Automatically.**

Organizational design matters.

---

### 18. AI Internal Audit Objectivity

Objectivity requires auditors to evaluate evidence and reach conclusions without inappropriate influence.

Potential threats include:

* Personal interests
* Management pressure
* Prior ownership of the control
* Conflicts of interest
* Incentive structures
* Excessive familiarity with the auditee

Objectivity should be considered throughout the engagement.

---

### 19. AI Internal Auditor Responsibilities

An AI internal auditor may be responsible for:

* Planning audit procedures
* Evaluating risks
* Determining evidence requirements
* Performing testing
* Evaluating exceptions
* Documenting findings
* Forming conclusions
* Reporting results
* Performing follow-up

The auditor should not assume management's responsibility for designing or operating the audited controls.

---

### 20. AI Internal Audit Advisory Role

Internal Audit may provide advisory activities within appropriate boundaries.

Examples include:

* Advising on emerging AI risks
* Reviewing proposed control frameworks
* Providing control-design observations
* Participating in risk workshops
* Advising on assurance planning

Advisory participation should not impair future independence.

**Advisory Support ≠ Management Responsibility.**

---

### 21. AI Internal Audit Management Responsibility

Management remains responsible for:

* AI governance
* Risk decisions
* Control ownership
* Risk acceptance
* Remediation
* Resource allocation
* Business decisions

Internal Audit evaluates and reports on these areas rather than taking ownership of them.

### 29. AI Internal Audit Professional Skepticism

Auditors should consider:

* Missing records

* Management incentives
* Supplier claims

**Management Assertion ≠ Independent Evidence Automatically.**

### 30. AI Internal Audit Audit Risk

Audit risk is the risk that an audit conclusion may be inappropriate because material conditions were not properly identified, evaluated, or reported.

Potential components include:

* Risk inherent in the subject matter
* Risk that controls do not prevent or detect relevant conditions
* Risk that audit procedures fail to identify relevant conditions

The exact terminology and methodology may depend on the applicable audit framework.

### 33. AI Internal Audit Procedure

An audit procedure specifies an action performed to obtain and evaluate evidence.

Examples include:

* Inquiry
* Inspection
* Observation
* Reperformance
* Recalculation
* Confirmation
* Data analytics
* Configuration review

Each procedure should have a defined purpose.

---

### 34. AI Internal Audit Inquiry

Inquiry obtains information from relevant personnel.

Examples include asking:

* Who approves AI deployments?
* How are AI risks assessed?
* Who owns model monitoring?
* How are AI incidents escalated?

Inquiry can provide context but may require corroboration.

---

### 35. AI Internal Audit Inspection

Inspection involves examining records, documents, configurations, systems, or other evidence.

Examples include:

* Inspecting AI policies
* Reviewing model approvals
* Examining access records
* Reviewing security configurations
* Inspecting risk assessments

---

### 36. AI Internal Audit Observation

Observation involves directly observing a process or activity.

Examples include:

* Observing an AI approval workflow
* Observing incident escalation
* Observing model deployment procedures
* Observing control operation

Observation provides evidence about the observed condition but does not automatically prove historical operation.

---

### 37. AI Internal Audit Reperformance

Reperformance independently executes a control or procedure to determine whether the expected result can be reproduced.

Examples include:

* Reperforming a risk calculation
* Reperforming access-review logic
* Reperforming model-performance calculations
* Reexecuting an approval test

---

### 38. AI Internal Audit Recalculation

Recalculation independently verifies mathematical or computational accuracy.

Potential examples include:

* Risk scores
* AI KPI calculations
* Sampling calculations
* Control effectiveness calculations

---

### 39. AI Internal Audit Confirmation

Confirmation obtains evidence directly from an independent or otherwise relevant source.

Examples may include:

* Supplier confirmation
* Control-owner confirmation
* External confirmation

The reliability of confirmation depends on the source and circumstances.

### 42. AI Internal Audit Evidence Relevance

Evidence is relevant when it meaningfully relates to the audit objective or criterion being evaluated.

For example:

A current AI security policy may be relevant to policy governance but may not independently prove that a security control operated effectively throughout the audit period.

### 48. AI Internal Audit Finding

An audit finding identifies a condition that differs from the defined audit criterion or expected state.

A finding should be supported by sufficient appropriate evidence.

### 49. AI Internal Audit Observation

An audit observation may identify an opportunity, weakness, or condition that does not necessarily constitute a formal nonconformity or finding.

The terminology should follow the organization's audit methodology.

**Observation ≠ Finding Automatically.**

### 50. Core AI Internal Audit Principle

AI Internal Audit should provide **independent, objective, risk-based evaluation of defined AI governance, risk-management, and control processes using appropriate criteria, sufficient evidence, professional judgment, and documented audit procedures while preserving management accountability and audit independence**.

The fundamental relationship can be represented as:

**AI Internal Audit ≠ AI Assessment.**

**AI Internal Audit ≠ Management.**

**Evidence Existence ≠ Evidence Sufficiency.**

**Evidence Quantity ≠ Evidence Quality.**

**Specialist Support ≠ Transfer of Audit Accountability.**

**Control Testing ≠ Internal Audit Automatically.**

**Audit Independence ≠ Absolute Organizational Separation.**

**Audit Assurance ≠ Elimination of AI Risk.**
