# 19.04 — ISO/IEC 42001 Audit

## Part 1 — ISO/IEC 42001 Audit Foundations, AIMS, Scope, Criteria and Core Concepts

### 1. Definition of ISO/IEC 42001 Audit

An ISO/IEC 42001 audit is a systematic, independent, and documented process used to obtain and evaluate evidence against defined audit criteria relating to an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS).

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 specifies requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an AIMS. It is a management-system standard rather than a technical security standard for individual AI models. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

An audit therefore examines whether the organization's AIMS is established and operated in accordance with the applicable audit criteria.

---

### 2. Purpose of ISO/IEC 42001 Audit

The purpose of an ISO/IEC 42001 audit is to provide structured evidence about the conformity and effectiveness of the AIMS against the applicable audit criteria.

An audit may examine:

* Organizational context
* Leadership
* AI policy
* AI objectives
* Planning
* AI risk management
* AI impact considerations
* Operational controls
* Performance evaluation
* Internal audit
* Management review
* Continual improvement
* Applicable documented information

The audit should evaluate the management system rather than treating a single AI model or application as the entire audit subject.

---

### 3. ISO/IEC 42001 vs ISO/IEC 27001

ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 are both management-system standards, but they address different management domains.

### 4. ISO/IEC 42001 Audit vs AI Security Audit

An ISO/IEC 42001 audit evaluates an AIMS against applicable ISO/IEC 42001 audit criteria.

An AI Security Audit focuses more specifically on security-related controls, risks, technologies, architectures, and security outcomes.

An ISO/IEC 42001 audit may include security considerations, but it is not automatically a complete technical AI security audit.

```text id="iso42001p1-02"

        +--> Risk Management

        +--> Impacts
        +--> Objectives

        +--> Performance
        +--> Improvement
        |

```

**ISO/IEC 42001 Audit ≠ AI Security Audit.**

### 5. ISO/IEC 42001 Audit vs AI Assessment

An assessment may determine the current state of an AI governance capability against selected criteria.

An audit generally requires a more structured process for obtaining and evaluating evidence against defined audit criteria.

An assessment may be performed for:

* Readiness
* Gap analysis
* Risk evaluation
* Maturity analysis
* Pre-certification preparation

An audit may be performed to determine conformity against formally defined criteria.

**Assessment ≠ Audit.**

**Gap analysis ≠ Certification Audit.**

---

### 6. ISO/IEC 42001 Audit vs Certification Audit

An internal ISO/IEC 42001 audit can be performed by the organization as part of its management-system processes.

A certification audit is performed by an independent certification body operating within the applicable conformity-assessment framework.

The two activities have different purposes and independence requirements.

### 7. ISO/IEC 42001 Audit Criteria

Audit criteria are the requirements, policies, procedures, controls, objectives, or other established criteria against which audit evidence is evaluated.

For an ISO/IEC 42001 audit, the primary criteria are the applicable requirements of ISO/IEC 42001.

Additional criteria may apply depending on the audit objective and scope.

Examples include:

* Organizational policies
* Internal procedures
* Contractual requirements
* Regulatory requirements
* Customer requirements
* Approved AI governance requirements
* Risk-treatment commitments

The auditor should identify which criteria apply before drawing audit conclusions.

### 10. ISO/IEC 42001 as a Management-System Standard

ISO/IEC 42001 is a management-system standard designed to establish structured organizational processes for managing AI.

ISO describes it as a standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an AIMS. It applies to organizations involved in developing, providing, or using AI-based products or services. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### 11. Artificial Intelligence Management System

An AIMS is the organizational system used to establish policies, objectives, processes, and controls relating to the responsible development, provision, or use of AI systems.

The AIMS should not be confused with an individual AI application.

### 12. AIMS Scope

AIMS scope defines the organizational and operational boundaries within which the AI management system applies.

The auditor should determine whether the stated scope is appropriate and whether the evidence examined corresponds to that scope.

### 13. AIMS Scope vs AI Inventory

The AIMS scope and AI inventory serve different purposes.

The AIMS scope defines the boundary of the management system.

The AI inventory identifies AI systems, applications, models, use cases, or other relevant AI assets within the organization's governance environment.

### 14. AIMS Context

### 15. Interested Parties

### 50. Core ISO/IEC 42001 Audit Principle

An ISO/IEC 42001 audit should evaluate the organization's **Artificial Intelligence Management System against defined audit criteria using sufficient and appropriate evidence**, while maintaining a clear distinction between ISO/IEC 42001 conformity, organizational controls, contractual commitments, regulatory obligations, and legal requirements.

The fundamental distinctions are:

**ISO/IEC 42001 ≠ Legislation.**

**ISO/IEC 42001 Requirement ≠ Legal Requirement Automatically.**

**AIMS ≠ Individual AI System.**

**AIMS Scope ≠ AI Inventory.**

**ISO/IEC 42001 Audit ≠ AI Security Audit.**

**ISO/IEC 42001 Audit ≠ AI Assessment.**

**Internal Audit ≠ Certification Audit.**

**Certification Audit ≠ Certification Decision.**

**Audit Criteria ≠ Legal Requirements Automatically.**

**Annex A Control ≠ Automatically Applicable Control.**

**Control Selection ≠ Control Effectiveness.**

**Control Design ≠ Control Implementation.**

**Control Implementation ≠ Operating Effectiveness.**

**Policy Existence ≠ Policy Effectiveness.**

**Risk Register ≠ Risk Management Effectiveness.**

**Documentation ≠ Effective Implementation.**

**Documented Information ≠ Sufficient Audit Evidence Automatically.**

**Evidence Quantity ≠ Evidence Sufficiency.**

**Evidence Sufficiency ≠ Evidence Appropriateness Automatically.**

**Audit Finding ≠ Risk Automatically.**

**ISO Nonconformity ≠ Legal Violation Automatically.**

**Corrective Action Completion ≠ Corrective Action Effectiveness.**

**Audit Conclusion ≠ Absolute Certainty.**

**Internal Control ≠ ISO/IEC 42001 Requirement Automatically.**

**ISO/IEC 42001 Conformity ≠ Legal or Regulatory Compliance Automatically.**

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is an international management-system standard for AI management. Its auditability should therefore be understood through the management-system cycle of establishing, implementing, maintaining, evaluating, and continually improving the AIMS rather than through technical testing of individual AI models alone. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}
