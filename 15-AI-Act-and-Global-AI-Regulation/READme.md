# Chapter 15 — AI Act and Global AI Regulation

## Overview

Chapter 15 provides a comprehensive **AI regulatory governance and compliance knowledge base** covering the European Union AI Act and major AI regulatory developments across the United States, United Kingdom, Singapore, Canada, and China.

The chapter is designed for **AI Governance, GRC, Risk, Compliance, Legal, Privacy, Cybersecurity, Internal Audit, AI Engineering, and executive leadership** teams responsible for understanding and operationalizing AI regulatory requirements.

The chapter distinguishes between:

* **Binding legal requirements**
* **Regulatory requirements and supervisory expectations**
* **Regulatory powers and enforcement mechanisms**
* **Government policies and implementation guidance**
* **Voluntary standards and governance frameworks**
* **Proposed legislation and consultation materials**
* **Internal governance controls**
* **Recommended implementation practices**

This distinction is essential because an internal GRC control designed to address a regulatory risk is not automatically a provision of law.

---

This chapter enables practitioners to:

1. Understand the global AI regulatory landscape.

15. Compare UK, Singapore, Canada, and China AI regulation.
16. Establish global regulatory monitoring and horizon-scanning capabilities.
17. Integrate AI regulation into enterprise GRC.

```text

            ↓

            ↓
Applicability Assessment
            ↓
Regulatory Obligation
            ↓

            ↓

            ↓

            ↓
Control Testing
            ↓
Assurance
            ↓

            ↓
Continuous Monitoring
            ↓

```

This architecture supports traceability between external regulatory requirements and internal AI governance activities.

# Regulatory Status Model

The knowledge base uses the following conceptual hierarchy:

```text
Binding Law
    ↓
Binding Regulation / Regulatory Requirement
    ↓
Regulatory Authority / Supervisory Requirement
    ↓
Official Guidance / Government Policy
    ↓
Proposed Legislation / Consultation
    ↓
Voluntary Framework / Standard
    ↓
Internal Governance Control
    ↓
Recommended Implementation Practice
```

The hierarchy is analytical rather than a universal hierarchy of legal authority. The legal effect of each instrument must always be determined according to the applicable jurisdiction.

# Key Governance Principle

The central principle of Chapter 15 is:

> **AI regulatory compliance must distinguish what the law requires from how the organization chooses to implement and govern that requirement.**

This prevents internal GRC controls, voluntary frameworks, regulatory expectations, draft legislation, and recommended practices from being incorrectly represented as statutory provisions.

**Global AI Regulation → Legal Interpretation → Applicability → Risk → Governance → Controls → Implementation → Evidence → Monitoring → Assurance → Regulatory Change → Continuous Compliance**
