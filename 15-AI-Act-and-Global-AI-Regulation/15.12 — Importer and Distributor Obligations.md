# 15.12 — Importer and Distributor Obligations

## Part 1 — Foundations, Role Determination, and Regulatory Responsibility

### 1. Importer and Distributor Obligations as AI Supply-Chain Controls

Importer and distributor obligations establish an important layer of AI regulatory governance between the provider and the final market or user.

An organization may not develop an AI system and may not operate it as a deployer, but it can still have regulatory responsibilities because it:

* imports an AI system into the European Union;
* makes an AI system available on the EU market;
* distributes an AI system;
* supplies an AI system through commercial channels; or
* participates in the AI supply chain between the provider and the deployer.

Importer and distributor governance therefore extends the AI Act control environment beyond the provider and deployer.

### 2. Importer Definition

Under the EU AI Act, an **importer** is a natural or legal person located or established in the Union that places on the Union market an AI system bearing the name or trademark of a provider established outside the Union.

The concept is particularly relevant when an organization in the EU introduces an AI system supplied by a provider established outside the EU.

The importer should therefore understand:

* who the provider is;
* where the provider is established;
* who places the system on the EU market;
* which entity bears the provider name or trademark;
* whether the organization is acting as importer;
* whether another organization already performs the importer role; and
* what obligations apply to the specific AI system.

### 3. Distributor Definition

A **distributor** is a natural or legal person in the supply chain, other than the provider or importer, that makes an AI system available on the Union market.

The distributor can therefore operate downstream from both the provider and importer.

Examples can include organizations that:

* resell AI-enabled products;
* distribute AI systems through commercial channels;
* supply AI-enabled devices;
* make AI systems available to customers;
* distribute software containing AI functionality; or
* operate as intermediaries in the supply chain.

### 4. Provider, Importer, Distributor and Deployer Roles

The AI supply chain may contain several legally distinct roles.

A simplified model is:

**Provider → Importer → Distributor → Deployer → User**

However, the actual supply chain may contain multiple organizations and overlapping roles.

For example:

**Non-EU Provider → EU Importer → EU Distributor → Enterprise Customer → Deployer**

Each role should be separately assessed.

### 5. Role Determination Is Entity- and System-Specific

An organization should not be classified globally as simply an "importer" or "distributor."

The determination should be performed for each relevant AI system and supply-chain transaction.

The same organization could be:

* an importer for one AI product;
* a distributor for another;
* a deployer for a third; and
* a provider for an internally developed system.

Role determination should therefore be maintained in the AI governance inventory.

### 6. Importer Status and Provider Location

Importer status is particularly relevant when the provider is established outside the European Union.

The organization should establish:

* provider legal entity;
* provider establishment location;
* Union representative where applicable;
* importer identity;
* distribution chain;
* market-placement arrangements; and
* contractual responsibilities.

The provider's physical location, legal establishment and contractual structure should be verified rather than inferred from a product website or sales representative.

### 7. Distributor Status

A distributor should understand whether it is merely making an AI system available on the market or whether its activities create another regulatory role.

Relevant questions include:

* Who is the provider?
* Is an importer involved?
* Has the AI system already been placed on the EU market?
* Has the distributor modified the system?
* Has the distributor changed the system's name or trademark?
* Has the distributor altered its intended purpose?
* Is the distributor acting under its own name?
* Does the distributor become responsible for the system under another provision?

### 8. AI-IMP-01 — Importer Role Determination

Organizations potentially acting as importers should establish a formal importer-role assessment.

The assessment should document:

* AI system;
* provider;
* provider establishment;
* importer entity;
* market-placement activity;
* supply-chain relationship;
* contractual structure;
* applicable obligations;
* responsible owner; and
* approval.

### 9. AI-DIS-01 — Distributor Role Determination

Organizations potentially acting as distributors should establish a corresponding distributor-role assessment.

The assessment should establish:

* system identity;
* provider;
* importer, if applicable;
* supply-chain position;
* distribution activity;
* market;
* intended purpose;
* regulatory classification;
* required verification; and
* responsible owner.

### 10. Supply-Chain Visibility

Importer and distributor compliance requires sufficient visibility into the AI supply chain.

The organization should understand:

* provider;
* manufacturer where relevant;
* importer;
* distributors;
* authorized representatives;
* downstream customers;
* deployers;
* critical subcontractors; and
* relevant technical dependencies.

A lack of supply-chain visibility can make regulatory verification difficult.

### 11. Provider Due Diligence

Importers and distributors should obtain sufficient information about the provider and AI system before making the system available on the Union market.

Due diligence should consider:

* provider identity;
* provider establishment;
* regulatory status;
* AI classification;
* documentation;
* conformity;
* CE marking where applicable;
* registration where applicable;
* instructions;
* contact information;
* known risks;
* corrective actions; and
* incidents.

### 12. AI-IMP-02 — Provider Verification

The importer should verify that the provider has fulfilled applicable obligations before placing the AI system on the Union market.

For high-risk AI systems, the AI Act specifies particular checks for importers, including verification concerning conformity assessment, technical documentation, CE marking, registration and required provider information.

The verification should be documented rather than treated as an informal procurement activity.

### 13. AI-DIS-02 — Distributor Verification

A distributor should perform the checks applicable to its role before making an AI system available on the Union market.

The distributor should verify, where applicable:

* required conformity indications;
* CE marking;
* required documentation;
* instructions;
* provider identification;
* importer identification;
* storage and transport conditions; and
* other applicable regulatory information.

### 14. High-Risk AI Systems

Importer and distributor obligations become particularly important when the AI system is classified as high-risk.

High-risk systems may involve areas such as:

* employment;
* education;
* access to essential services;
* law enforcement;
* migration;
* justice;
* critical infrastructure; and
* biometric applications.

The organization should first establish whether the system is legally high-risk before determining the applicable importer or distributor controls.

### 15. High-Risk Verification Principle

An importer or distributor should not rely solely on a supplier's statement that an AI system is compliant.

Appropriate evidence should be obtained.

Evidence may include:

* EU declaration of conformity;
* technical documentation availability;
* CE marking;
* registration information;
* provider identity;
* instructions for use;
* conformity-assessment evidence;
* quality-management information where appropriate; and
* relevant post-market information.

### 16. AI-IMP-03 — High-Risk AI Verification

Before placing a high-risk AI system on the Union market, the importer should perform the checks required by the AI Act.

The importer should verify, as applicable, that:

* the appropriate conformity assessment has been carried out;
* the provider has prepared the required technical documentation;
* the appropriate CE marking is present;
* the provider has identified the required information;
* the system has been registered where required; and
* the provider has complied with applicable requirements.

### 17. AI-DIS-03 — High-Risk Distribution Verification

Before making a high-risk AI system available on the Union market, the distributor should perform the checks required by its role.

The distributor should verify relevant:

* conformity indicators;
* documentation;
* instructions;
* provider information;
* importer information;
* storage conditions; and
* other required markings or information.

### 18. Do Not Make a Non-Compliant System Available

Importers and distributors should not make an AI system available on the Union market when required compliance conditions are not satisfied.

Where a system appears not to comply, the organization should:

1. suspend market availability;
2. investigate;
3. notify the appropriate party;
4. document the issue;
5. determine whether authorities must be informed; and
6. resume availability only when appropriate conditions have been satisfied.

### 19. AI-IMP-04 — Non-Compliance Blocking

The importer should establish a control capable of preventing market placement when mandatory verification has failed.

Blocking conditions may include:

* missing conformity assessment;
* missing CE marking where required;
* missing documentation;
* incomplete provider identification;
* missing registration where required;
* suspected prohibited use;
* known serious non-compliance; or
* unresolved regulatory concerns.

### 20. AI-DIS-04 — Distribution Blocking

Distributors should have equivalent controls preventing distribution when required compliance conditions are not met.

The control should integrate with:

* order management;
* inventory management;
* product management;
* warehouse systems;
* sales systems; and
* customer fulfillment.

### 21. Importer Identification

Importers have identification responsibilities for relevant AI systems.

The importer should ensure that its identity and required contact information are appropriately associated with the AI system where required.

The organization should maintain evidence demonstrating that the required information was applied correctly.

### 22. Distributor Identification

Distributors should maintain sufficient information to identify:

* provider;
* importer;
* AI system;
* relevant model or product;
* version where available;
* supply-chain transaction;
* customer; and
* date of availability.

This supports traceability and regulatory investigation.

### 23. Traceability Through the Supply Chain

Importer and distributor controls should provide sufficient traceability to determine:

* where the AI system came from;
* who supplied it;
* when it entered the Union market;
* who received it;
* which version was supplied;
* which documentation accompanied it; and
* whether subsequent corrective action affected the system.

### 24. AI-IMP-05 — Supply-Chain Traceability

Importers should maintain records sufficient to trace the relevant AI system through the supply chain.

Records may include:

* purchase orders;
* invoices;
* provider records;
* shipment records;
* system identifiers;
* versions;
* conformity documentation;
* customer information;
* distribution dates; and
* corrective-action records.

### 25. AI-DIS-05 — Distribution Traceability

Distributors should maintain records sufficient to identify the source and recipient of relevant AI systems.

Traceability should support:

* product recall;
* corrective action;
* incident investigation;
* regulatory requests;
* customer notification; and
* market-surveillance activities.

### 26. Documentation Availability

Importers and distributors should understand which documents must accompany or be available for the AI system.

Depending on the applicable system and role, documentation may include:

* instructions for use;
* declaration of conformity;
* technical documentation;
* identification information;
* CE marking;
* registration information; and
* relevant safety or compliance information.

### 27. AI-IMP-06 — Documentation Verification

Importers should verify the availability and consistency of required documentation before market placement.

The verification should identify:

* document;
* source;
* version;
* date;
* applicable AI system;
* completeness;
* discrepancies; and
* verification result.

### 28. AI-DIS-06 — Distributor Documentation Control

Distributors should ensure that required documentation is available to customers and authorities as applicable.

Documentation should remain associated with the correct product and version.

### 29. Instructions for Use

Instructions are particularly important for AI systems that require controlled operation.

The importer and distributor should verify that required instructions are:

* available;
* understandable;
* appropriately localized where necessary;
* associated with the correct system;
* current; and
* supplied through the appropriate channel.

### 30. Storage and Transport Conditions

Where applicable, importers and distributors should ensure that storage and transport conditions do not compromise AI-system compliance.

This is particularly relevant for AI systems incorporated into physical products.

Controls may address:

* environmental conditions;
* hardware integrity;
* packaging;
* tamper protection;
* software integrity;
* configuration;
* firmware;
* secure delivery; and
* chain of custody.

### 31. Software Supply-Chain Integrity

For software-based AI systems, distribution controls should protect against unauthorized modification.

Controls may include:

* cryptographic signatures;
* secure repositories;
* hash verification;
* secure download channels;
* access controls;
* release verification;
* version control; and
* integrity monitoring.

### 32. AI-IMP-07 — Product Integrity Verification

Importers should verify the integrity of the AI system before market placement.

The verification may include:

* version verification;
* package integrity;
* signature validation;
* configuration;
* security controls;
* documentation;
* conformity information; and
* identification.

### 33. AI-DIS-07 — Distribution Integrity Control

Distributors should protect AI systems from unauthorized modification while under their control.

The control should address:

* storage;
* access;
* transmission;
* packaging;
* software distribution;
* configuration; and
* customer delivery.

### 34. Modification of an AI System

Importers and distributors should understand the regulatory implications of modifying an AI system.

A material modification may alter:

* functionality;
* intended purpose;
* risk;
* performance;
* security;
* regulatory classification; or
* the legal role of the organization.

An organization should therefore not assume that a modification is operationally insignificant.

### 35. AI-IMP-08 — Modification Assessment

Before modifying an imported AI system, the organization should assess whether the modification could create additional regulatory responsibilities.

The assessment should consider:

* technical change;
* intended-purpose change;
* provider identity;
* branding;
* conformity;
* risk classification;
* documentation;
* security; and
* downstream obligations.

### 36. AI-DIS-08 — Distributor Modification Control

Distributors should establish controls preventing unauthorized modification of AI systems.

If a distributor modifies an AI system, Legal, Compliance and AI Governance should determine whether the modification changes the organization's regulatory role.

### 37. Own Name or Trademark

Use of an organization's own name or trademark can have significant regulatory implications.

Organizations should carefully assess situations where they:

* rebrand an AI product;
* sell it under a private label;
* place their own trademark on the system;
* represent the AI system as their own product; or
* combine third-party AI functionality into their own product.

Such activities may affect provider responsibilities.

### 38. AI-IMP-09 — Rebranding Assessment

Before rebranding an AI system, the organization should perform a formal regulatory assessment.

The assessment should consider:

* provider role;
* importer role;
* distributor role;
* own-name or trademark implications;
* conformity;
* technical documentation;
* registration;
* liability;
* contracts; and
* post-market responsibilities.

### 39. AI Supply-Chain Contracts

Contracts should clearly allocate relevant responsibilities among:

* provider;
* authorized representative;
* importer;
* distributor;
* integrator;
* deployer; and
* other supply-chain participants.

Contractual allocation does not necessarily eliminate statutory obligations imposed directly by law.

### 40. AI-IMP-10 — Importer Contract Control

Importer agreements should address, as applicable:

* provider representations;
* conformity;
* documentation;
* registration;
* regulatory cooperation;
* incident notification;
* corrective action;
* product withdrawal;
* recall;
* supply continuity;
* audit rights; and
* indemnification.

### 41. AI-DIS-09 — Distributor Contract Control

Distributor agreements should address:

* product compliance;
* required documentation;
* provider and importer information;
* storage;
* transport;
* modification restrictions;
* incident escalation;
* corrective action;
* withdrawal;
* recall; and
* regulatory cooperation.

### 42. Non-EU Provider Governance

When the provider is established outside the Union, importer governance should verify the relationship between the provider, any authorized representative and the EU importer.

The importer should understand:

* who represents the provider in the Union;
* who maintains regulatory documentation;
* who communicates with authorities;
* who handles incidents;
* who supports corrective action; and
* who retains required evidence.

### 43. Authorized Representative Coordination

Where an authorized representative is required or appointed, the importer should maintain appropriate coordination.

The organization should identify:

* representative;
* mandate;
* scope;
* contact information;
* regulatory responsibilities;
* documentation access; and
* escalation procedures.

### 44. Market Surveillance

Importers and distributors form part of the broader market-surveillance ecosystem.

They should be prepared to cooperate with competent authorities and provide relevant information or documentation when legally required.

The European Commission describes market-surveillance authorities as having powers to investigate compliance and take corrective measures, while providers, importers and distributors have defined responsibilities within the AI Act supply chain.

### 45. AI-IMP-11 — Market-Surveillance Cooperation

Importers should establish procedures for responding to market-surveillance requests.

The procedure should define:

* responsible contact;
* evidence sources;
* response authority;
* legal review;
* deadlines;
* submission process;
* evidence retention; and
* follow-up.

### 46. AI-DIS-10 — Distributor Market-Surveillance Cooperation

Distributors should maintain sufficient information to respond to competent-authority requests.

The distributor should be able to identify:

* supplier;
* provider;
* importer;
* product;
* version;
* customers where legally permissible;
* distribution date; and
* relevant documentation.

### 47. Corrective Measures

When an AI system is determined to be non-compliant, the importer or distributor may need to support corrective action.

Corrective measures can include:

* bringing the system into compliance;
* preventing further availability;
* withdrawal;
* recall;
* customer notification;
* replacement;
* technical correction; or
* cooperation with authorities.

### 48. AI-IMP-12 — Corrective-Action Coordination

Importers should maintain a formal process for coordinating provider corrective actions.

The process should track:

* affected systems;
* versions;
* customers;
* issue;
* corrective measure;
* responsible party;
* deadline;
* evidence; and
* closure.

### 49. AI-DIS-11 — Distributor Corrective Action

Distributors should be capable of executing relevant corrective measures.

The process should cover:

* identification of affected inventory;
* suspension of sales;
* customer notification;
* product retrieval;
* replacement;
* provider coordination;
* documentation; and
* regulatory communication.

### 50. Serious Incidents and Safety Concerns

Importers and distributors should have mechanisms for identifying and escalating serious incidents or significant compliance concerns involving AI systems.

Potential triggers include:

* serious harm;
* major security compromise;
* significant non-compliance;
* unsafe behavior;
* regulatory intervention;
* widespread product defect; or
* provider notification.

### 51. AI-IMP-13 — Incident Escalation

Importer incident procedures should establish escalation to:

* provider;
* authorized representative where applicable;
* distributor;
* deployer;
* Legal;
* Compliance;
* Cybersecurity;
* AI Governance; and
* competent authorities where required.

### 52. AI-DIS-12 — Distributor Incident Escalation

Distributors should promptly escalate material AI incidents and compliance concerns through defined supply-chain channels.

The organization should preserve relevant information and avoid uncontrolled modification or destruction of evidence.

### 53. Recall and Withdrawal Readiness

Organizations distributing or importing AI systems should be capable of supporting withdrawal or recall where required.

Readiness should include:

* affected-product identification;
* customer traceability;
* inventory control;
* communication;
* provider coordination;
* technical remediation;
* replacement;
* evidence preservation; and
* regulatory cooperation.

### 54. AI-IMP-14 — Importer Recall Control

The importer should maintain a controlled recall or withdrawal process appropriate to its role.

The process should identify:

* trigger;
* authority;
* affected systems;
* supply-chain partners;
* customers;
* communications;
* remediation;
* evidence; and
* closure criteria.

### 55. Importer and Distributor Compliance Gate

Before an organization imports or distributes a material AI system, it should establish that:

* its regulatory role is documented;
* the provider and supply chain are identified;
* the AI system classification is established;
* required conformity information is verified;
* required documentation is available;
* required identification and markings are verified;
* modifications are controlled;
* storage and distribution conditions are appropriate;
* traceability is established;
* incident escalation is operational;
* corrective-action procedures exist;
* withdrawal and recall procedures are available; and
* regulatory cooperation responsibilities are understood.

The control objective is to ensure that AI systems entering or moving through the Union market are subject to appropriate regulatory verification and supply-chain accountability before they reach downstream users.
