# 15.19 — UK, Singapore, Canada and China AI Regulation — Part 1

## Part 1 — Foundations, Regulatory Models, Scope, and Governance

1. **UK, Singapore, Canada and China AI Regulation — Global Context**
   The United Kingdom, Singapore, Canada, and China represent materially different approaches to AI governance. Their regulatory models should therefore be analyzed separately rather than treated as variations of a single global AI law.

2. **Global Regulatory Model Classification**
   For GRC purposes, each jurisdiction should be classified according to its combination of legislation, regulations, regulator powers, government policy, technical standards, voluntary frameworks, sector-specific rules, and enforcement mechanisms.

3. **Jurisdictional Legal-Status Classification**
   AI governance inventories should distinguish:

   * enacted legislation;
   * binding regulations;
   * regulator rules;
   * government policy;
   * executive or administrative instruments;
   * consultation documents;
   * voluntary frameworks;
   * technical standards;
   * industry codes;
   * proposed legislation; and
   * non-binding guidance.

4. **Regulatory Model — United Kingdom**
   The UK has generally pursued a **principles-based, regulator-led and sectoral approach**, rather than establishing a single comprehensive AI statute equivalent in structure to the EU AI Act. The UK government's 2023 AI regulation white paper proposed a pro-innovation framework based on cross-sectoral principles applied through existing regulators.

5. **UK Regulatory Principle**
   UK AI governance should therefore consider the existing legal and regulatory mandates of individual regulators rather than assuming that a single AI regulator governs all AI activity.

6. **UK Existing-Regulator Model**
   Relevant UK regulatory functions can involve bodies such as:

   * Information Commissioner's Office (ICO);
   * Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC);
   * Competition and Markets Authority (CMA);
   * Financial Conduct Authority (FCA);
   * Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA);
   * Health and Safety Executive (HSE); and
   * other sector-specific regulators.

7. **UK AI Governance Implication**
   Organizations operating in the UK should map each AI use case to the regulator or regulators whose existing statutory responsibilities may apply.

8. **UK Legislation Monitoring**
   UK AI legislation should be monitored separately from government policy and voluntary guidance.

9. **UK AI Regulatory Change**
   The UK regulatory model is evolving, so organizations should maintain a regulatory-change process rather than relying indefinitely on the 2023 white-paper framework.

10. **UK Digital Standards Strategy**
    The UK government published its 2026–2030 Digital Standards Strategy, which identifies AI, cybersecurity, quantum, advanced connectivity, and semiconductors as areas for international standards leadership. This is a government standards strategy rather than a comprehensive AI statute.

11. **UK Standards Governance**
    Organizations should distinguish UK technical standards and standards strategy from legally binding regulatory obligations.

12. **UK AI Regulatory Inventory**
    A UK AI regulatory inventory should identify:

* applicable legislation;
* regulator requirements;
* sector rules;
* privacy requirements;
* equality requirements;
* consumer-protection requirements;
* competition requirements;
* cybersecurity requirements;
* contractual requirements; and
* applicable standards or guidance.

13. **UK AI Scope Assessment**
    Each material AI system should be assessed against its intended use, sector, affected persons, data processing, decision-making function, deployment environment, and relevant regulator.

14. **UK AI Governance Ownership**
    Organizations should assign ownership for UK AI regulatory interpretation and implementation.

15. **UK Privacy and AI Governance**
    AI systems processing personal data should be incorporated into applicable UK data-protection governance.

16. **UK Equality and AI Governance**
    AI systems influencing employment, access to services, or other potentially protected decisions should be assessed against applicable equality requirements.

17. **UK Consumer AI Governance**
    Consumer-facing AI systems should be assessed against applicable consumer-protection requirements.

18. **UK Financial AI Governance**
    AI used by regulated financial organizations should be incorporated into applicable financial-services governance and risk-management processes.

19. **UK Healthcare AI Governance**
    AI used in healthcare or medical contexts should be assessed against applicable health, medical-device, safety, data-protection, and sector-specific requirements.

20. **UK Competition AI Governance**
    Organizations should assess material AI activities for applicable competition and market-conduct requirements.

21. **UK AI Safety Governance**
    AI safety should be integrated into existing organizational safety, risk, security, and regulatory frameworks.

22. **UK AI Assurance**
    Organizations should maintain appropriate assurance over material AI controls.

23. **UK AI Regulatory Evidence**
    Evidence supporting material AI regulatory decisions should be retained.

24. **UK AI Regulatory Change Monitoring**

25. **UK AI Governance Classification Control — AI-UK-01**

## Singapore AI Governance Model

31. **Singapore AI Regulatory Model**
    Singapore has developed a combination of legislation, sectoral regulation, governance frameworks, technical testing initiatives, and government-led responsible-AI guidance.

32. **Singapore Model AI Governance Framework**
    Singapore's Model AI Governance Framework translates AI governance principles into practical organizational recommendations and is intended to support responsible deployment of AI. The framework is a governance resource rather than a generally applicable AI statute.

33. **Singapore Voluntary Framework Classification**
    Organizations should distinguish Singapore's voluntary AI governance frameworks from binding Singapore legislation and regulatory requirements.

34. **Singapore AI Verify**
    Singapore has also developed AI testing and governance capabilities around AI Verify and related initiatives, supporting organizations in evaluating AI governance characteristics.

35. **Singapore Regulatory Philosophy**
    Singapore's model places substantial emphasis on practical governance, testing, accountability, responsible innovation, and interoperability with international AI governance frameworks.

36. **Singapore AI Governance Scope**
    Organizations should assess Singapore AI requirements according to:

* business activity;
* sector;
* AI use case;
* data processing;
* affected persons;
* regulatory status;
* contractual obligations; and
* applicable voluntary frameworks.

37. **Singapore AI Regulatory Inventory**

38. **Singapore Data-Protection Governance**

39. **Singapore Model Governance**

## China AI Regulatory Model

74. **China AI Regulatory Structure**
    China has developed a comparatively detailed administrative and regulatory framework for specific AI activities, including algorithm recommendation services, deep synthesis services, and generative AI services.

75. **China Algorithm Recommendation Regulation**
    China's algorithm recommendation regime establishes requirements for certain algorithm recommendation services, including governance, filing, user rights, information-content management, and related oversight.

76. **China Deep-Synthesis Regulation**
    The Internet Information Service Deep Synthesis Management Provisions establish requirements for certain deep-synthesis services.

77. **China Generative AI Regulation**
    China's Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services establish requirements applicable to specified generative-AI services.

78. **China AI-Generated Content Identification**
    China has established requirements concerning identification and labeling of AI-generated and synthesized content.

79. **China AI Filing Governance**
    Certain algorithmic services with specified public-opinion or social-mobilization characteristics are subject to filing requirements.

80. **China Algorithm Governance**
    Organizations providing covered algorithmic services should establish controls for applicable algorithm filing, modification, cancellation, documentation, and regulatory interaction.

81. **China AI Security Governance**
    AI service providers should incorporate applicable cybersecurity, data-security, content-security, and operational controls.

82. **China AI Data Governance**
    AI training and service data should comply with applicable Chinese data, cybersecurity, personal-information, and intellectual-property requirements.

83. **China AI Content Governance**
    Covered AI services should maintain content-management controls appropriate to applicable Chinese requirements.

84. **China AI Labeling Governance**
    AI-generated or synthesized content should be assessed for applicable explicit and implicit labeling requirements.

85. **China AI Safety Governance**
    Material AI service risks should be monitored and managed.

86. **China AI Incident Governance**
    AI service providers should have mechanisms for identifying and responding to significant security or content risks.

87. **China AI Regulatory Oversight**
    Chinese regulatory authorities can exercise supervisory and enforcement functions over covered AI services.

88. **China AI Regulatory Development — 2026**
    China continued expanding AI-specific administrative requirements in 2026. For example, China's Cyberspace Administration and other authorities issued the Interim Measures for the Management of Anthropomorphic Interactive AI Services, effective 15 July 2026. The measures address areas including sensitive personal information, minors, AI-content identification, algorithm filing, safety assessments, supervision, and penalties.

89. **China AI Labeling Development — 2026**
    China's 2026 AI-generated-content identification rules address explicit and implicit identification of AI-generated or synthesized text, images, audio, video, and virtual-scene information.

90. **China AI Enforcement Monitoring — 2026**
    Chinese authorities also continued enforcement campaigns addressing issues including model filing, AI-platform security, data poisoning, and AI-generated-content labeling. A July 2026 CAC announcement described a first-stage campaign that had addressed more than 14,000 non-compliant AI products, applications, and agents. This is an enforcement activity reported by the Chinese regulator and should be treated as evidence of regulatory activity rather than as a general measure of all Chinese AI compliance.

91. **China AI Regulatory Control — AI-CN-01**
    Every material AI service operating in or targeting the applicable Chinese market should undergo a documented regulatory applicability assessment.

92. **China Algorithm Filing Control — AI-CN-02**
    Covered algorithmic services should maintain required filing and change-filing processes.

93. **China Deep-Synthesis Control — AI-CN-03**
    Covered deep-synthesis services should implement applicable governance, labeling, and safety controls.

94. **China Generative-AI Control — AI-CN-04**
    Covered generative-AI services should maintain controls addressing applicable Chinese requirements.

95. **China AI Content-Control Governance — AI-CN-05**
    Covered AI services should maintain appropriate content-management and risk-control mechanisms.

96. **China AI Labeling Control — AI-CN-06**
    Applicable AI-generated-content labeling requirements should be implemented.

97. **China AI Data-Control Governance — AI-CN-07**
    AI data processing should comply with applicable Chinese data-security and personal-information requirements.

98. **China AI Security Control — AI-CN-08**
    AI systems and services should maintain applicable cybersecurity and safety controls.

99. **China AI Incident Control — AI-CN-09**
    Material AI security or regulatory incidents should be escalated and handled according to applicable Chinese requirements.

100. **China AI Regulatory Evidence Control — AI-CN-10**
     Evidence supporting material Chinese AI regulatory compliance should be retained.

## United Kingdom — Operational Regulatory Controls

111. **UK AI Regulatory Operating Model**
     UK AI compliance should be operationalized through existing legal and sectoral regulatory frameworks together with appropriate AI-specific governance.

112. **AI-UK-07 — UK AI Inventory Control**
     Material AI systems used, developed, supplied, or procured in the UK should be identified within the organization's AI inventory.

113. **AI-UK-08 — UK Applicability Assessment**
     Each material UK AI use case should undergo documented regulatory applicability assessment.

114. **AI-UK-09 — UK Regulator Mapping**
     The organization should identify regulators whose statutory remit may apply to each material AI use case.

115. **AI-UK-10 — UK Regulatory Principle Mapping**
     Where the organization uses the government's cross-sector AI principles as a governance framework, controls should be mapped to:

* safety, security and robustness;
* appropriate transparency and explainability;
* fairness;
* accountability and governance; and
* contestability and redress.

The UK government's framework established these five principles for regulators to interpret within their existing remits. The original framework did not place the principles themselves on a general statutory footing, and the government's implementation guidance described them as voluntary guidance for regulators.

116. **AI-UK-11 — Safety Control**
     Material AI systems should be assessed for safety, security, reliability, robustness, and foreseeable misuse according to applicable regulatory requirements and organizational risk.

117. **AI-UK-12 — AI Security Control**
     AI cybersecurity risks should be incorporated into security risk management.

118. **AI-UK-13 — AI Robustness Control**
     Material AI systems should undergo testing appropriate to their operational criticality.

119. **AI-UK-14 — Transparency Control**
     AI systems should provide transparency appropriate to applicable legal requirements, regulator expectations, use context, and affected persons.

120. **AI-UK-15 — Explainability Control**
     Where decisions require explanation, appropriate mechanisms should exist for explaining AI involvement and relevant decision factors.

121. **AI-UK-16 — Fairness Control**
     Material AI systems should be assessed for relevant fairness and discrimination risks.

122. **AI-UK-17 — Accountability Control**
     Each material AI system should have identifiable accountable business and technical owners.

123. **AI-UK-18 — Contestability Control**
     Where appropriate or legally required, mechanisms should exist for affected persons to challenge AI-supported outcomes.

124. **AI-UK-19 — Redress Control**
     Applicable complaint, correction, appeal, and redress mechanisms should be integrated into AI processes.

125. **AI-UK-20 — Human Oversight Control**
     Material AI decisions should have human oversight appropriate to their impact and applicable regulatory context.

126. **AI-UK-21 — AI Risk Assessment**
     Material AI systems should undergo documented risk assessment.

127. **AI-UK-22 — Lifecycle Risk Management**
     AI risks should be assessed across design, development, procurement, testing, deployment, operation, modification, and retirement.

The UK regulator guidance encourages consideration of safety-risk assessment, mitigation, lifecycle cybersecurity resilience, transparency, explainability, fairness, accountability, and contestability within regulators' respective remits.
