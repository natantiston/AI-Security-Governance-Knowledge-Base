# 15.15 — Post-Market Monitoring and Incident Reporting

## Part 1 — Foundations, Regulatory Purpose, Scope, and Monitoring Governance

1. **Post-Market Monitoring and Incident Reporting as AI Governance Controls**
   Post-market monitoring and incident reporting are core AI governance controls for identifying whether an AI system continues to operate safely, securely, lawfully, and in accordance with its intended purpose after deployment or placing on the market. The controls provide the feedback mechanism between real-world operation and the organization's risk, compliance, security, quality, and regulatory-management processes.

2. **Regulatory Purpose of Post-Market Monitoring**
   Post-market monitoring is designed to identify information arising during the operational lifetime of an AI system that may indicate emerging risks, non-compliance, performance deterioration, misuse, security weaknesses, or the need for corrective measures.

3. **Regulatory Purpose of Incident Reporting**
   Incident reporting provides a controlled mechanism for identifying, assessing, documenting, escalating, and, where legally required, reporting serious incidents and other relevant events to competent authorities.

4. **Post-Market Monitoring Versus Incident Management**
   Post-market monitoring is a continuing surveillance activity, whereas incident management is generally event-driven. An effective governance framework connects both processes so that monitoring can identify incidents and incidents can generate additional monitoring requirements.

5. **Post-Market Monitoring Versus Post-Deployment Monitoring**
   Post-deployment monitoring is a broader operational concept that may include performance, security, availability, user behavior, and service-quality monitoring. Post-market monitoring is specifically concerned with information relevant to regulatory compliance and the continuing safety and conformity of AI systems placed on the market or put into service.

6. **AI Lifecycle Relationship**
   Post-market monitoring begins after deployment or market placement but should be designed during earlier lifecycle stages. Monitoring requirements should therefore be established during requirements engineering, risk management, validation, conformity assessment, deployment planning, and operational readiness.

7. **Provider Responsibility**
   For AI systems subject to provider obligations, the provider should establish and document an appropriate post-market monitoring system proportionate to the nature of the AI system and its risks.

8. **Deployer Responsibility**
   Deployers should monitor operational use and report relevant information to providers and appropriate authorities where required. Deployers should also maintain internal processes for identifying incidents, misuse, unexpected outcomes, and material deviations from intended use.

9. **Importer and Distributor Interface**
   Importers and distributors can become important sources of post-market information because they may receive complaints, safety information, product information, or evidence of non-compliance from customers, downstream organizations, or market-surveillance authorities.

10. **GPAI and System-Level Monitoring Distinction**
    General-purpose AI model providers have model-level obligations that may interact with downstream system-level monitoring. Organizations integrating GPAI models should distinguish monitoring of the underlying model from monitoring of the complete AI system and its particular intended purpose.

11. **AI-PMM-01 — Post-Market Monitoring Applicability Determination**
    The organization should determine which post-market monitoring requirements apply to each AI system based on its regulatory classification, role, intended purpose, deployment environment, and applicable legal obligations.

12. **AI-PMM-02 — Monitoring Scope Definition**
    The organization should formally define the scope of post-market monitoring for each applicable AI system.

13. **AI-PMM-03 — Monitoring Responsibility Assignment**
    Every monitored AI system should have clearly assigned responsibility for monitoring execution, analysis, escalation, reporting, and remediation.

14. **AI-PMM-04 — Monitoring Governance Ownership**
    A designated control owner should be accountable for ensuring that post-market monitoring requirements remain implemented and effective.

15. **AI-PMM-05 — Monitoring RACI**
    The organization should maintain a RACI or equivalent responsibility model covering monitoring operations, incident detection, technical investigation, legal assessment, regulatory reporting, communications, remediation, and closure.

16. **AI-PMM-06 — Monitoring Governance Integration**
    Post-market monitoring should be integrated with AI governance, enterprise risk management, information security, privacy, quality management, business continuity, legal, compliance, and incident-management processes.

17. **Post-Market Monitoring System**
    The monitoring system should establish procedures for collecting and analyzing relevant information throughout the AI system's operational lifecycle.

18. **Monitoring Inputs**
    Potential monitoring inputs include:

* system logs;
* performance metrics;
* validation results;
* user complaints;
* customer feedback;
* operational incidents;
* security alerts;
* vulnerability reports;
* privacy incidents;
* human-oversight events;
* model-performance degradation;
* accuracy degradation;
* bias or discrimination indicators;
* unexpected outputs;
* misuse reports;
* provider notices;
* supplier notifications;
* regulatory communications;
* market-surveillance information; and
* information from other deployed instances of the same or related system.

19. **AI-PMM-07 — Monitoring Input Inventory**
    The organization should identify and document the information sources required to support effective post-market monitoring.

20. **AI-PMM-08 — Monitoring Input Ownership**
    Each material monitoring input should have an accountable owner responsible for availability, quality, and escalation.

21. **AI-PMM-09 — Monitoring Input Integrity**
    Monitoring information should be protected against unauthorized alteration, deletion, manipulation, or loss.

22. **AI-PMM-10 — Monitoring Input Availability**
    Critical monitoring information should remain available for the period necessary to support operational, regulatory, investigative, and assurance requirements.

23. **AI-PMM-11 — Monitoring Data Quality**
    Monitoring data should be sufficiently accurate, complete, timely, relevant, and reliable to support meaningful risk and compliance decisions.

24. **AI-PMM-12 — Monitoring Data Reconciliation**
    Where multiple monitoring systems contain overlapping information, material records should be reconciled to identify discrepancies.

25. **AI-PMM-13 — Monitoring Evidence Traceability**
    Material monitoring observations should be traceable to their source, timestamp, AI system, model version, deployment environment, and relevant operational context.

26. **Intended-Purpose Monitoring**
    Post-market monitoring should evaluate whether the AI system continues to operate within its documented intended purpose and authorized deployment context.

27. **AI-PMM-14 — Intended-Purpose Deviation Detection**
    The organization should establish mechanisms for identifying material deviations between approved intended use and actual operational use.

28. **AI-PMM-15 — Unauthorized Use Monitoring**
    Where appropriate, the organization should monitor for unauthorized uses that could materially change the AI system's risk profile.

29. **AI-PMM-16 — Use-Case Change Escalation**
    Material changes in use cases should be escalated for risk, compliance, and regulatory assessment.

30. **AI-PMM-17 — Intended-Purpose Reassessment Trigger**
    A material change in intended purpose should trigger an assessment of whether existing risk assessments, technical documentation, conformity assessments, transparency controls, and post-market monitoring arrangements remain appropriate.

31. **Performance Monitoring**
    Operational performance should be monitored against defined expectations and relevant regulatory, safety, quality, or contractual requirements.

32. **AI-PMM-18 — Performance Baseline**
    The organization should establish an approved performance baseline for material AI systems.

33. **AI-PMM-19 — Performance Thresholds**
    Monitoring should use defined thresholds or decision criteria for identifying material performance deterioration.

34. **AI-PMM-20 — Performance Degradation Detection**
    Material deterioration from approved performance levels should generate an investigation or escalation according to risk.

35. **AI-PMM-21 — Accuracy Monitoring**
    Where accuracy is material to the AI system's purpose, accuracy should be monitored using appropriate operational measures.

36. **AI-PMM-22 — Accuracy Drift Detection**
    Material degradation in accuracy should be detected and evaluated for potential regulatory, safety, business, or human-rights impact.

37. **AI-PMM-23 — Robustness Monitoring**
    The organization should monitor material failures associated with unusual inputs, environmental conditions, adversarial conditions, or operational stress.

38. **AI-PMM-24 — Resilience Monitoring**
    Operational resilience indicators should be monitored where disruption of the AI system could create material harm or regulatory exposure.

39. **AI-PMM-25 — Reliability Monitoring**
    Material reliability failures should be captured and analyzed for systemic patterns.

40. **AI-PMM-26 — Availability Monitoring**
    Availability should be monitored where service interruption could materially affect safety, compliance, or critical decision-making.

41. **AI-PMM-27 — Output Quality Monitoring**
    Where AI outputs influence material processes or decisions, output quality should be monitored using appropriate validation or sampling mechanisms.

42. **AI-PMM-28 — Unexpected Output Monitoring**
    Unexpected, anomalous, unsafe, misleading, or materially inappropriate outputs should be captured and investigated where relevant.

43. **AI-PMM-29 — Output Distribution Monitoring**
    Material changes in the distribution or characteristics of AI outputs should be monitored for potential model drift, data changes, misuse, or environmental changes.

44. **AI-PMM-30 — Human Override Monitoring**
    Human overrides of AI outputs should be monitored where override frequency or patterns can indicate performance problems, automation bias, inadequate human oversight, or inappropriate system use.

45. **AI-PMM-31 — Human Escalation Monitoring**
    Escalations from AI users or human overseers should be monitored for recurring problems and emerging risks.

46. **AI-PMM-32 — Automation Bias Monitoring**
    Where AI recommendations influence human decision-making, the organization should monitor indicators suggesting excessive reliance on AI outputs.

47. **AI-PMM-33 — Human Oversight Failure Monitoring**
    Failures or ineffective operation of required human-oversight mechanisms should be captured as material monitoring events where applicable.

48. **Security Monitoring Interface**
    Post-market monitoring should integrate with cybersecurity monitoring to identify vulnerabilities, attacks, compromises, adversarial manipulation, unauthorized access, data poisoning, model theft, prompt-related attacks, and other security events affecting AI systems.

49. **AI-PMM-34 — Cybersecurity Event Integration**
    Security events affecting monitored AI systems should be integrated into the post-market monitoring and incident-assessment workflow.

50. **AI-PMM-35 — Vulnerability Monitoring**
    Material vulnerabilities affecting models, AI software, dependencies, infrastructure, interfaces, or supporting services should be monitored.

51. **AI-PMM-36 — Vulnerability Impact Assessment**
    Material vulnerabilities should be assessed for potential effects on AI-system safety, security, regulatory compliance, and continuing conformity.

52. **AI-PMM-37 — Security Incident Escalation**
    Security incidents that may constitute serious AI incidents or materially affect regulatory compliance should be escalated for regulatory assessment.

53. **AI-PMM-38 — Cybersecurity Remediation Tracking**
    Remediation of AI-related cybersecurity issues should be tracked through controlled corrective-action processes.

54. **AI-PMM-39 — Security Monitoring Evidence**
    Security monitoring records relevant to post-market compliance should be retained and associated with the affected AI system and applicable version or configuration.

55. **AI-PMM-40 — Security Monitoring Reassessment Trigger**
    Material cybersecurity events should be evaluated as potential triggers for risk reassessment, conformity reassessment, technical-documentation updates, or changes to monitoring requirements.

# Part 2 — Post-Market Monitoring and Incident Reporting Controls, Implementation Requirements, and Operational Management

56. **Post-Market Monitoring Control Framework**
    The organization should establish a documented control framework for collecting, evaluating, escalating, reporting, and remediating information arising from the operation of AI systems after deployment or market placement.

57. **AI-PMM-41 — Post-Market Monitoring Policy Control**
    A formal policy should define the organization's post-market monitoring objectives, scope, responsibilities, escalation requirements, evidence expectations, and regulatory interfaces.

58. **AI-PMM-42 — Post-Market Monitoring Procedure Control**
    Operational procedures should define how monitoring data is collected, analyzed, investigated, escalated, reported, and closed.

59. **AI-PMM-43 — System Monitoring Profile**
    Each applicable AI system should have a monitoring profile specifying relevant indicators, thresholds, data sources, review frequency, escalation criteria, and accountable personnel.

60. **AI-PMM-44 — Risk-Based Monitoring Frequency**
    Monitoring frequency should be proportionate to the AI system's regulatory classification, risk level, intended purpose, deployment scale, affected population, and potential impact.

61. **AI-PMM-45 — High-Risk Monitoring Profile**
    High-risk AI systems should have enhanced monitoring arrangements addressing their specific operational, regulatory, safety, security, and fundamental-rights risks.

62. **AI-PMM-46 — Monitoring Requirement Traceability**
    Each material monitoring activity should be traceable to an applicable regulatory requirement, risk, control objective, technical requirement, or approved operational need.

63. **AI-PMM-47 — Monitoring Control Mapping**
    Monitoring controls should be mapped to the relevant AI system, risk, regulatory obligation, process, and evidence repository.

64. **AI-PMM-48 — Monitoring Coverage Assessment**
    The organization should periodically assess whether monitoring provides adequate coverage of material AI risks.

65. **AI-PMM-49 — Monitoring Coverage Gap Management**
    Identified monitoring gaps should be documented, risk-assessed, assigned to owners, and remediated according to defined priorities.

66. **AI-PMM-50 — Monitoring Exception Management**
    Temporary inability to perform required monitoring should be recorded as a controlled exception with appropriate compensating measures.

67. **AI-PMM-51 — Monitoring Control Testing**
    Material monitoring controls should be periodically tested to verify that they operate as designed.

68. **AI-PMM-52 — Monitoring Control Failure Escalation**
    Failure of a material monitoring control should generate an appropriate compliance, risk, security, or operational escalation.

69. **AI-PMM-53 — Monitoring Control Remediation**
    Monitoring-control deficiencies should be tracked through corrective-action processes.

70. **AI-PMM-54 — Monitoring Control Evidence**
    Evidence should demonstrate that required monitoring activities were performed, reviewed, and acted upon where necessary.

## Post-Market Monitoring Plan

71. **Post-Market Monitoring Plan**
    For applicable AI systems, the organization should maintain a documented post-market monitoring plan appropriate to the system and its risk profile.

72. **AI-PMM-55 — Monitoring Plan Identification**
    Each monitoring plan should identify the AI system, provider or deployer role, intended purpose, applicable requirements, responsible parties, monitoring period, and relevant versions.

73. **AI-PMM-56 — Monitoring Objective Definition**
    Monitoring objectives should specify what risks, failures, changes, or regulatory conditions the organization intends to detect.

74. **AI-PMM-57 — Monitoring Indicator Definition**
    The plan should identify measurable indicators relevant to system performance, safety, security, compliance, and operational behavior.

75. **AI-PMM-58 — Monitoring Threshold Definition**
    Thresholds should be established for indicators where predefined escalation criteria are appropriate.

76. **AI-PMM-59 — Monitoring Alert Definition**
    Alerts should be defined for conditions requiring investigation, escalation, corrective action, or regulatory assessment.

77. **AI-PMM-60 — Monitoring Review Frequency**
    The monitoring plan should specify the frequency of automated monitoring, human review, trend analysis, and formal reassessment.

78. **AI-PMM-61 — Monitoring Responsibility Matrix**
    The plan should identify responsibility for data collection, analysis, investigation, decision-making, escalation, reporting, remediation, and closure.

79. **AI-PMM-62 — Monitoring Escalation Matrix**
    Escalation paths should define when operational personnel must involve security, privacy, legal, compliance, safety, quality, risk management, senior management, or regulatory affairs.

80. **AI-PMM-63 — Monitoring Communication Plan**
    The organization should establish communication procedures for material monitoring events and incidents.

81. **AI-PMM-64 — Monitoring Plan Approval**
    Monitoring plans should be approved by appropriate accountable personnel before becoming operational.

82. **AI-PMM-65 — Monitoring Plan Version Control**
    Changes to monitoring plans should be version-controlled and traceable.

83. **AI-PMM-66 — Monitoring Plan Review**
    Monitoring plans should be periodically reviewed and updated based on operational experience, incidents, changes, regulatory developments, and risk assessments.

84. **AI-PMM-67 — Monitoring Plan Change Trigger**
    Material changes to the AI system or its environment should trigger an assessment of whether the monitoring plan requires modification.

## Operational Monitoring

85. **Operational Monitoring Architecture**
    Monitoring should collect relevant information from the AI application, model, data pipelines, infrastructure, interfaces, users, security systems, and supporting services.

86. **AI-PMM-68 — Application Monitoring**
    The organization should monitor application-level events relevant to the AI system's approved operation.

87. **AI-PMM-69 — Model Monitoring**
    Model behavior should be monitored for performance deterioration, unexpected behavior, drift, anomalies, or other material changes.

88. **AI-PMM-70 — Data Pipeline Monitoring**
    Material data-processing pipelines should be monitored for failures, unexpected changes, quality issues, and integrity problems.

89. **AI-PMM-71 — Input Monitoring**
    Where appropriate, material changes in AI inputs should be monitored for unusual patterns, quality degradation, or unauthorized use.

90. **AI-PMM-72 — Output Monitoring**
    AI outputs should be monitored where output behavior can create material safety, regulatory, operational, or rights-related risks.

91. **AI-PMM-73 — Infrastructure Monitoring**
    Supporting infrastructure should be monitored where infrastructure conditions can affect AI-system operation or compliance.

92. **AI-PMM-74 — API and Interface Monitoring**
    Material APIs and interfaces should be monitored for availability, integrity, abnormal behavior, and unauthorized use.

93. **AI-PMM-75 — Dependency Monitoring**
    Critical software, model, cloud, data, and service dependencies should be monitored for changes or failures that may affect the AI system.

94. **AI-PMM-76 — Third-Party Model Monitoring**
    Third-party models used within the AI system should be monitored for provider updates, behavior changes, service changes, vulnerabilities, and other material developments.

95. **AI-PMM-77 — External Service Monitoring**
    External services supporting AI functionality should be monitored for changes affecting performance, security, availability, or regulatory compliance.

96. **AI-PMM-78 — Environment Monitoring**
    Changes in the operational environment that could affect system performance or risk should be monitored.

97. **AI-PMM-79 — User Behavior Monitoring**
    Where appropriate and proportionate, user behavior should be monitored for misuse, unsafe operation, unauthorized use, or indications of inadequate human oversight.

98. **AI-PMM-80 — Privileged Activity Monitoring**
    Privileged activities affecting monitored AI systems should be logged and monitored for unauthorized or anomalous behavior.

99. **AI-PMM-81 — Configuration Monitoring**
    Material configuration changes should be detected and evaluated for impact on the monitored system.

100. **AI-PMM-82 — Model Version Monitoring**
     Changes in deployed model versions should be automatically or procedurally detected.

101. **AI-PMM-83 — Software Version Monitoring**
     Changes to software components supporting the AI system should be monitored.

102. **AI-PMM-84 — Configuration Baseline Monitoring**
     Production configurations should be compared against approved baselines.

103. **AI-PMM-85 — Baseline Deviation Alerting**
     Material deviations from approved configurations should generate an investigation or escalation.

104. **AI-PMM-86 — Deployment Reconciliation**
     The deployed AI system should be reconciled against the approved model, software, configuration, infrastructure, and documentation baseline.

105. **AI-PMM-87 — Unauthorized Change Detection**
     Unauthorized material changes should generate an incident or compliance event.

107. **AI-PMM-89 — Performance Anomaly Detection**
     Material deviations from normal operating patterns should be detected.

108. **AI-PMM-90 — Performance Threshold Escalation**
     Threshold breaches should generate defined investigation and escalation actions.

109. **AI-PMM-91 — Accuracy Sampling**
     Where accuracy is material, representative samples should be evaluated periodically.

110. **AI-PMM-92 — Accuracy Validation**
     Operational accuracy measurements should be validated against appropriate ground truth, reference datasets, expert review, or other reliable methods.

111. **AI-PMM-93 — Accuracy Degradation Investigation**
     Material accuracy degradation should be investigated to determine root causes and potential consequences.

112. **AI-PMM-94 — Model Drift Detection**
     The organization should monitor for relevant changes in model behavior or statistical characteristics over time.

113. **AI-PMM-95 — Data Drift Detection**
     Relevant changes in input-data distributions should be monitored.

114. **AI-PMM-96 — Concept Drift Assessment**

115. **AI-PMM-97 — Drift Thresholds**
     The organization should define thresholds for determining when observed drift requires investigation or action.

116. **AI-PMM-98 — Drift Investigation**

118. **AI-PMM-100 — Drift Reassessment Trigger**

119. **AI-PMM-101 — Bias Monitoring**

120. **AI-PMM-102 — Bias Investigation**

121. **AI-PMM-103 — Bias Remediation Tracking**
     Approved remediation measures should be tracked and validated.

122. **AI-PMM-104 — Fairness Monitoring Evidence**

## Safety and Fundamental-Rights Monitoring

     AI systems that may affect health, safety, fundamental rights, access to services, employment, education, or other high-impact areas should receive monitoring appropriate to their potential consequences.

124. **AI-PMM-105 — Safety Event Monitoring**
     Safety-related events associated with an AI system should be captured and assessed.

125. **AI-PMM-106 — Harm Indicator Monitoring**
     Relevant indicators of actual or potential harm should be monitored where appropriate.

126. **AI-PMM-107 — Fundamental-Rights Impact Monitoring**
     For systems capable of materially affecting fundamental rights, relevant indicators and complaints should be monitored.

127. **AI-PMM-108 — Discrimination Event Monitoring**
     Material allegations or indicators of discriminatory outcomes should be captured for assessment.

128. **AI-PMM-109 — Human Oversight Monitoring**
     The organization should monitor whether required human-oversight mechanisms are functioning effectively.

129. **AI-PMM-110 — Override Effectiveness Monitoring**
     Human overrides should be evaluated to determine whether they effectively mitigate inappropriate AI outputs.

130. **AI-PMM-111 — Human Intervention Monitoring**
     The frequency, timing, and outcomes of human interventions should be monitored where these are relevant to safe operation.

131. **AI-PMM-112 — Automation Bias Indicators**
     Where appropriate, indicators of excessive human reliance on AI outputs should be monitored.

132. **AI-PMM-113 — User Competence Monitoring**
     Where material to safe operation, organizations should monitor whether users remain appropriately trained and authorized.

133. **AI-PMM-114 — Training Deficiency Escalation**
     Material deficiencies in user competence should be escalated and remediated.

134. **AI-PMM-115 — Human-Oversight Failure Escalation**
     Material failures of human oversight should be escalated and assessed for incident and regulatory-reporting implications.

135. **AI-PMM-116 — Fundamental-Rights Incident Escalation**
     Potentially material fundamental-rights impacts should be escalated for appropriate legal, compliance, risk, and governance assessment.
