# 15.14 — Conformity Assessment

## Part 1 — Foundations, Regulatory Purpose, Scope, and Conformity Governance

1. **Conformity Assessment as an AI Governance Control**
   Conformity assessment is the structured process used to determine and demonstrate whether an AI system satisfies applicable regulatory requirements before it is placed on the market or put into service, where such assessment is required.

2. **Purpose of Conformity Assessment**
   The purpose is not merely to produce a certificate. It is to establish objective evidence that applicable requirements have been identified, implemented, evaluated, and satisfied.

3. **EU AI Act Context**
   Under the EU AI Act, conformity assessment is particularly important for regulated high-risk AI systems and forms part of the broader compliance architecture involving risk management, technical documentation, quality management, logging, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, and post-market monitoring.

4. **Conformity Assessment Versus General Assurance**
   Conformity assessment is a regulatory compliance process and should be distinguished from internal assurance, penetration testing, quality assurance, model validation, internal audit, certification against voluntary standards, and general risk assessment.

5. **Conformity Assessment Versus Certification**
   A conformity assessment may involve internal assessment or an assessment by a notified body depending on the applicable regulatory pathway. It should not automatically be equated with third-party certification.

6. **Legal Requirement Versus Recommended Practice**
   The organization should distinguish mandatory conformity-assessment obligations from recommended assurance activities. Internal controls may exceed the minimum legal requirements without being represented as legally mandatory.

7. **Applicability Determination**
   The first governance question is whether the specific AI system is subject to a conformity-assessment requirement and, if so, which assessment procedure applies.

8. **AI-CA-01 — Conformity Applicability Assessment**
   The organization should document whether conformity assessment applies to each regulated AI system and identify the legal provisions supporting that determination.

9. **System-Specific Assessment**
   Conformity obligations should be assessed for the specific AI system rather than inferred solely from the organization's general AI classification.

10. **Intended Purpose and Conformity**
    The intended purpose of the AI system is central to determining which regulatory requirements apply and whether the system conforms to them.

11. **AI-CA-02 — Intended-Purpose Conformity Mapping**
    The organization should map the intended purpose, users, deployment context, outputs, and regulated use cases to applicable conformity requirements.

12. **Risk Classification and Conformity**
    The AI system's regulatory risk classification should be established before determining the applicable conformity-assessment pathway.

13. **AI-CA-03 — Risk Classification Verification**
    The organization should verify the regulatory classification used as the basis for conformity assessment and retain the underlying rationale.

14. **High-Risk AI and Conformity Assessment**
    High-risk AI systems are subject to specific conformity requirements under the EU AI Act, subject to the applicable classification pathway, exemptions, transition arrangements, and implementing requirements.

15. **Annex I High-Risk Systems**
    AI systems that are safety components of products or themselves products covered by specified Union harmonisation legislation may follow a conformity pathway connected to the relevant product regulatory framework.

16. **Annex III High-Risk Systems**
    AI systems falling within specified areas in Annex III may be subject to high-risk obligations and the corresponding conformity-assessment framework.

17. **AI-CA-04 — Annex Applicability Verification**
    The organization should document whether the AI system falls within an applicable Annex I or Annex III category and identify the resulting conformity requirements.

18. **Conformity Assessment Procedure Selection**
    The organization should determine the appropriate conformity-assessment procedure based on the applicable legal framework and characteristics of the AI system.

19. **AI-CA-05 — Assessment Procedure Determination**
    A documented decision should identify the applicable conformity procedure, assessment authority where relevant, required evidence, and approval conditions.

20. **Internal Conformity Assessment**
    Where the applicable legal pathway permits internal assessment, the organization remains responsible for demonstrating that the required conditions have been satisfied.

21. **Third-Party Conformity Assessment**
    Where the applicable regulatory framework requires involvement of a notified body or another authorized third party, the organization should establish the appropriate engagement and evidence process.

22. **AI-CA-06 — Notified Body Applicability Assessment**
    The organization should determine whether third-party conformity assessment is legally required and document the basis for that determination.

23. **Notified Body Governance**
    Where a notified body is required, the organization should verify that the selected body has the appropriate designation and scope for the relevant conformity-assessment activity.

24. **AI-CA-07 — Notified Body Scope Verification**
    The organization should verify the notified body's applicable scope before relying on its conformity-assessment activities.

25. **Assessment Independence**
    Where independent assessment is required or adopted as a governance practice, assessors should have appropriate independence from the activities being assessed.

26. **Assessment Competence**
    Conformity assessment should be performed by personnel or organizations with appropriate competence in AI technology, applicable regulation, quality management, cybersecurity, data governance, and the relevant technical domain.

27. **AI-CA-08 — Assessor Competence Control**
    The organization should establish qualification and competence criteria for personnel performing conformity-assessment activities.

28. **Conformity Assessment Scope**
    The assessment scope should clearly identify the AI system, model or models, software, interfaces, datasets where relevant, deployment environment, intended purpose, applicable versions, and organizational responsibilities.

29. **AI-CA-09 — Assessment Scope Definition**
    The assessment scope should be formally documented and approved before substantive conformity activities begin.

30. **Conformity Assessment Boundary**
    The assessment boundary should include relevant components that materially influence the regulated characteristics of the AI system.

31. **Third-Party Components**
    External models, APIs, datasets, software libraries, cloud services, and other dependencies should be considered where they affect conformity.

32. **AI-CA-10 — Dependency Inclusion Control**
    Material dependencies should be evaluated to determine whether their characteristics affect the conformity assessment of the overall AI system.

33. **System Version Identification**
    Conformity assessment must be associated with a clearly identified system version.

34. **AI-CA-11 — Assessed-Version Identification**
    The organization should record the model, software, configuration, data, and deployment versions relevant to the assessed system.

35. **Configuration Baseline**
    The assessed configuration should be established as a controlled baseline.

36. **AI-CA-12 — Conformity Configuration Baseline**
    The organization should preserve the configuration baseline used during conformity assessment.

37. **Change After Assessment**
    Material changes following assessment may affect the continuing validity of the conformity determination.

38. **AI-CA-13 — Post-Assessment Change Evaluation**
    Material changes should trigger an assessment of whether conformity must be reassessed.

39. **Significant Modification**
    Changes that materially affect an AI system's characteristics, intended purpose, compliance profile, or risk may require additional conformity activity depending on the applicable regulatory framework.

40. **AI-CA-14 — Significant Modification Assessment**
    The organization should establish a controlled process for determining whether proposed changes constitute a significant modification or otherwise affect conformity obligations.

41. **Conformity Requirements Register**
    Applicable conformity requirements should be maintained in a controlled requirements register.

42. **AI-CA-15 — Conformity Requirements Register**
    The register should identify each applicable requirement, interpretation, evidence requirement, responsible owner, status, and supporting documentation.

43. **Regulatory Requirement Mapping**
    Each applicable requirement should be mapped to one or more controls, technical characteristics, procedures, or evidence sources.

44. **AI-CA-16 — Requirement-to-Control Traceability**
    The organization should maintain traceability from applicable legal requirements through controls and implementation evidence to assessment conclusions.

45. **Conformity Evidence**
    Evidence should demonstrate not only that a control exists but that the relevant requirement has been implemented and evaluated.

46. **AI-CA-17 — Evidence Sufficiency Assessment**
    The organization should evaluate whether evidence is sufficient, relevant, reliable, current, and directly applicable to the assessed system.

47. **Evidence Quality**
    Evidence should be objective and should preferably originate from authoritative technical, operational, governance, or testing systems.

48. **AI-CA-18 — Evidence Integrity Control**
    Material conformity evidence should be protected against unauthorized alteration and should remain traceable to its source.

49. **Evidence Versioning**
    Evidence should identify the system version and assessment period to which it relates.

50. **AI-CA-19 — Evidence Version Association**
    The organization should prevent evidence from one system version from being incorrectly used to demonstrate conformity of another version.

51. **Technical Documentation Relationship**
    Technical documentation provides a major evidence base for conformity assessment.

52. **AI-CA-20 — Technical Documentation Conformity Mapping**
    The organization should map applicable conformity requirements to relevant sections of the technical documentation.

53. **Risk Management Relationship**
    Risk-management evidence should demonstrate that relevant AI risks have been identified, evaluated, treated, and monitored.

54. **AI-CA-21 — Risk Evidence Integration**
    Risk-management records should be linked to conformity-assessment evidence where they support applicable requirements.

55. **Quality Management Relationship**
    For applicable high-risk AI systems, quality-management processes form an important part of the broader conformity framework.

56. **AI-CA-22 — Quality Management Evidence**
    The organization should maintain evidence demonstrating implementation of applicable quality-management processes.

57. **Data Governance Relationship**
    Data governance may provide evidence supporting data-quality, relevance, representativeness, provenance, processing, and other applicable requirements.

58. **AI-CA-23 — Data Evidence Integration**
    Relevant data-governance records should be linked to conformity evidence.

59. **Cybersecurity Relationship**
    Cybersecurity controls and testing may provide evidence supporting applicable AI cybersecurity requirements.

60. **AI-CA-24 — Cybersecurity Conformity Evidence**
    Security architecture, threat assessments, vulnerability management, penetration testing, adversarial testing, access controls, and incident controls should be evaluated for relevance to conformity.

61. **Human Oversight Relationship**
    Human-oversight arrangements should be assessed against applicable requirements and documented sufficiently to demonstrate operational implementation.

62. **AI-CA-25 — Human Oversight Evidence**
    The organization should maintain evidence showing who performs oversight, what authority they have, what information they receive, and how intervention or escalation operates.

63. **Accuracy and Performance Evidence**
    Performance evidence should demonstrate compliance with applicable accuracy and performance requirements under relevant operating conditions.

64. **AI-CA-26 — Performance Conformity Evidence**
    Performance evidence should identify datasets, test methodology, system version, metrics, acceptance criteria, results, limitations, and exceptions.

65. **Robustness Evidence**
    Robustness evidence should address expected operating conditions and relevant failure modes.

66. **AI-CA-27 — Robustness Evidence Control**
    The organization should maintain evidence demonstrating that relevant robustness requirements have been evaluated.

67. **Resilience Evidence**
    Where applicable, resilience testing should demonstrate that the system remains appropriately controlled under foreseeable disruptions and failures.

68. **AI-CA-28 — Resilience Conformity Evidence**
    Material resilience evidence should be associated with the assessed system version and applicable requirements.

69. **Logging Evidence**
    Where logging is required, conformity evidence should demonstrate that relevant events can be recorded and retained as required.

70. **AI-CA-29 — Logging Conformity Verification**
    The assessment should verify the existence, completeness, integrity, retention, and accessibility of required logs.

71. **Transparency Evidence**
    Applicable transparency requirements should be incorporated into the conformity assessment.

72. **AI-CA-30 — Transparency Conformity Verification**
    The organization should verify that required information, instructions, notices, disclosures, and other transparency mechanisms are appropriately implemented.

73. **Instructions for Use**
    Instructions should be reviewed where they form part of the applicable regulatory compliance framework.

74. **AI-CA-31 — Instructions Conformity Review**
    The organization should verify that applicable instructions accurately describe intended use, limitations, risks, human oversight, and other required information.

75. **Fundamental Rights Considerations**
    Where applicable, conformity activities should consider requirements relating to fundamental rights and associated assessments.

76. **AI-CA-32 — Fundamental Rights Evidence Integration**
    Relevant fundamental-rights assessments should be linked to the conformity evidence package.

77. **Privacy Relationship**
    Privacy compliance may provide supporting evidence but should not automatically be treated as proof of conformity with every AI Act requirement.

78. **AI-CA-33 — Privacy Conformity Evidence Mapping**
    Privacy assessments should be mapped to applicable conformity requirements where they provide relevant evidence.

79. **Safety Relationship**
    For AI systems connected with regulated products or safety functions, safety evidence should be integrated with the applicable conformity framework.

80. **AI-CA-34 — Safety Evidence Integration**
    Safety analyses, testing, risk assessments, controls, and validation records should be linked to conformity evidence where applicable.

## Conformity Assessment Planning

81. **Conformity Assessment Plan**
    A formal assessment plan should define how conformity will be evaluated.

82. **AI-CA-35 — Assessment Planning Control**
    The assessment plan should define:

* scope;
* applicable requirements;
* assessment methodology;
* responsibilities;
* evidence requirements;
* testing;
* review activities;
* acceptance criteria;
* findings management;
* approval requirements; and
* final decision process.

83. **Assessment Schedule**
    The assessment schedule should align with the AI system's development and deployment lifecycle.

84. **AI-CA-36 — Assessment Scheduling Control**
    Assessment activities should be planned early enough to prevent regulatory compliance from becoming a final-stage documentation exercise.

85. **Assessment Readiness**
    Before assessment begins, the organization should verify that the system and supporting evidence are sufficiently mature.

86. **AI-CA-37 — Assessment Readiness Gate**
    The readiness gate should evaluate technical documentation, risk management, testing, quality controls, cybersecurity, human oversight, and other applicable evidence.

87. **Assessment Criteria**
    Assessment criteria should be explicitly defined before evaluation begins.

88. **AI-CA-38 — Assessment Criteria Control**
    Criteria should identify the requirement being assessed, evidence expected, evaluation method, acceptance threshold, and responsible assessor.

89. **Assessment Methodology**
    Assessment methodology should be appropriate to the system's complexity, risk, regulatory pathway, and applicable requirements.

90. **AI-CA-39 — Assessment Methodology Approval**
    The selected assessment methodology should be reviewed and approved by the appropriate governance authority.

91. **Assessment Sampling**
    Where sampling is appropriate, the sample should be risk-based and representative of the relevant system characteristics.

92. **AI-CA-40 — Conformity Sampling Control**
    Sampling decisions should be documented, including the rationale, population, sample size, selection method, and limitations.

93. **Testing as Assessment Evidence**
    Testing may provide evidence for conformity but should be performed against defined requirements and acceptance criteria.

94. **AI-CA-41 — Test-to-Requirement Mapping**
    Tests used for conformity should be traceable to the requirements they support.

95. **Independent Validation**
    Where appropriate, validation should be performed independently of the development activity.

96. **AI-CA-42 — Independent Validation Control**
    The organization should define when independent validation is required based on system risk and regulatory significance.

97. **Assessment Findings**
    Nonconformities, evidence gaps, weaknesses, ambiguities, and other findings should be formally recorded.

98. **AI-CA-43 — Conformity Finding Register**
    The finding register should identify the requirement, condition, evidence, severity, owner, corrective action, deadline, and closure status.

99. **Major Nonconformity**
    A material failure to satisfy an applicable requirement should prevent a conformity conclusion until appropriately resolved or otherwise addressed under the applicable legal procedure.

100. **AI-CA-44 — Material Nonconformity Control**
     Material nonconformities should be escalated to the appropriate compliance and management authorities.

101. **Minor Findings**
     Less significant findings should nevertheless be tracked and evaluated for potential cumulative impact.

102. **AI-CA-45 — Minor Finding Management**
     Minor findings should have defined owners and remediation expectations.

103. **Corrective Action**
     Corrective actions should address the underlying cause of conformity failures.

104. **AI-CA-46 — Conformity Corrective Action Control**
     Corrective actions should identify root cause, remediation, responsible owner, deadline, evidence, and validation method.

105. **Corrective Action Validation**
     A corrective action should not be considered effective solely because the related document or configuration has been changed.

106. **AI-CA-47 — Corrective Action Effectiveness Verification**
     Material corrective actions should be tested or independently reviewed to confirm that the underlying conformity issue has been resolved.

107. **Repeat Findings**
     Repeated conformity findings may indicate systemic deficiencies in the AI governance or quality-management framework.

108. **AI-CA-48 — Repeat Finding Analysis**
     Recurring findings should trigger root-cause analysis and consideration of broader corrective measures.

109. **Conformity Decision**
     The conformity decision should be based on documented evidence against applicable criteria.

110. **AI-CA-49 — Conformity Decision Control**
     The final decision should identify the assessed system, version, scope, applicable requirements, evidence, findings, decision authority, date, and limitations.

111. **Conformity Decision Independence**
     Where independence is required or appropriate, the final conformity decision should not be controlled solely by the development team.

112. **AI-CA-50 — Decision Authority Control**
     The organization should define who is authorized to approve the conformity outcome.

113. **Conformity Documentation Package**
     The completed assessment should produce a controlled evidence package.

114. **AI-CA-51 — Assessment Package Control**
     The package should contain or reference the relevant assessment plan, requirements, evidence, findings, corrective actions, approvals, and final decision.

115. **Declaration of Conformity**
     Where required, the organization should prepare the applicable EU declaration of conformity or equivalent regulatory declaration.

116. **AI-CA-52 — Declaration Preparation Control**
     The declaration should be based on the applicable legal requirements and verified against the final assessed system and evidence package.

117. **Declaration Accuracy**
     The declaration should not contain claims broader than the assessment supports.

118. **AI-CA-53 — Declaration Accuracy Verification**
     Legal, compliance, technical, and other appropriate functions should review material conformity declarations before issuance.

119. **Declaration Version Control**
     The declaration should identify the relevant system and remain aligned with the applicable conformity state.

120. **AI-CA-54 — Declaration Version Association**
     The organization should maintain traceability between the declaration and the assessed system version.

## CE Marking and Registration Governance

121. **CE Marking Relationship**
     Where the EU AI Act requires CE marking for the relevant AI system, CE marking forms part of the regulatory conformity framework.

122. **AI-CA-55 — CE Marking Applicability**
     The organization should determine whether CE marking requirements apply and document the basis.

123. **CE Marking Accuracy**
     CE marking should not be applied before the applicable conformity requirements have been satisfied.

124. **AI-CA-56 — CE Marking Authorization Control**
     The organization should establish an approval control governing when CE marking may be applied.

125. **Notified Body Identification**
     Where a notified body is involved and applicable law requires identification, the relevant identification information should be correctly represented.

126. **AI-CA-57 — Notified Body Identification Control**
     The organization should verify the accuracy of notified-body information associated with conformity documentation and markings.

127. **Registration Requirements**
     Where registration in an applicable EU database is required, registration should be completed through the appropriate regulatory process.

128. **AI-CA-58 — Registration Readiness Control**
     The organization should verify that required conformity information is complete before registration.

129. **Registration Data Consistency**
     Registration information should remain consistent with the technical documentation and conformity records.

130. **AI-CA-59 — Registration Consistency Control**
     Material discrepancies between registration information and technical documentation should be investigated and corrected.

131. **Market Placement Gate**
     Where conformity assessment is a prerequisite to lawful market placement or putting into service, the organization should prevent release until the required conditions have been satisfied.

132. **AI-CA-60 — Market Placement Conformity Gate**
     The release process should verify conformity status before the regulated AI system is made available or put into service.

133. **Deployment Gate**
     Internal deployment should be aligned with the applicable regulatory status.

134. **AI-CA-61 — Deployment Conformity Gate**
     Production deployment should require verification that all mandatory conformity activities applicable to that deployment have been completed.

135. **Procurement Gate**
     Organizations procuring regulated AI from third parties should determine how conformity responsibility is allocated between provider, importer, distributor, and deployer.

136. **AI-CA-62 — Procurement Conformity Verification**
     Procurement should verify applicable conformity evidence before approving acquisition of regulated AI systems.

137. **Supplier Conformity Evidence**
     Supplier-provided declarations, assessments, certifications, or reports should be reviewed for applicability to the actual system being acquired.

138. **AI-CA-63 — Supplier Conformity Evidence Validation**
     The organization should validate supplier conformity evidence against the supplier, system identity, intended purpose, version, and applicable regulatory requirements.

139. **Contractual Conformity Obligations**
     Contracts should establish responsibilities for maintaining conformity information and notifying the organization of material changes.

140. **AI-CA-64 — Contractual Conformity Control**
     Material supplier agreements should address conformity evidence, changes, incidents, regulatory cooperation, documentation, and audit rights as appropriate.

## Conformity Assessment Governance

141. **Conformity Governance Framework**
     Conformity assessment should be integrated into the organization's AI governance operating model.

142. **AI-CA-65 — Conformity Governance Policy**
     The organization should establish a policy defining applicability, responsibilities, procedures, evidence, approvals, exceptions, and monitoring.

143. **Governance Ownership**
     A designated function should own the conformity-assessment framework while technical and business functions remain accountable for their respective evidence and controls.

144. **AI-CA-66 — Conformity Framework Ownership**
     The organization should formally assign ownership for the conformity-assessment framework.

145. **Business Accountability**
     Business owners remain responsible for ensuring that the AI system's intended purpose and operating context are accurately represented.

146. **Technical Accountability**
     Technical owners remain responsible for providing accurate evidence about system architecture, models, data, performance, dependencies, and configuration.

147. **Compliance Accountability**
     Compliance functions should provide oversight of regulatory applicability and conformity obligations.

148. **Legal Accountability**
     Legal functions should support interpretation of applicable legal requirements and material regulatory questions.

149. **Security Accountability**
     Security functions should provide appropriate cybersecurity evidence and assurance relevant to conformity.

150. **Privacy Accountability**
     Privacy functions should contribute relevant privacy evidence where it supports applicable conformity requirements.

151. **Quality Accountability**
     Quality functions should support quality-management and assurance requirements where applicable.

152. **AI-CA-67 — Conformity RACI**
     A formal RACI should identify responsibility and accountability across business, engineering, security, privacy, quality, legal, compliance, procurement, and assurance functions.

153. **Three Lines Model**
     Conformity governance should align with the Three Lines Model.

154. **AI-CA-68 — First-Line Conformity Responsibility**
     First-line functions should implement and maintain the technical and operational controls supporting conformity.

155. **AI-CA-69 — Second-Line Conformity Oversight**
     Second-line functions should challenge conformity applicability, evidence sufficiency, risk treatment, and compliance status.

156. **AI-CA-70 — Third-Line Conformity Assurance**
     Internal Audit should independently assess conformity governance and controls when included within its risk-based audit scope.

157. **Segregation of Duties**
     Material conformity decisions should incorporate appropriate separation between system development, assessment, approval, and assurance.

158. **AI-CA-71 — Conformity Segregation of Duties**
     The organization should identify and enforce segregation requirements for material conformity activities.

159. **Conformity Risk Appetite**
     The organization should establish appropriate tolerance for conformity risk.

160. **AI-CA-72 — Conformity Risk Appetite Control**
     The risk appetite framework should identify which conformity failures are unacceptable and which deficiencies may be temporarily managed through approved remediation processes where legally permissible.

161. **Conformity Risk Register**
     Material conformity risks should be recorded in the AI risk-management framework.

162. **AI-CA-73 — Conformity Risk Register Control**
     The risk register should identify applicable requirement, deficiency, impact, likelihood, owner, treatment, deadline, and residual risk.

163. **Conformity Exceptions**
     Exceptions should not be used to override mandatory legal requirements.

164. **AI-CA-74 — Conformity Exception Control**
     Permitted internal exceptions should be documented, risk-assessed, approved, time-limited, and subject to review.

165. **Conformity Escalation**
     Material conformity concerns should be escalated promptly.

166. **AI-CA-75 — Conformity Escalation Matrix**
     Escalation criteria should address regulatory exposure, safety impact, security impact, fundamental-rights impact, operational impact, and market-placement implications.

167. **Conformity Stop Authority**
     Authorized personnel should be able to block release when mandatory conformity requirements have not been satisfied.

168. **AI-CA-76 — Conformity Stop Control**
     Release workflows should include a documented mechanism for preventing unauthorized market placement or deployment.

169. **Management Oversight**
     Management should receive appropriate information about conformity status and material deficiencies.

170. **AI-CA-77 — Conformity Management Reporting**
     Management reports should include assessment status, material findings, overdue remediation, significant changes, regulatory concerns, and upcoming conformity activities.

171. **Executive Escalation**
     Material conformity failures should reach appropriate executive authorities.

172. **AI-CA-78 — Executive Conformity Escalation**
     Executive escalation thresholds should be defined based on regulatory and business risk.

173. **Board Oversight**
     Board or board-committee oversight may be appropriate where conformity failure could materially affect legal, safety, financial, or reputational exposure.

174. **AI-CA-79 — Board Conformity Reporting**
     Where appropriate, board reporting should focus on material conformity risk and management response.

175. **Conformity Training**
     Personnel involved in conformity assessment should understand their responsibilities and applicable requirements.

176. **AI-CA-80 — Conformity Competence Program**
     The organization should maintain role-based training covering regulatory requirements, evidence, assessment procedures, technical documentation, findings, and escalation.

177. **Assessor Training**
     Assessors should receive training appropriate to their assessment responsibilities.

178. **AI-CA-81 — Assessor Qualification Control**
     The organization should maintain records demonstrating assessor qualifications and relevant experience.

179. **Conformity Procedure Management**
     Assessment procedures should be documented and controlled.

180. **AI-CA-82 — Conformity Procedure Control**
     Procedures should define required activities, responsibilities, evidence, decision criteria, escalation, and records.

181. **Conformity Template Management**
     Standard templates should support consistent execution without replacing professional judgment.

182. **AI-CA-83 — Assessment Template Control**
     Assessment templates should be reviewed periodically and updated when applicable requirements or organizational processes change.

183. **Conformity Methodology Change**
     Changes to assessment methodology should undergo controlled review.

184. **AI-CA-84 — Methodology Change Control**
     Material changes should assess effects on consistency, regulatory interpretation, evidence requirements, and prior assessments.

185. **Conformity Knowledge Management**
     Interpretations, lessons learned, regulatory decisions, assessment findings, and recurring issues should be captured for organizational learning.

186. **AI-CA-85 — Conformity Knowledge Repository**
     The organization should maintain an authoritative repository for approved conformity methodologies, interpretations, lessons learned, and reusable evidence guidance.

## Regulatory and Lifecycle Integration

187. **Regulatory Change Management**
     Conformity requirements should be monitored for changes in legislation, implementing measures, guidance, standards, supervisory expectations, and transition provisions.

188. **AI-CA-86 — Regulatory Change Monitoring**
     The organization should maintain a process for identifying changes that may affect conformity obligations.

189. **Effective-Date Management**
     Regulatory changes should be evaluated against applicable effective dates and transition arrangements.

190. **AI-CA-87 — Conformity Effective-Date Control**
     The organization should maintain a schedule of relevant conformity requirements and implementation deadlines.

191. **Regulatory Interpretation**
     Where regulatory wording is ambiguous, interpretation should be documented and approved by appropriate legal or compliance authorities.

192. **AI-CA-88 — Regulatory Interpretation Record**
     Material interpretations affecting conformity decisions should be recorded with rationale, sources, date, owner, and review requirements.

193. **Standards Relationship**
     Harmonised standards and recognized technical standards may support conformity activities where applicable, but their legal effect depends on the relevant regulatory framework.

194. **AI-CA-89 — Standards Applicability Assessment**
     The organization should document which standards are used to support conformity and how they relate to applicable legal requirements.

195. **Standards-to-Requirements Mapping**
     Standards should be mapped to applicable legal requirements rather than treated as automatically equivalent to legislation.

196. **AI-CA-90 — Standards Traceability Control**
     The organization should maintain traceability between applicable regulatory requirements and supporting standards.

197. **Lifecycle Integration**
     Conformity assessment should be integrated into the AI system lifecycle.

198. **AI-CA-91 — Lifecycle Conformity Control**
     Conformity activities should be considered during requirements, design, development, validation, release, operation, change, incident management, and retirement.

199. **Development Integration**
     Development teams should identify conformity requirements before implementation of material regulated functionality.

200. **AI-CA-92 — Development Conformity Gate**
     Material conformity requirements should be incorporated into development acceptance criteria.

201. **Validation Integration**
     Validation should generate evidence usable for conformity where the evidence satisfies the applicable requirements.

202. **AI-CA-93 — Validation-to-Conformity Mapping**
     Validation results should be mapped to applicable conformity requirements.

203. **Release Integration**
     Conformity status should be part of the production-release decision.

204. **AI-CA-94 — Release Conformity Verification**
     Release workflows should verify that the assessed configuration matches the configuration approved for deployment.

205. **Operational Integration**
     Post-market monitoring and operational events may affect continuing conformity.

206. **AI-CA-95 — Operational Conformity Monitoring**
     Operational monitoring should identify events that may require conformity reassessment.

207. **Change Integration**
     Change management should identify changes that may affect conformity.

208. **AI-CA-96 — Change-to-Conformity Assessment**
     Material changes should be assessed for their potential effect on the conformity determination.

209. **Incident Integration**
     Material incidents should be assessed for potential conformity implications.

210. **AI-CA-97 — Incident-to-Conformity Assessment**
     Incident-management procedures should identify when technical or regulatory review of conformity is required.

211. **Corrective-Action Integration**
     Corrective actions resulting from incidents, audits, testing, or regulatory findings should be evaluated for conformity impact.

212. **AI-CA-98 — Corrective-Action Conformity Review**
     Material corrective actions should determine whether reassessment or updates to conformity evidence are required.

213. **Post-Market Integration**
     Post-market monitoring should provide feedback into conformity governance.

214. **AI-CA-99 — Post-Market Conformity Feedback**
     Material post-market findings should be evaluated for effects on conformity status.

215. **Retirement Integration**
     System retirement should preserve applicable conformity evidence and historical assessment records.

216. **AI-CA-100 — Retirement Conformity Records**
     The organization should retain required conformity records after retirement in accordance with applicable legal and organizational retention requirements.

## Conformity Assessment Evidence Architecture

217. **Evidence Architecture**
     Conformity evidence should be structured so that requirements, controls, tests, documentation, findings, approvals, and decisions can be traced.

218. **AI-CA-101 — Conformity Evidence Repository**
     A controlled repository should maintain or reference the authoritative conformity evidence package.

219. **Evidence Index**
     A conformity evidence index should identify the evidence supporting each material requirement.

220. **AI-CA-102 — Evidence Index Control**
     The evidence index should identify requirement, evidence reference, version, owner, date, status, and assessment conclusion.

221. **Evidence Completeness**
     The evidence package should be checked for completeness before the conformity decision.

222. **AI-CA-103 — Evidence Completeness Gate**
     The organization should verify that mandatory evidence is available before final assessment approval.

223. **Evidence Currency**
     Evidence should remain current throughout the period in which the conformity determination is relied upon.

224. **AI-CA-104 — Evidence Currency Monitoring**
     The organization should identify evidence that has become stale because of changes to the system, data, configuration, controls, or regulatory environment.

225. **Evidence Consistency**
     Technical documentation, risk records, testing, declarations, registration information, and other conformity evidence should not contain material contradictions.

226. **AI-CA-105 — Evidence Consistency Control**
     The organization should perform cross-document reconciliation before final conformity approval and after material changes.

227. **Evidence Traceability**
     Every material conformity conclusion should be traceable to objective evidence.

228. **AI-CA-106 — Conformity Traceability Control**
     Traceability should connect legal requirement → control → implementation → test → evidence → assessment conclusion.

229. **Evidence Retention**
     Conformity evidence should be retained for the period required by applicable law and organizational policy.

230. **AI-CA-107 — Conformity Evidence Retention**
     Retention controls should account for regulatory, contractual, audit, litigation, incident, and investigation requirements.

231. **Evidence Security**
     Technical conformity evidence may contain sensitive architecture, security, model, data, or proprietary information.

232. **AI-CA-108 — Conformity Evidence Security**
     Evidence repositories should apply appropriate confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, authorization, and monitoring controls.

233. **Evidence Access**
     Access should be granted according to role and legitimate business or regulatory need.

234. **AI-CA-109 — Conformity Evidence Access Review**
     Access to material conformity evidence should be periodically reviewed and recertified.

235. **Evidence Disclosure**
     External disclosure should be controlled to prevent unauthorized exposure of sensitive technical information.

236. **AI-CA-110 — Conformity Evidence Disclosure Control**
     Evidence provided to regulators, auditors, customers, notified bodies, or other authorized parties should undergo appropriate authorization and review.

237. **Evidence Redaction**
     Where disclosure is permitted but sensitive information is not required, controlled redaction may be appropriate.

238. **AI-CA-111 — Evidence Redaction Control**
     Redactions should preserve the integrity and meaning of the material evidence while preventing unnecessary disclosure.

239. **Evidence Chain of Custody**
     Where evidence may be used in a formal regulatory, legal, or investigative context, sufficient provenance should be maintained.

240. **AI-CA-112 — Evidence Chain-of-Custody Control**
     The organization should maintain evidence of collection, storage, transfer, access, and preservation where chain-of-custody requirements are material.

## Conformity Assurance and Management Review

241. **Conformity Assurance Program**
     The organization should establish assurance activities to determine whether conformity controls are properly designed and operating effectively.

242. **AI-CA-113 — Conformity Assurance Plan**
     The assurance plan should define scope, frequency, methodology, independence, evidence requirements, reporting, and remediation.

243. **Control Design Assessment**
     The organization should assess whether conformity controls are appropriately designed to address applicable requirements.

244. **AI-CA-114 — Conformity Control Design Review**
     Control design reviews should consider legal applicability, risk, evidence sufficiency, operational feasibility, and lifecycle integration.

245. **Control Operating Effectiveness**
     The organization should assess whether conformity controls are actually operating as designed.

246. **AI-CA-115 — Conformity Operating Effectiveness Testing**
     Testing should examine real assessments, evidence, approvals, changes, findings, and system versions.

247. **Management Review**
     Management should periodically review conformity status for material AI systems.

248. **AI-CA-116 — Management Conformity Review**
     Management reviews should consider assessment status, material findings, changes, regulatory developments, exceptions, supplier risks, and remediation.

249. **Internal Audit Review**
     Internal Audit may evaluate conformity governance as part of a risk-based assurance program.

250. **AI-CA-117 — Internal Audit Conformity Scope**
     Internal Audit scope may include applicability determinations, assessment procedures, evidence, approvals, change management, supplier controls, and continuing conformity.

251. **Independent Assurance**
     Independent assurance may be particularly valuable for high-impact or highly regulated AI systems.

252. **AI-CA-118 — Independent Conformity Assurance**
     The organization should establish criteria for when independent assurance is required or recommended.

253. **Assessment Quality Review**
     Completed assessments should undergo quality review before finalization where appropriate.

254. **AI-CA-119 — Assessment Quality Control**
     Quality review should verify scope, requirements, evidence, findings, conclusions, approvals, and version alignment.

255. **Assessment Consistency**
     Organizations with multiple AI systems should seek consistency in assessment methodology while allowing risk-based differences.

256. **AI-CA-120 — Assessment Consistency Monitoring**
     The organization should periodically review assessments for unexplained differences in interpretation, evidence thresholds, or decision criteria.

257. **Conformity Finding Trends**
     Finding trends should be analyzed to identify recurring weaknesses.

258. **AI-CA-121 — Conformity Finding Trend Analysis**
     Trend analysis should consider findings by business unit, system type, provider, requirement, severity, root cause, and lifecycle stage.

259. **Conformity Risk Trends**
     Management should monitor whether conformity risk is increasing or decreasing across the AI portfolio.

260. **AI-CA-122 — Conformity Risk Dashboard**
     A portfolio dashboard should provide visibility into conformity status, material findings, overdue remediation, upcoming assessments, and high-risk systems.

261. **Assessment Timeliness**
     The organization should monitor whether assessments are completed within required lifecycle and regulatory timeframes.

262. **AI-CA-123 — Assessment Timeliness KPI**
     Assessment timeliness should be measured against defined internal or legally applicable deadlines.

263. **Finding Remediation KPI**
     Management should monitor the timeliness and effectiveness of conformity finding remediation.

264. **AI-CA-124 — Conformity Remediation KPI**
     The KPI should measure remediation completion, aging, recurrence, and validation status.

265. **Evidence Completeness KPI**
     The organization should measure the completeness of conformity evidence packages.

266. **AI-CA-125 — Evidence Completeness KPI**
     The metric should identify missing mandatory evidence separately from optional or recommended evidence.

267. **Assessment Coverage**
     Management should understand what proportion of applicable AI systems have undergone required conformity activities.

268. **AI-CA-126 — Conformity Coverage KPI**
     The organization should monitor assessment coverage across the AI inventory.

269. **High-Risk Coverage**
     High-risk AI systems should receive particular attention in conformity-assessment coverage reporting.

270. **AI-CA-127 — High-Risk Conformity Coverage KPI**
     Management should monitor the conformity status of all applicable high-risk systems.

271. **Overdue Assessment KRI**
     Overdue required conformity activities should be treated as a compliance risk indicator.

272. **AI-CA-128 — Overdue Conformity KRI**
     The organization should monitor overdue assessments and escalate material delays.

273. **Conformity Evidence Gap KRI**
     Material evidence gaps should be monitored as indicators of potential compliance weakness.

274. **AI-CA-129 — Evidence Gap KRI**
     The organization should track unresolved evidence gaps by severity and regulatory significance.

275. **Conformity Change KRI**
     The number of material system changes requiring reassessment should be monitored.

276. **AI-CA-130 — Change-Triggered Reassessment KRI**
     The organization should monitor whether change-management processes are consistently identifying conformity impacts.

277. **Supplier Conformity KRI**
     For externally sourced AI, supplier conformity deficiencies should be monitored.

278. **AI-CA-131 — Supplier Conformity Risk Monitoring**
     Material supplier conformity gaps should be escalated through third-party risk management.

279. **Regulatory Readiness**
     The organization should be able to demonstrate how it reached its conformity conclusion and what evidence supports it.

280. **AI-CA-132 — Regulatory Readiness Control**
     The organization should periodically test its ability to retrieve and explain conformity evidence.

281. **Conformity Inspection Simulation**
     Simulation exercises can test whether the organization can respond coherently to an authorized regulatory or assurance review.

282. **AI-CA-133 — Conformity Inspection Exercise**
     Exercises should test evidence retrieval, responsible-person identification, version reconciliation, finding explanation, and response coordination.

283. **Documentation Reconciliation**
     Conformity evidence should remain synchronized with technical documentation.

284. **AI-CA-134 — Conformity Documentation Reconciliation**
     Material changes to technical documentation should be evaluated for effects on the conformity evidence package.

285. **Risk Reconciliation**
     Conformity status should remain consistent with current risk assessments.

286. **AI-CA-135 — Conformity Risk Reconciliation**
     Changes to documented risks should trigger assessment of whether conformity evidence remains adequate.

287. **Operational Reconciliation**
     The assessed system should continue to correspond to the system actually operated.

288. **AI-CA-136 — Conformity-to-Production Reconciliation**
     The organization should periodically reconcile assessed configurations against production configurations.

289. **Model Registry Reconciliation**
     Where model registries are used, the production model should be traceable to the assessed model version.

290. **AI-CA-137 — Model Conformity Reconciliation**
     Model-version discrepancies should trigger investigation and assessment of conformity impact.

291. **Deployment Reconciliation**
     Deployment records should support verification that the assessed system was deployed as approved.

292. **AI-CA-138 — Deployment Conformity Reconciliation**
     Material deviations between assessed and deployed configurations should be identified and evaluated.

293. **Continuous Compliance**
     Conformity should not be treated as permanently established merely because an assessment was completed at an earlier point in time.

294. **AI-CA-139 — Continuing Conformity Monitoring**
     The organization should monitor changes and events that may affect continuing conformity.

295. **Reassessment Triggers**
     Triggers should include material technical changes, intended-purpose changes, significant modifications, new risks, incidents, supplier changes, regulatory changes, and other applicable events.

296. **AI-CA-140 — Reassessment Trigger Control**
     The organization should maintain documented criteria for determining when conformity reassessment is required.

297. **Conformity Reassessment Decision**
     Each material reassessment trigger should result in a documented decision regarding whether reassessment is required.

298. **AI-CA-141 — Reassessment Decision Record**
     The decision record should identify the trigger, affected system version, regulatory analysis, decision, responsible authority, and required actions.

299. **Conformity Lifecycle Assurance**
     The organization should maintain assurance that conformity requirements, assessment evidence, system implementation, operational controls, and regulatory status remain aligned throughout the AI lifecycle.

300. **AI-CA-142 — Conformity Lifecycle Assurance Control**
     A formal lifecycle-assurance control should verify that applicable conformity obligations remain identified, evidence remains valid, material changes are assessed, findings are remediated, and the organization can demonstrate the continuing basis for its conformity status.
