# 15.17 — EU AI Act Penalties and Enforcement

## Part 1 — Foundations, Regulatory Purpose, Scope, and Enforcement Governance

1. **EU AI Act Enforcement as a Governance Function**
   EU AI Act enforcement converts regulatory obligations into enforceable requirements through competent authorities, investigations, corrective measures, administrative fines, complaints, market surveillance, and other supervisory mechanisms.

2. **Purpose of AI Act Enforcement**
   Enforcement is intended to support compliance with the AI Act and ensure that its requirements are implemented effectively. The Regulation requires Member States to establish applicable penalties and other enforcement measures that are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

3. **Enforcement Versus Compliance**
   Compliance is the organization's responsibility to satisfy applicable requirements. Enforcement is the activity of competent authorities and other authorized bodies used to supervise compliance and respond to violations.

4. **Enforcement Versus Assurance**
   Internal assurance evaluates whether an organization has designed and operated appropriate controls. Regulatory enforcement is performed by legally authorized authorities and can result in binding measures or penalties.

5. **Enforcement Versus Certification**
   Certification or conformity assessment does not eliminate the possibility of regulatory investigation or enforcement where applicable.

6. **Enforcement Versus Regulatory Guidance**
   Regulatory guidance helps organizations understand requirements and implementation expectations. Enforcement involves supervisory or legally authorized action concerning compliance.

7. **Enforcement Scope**
   The AI Act establishes a distributed enforcement architecture involving the European Commission's AI Office, national competent authorities, market surveillance authorities, and the European Data Protection Supervisor for AI systems used by EU institutions.

8. **AI Office Enforcement Role**
   The AI Office has Union-level enforcement responsibilities particularly concerning general-purpose AI models and certain AI systems associated with those models or with very large online platforms and search engines designated under the Digital Services Act.

9. **National Enforcement Role**
   National competent authorities enforce the AI Act for AI systems falling within their respective responsibilities, subject to the allocation of responsibilities established by the Regulation and national implementation arrangements.

10. **European Data Protection Supervisor Role**
    The European Data Protection Supervisor is responsible for enforcement concerning AI systems used by EU institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies within its jurisdiction.

11. **AI Office and National Authority Boundary**
    Organizations should determine whether a regulatory matter falls under the AI Office, a national competent authority, a market surveillance authority, the EDPS, or another relevant authority.

12. **AI-ENF-01 — Enforcement Authority Mapping**
    Organizations should maintain a documented mapping of the authorities responsible for the AI systems and activities in their portfolio.

13. **AI-ENF-02 — Enforcement Responsibility Determination**
    For each material AI system, the organization should determine which authorities may supervise, investigate, or enforce applicable requirements.

14. **AI-ENF-03 — Regulatory Jurisdiction Register**
    Relevant jurisdictions, competent authorities, regulatory responsibilities, and applicable enforcement mechanisms should be maintained in a controlled register.

15. **AI-ENF-04 — Authority Contact Register**
    Material regulatory contacts should be recorded and maintained by authorized personnel.

## Enforcement Governance Structure

16. **European Commission Role**
    The European Commission provides Union-level implementation and enforcement functions through the AI Office and has specific responsibilities under the AI Act concerning GPAI models and certain related systems.

17. **AI Office Investigative Powers**
    The AI Office can use investigative mechanisms including requests for information and, under the applicable framework, more formal investigative measures.

18. **AI Office Corrective Powers**
    The AI Office may require appropriate corrective measures within the scope of its enforcement responsibilities.

19. **AI Office Sanctioning Powers**
    The AI Office has sanctioning powers within its assigned area of responsibility, including the ability to impose fines under applicable provisions of the AI Act.

20. **National Competent Authority Powers**
    National authorities exercise enforcement powers according to the AI Act and applicable Member State arrangements.

21. **Market Surveillance Interface**
    Market surveillance authorities provide an important enforcement interface for AI systems subject to market-surveillance requirements.

22. **EDPS Enforcement Interface**
    AI systems used by EU institutions are subject to the applicable supervisory and enforcement role of the EDPS.

23. **AI Board Governance Role**
    The European Artificial Intelligence Board supports consistent application and implementation of the AI Act across Member States.

24. **Scientific Panel Support**
    The Scientific Panel provides independent scientific and technical expertise supporting implementation and enforcement, including matters concerning GPAI and systemic risks.

25. **Advisory Forum Support**
    The Advisory Forum provides stakeholder and technical advice concerning implementation, standardization, and other AI Act matters.

26. **AI-ENF-05 — Enforcement Governance Framework**
    Organizations should establish an internal governance framework for responding to regulatory supervision and enforcement.

27. **AI-ENF-06 — Enforcement Accountability**
    A senior accountable executive should be designated for material AI regulatory enforcement matters.

28. **AI-ENF-07 — Enforcement RACI**
    Responsibilities should be defined across legal, compliance, risk, security, privacy, product, engineering, AI governance, communications, and executive management.

29. **AI-ENF-08 — Enforcement Decision Authority**

30. **AI-ENF-09 — Enforcement Escalation Authority**
    Material regulatory matters should have predefined escalation routes.

31. **AI-ENF-10 — Regulatory Response Committee**
    Organizations with significant AI regulatory exposure should consider establishing an AI regulatory response committee or integrating enforcement matters into an existing compliance or AI governance committee.

32. **AI-ENF-11 — Enforcement Governance Cadence**

33. **AI-ENF-12 — Enforcement Management Information**

35. **AI-ENF-14 — Enforcement Governance Assurance**
    The effectiveness of the organization's enforcement-response governance should be periodically assessed.

## Penalty Framework

36. **Article 99 Penalty Framework**
    Article 99 establishes the principal penalty framework for infringements of the AI Act by operators and requires Member States to establish applicable penalties and other enforcement measures.

37. **Effective, Proportionate, and Dissuasive Penalties**
    The AI Act requires penalties to be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.

38. **Member State Penalty Rules**
    Member States establish their national rules on penalties and other enforcement measures within the framework established by the AI Act.

39. **AI-ENF-15 — Penalty Requirement Register**
    Organizations should maintain a register of applicable penalty provisions relevant to their AI systems.

40. **AI-ENF-16 — National Penalty Mapping**
    Organizations operating across multiple Member States should map relevant national penalty arrangements.

41. **AI-ENF-17 — Penalty Applicability Assessment**
    Potential penalties should be assessed according to the specific obligation, entity, conduct, jurisdiction, and circumstances involved.

42. **AI-ENF-18 — Penalty Exposure Assessment**
    Organizations should periodically assess potential regulatory penalty exposure as part of AI compliance risk management.

43. **AI-ENF-19 — Penalty Risk Escalation**

    Organizations should prioritize preventive and corrective controls designed to reduce the likelihood and impact of non-compliance.

## Prohibited AI Practice Penalties

46. **Article 5 Infringements**
    Non-compliance with the AI Act's prohibited AI practices is subject to the highest general administrative-fine category under Article 99.

47. **Maximum Fine for Prohibited Practices**
    Under the current amended text, infringement of Article 5 may result in an administrative fine of up to **EUR 35 million or, for an undertaking, 7% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher**.

48. **AI-ENF-22 — Prohibited-Practice Exposure Control**
    Organizations should identify whether any AI system, feature, use case, or development activity could fall within Article 5.

49. **AI-ENF-23 — Prohibited-Practice Screening**
    AI systems should undergo documented screening for prohibited practices before deployment and when material changes occur.

50. **AI-ENF-24 — Prohibited-Practice Escalation**
    Potential prohibited practices should be immediately escalated to appropriate legal, compliance, and governance authorities.

51. **AI-ENF-25 — Prohibited-Practice Stop Control**
    Where credible evidence indicates that an AI activity may constitute a prohibited practice, the organization should have authority to suspend the activity pending assessment.

52. **AI-ENF-26 — Prohibited-Practice Evidence**
    The organization should retain evidence demonstrating the assessment and conclusion regarding potential Article 5 applicability.

53. **AI-ENF-27 — Prohibited-Practice Remediation**
    Where a prohibited practice is identified, remediation should address cessation, containment, affected systems, data, users, suppliers, evidence, and regulatory obligations.

54. **AI-ENF-28 — Prohibited-Practice Supplier Control**
    Third-party AI systems should be screened for prohibited practices before procurement or deployment.

55. **AI-ENF-29 — Prohibited-Practice Change Monitoring**
    Changes to system capabilities, intended purpose, data, model, deployment context, or user population should trigger reassessment for prohibited practices.

## Provider, Importer, Distributor, and Deployer Penalties

56. **Article 16 Provider Obligations**
    Provider obligations under Article 16 fall within the Article 99 penalty framework for non-compliance other than Article 5 violations.

57. **Article 22 Authorized Representative Obligations**
    Authorized representative obligations are included within the applicable Article 99 penalty category.

58. **Article 23 Importer Obligations**
    Importer obligations are included within the applicable Article 99 penalty category.

59. **Article 24 Distributor Obligations**
    Distributor obligations are included within the applicable Article 99 penalty category.

60. **Article 25 Role-Change Obligations**
    The amended Article 99 framework also expressly includes certain provider and operator obligations arising under Article 25(2) and (4).

61. **Article 26 Deployer Obligations**
    Deployer obligations are included within the applicable Article 99 penalty category.

62. **Transparency Obligations**
    Article 50 transparency obligations for providers and deployers are included within the Article 99 penalty framework.

63. **Maximum Fine for Covered Operator Obligations**
    For the specified Article 99(4) infringements, the maximum administrative fine is **EUR 15 million or, for an undertaking, 3% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher**.

64. **AI-ENF-30 — Operator Obligation Penalty Mapping**
    Organizations should map provider, importer, distributor, deployer, authorized-representative, role-change, and transparency obligations to their potential enforcement consequences.

65. **AI-ENF-31 — Operator Compliance Evidence**

66. **AI-ENF-32 — Provider Penalty Exposure Control**
    Providers should assess exposure associated with provider obligations and establish corresponding preventive controls.

67. **AI-ENF-33 — Deployer Penalty Exposure Control**
    Deployers should assess exposure associated with deployer obligations and establish corresponding controls.

68. **AI-ENF-34 — Importer Penalty Exposure Control**

69. **AI-ENF-35 — Distributor Penalty Exposure Control**
    Distributors should assess exposure associated with distributor obligations.

70. **AI-ENF-36 — Authorized Representative Penalty Exposure Control**
    Authorized representatives should assess their specific regulatory responsibilities and potential enforcement exposure.

## Incorrect, Incomplete, or Misleading Information

71. **Regulatory Information Integrity**
    Providing incorrect, incomplete, or misleading information to notified bodies or national competent authorities in response to a request is separately subject to an administrative-fine category.

72. **Maximum Fine for Incorrect Information**
    Article 99 provides for fines of up to **EUR 7.5 million or, for an undertaking, 1% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher**, for the specified supply of incorrect, incomplete, or misleading information.

73. **AI-ENF-37 — Regulatory Information Accuracy Control**
    Organizations should establish controls ensuring that information supplied to competent authorities is accurate, complete, consistent, and supported by evidence.

74. **AI-ENF-38 — Regulatory Information Completeness Control**

75. **AI-ENF-39 — Regulatory Information Consistency Control**

76. **AI-ENF-40 — Regulatory Information Approval**

77. **AI-ENF-41 — Regulatory Evidence Verification**

78. **AI-ENF-42 — Regulatory Response Audit Trail**

79. **AI-ENF-43 — Regulatory Correction Control**

80. **AI-ENF-44 — Misleading-Information Escalation**

## SME and Small Mid-Cap Considerations

81. **SME Penalty Treatment**
    The AI Act provides specific treatment for SMEs, including start-ups, under Article 99. For SMEs, the applicable fine is capped using the lower of the specified percentage or monetary amount for the relevant penalty category.

82. **Small Mid-Cap Treatment**
    The amended AI Act also provides specific treatment for small mid-cap companies for the Article 99(4) and 99(5) penalty categories, using the lower of the applicable percentage or monetary amount.

83. **AI-ENF-45 — Entity-Size Classification**
    Organizations should maintain an accurate classification of whether they qualify as an SME, start-up, or applicable small mid-cap company for purposes of regulatory analysis.

84. **AI-ENF-46 — Penalty Calculation Classification**
    Penalty exposure assessments should use the entity classification applicable under the AI Act and relevant national implementation.

85. **AI-ENF-47 — Entity-Size Evidence**
    The organization should maintain evidence supporting the classification used in regulatory assessments.

86. **AI-ENF-48 — Entity-Size Change Monitoring**
    Changes in organizational size or corporate structure should trigger reassessment where they could affect applicable penalty treatment.

## Factors Affecting Penalty Decisions

87. **Individualized Penalty Assessment**
    The AI Act requires relevant circumstances of the specific situation to be considered when deciding whether to impose an administrative fine and determining its amount.

88. **Nature of the Infringement**
    The nature of the infringement is one factor relevant to penalty determination.

89. **Gravity of the Infringement**
    The gravity of the infringement is considered in determining an administrative fine.

90. **Duration of the Infringement**
    The duration of the infringement is relevant to the penalty assessment.

91. **Consequences of the Infringement**
    The consequences associated with the infringement are relevant to the assessment.

92. **Purpose of the AI System**
    The purpose of the AI system is among the factors identified by Article 99.

93. **Affected Persons**
    Where appropriate, the number of affected persons may be considered.

94. **Level of Damage**
    Where appropriate, the level of damage suffered by affected persons may be considered.

95. **AI-ENF-49 — Penalty-Factor Evidence**
    Organizations should preserve evidence relevant to factors that may be considered in enforcement proceedings.

96. **AI-ENF-50 — Infringement Duration Evidence**

97. **AI-ENF-51 — Impact Evidence**

98. **AI-ENF-52 — Remediation Evidence**

99. **AI-ENF-53 — Cooperation Evidence**
