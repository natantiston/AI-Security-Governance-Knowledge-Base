# 15.16 — AI Regulatory Sandboxes and EU AI Office

## Part 1 — Foundations, Regulatory Purpose, Scope, and Governance

1. **AI Regulatory Sandboxes and EU AI Office as Governance Mechanisms**
   AI regulatory sandboxes and the EU AI Office form important components of the European Union's AI governance architecture. They support practical implementation of AI regulation by creating structured mechanisms for regulatory guidance, experimentation, coordination, supervision, standardization, and development of organizational compliance capabilities.

2. **Regulatory Sandbox Concept**
   An AI regulatory sandbox is a controlled framework established by a competent authority or other authorized body in which AI systems can be developed, tested, validated, and evaluated under defined conditions while receiving regulatory guidance and oversight.

3. **Purpose of Regulatory Sandboxes**
   Regulatory sandboxes are intended to facilitate responsible AI innovation while improving understanding of regulatory requirements, technical risks, compliance approaches, and practical implementation challenges.

4. **Sandbox Versus Ordinary AI Testing**
   A regulatory sandbox is not simply a development or testing environment. It is a structured regulatory mechanism involving defined participation conditions, supervisory interaction, documentation, safeguards, and regulatory learning.

5. **Sandbox Versus Regulatory Exemption**
   Participation in a regulatory sandbox does not generally mean that an organization is exempt from applicable legal obligations. Sandbox participation should not be treated as permission to disregard mandatory requirements.

6. **Sandbox Versus Conformity Assessment**
   A regulatory sandbox may support development, testing, and regulatory understanding, but it should not automatically be treated as equivalent to a formal conformity assessment or certification process.

7. **Sandbox Versus Regulatory Approval**
   Participation or successful completion of sandbox activities should not automatically be represented as regulatory approval of an AI system unless the applicable authority expressly provides such a legal status.

8. **EU AI Act Context**
   The EU AI Act establishes a framework for AI regulatory sandboxes and requires Member States to ensure that appropriate sandbox arrangements are available in accordance with the Regulation.

9. **AI Office Context**
   The European Commission's AI Office has a central role in implementing and enforcing parts of the EU AI Act, particularly in relation to general-purpose AI models and systems, guidance, coordination, codes of practice, and Union-level regulatory activities.

10. **European AI Office**
    The European AI Office operates within the European Commission and supports implementation and enforcement of the EU AI Act at Union level.

11. **AI Office Versus National Competent Authorities**
    The AI Office and national competent authorities have distinct responsibilities. The AI Office does not replace the Member States' competent authorities for all AI regulatory functions.

12. **AI Office Versus AI Board**
    The AI Office is an organizational function within the European Commission, whereas the European Artificial Intelligence Board is a separate governance body established under the EU AI Act to contribute to consistent implementation across Member States.

13. **AI Office Versus Advisory Forum**
    The Advisory Forum provides stakeholder and expert input into AI Act implementation. It should not be confused with the AI Office's administrative and regulatory-support functions.

14. **AI Office Versus Scientific Panel**
    The Scientific Panel of independent experts provides scientific and technical expertise relevant to AI Act implementation, including matters concerning general-purpose AI and systemic risks.

15. **AI-ARS-01 — Sandbox Governance Framework**
    Organizations participating in an AI regulatory sandbox should maintain a documented governance framework defining objectives, responsibilities, participation conditions, supervisory interactions, documentation requirements, safeguards, and exit criteria.

16. **AI-ARS-02 — Sandbox Eligibility Assessment**
    Before participation, the organization should assess whether the proposed AI system and planned activities fall within the applicable sandbox eligibility criteria.

17. **AI-ARS-03 — Sandbox Scope Definition**
    The organization should clearly define the AI system, use case, testing objectives, data, participants, deployment environment, duration, limitations, and regulatory questions within the sandbox scope.

18. **AI-ARS-04 — Sandbox Participation Record**
    The organization should maintain evidence of its application, acceptance, participation conditions, supervisory arrangements, decisions, communications, and completion status.

19. **AI-ARS-05 — Sandbox Accountability**
    The organization should assign accountable personnel for technical activities, regulatory coordination, risk management, data governance, security, privacy, documentation, and communication with sandbox authorities.

20. **AI-ARS-06 — Sandbox RACI**
    A RACI or equivalent responsibility model should identify responsibilities among the organization, sandbox authority, technical teams, legal and compliance functions, security, privacy, domain experts, and other relevant stakeholders.

21. **AI-ARS-07 — Sandbox Decision Authority**
    The organization should define who can approve sandbox entry, testing activities, material changes, risk acceptance, suspension, corrective measures, and exit.

22. **AI-ARS-08 — Sandbox Escalation Authority**
    The organization should define escalation routes for safety concerns, security incidents, privacy events, fundamental-rights concerns, non-compliance, data problems, or other material issues.

23. **AI-ARS-09 — Sandbox Stop Authority**
    The organization should establish authority to pause or terminate sandbox activities when predefined safety, security, privacy, legal, ethical, or operational thresholds are exceeded.

24. **AI-ARS-10 — Sandbox Segregation of Duties**
    Where appropriate, development, testing, risk approval, regulatory assessment, and independent assurance responsibilities should be appropriately segregated.

25. **AI-ARS-11 — Sandbox Three Lines Integration**
    Sandbox activities should be integrated with the organization's Three Lines Model, with operational ownership, second-line oversight, and independent assurance responsibilities clearly distinguished.

## Regulatory Sandbox Objectives

26. **Sandbox Innovation Objective**
    Regulatory sandboxes can allow organizations to explore innovative AI applications while obtaining structured feedback concerning regulatory requirements and risk-management practices.

27. **Sandbox Regulatory Learning Objective**
    Sandbox activities can help organizations and regulators identify practical challenges in applying legal requirements to emerging AI technologies.

28. **Sandbox Compliance Learning Objective**
    Organizations can use sandbox participation to validate compliance approaches, documentation structures, risk controls, testing methods, monitoring mechanisms, and governance processes.

29. **Sandbox Technical Learning Objective**
    Technical experimentation can provide evidence concerning model behavior, performance, robustness, security, data quality, human oversight, and operational limitations.

30. **Sandbox Governance Learning Objective**
    Sandbox participation can help organizations identify weaknesses in accountability, risk ownership, decision rights, escalation, documentation, and control effectiveness.

31. **Sandbox Regulatory Dialogue**
    A regulatory sandbox provides a structured environment for interaction between participating organizations and relevant authorities.

32. **AI-ARS-12 — Regulatory Dialogue Record**
    Material interactions with sandbox authorities should be documented, including questions raised, guidance received, assumptions, decisions, and agreed actions.

33. **AI-ARS-13 — Regulatory Guidance Interpretation**
    Organizations should distinguish formal legal requirements from informal guidance, supervisory observations, technical recommendations, and exploratory discussions.

34. **AI-ARS-14 — Regulatory Guidance Traceability**
    Material regulatory guidance received during sandbox activities should be traceable to the relevant system, issue, decision, and resulting control.

35. **AI-ARS-15 — Regulatory Guidance Limitation**
    Sandbox participants should avoid treating informal regulatory feedback as a general legal authorization beyond the specific context in which it was provided.

36. **AI-ARS-16 — Regulatory Assumption Register**
    Material regulatory assumptions made during sandbox participation should be documented and periodically reviewed.

37. **AI-ARS-17 — Regulatory Question Register**
    Open regulatory questions should be maintained in a controlled register with owners and resolution status.

38. **AI-ARS-18 — Regulatory Interpretation Escalation**
    Unresolved material legal or regulatory interpretations should be escalated to qualified legal or compliance personnel.

39. **AI-ARS-19 — Regulatory Conflict Management**
    Where different regulatory requirements or interpretations appear to conflict, the organization should document the conflict and obtain appropriate legal or regulatory clarification.

40. **AI-ARS-20 — Regulatory Evidence Management**
    Material evidence supporting sandbox-related regulatory conclusions should be preserved.

## Sandbox Risk Management

41. **Sandbox Risk Management**
    Sandbox activities should operate under a documented risk-management process appropriate to the AI system, testing environment, data, affected persons, and potential consequences.

42. **AI-ARS-21 — Sandbox Risk Assessment**
    Before material sandbox activities begin, the organization should identify and assess relevant risks.

43. **AI-ARS-22 — Sandbox Risk Register**
    Material sandbox risks should be recorded in a dedicated risk register or integrated into the organization's AI risk-management system.

44. **AI-ARS-23 — Sandbox Risk Classification**
    Risks should be categorized according to appropriate regulatory, technical, operational, security, privacy, safety, and fundamental-rights dimensions.

45. **AI-ARS-24 — Sandbox Risk Criteria**
    Risk assessment should use documented criteria appropriate to the AI system and experimental context.

46. **AI-ARS-25 — Sandbox Risk Appetite**
    Sandbox activities should operate within approved organizational risk appetite and applicable regulatory constraints.

47. **AI-ARS-26 — Sandbox Risk Treatment**
    Identified risks should be treated through appropriate controls, restrictions, monitoring, mitigation, avoidance, or other approved measures.

48. **AI-ARS-27 — Sandbox Residual Risk**
    Residual risk should be documented after controls have been implemented.

49. **AI-ARS-28 — Sandbox Risk Acceptance**
    Residual risks should only be accepted by appropriately authorized personnel.

50. **AI-ARS-29 — Sandbox Risk Escalation**
    Material risks exceeding defined thresholds should be escalated.

51. **AI-ARS-30 — Sandbox Risk Monitoring**
    Risk conditions should be monitored throughout sandbox participation.

52. **AI-ARS-31 — Sandbox Risk Reassessment**
    Material changes in the AI system, data, use case, testing conditions, affected population, or regulatory interpretation should trigger risk reassessment.

53. **AI-ARS-32 — Sandbox Risk Closure**
    Sandbox risks should be formally closed, transferred, or retained when the relevant activity ends.

54. **AI-ARS-33 — Sandbox Risk-to-Control Mapping**
    Material sandbox risks should be mapped to controls designed to prevent, detect, mitigate, or manage those risks.

55. **AI-ARS-34 — Sandbox Risk-to-Evidence Mapping**
    Risk assessments and treatment decisions should be supported by appropriate evidence.

62. **AI-ARS-40 — Geographic Scope**
    Where relevant, the geographic scope of sandbox activity should be documented.

63. **AI-ARS-41 — User Population Scope**

64. **AI-ARS-42 — Affected-Person Identification**
    Potentially affected individuals or groups should be identified where relevant.

65. **AI-ARS-43 — Deployment Environment Definition**
    The sandbox environment should be documented, including infrastructure, software, model, data, interfaces, and security boundaries.

66. **AI-ARS-44 — Production Separation**
    Sandbox environments should be appropriately separated from production environments unless controlled production testing is explicitly permitted.

67. **AI-ARS-45 — Sandbox Data Boundary**
    Data used within sandbox activities should be explicitly identified and controlled.

68. **AI-ARS-46 — Sandbox Data Authorization**
    The organization should verify that data used during sandbox activities is authorized for the intended purpose.

69. **AI-ARS-47 — Sandbox Data Protection**
    Sandbox data should receive appropriate security and privacy protections.

70. **AI-ARS-48 — Sandbox Data Minimization**
    Data collection should be limited to what is necessary for approved sandbox objectives.

71. **AI-ARS-49 — Sandbox Data Retention**
    Sandbox data should have defined retention and deletion requirements.

72. **AI-ARS-50 — Sandbox Data Lineage**
    Material sandbox data should be traceable to its source, transformation, use, and resulting outputs where appropriate.

73. **AI-ARS-51 — Sandbox Third-Party Data Control**
    Third-party data used in sandbox activities should be subject to appropriate contractual, licensing, privacy, security, and governance controls.

74. **AI-ARS-52 — Sandbox Open-Source Component Control**
    Open-source AI and software components used in sandbox activities should be inventoried and evaluated for relevant security, licensing, and operational risks.

75. **AI-ARS-53 — Sandbox External Service Control**
    External AI services, APIs, cloud services, or model providers should be identified and governed.

## Sandbox Safeguards

76. **Sandbox Safeguards**
    Sandbox activities should incorporate safeguards proportionate to potential harm and should not rely solely on the experimental nature of the environment as a risk-control measure.

77. **AI-ARS-54 — Participant Safeguards**
    Appropriate safeguards should protect sandbox participants and affected individuals.

78. **AI-ARS-55 — Human Oversight Safeguards**
    Where AI outputs can materially affect people, appropriate human oversight should be established.

79. **AI-ARS-56 — Safety Safeguards**
    Safety controls should be implemented where the AI system could create physical or other significant harm.

80. **AI-ARS-57 — Security Safeguards**
    Security controls should protect sandbox environments, models, data, interfaces, credentials, and supporting infrastructure.

81. **AI-ARS-58 — Privacy Safeguards**
    Privacy protections should be integrated into sandbox design where personal data is processed.

82. **AI-ARS-59 — Fundamental-Rights Safeguards**
    Potential fundamental-rights impacts should be identified and mitigated where relevant.

83. **AI-ARS-60 — Access Control Safeguards**
    Access to sandbox systems and data should be restricted according to defined roles.

84. **AI-ARS-61 — Privileged Access Safeguards**
    Privileged access should be controlled, logged, monitored, and periodically reviewed.

85. **AI-ARS-62 — Network Segmentation Safeguards**
    Sandbox environments should use appropriate network segmentation and security boundaries.

86. **AI-ARS-63 — Secrets Management Safeguards**
    Credentials, keys, tokens, and other secrets should be securely managed.

87. **AI-ARS-64 — Model Integrity Safeguards**
    Model artifacts should be protected against unauthorized modification or replacement.

88. **AI-ARS-65 — Data Integrity Safeguards**
    Material sandbox data should be protected against unauthorized alteration.

89. **AI-ARS-66 — Output Safeguards**
    AI outputs should be controlled where they could create material safety, privacy, security, regulatory, or rights-related consequences.

90. **AI-ARS-67 — External Action Safeguards**
    AI systems capable of initiating external actions should operate within defined authorization boundaries.

91. **AI-ARS-68 — Sandbox Action Approval**
    Material AI-initiated actions should require appropriate human authorization where risk warrants it.

92. **AI-ARS-69 — Sandbox Kill-Switch Capability**
    Where technically appropriate, high-impact experimental systems should have a mechanism for rapid suspension or shutdown.

93. **AI-ARS-70 — Sandbox Emergency Escalation**
    Emergency conditions should have predefined escalation routes.

94. **AI-ARS-71 — Sandbox Emergency Procedures**
    Emergency procedures should define containment, suspension, communication, investigation, and recovery actions.

95. **AI-ARS-72 — Sandbox Safeguard Testing**
    Material safeguards should be tested before or during sandbox participation.

## Sandbox Lifecycle

146. **Sandbox Lifecycle Management**
     Sandbox participation should be managed through controlled lifecycle stages.

147. **AI-ARS-105 — Sandbox Application Stage**
     The application stage should establish objectives, scope, eligibility, risks, responsibilities, and proposed safeguards.

148. **AI-ARS-106 — Sandbox Admission Stage**
     Admission should confirm participation conditions, scope, responsibilities, safeguards, and regulatory interfaces.

149. **AI-ARS-107 — Sandbox Preparation Stage**
     The preparation stage should establish the technical environment, data controls, security controls, documentation, testing procedures, and monitoring.

150. **AI-ARS-108 — Sandbox Experimentation Stage**
     Experimentation should follow approved scope, methodology, safeguards, and monitoring requirements.

151. **AI-ARS-109 — Sandbox Monitoring Stage**
     Sandbox activities should be monitored for safety, security, privacy, performance, regulatory, and operational issues.

152. **AI-ARS-110 — Sandbox Change Stage**
     Material changes to the system, data, scope, participants, testing methodology, or environment should be subject to controlled review.

153. **AI-ARS-111 — Sandbox Incident Stage**
     Material incidents should trigger appropriate containment, investigation, escalation, and reporting processes.

154. **AI-ARS-112 — Sandbox Evaluation Stage**
     Results should be evaluated against defined experimental and regulatory objectives.

155. **AI-ARS-113 — Sandbox Exit Stage**
     Exit should confirm completion of required testing, documentation, evidence, remediation, communications, and regulatory activities.

156. **AI-ARS-114 — Sandbox Post-Exit Review**
     Following exit, the organization should evaluate lessons learned, unresolved risks, regulatory implications, and required lifecycle actions.

157. **AI-ARS-115 — Sandbox Transition Control**
     Where the AI system proceeds toward production, sandbox outputs should feed into the organization's normal risk, compliance, security, privacy, testing, conformity, and deployment processes.

158. **AI-ARS-116 — Sandbox-to-Production Boundary**
     Sandbox participation should not automatically authorize production deployment.

159. **AI-ARS-117 — Sandbox Exit Evidence**
     Completion and exit decisions should be supported by documented evidence.

160. **AI-ARS-118 — Sandbox Closure Record**
     A formal closure record should document the final status, unresolved issues, decisions, evidence, and transition activities.

## End-to-End Operational Control

511. **AI-ARS-466 — Sandbox Intake-to-Exit Traceability**
     The organization should maintain traceability from sandbox application through admission, planning, experimentation, monitoring, incidents, regulatory interactions, evaluation, and exit.

512. **AI-ARS-467 — Regulatory Requirement-to-Control Traceability**
     Applicable requirements should map to implemented controls and evidence.

513. **AI-ARS-468 — Risk-to-Safeguard Traceability**
     Material risks should map to safeguards and monitoring mechanisms.

514. **AI-ARS-469 — Test-to-Evidence Traceability**
     Tests should be traceable to results and supporting evidence.

515. **AI-ARS-470 — Incident-to-Remediation Traceability**
     Material incidents should be traceable through containment, investigation, root cause, corrective action, validation, and closure.

516. **AI-ARS-471 — Regulatory-Decision Traceability**
     Material regulatory interpretations and decisions should be traceable to their underlying facts, evidence, authority, and resulting actions.

519. **AI-ARS-474 — Sandbox-to-Production Traceability**
     Where an AI system transitions from sandbox experimentation toward operational deployment, relevant sandbox results, risks, controls, and lessons should be traceable into the production lifecycle.

520. **AI-ARS-475 — End-to-End Sandbox Compliance Assurance**
     The organization should maintain an end-to-end assurance mechanism demonstrating that sandbox participation is governed, controlled, monitored, evidenced, and appropriately transitioned or closed in accordance with the approved sandbox arrangements and applicable regulatory requirements. The EU AI Act requires sandboxes to operate as controlled environments under specific plans and safeguards, while preserving competent authorities' supervisory and corrective powers.
