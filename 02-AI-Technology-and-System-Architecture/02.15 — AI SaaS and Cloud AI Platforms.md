# 02.15 — AI SaaS and Cloud AI Platforms

## Part 1 — AI SaaS and Cloud AI Platform Foundations, Architecture, Components, and Core Concepts

---

# 1. Introduction to AI SaaS and Cloud AI Platforms

AI SaaS and cloud AI platforms provide organizations with access to artificial intelligence capabilities through cloud-based services rather than requiring the organization to build and operate the complete AI technology stack itself.

For a GRC professional, understanding these platforms is important because they introduce governance considerations involving:

* third-party providers;
* cloud infrastructure;
* data processing;
* identity and access;
* security;
* privacy;
* regulatory compliance;
* operational resilience;
* vendor dependency;
* shared responsibility.

---

# 2. AI Software as a Service

AI Software as a Service, or AI SaaS, refers to software applications delivered through a service model in which AI capabilities are embedded into or form a major part of the software functionality.

Examples may include applications that provide:

* AI assistants;
* document analysis;
* content generation;
* enterprise search;
* automated summarization;
* predictive analytics;
* customer-service automation;
* AI-powered productivity features.

---

# 3. AI SaaS as a Consumption Model

AI SaaS allows organizations to consume AI functionality without necessarily managing:

* model infrastructure;
* GPU infrastructure;
* model-serving infrastructure;
* operating systems;
* application infrastructure;
* model deployment pipelines.

The provider manages much of the underlying technology.

---

# 4. AI SaaS versus Traditional SaaS

Traditional SaaS primarily delivers application functionality through cloud infrastructure.

AI SaaS additionally incorporates AI capabilities such as:

* machine learning;
* foundation models;
* generative AI;
* computer vision;
* speech processing;
* recommendation systems;
* intelligent automation.

This creates additional risks because AI behavior can depend on models, training data, prompts, context, and probabilistic outputs.

---

# 5. Cloud AI Platform

A cloud AI platform provides infrastructure, services, tools, and APIs that organizations can use to build, deploy, operate, and manage AI workloads.

A cloud AI platform may provide:

* AI models;
* model APIs;
* machine learning services;
* model training;
* model deployment;
* AI development environments;
* data services;
* vector databases;
* monitoring;
* security controls.

---

# 6. AI Cloud Service Provider

An AI cloud service provider delivers cloud-based infrastructure or AI capabilities to customers.

The provider may operate:

* data centers;
* computing infrastructure;
* GPU clusters;
* AI platforms;
* model services;
* storage;
* networking;
* security services.

---

# 11. AI Application Service

An AI application service provides business-facing functionality built on top of AI models and supporting services.

* AI customer support;
* enterprise copilots;
* intelligent document processing;
* AI-powered analytics.

---

# 12. Managed AI Service

A managed AI service is operated by the provider rather than requiring the customer to manage the underlying AI infrastructure directly.

The provider may manage:

* infrastructure;
* model serving;
* scaling;
* patching;
* availability;
* service operations.

---

# 13. Self-Managed AI Workload

A self-managed AI workload requires the customer to operate a larger portion of the technology stack.

The organization may manage:

* infrastructure;
* models;
* deployment;
* security;
* monitoring;
* upgrades.

---

# 14. Fully Managed AI Service

A fully managed AI service abstracts most of the underlying infrastructure and model operations from the customer.

This improves ease of adoption but can reduce direct visibility and control.

---

# 16. Cloud AI Shared Responsibility

Cloud AI security follows a shared-responsibility concept.

The provider and customer have different responsibilities depending on the service model.

---

# 17. AI Shared Responsibility Model

A simplified model is:

```text

    ↓
Infrastructure / Platform / Managed Services

Customer
    ↓
Identity / Data / Configuration / Usage
```

The exact responsibility boundary depends on the service.

* underlying cloud infrastructure.

* data;
* API usage;
* security policies;
* regulatory compliance.

---

# 20. Shared AI Responsibility

Examples include:

* security monitoring;
* incident response;
* encryption;
* access management;
* configuration;
* resilience.

---

# 21. AI SaaS Responsibility Boundary

AI SaaS introduces a broader responsibility boundary because the provider may manage:

* application;
* AI models;
* model infrastructure;
* data-processing infrastructure.

The customer may still control:

* users;
* business configuration;
* uploaded data;
* prompts;
* integrations;
* access.
