# 02.19 — Enterprise AI System Architecture

## Part 1 — Enterprise AI Architecture Foundations, Components, Layers, Operating Models, and Governance Structure

---

# 1. Introduction to Enterprise AI System Architecture

Enterprise AI system architecture describes how AI technologies are designed, integrated, operated, secured, governed, and connected to organizational business processes.

For a GRC professional, enterprise AI architecture provides the technical context needed to understand:

* where AI operates;
* what data it processes;
* which systems it connects to;
* who can use it;
* which external providers are involved;
* where security boundaries exist;
* where governance controls must be applied.

---

# 2. Enterprise AI Architecture Purpose

The purpose of enterprise AI architecture is to provide a structured design for deploying AI capabilities within an organization's technology and business environment.

It connects:

```text
Business Requirements
        ↓
AI Capabilities
        ↓
Technology Architecture
        ↓
Security Controls
        ↓
Governance
        ↓
Operational Management
```

---

# 3. Enterprise AI Architecture Scope

Enterprise AI architecture can include:

* AI applications;
* AI models;
* foundation models;
* machine learning platforms;
* AI APIs;
* data platforms;
* vector databases;
* AI agents;
* tools;
* enterprise applications;
* cloud infrastructure;
* security services;
* monitoring platforms;
* GRC processes.

---

# 4. Enterprise AI Architecture Versus AI Model Architecture

AI model architecture focuses primarily on the internal design of a model.

Enterprise AI architecture focuses on the complete environment surrounding AI.

For example:

```text
Enterprise AI Architecture
 ├── Users
 ├── Applications
 ├── Data
 ├── Models
 ├── APIs
 ├── Agents
 ├── Tools
 ├── Infrastructure
 ├── Security
 └── Governance
```

---

# 5. Enterprise AI Architecture Versus Enterprise Architecture

Enterprise architecture covers the organization as a whole.

Enterprise AI architecture is a specialized architectural domain focused on AI-enabled capabilities and their integration into the enterprise.

---

A comprehensive enterprise AI architecture can be divided into several domains:

# 7. Enterprise AI Business Architecture

Business architecture describes how AI capabilities support organizational objectives.

It answers questions such as:

* What business problem is AI solving?
* Which business process uses AI?
* Who owns the process?
* What business outcome is expected?
* What happens if the AI system fails?

---

An AI business capability represents an organizational capability enabled or enhanced by AI.

# 22. Centralized AI Operating Model

A centralized model places most AI capabilities under a central team.

Advantages may include:

* consistent standards;
* centralized expertise;
* stronger governance;
* standardized platforms.

Potential disadvantages include:

* slower business adoption;
* centralized bottlenecks;
* reduced business autonomy.

---

# 100. Enterprise AI Architecture — GRC Perspective

From a GRC perspective, enterprise AI architecture is not simply a technical diagram.

It is a **governance map** showing:

```text
Who
 ↓
Uses What
 ↓
With Which Data
 ↓
Through Which AI Components
 ↓
Using Which Models
 ↓
Connected to Which Systems
 ↓
Under Which Controls
 ↓
With Which Risks
```

A GRC professional should be able to look at an enterprise AI architecture and identify:

* **assets** that require protection;
* **data flows** that require governance;
* **trust boundaries** that require security controls;
* **external dependencies** that require third-party risk management;
* **models** that require lifecycle governance;
* **users and services** that require identity and access controls;
* **business processes** that create operational risk;
* **controls** that require testing and evidence;
* **risks** that require treatment or acceptance;
* **regulatory obligations** that require compliance monitoring.
