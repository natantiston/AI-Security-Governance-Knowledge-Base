# 02.20 — AI Security Architecture Diagrams and Reference Models

## Part 1 — AI Security Architecture Foundations, Diagram Types, Architectural Views, Security Boundaries, and Core Reference Models

---

# 1. Introduction to AI Security Architecture

AI security architecture defines how security principles, controls, technologies, processes, and responsibilities are organized to protect AI systems throughout their lifecycle.

For a GRC professional, AI security architecture provides the technical structure needed to understand:

* what must be protected;
* where security controls are located;
* how data moves through AI systems;
* where trust boundaries exist;
* which components introduce risk;
* how controls map to architectural elements.

---

# 2. Purpose of AI Security Architecture

The primary purpose of AI security architecture is to establish a structured security model for AI systems.

It should help organizations:

* identify attack surfaces;
* establish security boundaries;
* protect data;
* protect models;
* control access;
* secure integrations;
* monitor AI activity;
* support resilience;
* demonstrate compliance.

---

# 5. AI Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that AI-related information is accessible only to authorized parties.

Protected information may include:

* prompts;
* responses;
* training data;
* proprietary models;
* embeddings;
* credentials;
* business information.

---

# 6. AI Integrity

Integrity ensures that AI systems, models, data, configurations, and outputs are not improperly modified.

---

# 7. AI Availability

Availability ensures that authorized users can access AI capabilities when required.

---

# 8. AI Authenticity

Authenticity establishes confidence that:

* users are genuine;
* services are legitimate;
* models are trusted;
* data originates from approved sources.

---

# 9. AI Accountability

Accountability ensures that significant AI activities can be attributed to responsible users, systems, or processes.

* least privilege;
* defense in depth;
* zero trust;
* secure defaults;
* separation of duties;
* continuous monitoring;
* fail securely;

---

# 12. Security by Design for AI

Security requirements should be incorporated during AI system design rather than added after implementation.

---

# 13. Secure-by-Default AI Architecture

AI systems should begin with restrictive security settings and require deliberate authorization for additional capabilities.

---

# 14. Least Privilege AI Architecture

AI users, applications, models, agents, and services should receive only the permissions necessary for their approved functions.

---

# 16. Zero Trust AI Architecture

Zero Trust assumes that no user, device, application, service, or AI component should automatically be trusted.

Access decisions should be based on:

* verified identity;
* authorization;
* context;
* resource;
* policy.

---

# 17. Fail-Secure AI Architecture

When an AI security control fails, the system should default toward a secure state rather than automatically permitting access.

---

# 101. AI Threat Modeling

AI threat modeling is the structured process of identifying threats, attack paths, vulnerabilities, and security requirements associated with an AI system.

It helps answer:

* What are we protecting?
* Who might attack the system?
* How could the system be attacked?
* What could happen if the attack succeeds?
* Which controls reduce the risk?

---

# 102. Purpose of AI Threat Modeling

AI threat modeling helps organizations identify security risks before or during implementation.

It should consider:

* users;
* applications;
* models;
* data;
* APIs;
* tools;
* infrastructure;
* external providers.

---
