# 02.20 — AI Security Architecture Diagrams and Reference Models

## Part 1 — AI Security Architecture Foundations, Diagram Types, Architectural Views, Security Boundaries, and Core Reference Models

---

# 1. Introduction to AI Security Architecture

AI security architecture defines how security principles, controls, technologies, processes, and responsibilities are organized to protect AI systems throughout their lifecycle.

For a GRC professional, AI security architecture provides the technical structure needed to understand:

* what must be protected;
* where security controls are located;
* how data moves through AI systems;
* where trust boundaries exist;
* which components introduce risk;
* how controls map to architectural elements.

---

# 2. Purpose of AI Security Architecture

The primary purpose of AI security architecture is to establish a structured security model for AI systems.

It should help organizations:

* identify attack surfaces;
* establish security boundaries;
* protect data;
* protect models;
* control access;
* secure integrations;
* monitor AI activity;
* support resilience;
* demonstrate compliance.

---

# 5. AI Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that AI-related information is accessible only to authorized parties.

Protected information may include:

* prompts;
* responses;
* training data;
* proprietary models;
* embeddings;
* credentials;
* business information.

---

# 6. AI Integrity

Integrity ensures that AI systems, models, data, configurations, and outputs are not improperly modified.

---

# 7. AI Availability

Availability ensures that authorized users can access AI capabilities when required.

---

# 8. AI Authenticity

Authenticity establishes confidence that:

* users are genuine;
* services are legitimate;
* models are trusted;
* data originates from approved sources.

---

# 9. AI Accountability

Accountability ensures that significant AI activities can be attributed to responsible users, systems, or processes.

# 11. AI Security Architecture Principles

* security by design;
* least privilege;
* defense in depth;
* zero trust;
* secure defaults;
* separation of duties;
* continuous monitoring;
* fail securely;

---

# 12. Security by Design for AI

Security requirements should be incorporated during AI system design rather than added after implementation.

---

# 13. Secure-by-Default AI Architecture

AI systems should begin with restrictive security settings and require deliberate authorization for additional capabilities.

---

# 14. Least Privilege AI Architecture

AI users, applications, models, agents, and services should receive only the permissions necessary for their approved functions.

---

# 16. Zero Trust AI Architecture

Zero Trust assumes that no user, device, application, service, or AI component should automatically be trusted.

Access decisions should be based on:

* verified identity;
* authorization;
* context;
* resource;
* policy.

---

# 17. Fail-Secure AI Architecture

When an AI security control fails, the system should default toward a secure state rather than automatically permitting access.

---

* production deployment;

# 101. AI Threat Modeling

AI threat modeling is the structured process of identifying threats, attack paths, vulnerabilities, and security requirements associated with an AI system.

It helps answer:

* What are we protecting?
* Who might attack the system?
* How could the system be attacked?
* What could happen if the attack succeeds?
* Which controls reduce the risk?

---

# 102. Purpose of AI Threat Modeling

AI threat modeling helps organizations identify security risks before or during implementation.

It should consider:

* users;
* applications;
* models;
* data;
* APIs;
* tools;
* infrastructure;
* external providers.

---


# 159. AI Threat Categories

AI threat categories may include:

* unauthorized access;
* data exposure;
* prompt manipulation;
* model compromise;
* supply-chain attacks;
* malicious tools;
* insecure integrations.

---

# 160. AI Prompt Injection Threat

Prompt injection attempts to influence an AI system through malicious or unauthorized instructions.

---

# 161. Direct Prompt Injection

Direct prompt injection occurs when an attacker deliberately submits malicious instructions to the AI system.

---

# 162. Indirect Prompt Injection

Indirect prompt injection occurs when malicious instructions are embedded in content that the AI system later processes.

Potential sources include:

* websites;
* documents;
* emails;
* knowledge bases.

---

# 163. AI Instruction Boundary

AI architecture should distinguish between:

* trusted system instructions;
* application instructions;
* user input;
* retrieved content;
* tool output.

---

# 164. AI Context Contamination

Context contamination occurs when untrusted information influences an AI response or action beyond its intended role.

---

# 165. AI Retrieval Poisoning

Retrieval poisoning occurs when malicious or manipulated information is inserted into a knowledge source used by an AI system.

---

# 166. AI Knowledge Base Poisoning

Attackers may attempt to manipulate documents or knowledge repositories so that AI systems retrieve incorrect or malicious information.

---

# 167. AI Embedding Poisoning

Malicious content can potentially influence the representation and retrieval behavior of an embedding-based system.

---

# 168. AI Training Data Poisoning

Training-data poisoning occurs when malicious or manipulated information is introduced into training datasets.

---

# 169. AI Model Poisoning

Model poisoning involves compromising a model or model-related artifact so that it behaves maliciously or incorrectly.

---

# 170. AI Supply Chain Compromise

A compromised third-party model, library, dataset, or dependency can introduce malicious behavior into an AI system.

---

# 171. AI Model Theft

Model theft involves unauthorized acquisition or reproduction of model capabilities or artifacts.

---

# 172. AI Model Extraction

Model extraction attempts to approximate or reproduce a model through repeated interaction with its interface.

---

# 173. AI Model Inversion

Model inversion attempts to infer sensitive characteristics or information associated with model inputs or training data.

---

# 174. AI Membership Inference

Membership inference attempts to determine whether specific information or records were included in a model's training data.

---

# 175. AI Data Leakage

Data leakage occurs when information is exposed to unauthorized users, systems, or providers.

---

# 176. AI Sensitive Information Disclosure

An AI system may unintentionally disclose:

* personal information;
* credentials;
* proprietary information;
* confidential documents.

---

# 177. AI System Prompt Disclosure

Attackers may attempt to extract system instructions from an AI application.

---

# 178. AI Credential Exposure

Credentials can be exposed through:

* prompts;
* logs;
* tool parameters;
* application configuration;
* error messages.

---

# 179. AI Insecure Output Handling

AI-generated output may be dangerous when directly passed to another system without appropriate validation.

---

# 180. AI Output Injection

AI output can potentially contain content interpreted as commands or instructions by downstream systems.

---

# 181. AI Tool Abuse

An attacker may manipulate an AI system into invoking an authorized tool for an unauthorized purpose.

---

# 182. AI Excessive Agency

Excessive agency occurs when an AI system has more authority or autonomy than necessary.

---

# 183. AI Excessive Permissions

Excessive permissions increase the potential impact of:

* compromised accounts;
* prompt injection;
* agent errors;
* malicious instructions.

---

# 184. AI Autonomous Transaction Risk

AI systems capable of initiating transactions create additional security and governance requirements.

Examples include:

* financial transactions;
* account changes;
* data deletion;
* infrastructure changes.

---

# 185. AI Human Approval Control

High-impact AI actions can require human approval before execution.

---

# 186. AI Rate Limiting

Rate limiting restricts the number of requests or actions that can occur within a defined period.

---

# 187. AI Quota Management

Quotas can restrict:

* token usage;
* API calls;
* compute;
* financial expenditure;
* tool executions.

---

# 188. AI Denial-of-Service Risk

AI services can be affected by excessive or malicious requests.
