# 13.05 — AI Robustness Testing

## Part 1 — AI Robustness Testing Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Robustness Testing

AI robustness testing is the systematic evaluation of an AI system's ability to maintain acceptable behavior when exposed to variations, disturbances, unexpected inputs, adversarial conditions, environmental changes, and other conditions that differ from its nominal operating assumptions.

Robustness testing focuses on whether the system continues to behave within defined acceptable boundaries when conditions are imperfect rather than ideal.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Robustness Testing

The purpose of AI robustness testing is to establish evidence that an AI system remains sufficiently reliable, secure, and controlled when operating conditions deviate from expected conditions.

Robustness testing helps determine whether an AI system can:

* Tolerate unexpected inputs
* Resist manipulation
* Maintain security boundaries
* Handle degraded dependencies
* Manage ambiguous requests
* Maintain acceptable behavior under variation
* Fail safely when conditions exceed supported limits

---

### 3. AI Robustness Testing vs Functional Testing

Functional testing asks whether the system performs its intended functions.

Robustness testing asks whether those functions remain acceptable when conditions change or become difficult.

```text id="airob03a"
Functional Testing
        |

        |
        v
"Does the system work?"

Robustness Testing
        |
        v
Unexpected / Adversarial Conditions
        |
        v
"Does the system remain

```

A system may pass functional tests while failing robustness tests.

### 13. Probabilistic AI Robustness

AI systems may produce different outputs for the same or similar inputs.

Robustness testing should therefore distinguish between:

* Acceptable variation
* Material behavioral drift
* Security-relevant deviation
* Unacceptable instability

Statistical evaluation may be required where deterministic pass/fail criteria are inappropriate.

### 49. AI Robustness Testing and Assurance

Robustness testing provides evidence about how an AI system behaves under defined variations and disturbances.

It does not establish universal robustness.

```text id="airob49f"
Robustness Test
      |
      v

      |
      v
Observed Behavior
      |
      v
Evidence
      |
      v
Risk Assessment
      |
      v
Reassessment as Conditions Change
```

Passing a robustness test means that the system satisfied defined criteria under tested conditions. It does not prove that every possible perturbation or future condition will be handled safely.

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Robustness Testing

**AI robustness testing must establish systematic, risk-based evidence that an AI system remains within defined acceptable behavioral, security, and operational boundaries when exposed to relevant variations, unexpected inputs, adversarial conditions, degraded dependencies, environmental disturbances, distribution changes, and failure scenarios. Robustness testing must extend beyond the model to include applications, prompts, context, datasets, retrieval systems, memory, tools, agents, APIs, dependencies, configurations, and infrastructure. Testing should deliberately examine perturbations, semantic and syntactic variation, boundary conditions, uncertainty, out-of-distribution inputs, adversarial conditions, degraded services, and controlled failures. For probabilistic AI systems, robustness must be evaluated using appropriate behavioral, statistical, security, and operational criteria rather than assuming identical outputs are required. Every material robustness result must be attributable to the exact system, model, dataset, configuration, dependencies, and environment tested. Passing a robustness test establishes evidence about defined conditions at a defined point in time; it does not establish universal or permanent robustness. Robustness assurance must therefore remain risk-based, regression-capable, continuously reassessable, and capable of triggering remediation, retesting, release blocking, containment, rollback, or reauthorization when system behavior, threats, data, models, dependencies, or operating conditions materially change.**

### 117. Core Control Principle

**AI robustness testing controls must establish a controlled and repeatable mechanism for determining whether AI systems remain within approved behavioral, security, and operational boundaries when conditions vary from the nominal operating state. Controls must cover the complete AI system, including models, applications, prompts, context, datasets, retrieval, memory, tools, agents, APIs, dependencies, configurations, and infrastructure. Robustness testing must deliberately introduce relevant input perturbations, semantic and syntactic variations, boundary conditions, data shifts, adversarial conditions, uncertainty, degraded dependencies, infrastructure failures, and other risk-relevant disturbances. Each test must be authorized, attributable, associated with the exact system state being evaluated, and supported by integrity-protected evidence. Acceptance criteria must define meaningful thresholds for probabilistic behavior rather than assuming identical outputs are required. Critical robustness failures must be capable of triggering automated or human-enforced release blocking, escalation, remediation, containment, rollback, or reauthorization. Previously identified weaknesses should become regression tests where practical, and high-risk testing should receive independent validation. Robustness evidence must remain conditional and time-bound: a successful result demonstrates acceptable behavior only under the evaluated conditions and does not establish permanent robustness. Material changes in models, data, prompts, applications, dependencies, configurations, threats, or operating conditions must therefore be capable of triggering renewed robustness testing and reassessment.**

### 177. Strategic Governance Principle

**AI robustness assurance must be continuously governed rather than treated as a one-time demonstration of system stability. Every material change to a model, dataset, prompt, application, retrieval system, memory, tool, agent workflow, dependency, configuration, infrastructure, provider, or operating context can alter the system's robustness characteristics and potentially invalidate historical evidence. Robustness governance must therefore maintain a traceable chain from requirements and threats through test conditions, results, findings, remediation, retesting, risk decisions, and reauthorization. Governance must remain capable of challenging previously accepted evidence when new threats, distribution shifts, behavioral changes, failures, or incidents emerge. High-risk systems require deeper and appropriately independent validation, while critical robustness failures must be capable of triggering remediation, release blocking, containment, rollback, or suspension. Robustness evidence establishes confidence only within the conditions and assumptions under which it was generated; production trust must remain conditional, current, challengeable, and continuously reassessable.**

### 239. Continuous AI Robustness Testing and Assurance Architecture

**AI robustness-testing architecture must operate as a continuously reassessable technical assurance system rather than a collection of isolated stress or variation tests. The architecture must connect robustness requirements, risk and threat models, test conditions, perturbation mechanisms, controlled execution, evaluation, evidence, findings, remediation, regression testing, release gates, runtime observations, and reauthorization across the complete AI system. Models, applications, prompts, context, datasets, retrieval systems, memory, tools, agents, APIs, dependencies, configurations, and infrastructure must be testable under relevant variations and failure conditions while remaining protected by strong identity, authorization, segmentation, integrity, observability, and blast-radius controls. Probabilistic behavior must be evaluated against approved behavioral and statistical thresholds rather than requiring identical outputs, while security-critical properties must remain enforced throughout robustness testing. Test evidence must possess sufficient provenance and integrity to support audit and assurance, and the absence or invalidation of evidence must never be interpreted as a successful result. Robustness-testing gates must resist unauthorized bypass, testing infrastructure must fail securely, and high-impact experiments must remain suspendable, containable, and reversible. Testing capability must remain distinct from authorization authority: the ability to generate robustness evidence does not independently establish production trust. When models, datasets, prompts, applications, retrieval systems, memory, tools, agents, dependencies, configurations, infrastructure, threats, or operating conditions materially change, the architecture must support renewed evaluation and, where necessary, release blocking, remediation, containment, rollback, suspension, revocation, or reauthorization.**
