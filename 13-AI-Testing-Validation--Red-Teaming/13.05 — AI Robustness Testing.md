# 13.05 — AI Robustness Testing

## Part 1 — AI Robustness Testing Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Robustness Testing

AI robustness testing is the systematic evaluation of an AI system's ability to maintain acceptable behavior when exposed to variations, disturbances, unexpected inputs, adversarial conditions, environmental changes, and other conditions that differ from its nominal operating assumptions.

Robustness testing focuses on whether the system continues to behave within defined acceptable boundaries when conditions are imperfect rather than ideal.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Robustness Testing

The purpose of AI robustness testing is to establish evidence that an AI system remains sufficiently reliable, secure, and controlled when operating conditions deviate from expected conditions.

Robustness testing helps determine whether an AI system can:

* Tolerate unexpected inputs
* Resist manipulation
* Maintain security boundaries
* Handle degraded dependencies
* Manage ambiguous requests
* Maintain acceptable behavior under variation
* Fail safely when conditions exceed supported limits

---

### 3. AI Robustness Testing vs Functional Testing

Functional testing asks whether the system performs its intended functions.

Robustness testing asks whether those functions remain acceptable when conditions change or become difficult.

```text id="airob03a"
Functional Testing
        |
        v
Expected Conditions
        |
        v
"Does the system work?"

Robustness Testing
        |
        v
Unexpected / Adversarial Conditions
        |
        v
"Does the system remain
acceptable when conditions change?"
```

A system may pass functional tests while failing robustness tests.

### 5. AI Robustness Testing Objectives

Robustness testing should establish whether AI behavior remains within defined acceptable boundaries under relevant variations.

Objectives may include:

* Behavioral stability
* Security resilience
* Input tolerance
* Context resilience
* Model stability
* Dependency resilience
* Failure handling
* Graceful degradation

---

### 6. Risk-Based AI Robustness Testing

Robustness testing depth should be proportional to AI risk.

Higher-risk systems may require testing against:

* More severe perturbations
* Larger input variations
* More adversarial scenarios
* More environmental failures
* More complex workflows
* Longer-duration testing

Risk should determine which robustness dimensions require the greatest testing depth.

---

### 7. AI Robustness Test Scope

Robustness testing should consider the complete AI system rather than only the underlying model.

Scope may include:

* Model
* Application
* Prompts
* Context
* Dataset
* Retrieval
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* APIs
* Dependencies
* Infrastructure
* Human workflows

---

### 8. AI System Under Robustness Testing

The system under test must be clearly defined.

The test target should identify:

* Application version
* Model version
* Dataset version
* Prompt configuration
* Retrieval configuration
* Tool configuration
* Dependencies
* Infrastructure
* Security controls

Robustness results are meaningful only when the tested system state is known.

---

### 9. Robustness Test Conditions

Robustness testing should intentionally introduce conditions that differ from nominal operation.

Examples include:

* Unexpected input
* Ambiguous instructions
* Missing context
* Conflicting information
* Increased load
* Dependency failure
* Network interruption
* Malformed data

---

### 10. Input Robustness Testing

AI inputs should be varied systematically to determine whether unexpected input characteristics cause unacceptable behavior.

Testing may vary:

* Length
* Structure
* Encoding
* Language
* Formatting
* Complexity
* Ambiguity
* Ordering

---

### 11. Output Robustness Testing

Outputs should be evaluated for stability and acceptable behavior when inputs or operating conditions vary.

Testing should consider:

* Accuracy degradation
* Unsafe output
* Unexpected formatting
* Sensitive information disclosure
* Unauthorized recommendations
* Unstable decisions

---

### 12. Behavioral Robustness

Behavioral robustness evaluates whether the AI system maintains acceptable behavior across relevant variations.

The objective is not necessarily identical output.

For probabilistic systems, acceptable robustness may instead be defined as maintaining:

* Required constraints
* Safety properties
* Security properties
* Accuracy ranges
* Decision consistency
* Output-format requirements

---

### 13. Probabilistic AI Robustness

AI systems may produce different outputs for the same or similar inputs.

Robustness testing should therefore distinguish between:

* Acceptable variation
* Material behavioral drift
* Security-relevant deviation
* Unacceptable instability

Statistical evaluation may be required where deterministic pass/fail criteria are inappropriate.

---

### 14. Input Perturbation Testing

Input perturbation testing modifies inputs while preserving their essential meaning or intended purpose.

Examples include:

* Minor spelling changes
* Formatting changes
* Synonyms
* Reordered information
* Additional irrelevant content
* Different representations

The objective is to determine whether small changes cause disproportionate or unsafe behavior.

---

### 15. Semantic Robustness Testing

Semantic robustness evaluates whether AI behavior remains appropriate when equivalent meaning is expressed differently.

Testing may use:

* Paraphrases
* Synonyms
* Different sentence structures
* Different languages
* Different communication styles

Material security or policy differences caused by equivalent semantic inputs should be investigated.

---

### 16. Syntactic Robustness Testing

Syntactic robustness examines how the system responds to malformed or unusual input structures.

Examples include:

* Invalid JSON
* Missing fields
* Unexpected delimiters
* Broken markup
* Invalid encoding
* Unexpected nesting

The system should fail predictably rather than producing uncontrolled behavior.

---

### 17. Boundary Robustness Testing

Boundary conditions should be tested systematically.

Examples include:

* Maximum input size
* Minimum input size
* Maximum token count
* Maximum context
* Maximum tool parameters
* Rate limits
* Resource limits

Testing should determine whether behavior remains controlled at and beyond defined boundaries.

---

### 18. Context Robustness Testing

AI systems should be tested when context is incomplete, excessive, contradictory, or misleading.

Testing may include:

* Missing context
* Irrelevant context
* Conflicting context
* Repeated context
* Very long context
* Malicious context

---

### 19. Instruction Robustness Testing

Instruction-following should be evaluated under competing or conflicting instructions.

Testing should determine whether trusted instructions maintain appropriate priority over untrusted or lower-priority content.

### 20. Prompt Robustness Testing

Prompt variations should be used to determine whether small changes can bypass intended controls.

Testing may examine:

* Wording variations
* Formatting variations
* Multi-turn variations
* Language variations
* Instruction ordering
* Context placement

### 21. Adversarial Robustness Testing

Adversarial robustness testing intentionally introduces inputs designed to cause incorrect, unsafe, or unauthorized behavior.

Examples include:

* Adversarial prompts

* Malicious context
* Manipulated tool responses
* Poisoned retrieval content

### 22. Security Robustness Testing

Robustness testing must include security properties where failure under variation could create security consequences.

Testing may determine whether:

* Authorization remains enforced
* Sensitive data remains protected
* Tool permissions remain bounded
* Security policies remain active
* Monitoring remains effective

---

### 23. Data Robustness Testing

AI systems should be tested against variation and degradation in input data.

Testing may include:

* Missing values
* Noisy data
* Duplicates
* Unexpected formats
* Outliers
* Corrupted records
* Distribution changes

---

### 24. Dataset Distribution Robustness

Model behavior should be evaluated when operational data differs from the data used during development or evaluation.

Relevant conditions include:

* Population changes
* Geographic changes
* Temporal changes
* Input distribution shifts
* New data sources

Significant distribution changes may require additional evaluation or model reassessment.

---

### 25. Model Robustness Testing

Models should be evaluated against conditions that may expose weaknesses in their learned behavior.

Testing may examine:

* Input perturbations
* Distribution shifts
* Adversarial inputs
* Unexpected combinations
* Rare scenarios
* Out-of-distribution inputs

---

### 26. Out-of-Distribution Testing

AI systems should be evaluated using inputs that differ materially from their expected training or operational distribution.

The objective is to determine whether the system:

* Detects unfamiliar conditions
* Produces safe uncertainty behavior
* Avoids excessive confidence
* Escalates when appropriate
* Fails safely

---

### 27. Uncertainty Robustness Testing

AI systems should be tested to determine whether uncertainty is handled appropriately.

Testing may examine whether the system:

* Recognizes insufficient information
* Avoids unsupported certainty
* Requests clarification
* Escalates high-risk cases
* Produces appropriate uncertainty indicators

---

### 28. Confidence Robustness Testing

Where confidence scores or equivalent signals are available, testing should determine whether confidence remains meaningful under difficult conditions.

High confidence in incorrect or unsafe behavior should be treated as a potential robustness concern.

### 29. Retrieval Robustness Testing

### 30. Memory Robustness Testing

Robustness testing should produce evidence sufficient to reconstruct the conditions under which behavior was observed.

### 46. Robustness Findings and Defects

Robustness failures should be formally classified and assessed.

Potential findings include:

* Unsafe degradation
* Excessive sensitivity
* Security-control bypass
* Unstable behavior
* Unauthorized action
* Excessive confidence
* Failure to recognize uncertainty

---

### 47. Robustness Retesting

Robustness defects should be retested after remediation.

Retesting should verify:

1. The original robustness weakness has been addressed.
2. Security and functional behavior remain acceptable.
3. The remediation has not created a new weakness.

---

### 48. AI Robustness Testing Independence

High-risk robustness testing should receive an appropriate degree of independent validation.

Independent testing can challenge assumptions made by developers and model owners.

The required independence should be proportional to:

* AI risk
* System autonomy
* Potential impact
* Security sensitivity

### 49. AI Robustness Testing and Assurance

Robustness testing provides evidence about how an AI system behaves under defined variations and disturbances.

It does not establish universal robustness.

```text id="airob49f"
Robustness Test
      |
      v
Defined Conditions
      |
      v
Observed Behavior
      |
      v
Evidence
      |
      v
Risk Assessment
      |
      v
Reassessment as Conditions Change
```

Passing a robustness test means that the system satisfied defined criteria under tested conditions. It does not prove that every possible perturbation or future condition will be handled safely.

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Robustness Testing

**AI robustness testing must establish systematic, risk-based evidence that an AI system remains within defined acceptable behavioral, security, and operational boundaries when exposed to relevant variations, unexpected inputs, adversarial conditions, degraded dependencies, environmental disturbances, distribution changes, and failure scenarios. Robustness testing must extend beyond the model to include applications, prompts, context, datasets, retrieval systems, memory, tools, agents, APIs, dependencies, configurations, and infrastructure. Testing should deliberately examine perturbations, semantic and syntactic variation, boundary conditions, uncertainty, out-of-distribution inputs, adversarial conditions, degraded services, and controlled failures. For probabilistic AI systems, robustness must be evaluated using appropriate behavioral, statistical, security, and operational criteria rather than assuming identical outputs are required. Every material robustness result must be attributable to the exact system, model, dataset, configuration, dependencies, and environment tested. Passing a robustness test establishes evidence about defined conditions at a defined point in time; it does not establish universal or permanent robustness. Robustness assurance must therefore remain risk-based, regression-capable, continuously reassessable, and capable of triggering remediation, retesting, release blocking, containment, rollback, or reauthorization when system behavior, threats, data, models, dependencies, or operating conditions materially change.**
