  # 13.13 — AI Red Teaming

## Part 1 — AI Red Teaming Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Red Teaming

AI Red Teaming is an authorized, adversarial security-assurance activity that simulates realistic attackers attempting to discover, exploit, chain, and demonstrate weaknesses in AI systems.

Unlike narrowly scoped testing, red teaming evaluates how multiple weaknesses can interact across the AI attack surface.

Red-team activities may assess:

* Models
* Prompts
* Applications
* RAG
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* APIs
* Identity
* Authorization
* Infrastructure
* Security controls
* Monitoring
* Incident response

The objective is not merely to identify individual vulnerabilities but to determine whether an attacker can translate AI weaknesses into meaningful security, privacy, safety, operational, or business impact.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Red Teaming

The purpose of AI red teaming is to provide realistic adversarial evidence about the effectiveness of AI security controls.

Red teaming should determine whether an attacker can:

* Manipulate AI behavior
* Bypass security controls
* Obtain unauthorized information
* Escalate privileges
* Abuse tools
* Manipulate agents
* Cross trust boundaries
* Evade detection
* Maintain persistence
* Chain multiple weaknesses

Red teaming therefore provides a broader perspective than isolated vulnerability testing.

---

### 3. AI Red Teaming vs AI Security Testing

AI security testing evaluates defined security properties through controlled test cases. Red teaming takes a broader adversarial approach and attempts to achieve realistic attacker objectives.

```text id="redteam31a"
      |

```

Both disciplines are complementary.

### 5. AI Red Teaming Objectives

AI red teaming should establish whether realistic adversarial behavior can compromise defined security objectives.

Primary objectives include:

1. Discovering AI-specific attack paths.
2. Testing security-control effectiveness.
3. Identifying attack chains.
4. Evaluating blast radius.
5. Testing detection capability.
6. Testing containment and recovery.
7. Validating threat models.
8. Identifying previously unknown weaknesses.
9. Evaluating AI-specific risks in realistic workflows.
10. Producing actionable security evidence.

---

High-risk AI systems may include systems that:

* Access sensitive information

* Make high-impact decisions

* Control critical workflows
* Connect to enterprise infrastructure

Independence requirements should increase with:

* System impact
* Autonomy
* Privilege
* Data sensitivity
* External exposure

### 50. Core Principle of AI Red Teaming

AI Red Teaming must be treated as a **realistic, authorized, risk-based adversarial assurance discipline that evaluates not only individual AI vulnerabilities but the attack paths and chains through which those weaknesses can create meaningful enterprise impact**.

The complete red-team target is:

```text id="redteam50j"
Attacker Objective
       |
       v
AI Attack Surface
       |
       v
Initial Compromise
       |
       v
AI Behavioral Manipulation
       |
       v
Security-Control Interaction
       |
       v
Privilege / Data / Tool Access
       |
       v
Attack Chain
       |
       v
Business Impact
       |
       v
Detection
       |
       v
Containment
       |
       v
Recovery
```

Red teaming should therefore evaluate the full AI system, including models, prompts, context, retrieval, memory, tools, agents, identities, authorization, APIs, infrastructure, monitoring, and response capabilities.

The objective is **not to prove that the AI system is impossible to compromise**. The objective is to establish realistic evidence about:

* Attack-path resistance
* Security-control effectiveness
* Blast radius
* Detection
* Containment
* Recovery
* Residual risk

A successful red-team attack should not automatically become an enterprise compromise when independent authorization, segmentation, least privilege, monitoring, and containment controls are correctly designed.

Equally, a failed red-team scenario should not be interpreted as proof that all possible attacks will fail.

**Red-team capability ≠ security assurance.
Attack success ≠ automatic enterprise compromise.
Attack failure ≠ universal security.
Model capability ≠ authorization.
Agent capability ≠ enterprise authority.
Passing a red-team exercise ≠ permanent trust.**
