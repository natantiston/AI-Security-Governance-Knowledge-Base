# 13.08 — AI Privacy Testing

## Part 1 — AI Privacy Testing Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Privacy Testing

AI Privacy Testing is the systematic evaluation of whether an AI system appropriately protects personal, sensitive, confidential, and otherwise regulated information throughout its lifecycle and operational workflows.

Testing should examine whether the AI system:

* Collects only authorized information
* Uses data for approved purposes
* Prevents unauthorized disclosure
* Enforces access controls
* Protects data during processing
* Prevents unintended retention
* Handles deletion correctly
* Protects information across model and application boundaries

Privacy testing must consider the complete AI system rather than only the underlying model.

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Privacy Testing

The purpose of AI Privacy Testing is to generate evidence that AI systems operate within defined privacy requirements and do not create unacceptable privacy risks.

Testing should identify:

* Unauthorized data exposure
* Excessive collection
* Improper data use
* Data retention failures
* Cross-user leakage
* Cross-tenant leakage
* Memorization
* Inference of sensitive information
* Inadequate deletion
* Privacy-control failures

---

### 3. AI Privacy Testing vs Security Testing

Privacy testing and security testing overlap but address different assurance questions.

**Security testing** primarily evaluates whether unauthorized parties can compromise systems, data, identities, or controls.

**Privacy testing** evaluates whether personal or sensitive information is appropriately collected, processed, disclosed, retained, inferred, and deleted according to defined privacy requirements.

A system can be technically secure while still violating privacy requirements through excessive collection or inappropriate use.

---

AI privacy testing should follow a controlled lifecycle:

The lifecycle should remain connected to the AI system's data flows and processing purposes.

---

### 5. AI Privacy Testing Objectives

Privacy-testing objectives should be explicitly defined according to the system's intended use and risk.

Objectives may include determining whether:

* Personal data is collected appropriately
* Data is used only for approved purposes
* Access controls prevent unauthorized exposure
* Data is isolated between users or tenants
* Sensitive information is not unintentionally generated
* Retention requirements are enforced
* Deletion mechanisms work correctly
* Privacy controls remain effective after system changes

---

### 6. Risk-Based AI Privacy Testing

Privacy testing must be proportional to privacy risk.

Risk factors may include:

* Volume of personal data
* Sensitivity of data
* Number of individuals affected
* AI autonomy
* Model memorization potential
* Data-sharing arrangements
* External AI providers
* Geographic processing
* Retention requirements
* Consequences of disclosure

Higher-risk systems require deeper and more frequent privacy testing.

---

### 7. AI Privacy Test Scope

Privacy testing should cover components that collect, process, transmit, store, retrieve, infer, or expose personal information.

Potential scope includes:

* User interfaces
* Applications
* Models
* Prompts
* System instructions
* Context
* Retrieval systems
* Vector databases
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* APIs
* Logs
* Monitoring
* Third-party providers

---

### 8. Personal Data Testing

Privacy testing should identify whether personal data is handled according to approved requirements.

Examples include:

* Names
* Email addresses
* Identification information
* Location information
* Account information
* Employment information
* Financial information

Testing should verify that data flows correspond to documented purposes.

---

### 9. Sensitive Data Testing

Sensitive information requires enhanced testing.

Depending on applicable requirements, this may include:

* Health information
* Financial information
* Biometric information
* Authentication information
* Precise location
* Government identifiers

Testing should determine whether sensitive data can be unnecessarily exposed, inferred, retained, or transferred.

---

### 10. Privacy Data Flow Testing

AI privacy testing should trace personal information through the complete processing chain.

```text id="aipriv10b"

    |

    |
    +----> Retrieval
    |
    +----> Memory
    |
    +----> Tools
    |
    v
Output
    |
    v

```

Testing should identify where personal information enters, moves through, and leaves the AI system.

---

### 11. Data Collection Testing

Testing should evaluate whether AI applications collect only information necessary for approved purposes.

Testing may examine:

* Required fields
* Optional fields
* Hidden collection
* Metadata
* Telemetry
* Browser/device information
* Conversation content

Unnecessary collection can create privacy risk even when the collected information is securely stored.

---

### 12. Purpose Limitation Testing

Privacy testing should determine whether personal information is used only for approved purposes.

Testing should evaluate whether data collected for one purpose is unexpectedly:

* Used for another purpose
* Included in prompts
* Used for training
* Added to memory
* Sent to external providers
* Used for analytics

---

### 13. Data Minimization Testing

AI systems should be tested to determine whether unnecessary personal information is processed.

Testing may examine:

* Prompt construction
* Context assembly
* Retrieval
* Memory
* Logging
* Tool parameters

The objective is to ensure that AI components receive only the information necessary for the approved task.

---

### 14. Data Access Testing

Privacy testing should evaluate whether personal information is accessible only to authorized identities.

Testing should cover:

* Users
* Administrators
* Developers
* AI workloads
* Agents
* Tools
* Support personnel
* External providers

---

### 15. Cross-User Privacy Testing

AI systems must be tested for leakage between users.

Testing should determine whether one user's information can influence another user's:

* Prompt
* Context
* Retrieval
* Memory
* Output
* Recommendations

---

### 16. Cross-Tenant Privacy Testing

Multi-tenant AI systems require explicit tenant-isolation testing.

Testing should attempt to determine whether tenant A can access or influence:

* Tenant B's documents
* Tenant B's embeddings
* Tenant B's memory
* Tenant B's conversations
* Tenant B's model context

Tenant isolation failures can represent severe privacy and security vulnerabilities.

---

### 17. Session Privacy Testing

Session boundaries should be tested to ensure that personal information does not persist improperly between sessions.

Testing may include:

* Session creation
* Session termination
* Session expiration
* Reauthentication
* Session recovery
* New-session initialization

---

### 18. Conversation Privacy Testing

Conversational AI should be tested for unintended disclosure of previous conversation content.

Testing should determine whether information from:

* Previous sessions
* Other users
* Shared contexts
* Archived conversations

can appear in unauthorized interactions.

---

### 19. AI Memory Privacy Testing

Persistent AI memory introduces additional privacy risks.

Testing should evaluate:

* Memory creation
* Memory retrieval
* Memory scope
* Memory modification
* Memory deletion
* Memory isolation

Memory should not retain personal information beyond its authorized purpose.

---

### 20. RAG Privacy Testing

RAG systems must be tested for unauthorized retrieval of personal information.

Testing should evaluate:

* Document access controls
* Retrieval filtering
* Tenant isolation
* Metadata filtering
* Embedding access
* Retrieval ranking

A secure document repository does not automatically guarantee privacy-safe AI retrieval.

---

### 21. Vector Database Privacy Testing

Vector stores should be tested for privacy leakage.

Testing may examine whether unauthorized users can:

* Retrieve embeddings
* Infer document contents
* Search restricted information
* Access another tenant's vectors
* Exploit metadata

Embeddings should not automatically be considered harmless simply because they are not human-readable documents.

---

### 22. AI Model Memorization Testing

Models should be tested for inappropriate memorization of personal information.

Testing may evaluate whether the model can reproduce:

* Personal identifiers
* Private conversations
* Sensitive records
* Training examples
* Confidential documents

The risk is particularly important for models trained or fine-tuned using sensitive information.

---

### 23. Training Data Privacy Testing

AI training datasets should be evaluated for privacy risks.

Testing may examine:

* Personal data inclusion
* Sensitive data inclusion
* Unauthorized records
* Data provenance
* Consent or legal basis where applicable
* Data minimization
* Retention requirements

---

### 24. Fine-Tuning Privacy Testing

Fine-tuning can introduce privacy risks when sensitive data is incorporated into model adaptation.

Testing should evaluate whether fine-tuning causes:

* Memorization
* Data extraction
* Increased disclosure
* Cross-user leakage
* Sensitive information reproduction

---

### 25. Inference Privacy Testing

AI systems may reveal sensitive information without directly storing or returning the original record.

Privacy testing should consider whether users can infer:

* Individual attributes
* Membership in sensitive datasets
* Confidential relationships
* Sensitive classifications

Inference risks should be evaluated according to the intended system and threat model.

---

### 26. Membership Inference Testing

Where relevant, privacy testing should evaluate whether an attacker can determine whether a specific individual's information was included in a dataset.

Testing may examine:

* Model responses
* Confidence behavior
* Statistical differences
* Query patterns

The applicability depends on the model architecture and data-processing context.

---

### 27. Data Extraction Testing

AI systems should be tested for unintended extraction of sensitive information.

Testing may use controlled attempts to determine whether the system reveals:

* Personal records
* Training examples
* Private conversations
* Confidential documents
* Secrets

Testing must be performed within authorized environments and boundaries.

---

### 28. Prompt Privacy Testing

Prompts should be evaluated for unintended exposure of personal information.

Testing may identify:

* Excessive context
* Unnecessary personal attributes
* Hidden system data
* User information inserted into prompts
* Sensitive information passed to external models

---

### 29. Context Privacy Testing

AI context assembly should be tested for data minimization and authorization.

Testing should determine whether the model receives:

* Only relevant records
* Correct user context
* Correct tenant context
* Correct permissions
* No unnecessary personal information

---

### 30. Output Privacy Testing

AI outputs should be tested for unauthorized disclosure.

Testing may evaluate whether outputs reveal:

* Personal information
* Confidential information
* Hidden context
* Other users' data
* Internal records
* Sensitive attributes

Output filtering should be tested as a security and privacy control.

---

### 31. Privacy Leakage Testing

Privacy leakage testing evaluates whether information can escape intended boundaries.

Potential leakage channels include:

* Model output
* Logs
* Errors
* APIs
* Retrieval
* Memory
* Tool calls
* Analytics
* Monitoring
* Third-party services

---

### 32. Logging Privacy Testing

AI logs should be tested for excessive personal-data collection.

Testing should examine:

* Prompts
* Outputs
* User identifiers
* Tool parameters
* Retrieved documents
* Error messages
* Debug information

Logs can become a secondary privacy repository if not properly controlled.

---

### 33. Monitoring Privacy Testing

Monitoring systems should be evaluated to ensure that observability does not unnecessarily expose personal information.

Testing should examine:

* Metrics
* Traces
* Logs
* Alerts
* Dashboards
* SIEM events

Privacy-preserving telemetry should be used where possible.

---

### 34. Error and Exception Privacy Testing

Errors can unintentionally expose sensitive information.

Testing should evaluate:

* Error messages
* Stack traces
* Debug responses
* API errors
* Agent failures
* Tool failures

Production errors should not disclose information that the requester is not authorized to receive.

---

### 35. API Privacy Testing

AI APIs should be tested for privacy boundaries.

Testing should cover:

* Authentication
* Authorization
* Request data
* Response data
* Error handling
* Rate limits
* Tenant isolation

---

### 36. Third-Party AI Provider Privacy Testing

External AI providers should be included in privacy testing where personal information is transferred to them.

Testing should evaluate:

* Data transmission
* Provider processing
* Retention behavior
* Training usage
* Regional processing
* Deletion mechanisms
* Subprocessors

Provider behavior should be validated against contractual and organizational requirements where technically possible.

---

### 37. Data Retention Testing

AI systems must be tested to determine whether personal information is retained for the approved period.

Testing may examine:

* Conversations
* Prompts
* Outputs
* Memory
* Logs
* Backups
* Vector stores
* Provider-side storage

---

### 38. Data Deletion Testing

Deletion mechanisms should be tested rather than assumed to work.

Testing should verify whether deletion propagates appropriately across:

* Primary storage
* Databases
* Caches
* Memory
* Retrieval indexes
* Logs
* Backups
* Downstream systems

---

### 39. Data Subject Request Testing

Where applicable, AI systems should be tested for their ability to support privacy rights and related data-management processes.

Testing may cover:

* Data discovery
* Access
* Correction
* Deletion
* Restriction
* Export

The exact requirements depend on applicable law and organizational obligations.

---

### 40. Privacy Control Effectiveness Testing

Privacy controls must be tested for actual effectiveness.

Controls may include:

* Data masking
* Tokenization
* Encryption
* Access control
* Data-loss prevention
* Output filtering
* Retention controls
* Deletion controls

The existence of a privacy control does not prove that it works.

---

### 41. Privacy Configuration Testing

AI privacy configurations should be tested to ensure they match approved requirements.

Configuration may include:

* Logging
* Retention
* Memory
* Training settings
* Data-sharing options
* Provider settings
* Access policies

Configuration drift should be detectable.

---

### 42. Privacy Regression Testing

Previously identified privacy weaknesses should become regression tests where practical.

Regression testing should occur following:

* Model changes
* Dataset changes
* Prompt changes
* Application changes
* Provider changes
* Retrieval changes
* Memory changes

---

### 43. Privacy Test Evidence

Privacy testing must produce evidence sufficient to support assurance.

Evidence should identify:

* System
* Model
* Dataset
* Data flow
* Test scenario
* Tester
* Configuration
* Result
* Finding
* Limitations

Sensitive test evidence must itself be appropriately protected.

---

### 44. Privacy Findings and Defects

Privacy findings should document observed weaknesses and their potential impact.

A finding should include:

* Affected data
* Affected population
* Leakage mechanism
* Evidence
* Severity
* Root cause
* Remediation
* Retest status

---

### 45. Privacy Remediation

Privacy weaknesses should be addressed through controlled remediation.

Remediation may include:

* Data minimization
* Access-control changes
* Prompt changes
* Model changes
* Retrieval changes
* Memory changes
* Logging changes
* Provider configuration changes
* Deletion mechanisms

---

### 46. Privacy Retesting

Material privacy remediation must be retested.

Retesting should determine whether:

1. The original privacy weakness has been addressed.
2. Unauthorized disclosure is prevented.
3. Retention/deletion requirements remain satisfied.
4. New privacy weaknesses have not been introduced.
5. Security and functional properties remain acceptable.

---

### 47. Privacy Testing Independence

High-risk AI privacy assessments should receive appropriate independent validation.

Independent validation may involve:

* Privacy specialists
* AI security teams
* Data protection teams
* Internal audit
* Qualified external assessors

The system owner should not be the sole authority determining whether significant privacy risk is acceptable.

---

### 48. Continuous AI Privacy Evaluation

Privacy should be continuously evaluated where risk requires ongoing assurance.

Monitoring should consider:

* New data sources
* New data types
* Model changes
* Provider changes
* New retrieval sources
* New users
* New use cases
* Data-flow changes

---

### 49. AI Privacy Testing and Assurance

Privacy testing establishes evidence about defined privacy properties under defined conditions.

It does not independently establish:

* Legal compliance
* Complete security
* Fairness
* Reliability
* Overall AI safety

Privacy assurance requires interpretation of test evidence within the applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, organizational, and technical context.

---

### 50. Core Principle of AI Privacy Testing

**AI Privacy Testing must establish evidence that personal, sensitive, confidential, and otherwise protected information is collected, processed, transmitted, retrieved, generated, stored, retained, and deleted according to defined privacy requirements and authorized purposes. Testing must examine the complete AI data lifecycle and technical architecture, including applications, prompts, context, models, training data, fine-tuning, retrieval, vector stores, memory, tools, agents, APIs, logs, monitoring, backups, and external AI providers where they can affect privacy. Testing must evaluate data minimization, purpose limitation, access control, user and tenant isolation, memorization, inference, extraction, prompt and context exposure, output leakage, logging, retention, deletion, privacy rights processes, provider data handling, and privacy-control effectiveness according to risk. Sensitive test data must itself be protected, and fairness or privacy attributes used for controlled evaluation must not automatically become production inputs. Every test must be attributable and traceable to the exact system state, data flow, configuration, model, dataset, and test conditions evaluated. Privacy findings must be classified, remediated, independently validated where required, and retested. Previously identified privacy weaknesses should become regression tests where practical. Material changes to models, datasets, prompts, applications, retrieval, memory, tools, providers, data flows, logging, retention, or processing purposes must be capable of triggering renewed privacy testing and reassessment. Privacy evidence is conditional on the system state and processing conditions evaluated; passing a privacy test does not establish permanent privacy assurance or universal legal compliance.**
