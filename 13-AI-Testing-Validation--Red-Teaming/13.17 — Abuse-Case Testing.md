# 13.17 — AI Abuse-Case Testing

## Part 1 — AI Abuse-Case Testing Foundations

### 1. Definition of AI Abuse-Case Testing

AI Abuse-Case Testing is the structured, adversarial evaluation of how an AI system could be intentionally misused by legitimate users, malicious users, compromised identities, external attackers, insiders, automated agents, or other actors to produce outcomes outside the system's intended security, safety, privacy, compliance, or business boundaries.

Unlike functional testing, which asks **“Does the system perform its intended function?”**, abuse-case testing asks:

**“How could the system be intentionally used in a way that defeats its intended controls or creates unacceptable impact?”**

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Abuse-Case Testing

The purpose is to identify realistic misuse paths before they become operational incidents.

Testing should evaluate whether users or attackers can abuse:

* AI capabilities
* Prompts
* Models
* RAG
* Memory
* Tools
* Agents
* APIs
* Identities
* Privileges
* Workflows
* Data
* Integrations

The objective is to identify both direct abuse and compound abuse involving multiple system components.

---

### 3. AI Abuse-Case Testing vs Functional Testing

Functional testing validates intended behavior.

Abuse-case testing deliberately explores unintended or prohibited behavior.

```text id="abuse003a"
Functional Testing
       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

Abuse-Case Testing
       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

       |
       v

```

An AI system can pass functional testing while remaining highly vulnerable to abuse.

### 50. Core Principle of AI Abuse-Case Testing

AI Abuse-Case Testing is a **risk-based, adversarial, evidence-producing discipline for determining whether legitimate AI capabilities can be intentionally misused to violate security, privacy, safety, operational, compliance, or business boundaries**.

The assessment should cover the complete chain:

```text id="abuse050f"
Actor
  |
  v
Intent
  |
  v
AI Capability
  |
  v
Abuse Technique
  |
  v
AI Behavior
  |
  v
Security Boundary
  |
  v
Authorization
  |
  v
Execution
  |
  v
Impact
  |
  v
Detection
  |
  v
Containment
  |
  v
Recovery
```

The fundamental principles are:

**Legitimate access ≠ unrestricted use.
AI capability ≠ authorization.
Model output ≠ permission.
Tool capability ≠ tool permission.
Agent capability ≠ agent authority.
User identity ≠ unlimited authority.
Successful misuse ≠ automatic enterprise compromise.
A demonstrated abuse case ≠ permanent risk.
Passing an abuse test ≠ permanent trust.
Testing capability ≠ security assurance.**

Every significant abuse scenario should be attributable to the exact **AI version, configuration, identity, authorization state, data, prompt/context, RAG state, memory, tools, agent configuration, and execution environment** involved.

The objective is therefore not simply to discover ways an AI system can be misused, but to establish whether **independent authorization, least privilege, data isolation, security controls, monitoring, containment, and recovery mechanisms prevent legitimate AI capabilities from becoming uncontrolled enterprise capabilities**.
