# 11.19 — Agentic AI Risk Management and Governance

## 1. Definition of Agentic AI Risk Management

Agentic AI risk management is the structured process of identifying, assessing, treating, monitoring, and governing risks arising from AI agents and autonomous systems.

Unlike conventional AI risk management, agentic AI risk management must account for systems that can:

* Make decisions
* Select tools
* Access data
* Execute transactions
* Delegate tasks
* Communicate with other agents
* Modify persistent state
* Operate continuously
* Adapt their behavior

The central objective is to ensure that **autonomous capability remains within the enterprise's defined risk appetite and governance boundaries**.

---

## 2. Agentic AI Risk as an Enterprise Risk

Agentic AI risk should not be treated solely as a technical AI risk.

It can affect:

* Cybersecurity
* Privacy
* Financial operations
* Compliance
* Legal obligations
* Business continuity
* Operational resilience
* Reputation
* Customer trust
* Enterprise governance

Therefore:

```text id="a7k4mx"
Agentic AI Risk
       |
       +--> Cyber Risk
       +--> Data Risk
       +--> Operational Risk
       +--> Financial Risk
       +--> Compliance Risk
       +--> Legal Risk
       +--> Strategic Risk
       |
       v
Enterprise Risk Management
```

---

## 3. Agentic AI Risk Management vs Conventional AI Risk Management

Conventional AI risk management often focuses on model behavior, data quality, bias, privacy, and model performance.

Agentic AI risk management additionally considers autonomous execution.

| Conventional AI    | Agentic AI                              |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Model output       | Model-driven action                     |
| Prediction         | Decision + execution                    |
| Static interaction | Continuous interaction                  |
| Limited authority  | Potential operational authority         |
| Model risk         | Model + agent + tool + transaction risk |
| Output monitoring  | End-to-end behavior monitoring          |

The security concern therefore extends from **what the model produces** to **what the autonomous system can cause**.

---

## 4. Agentic AI Risk Management as a Governance Discipline

Risk management provides the governance structure through which organizations determine whether an agent should be:

* Approved
* Restricted
* Modified
* Monitored
* Suspended
* Reauthorized
* Retired

Risk management should operate throughout the agent lifecycle rather than only before deployment.

---

## 5. Agentic AI Risk Identification

Risk identification should consider the complete agent ecosystem.

```text id="f9m2vx"
  +--> Memory
  +--> Tools
  +--> Data

  |
```

## 6. Agent Risk Subject

The agent itself should be treated as a distinct enterprise risk subject.

A risk record should identify:

* Agent
* Owner
* Purpose
* Environment
* Autonomy
* Authority
* Data access
* Tools
* Dependencies
* Business impact
* Risk tier

This prevents agent risk from disappearing inside broader application or model inventories.

---

## 7. Agent Risk Appetite

Organizations should establish explicit risk appetite for agentic AI.

Risk appetite should define what levels of autonomous behavior are acceptable.

For example:

```text id="w5q8pn"
Low Risk
 -> Broad Automation

Moderate Risk
 -> Restricted Automation

High Risk
 -> Human Approval

Critical Risk
 -> Limited / Prohibited Autonomy
```

Risk appetite should be aligned with business impact and regulatory requirements.

---

## 8. Agent Risk Tolerance

Risk tolerance defines acceptable deviation from approved operating conditions.

Examples include:

* Maximum transaction value
* Maximum data volume
* Maximum execution duration
* Maximum delegation depth
* Maximum tool access
* Maximum external communication
* Maximum autonomous authority

Risk tolerance should be measurable wherever practical.

---

## 9. Agent Risk Classification

Agents should be classified according to their potential impact.

Classification factors may include:

* Autonomy
* Privilege
* Data sensitivity
* Transaction authority
* External connectivity
* Business criticality
* Reversibility
* Delegation capability

A high-autonomy agent with financial authority should receive significantly stronger governance than a read-only information agent.

---

## 10. Agent Risk Tiering

A practical risk-tiering model may include:

| Tier   | Characteristics                                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tier 1 | Low-impact, read-only, limited autonomy                 |
| Tier 2 | Moderate data or workflow authority                     |
| Tier 3 | High-impact tools, sensitive data, or transactions      |
| Tier 4 | Critical autonomous authority or enterprise-wide impact |

Higher tiers should require stronger controls and assurance.

---

## 11. Agent Purpose

Every agent should have a clearly defined business purpose.

The purpose should specify:

* Intended function
* Authorized users
* Approved data
* Approved tools
* Approved environments
* Expected outcomes

Purpose ambiguity creates unnecessary authority and monitoring challenges.

---

## 12. Purpose Limitation

Agent capabilities should remain limited to the approved business purpose.

For example:

```text id="q6x3mk"
Purpose:
Resolve customer support tickets

Authorized:
Read ticket
Update ticket
Send response

Unauthorized:
Modify IAM
Export customer database
Create payment
```

Purpose limitation prevents agents from gradually becoming general-purpose enterprise operators.

---

## 13. Agent Authority Risk

Agent authority should be assessed independently from model capability.

A capable model may be able to generate many possible actions, while the agent should only have authority to perform approved actions.

```text id="p8v4nz"
Model Capability
       |
       v
Possible Actions
       |
       X
Not Automatically Authorized
       |
       v
Enterprise Authorization
       |
       v
Permitted Actions
```

---

## 14. Agent Autonomy Risk

Autonomy increases the potential consequences of incorrect or malicious behavior.

Risk generally increases as the agent moves from:

```text id="j4m9vx"
Recommendation
     |
     v
Assisted Action
     |
     v
Human-Approved Action
     |
     v
Supervised Autonomous Action
     |
     v
Fully Autonomous Action
```

Higher autonomy requires stronger controls.

---

## 15. Agent Capability vs Agent Authority

Agent capability describes what the system can technically perform.

Agent authority describes what it is permitted to perform.

These must remain separate.

```text id="n7x2qc"
Capability
    ≠
Authority
    ≠
Authorization
    ≠
Execution
```

This distinction is foundational to agentic AI risk governance.

---

## 16. Agent Risk Register

Each significant agent should have an associated risk register.

The register may contain:

| Field         | Example                  |
| ------------- | ------------------------ |
| Agent         | Finance-Agent-01         |
| Risk          | Unauthorized payment     |
| Likelihood    | Medium                   |
| Impact        | High                     |
| Inherent Risk | High                     |
| Controls      | Authorization + approval |
| Residual Risk | Medium                   |
| Owner         | Finance Security         |
| Status        | Accepted                 |

The risk register should remain current as the agent changes.

## 17. Inherent Agent Risk

Inherent risk represents risk before considering mitigating controls.

Factors may include:

* Autonomy
* Privilege
* Data sensitivity
* Transaction authority
* External connectivity
* Persistence
* Delegation

Inherent risk provides a baseline for determining required controls.

## 18. Residual Agent Risk

Residual risk is the remaining risk after controls are applied.

```text id="v3q8mk"
Inherent Risk
      |
      v
Security Controls
      |
      v
Residual Risk
      |
      v

```

Residual risk should be explicitly documented rather than assumed to be eliminated.

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability
* Financial resources
* Customers
* Employees
* Business operations
* Compliance

* Existing threats
* Control strength
* Agent autonomy
* External connectivity
* Privilege

## 25. Agent Risk Treatment

Risk treatment options include:

* Avoid
* Reduce
* Transfer
* Accept

For agentic systems, reduction may involve:

* Lowering autonomy
* Removing tools
* Reducing permissions
* Adding human approval
* Restricting data
* Limiting transactions
* Increasing monitoring

---

## 26. Agent Risk Avoidance

Some autonomous use cases may be inappropriate because their potential impact exceeds the organization's risk appetite.

Examples may include autonomous execution of:

* Highly destructive actions
* Certain critical infrastructure changes
* Unrecoverable financial operations
* Sensitive administrative operations

Risk avoidance may therefore require prohibiting autonomous execution.

---

## 27. Agent Risk Reduction

Risk can be reduced by limiting agent authority.

```text id="c7m4zx"

     |
     v

     |
     v
Data Reduction
     |
     v
Transaction Limits
     |
```

Reducing authority is often more reliable than attempting to make the model perfectly safe.

## 28. Agent Risk Transfer

Some agentic AI risks may be partially transferred through:

* Contracts
* Insurance
* Provider agreements
* Service-level agreements
* Third-party assurance

Risk transfer does not eliminate the organization's responsibility for maintaining appropriate internal controls.

## 29. Agent Risk Acceptance

Residual risk may be formally accepted when it falls within approved risk appetite.

Risk acceptance should identify:

* Risk
* Business justification
* Residual exposure
* Compensating controls
* Risk owner
* Expiration/review date

The agent itself cannot accept its own risk.

## 49. Core Principles of Agentic AI Risk Management and Governance

The core principles are:

1. **Every production agent should be treated as an identifiable risk subject.**
2. **Agent risk should be assessed across the complete agent ecosystem.**
3. **Autonomy should increase governance requirements.**
4. **Agent capability must remain separate from agent authority.**
5. **Agent purpose should be explicitly defined and limited.**
6. **Risk appetite should determine acceptable autonomous behavior.**
7. **High-impact agents require stronger controls and assurance.**
8. **Risk assessments must consider both technical and business impact.**
9. **Residual risk must be explicitly accepted or treated.**
10. **Agent changes should trigger risk reassessment when material.**
11. **Behavioral monitoring should continuously inform risk management.**
12. **Agent incidents should update the agent's risk profile.**
13. **Third-party and supply-chain risks must be included.**
14. **Risk decisions must be supported by evidence.**
15. **Risk should remain traceable to security controls throughout the lifecycle.**

---

## 50. Core Principle of Agentic AI Risk Management and Governance

**Agentic AI risk must be governed according to what an autonomous system can actually do, what authority it possesses, what consequences its actions can create, and how effectively the enterprise can control and observe those actions.**

The fundamental governance relationship is:

```text id="x8v3qm"
Agent Capability
       |
       v
Agent Authority
       |
       v
Business Impact
       |
       v
Risk Assessment
       |
       v
Security Controls
       |
       v
Monitoring
       |
       v
Residual Risk
       |
       v
Risk Acceptance / Treatment
       |
       v
Continuous Reassessment
```

The central principle is:

**The greater the autonomy, authority, persistence, connectivity, and potential impact of an agent, the stronger its governance, security controls, monitoring, evidence, and independent assurance must be.**


## Part 2 — Security Controls

### 51. Agentic AI Risk Management Security Control Architecture

Agentic AI risk management requires controls that continuously connect identified risks to technical enforcement, monitoring, and response.

```text id="r7m4qx"
Risk Identification
       |
       v
Risk Assessment
       |
       v
Security Requirements
       |
       v
Control Implementation
       |
       v
Monitoring
       |
       v
Control Effectiveness
       |
       v
Residual Risk
```

The architecture should prevent risk assessments from becoming static documents disconnected from actual agent behavior.

---

### 52. Agent Risk Identity Control

Every risk record should be bound to a specific agent identity.

The control should associate:

* Agent ID
* Agent instance
* Owner
* Environment
* Business purpose
* Risk tier
* Lifecycle state

This prevents risk from being assigned only to an abstract AI application.

---

### 53. Agent Risk Authentication

Risk-management systems should authenticate systems and personnel submitting or modifying agent risk information.

Controls should verify:

* User identity
* Agent identity
* System identity
* Submission authority
* Change authority

Unauthorized users or agents should not be able to alter risk classifications.

---

### 54. Strong Risk Management Authentication

High-impact risk decisions should require strong authentication.

Examples include:

* Risk acceptance
* Critical-risk classification
* Autonomy approval
* Major exception approval
* Risk-tier reduction

Strong authentication provides assurance that significant governance decisions are attributable.

---

### 55. Fine-Grained Risk Authorization

Access to modify risk records should be granular.

```text id="k6x9vp"
Business Owner
 -> Business Risk

AI Security
 -> Security Risk

GRC
 -> Governance Record

Executive Risk Owner
 -> Risk Acceptance
```

No role should automatically receive unrestricted authority over all agent risk decisions.

---

### 56. Independent Risk Decision Service

Critical risk decisions should be evaluated independently from the agent runtime.

```text id="m8q3zn"
Agent
  |
  v
Risk Signal
  |
  v
Independent Risk Service
  |
  v
Control Decision
```

The agent should not be able to determine its own risk classification or risk acceptance.

---

### 57. Agent Risk Classification Allowlisting

Approved risk classifications should be defined centrally.

For example:

```text id="v5c8mx"
Tier 1
Tier 2
Tier 3
Tier 4
```

Agents should not be able to assign themselves a lower risk tier.

---

### 58. Autonomy Classification Controls

Autonomy levels should be explicitly controlled.

```text id="p7x4qm"
      |
      v
Human Approved
      |
      v
Supervised Autonomous
      |
      v
```

Moving an agent to a higher autonomy level should require appropriate authorization.

### 59. Agent Capability Segmentation

Capabilities should be segmented according to risk.

Examples include:

* Data access
* Tool use
* Transaction execution
* Administrative operations
* Delegation
* External communication
* Code execution

Risk controls should prevent unnecessary aggregation of high-risk capabilities.

### 62. Data Access Risk Controls

* Data classification
* Access volume
* User context
* Tenant
* Purpose
* Retention
* External transfer

### 63. Transaction Risk Limits

Autonomous transactions should have defined risk limits.

Limits may include:

* Transaction value
* Transaction frequency
* Daily aggregate
* Monthly aggregate
* Transaction type
* Counterparty

Exceeding thresholds should trigger additional controls.

### 64. Cumulative Risk Limits

```text id="j4w9mc"
 +

 +

 +
```

### 65. Time-Bounded Risk Authority

For example:


### 66. Short-Lived Risk Credentials

Systems that access sensitive risk-management functions should use short-lived credentials where appropriate.

This reduces the consequences of credential compromise.

### 67. Credential Isolation

Risk-management credentials should be isolated from:

* Agent credentials
* Tool credentials
* User credentials
* Application secrets

A compromised agent should not automatically gain the ability to modify its own risk record.

---

### 68. Secrets Management

Secrets used by risk-management systems should be centrally protected.

Controls should include:

* Secure storage
* Access control
* Rotation
* Expiration
* Revocation
* Usage monitoring

---

### 69. Risk Context Security Controls

Risk decisions should be based on trusted context.

Relevant context includes:

* Agent identity
* Business purpose
* Authority
* Data
* Tools
* Environment
* User
* Transaction
* Current risk state

Untrusted agent-generated claims should not automatically become authoritative risk inputs.

### 70. Prompt and Instruction Integrity

Risk-sensitive agent instructions should be protected against unauthorized modification.

Controls should monitor:

* System instructions
* Security policies
* Risk policies
* Governance instructions

### 71. Untrusted Input Isolation

* Attempts to alter risk classification
* Attempts to suppress findings
* Instructions to bypass controls

Risk governance should never rely solely on agent-generated reasoning.

* Approved governance platforms

Unexpected external destinations should be blocked or investigated.

### 84. External Communication Controls

Agentic systems participating in risk workflows should have controlled external communications.

Controls should define:

* Approved providers
* Approved endpoints
* Data classifications
* Communication purposes
* Logging

### 85. Code Execution Isolation

Where agents perform automated risk analysis involving code execution, execution should occur in isolated environments.

Controls should limit:

* File access
* Network access
* Credentials
* Runtime privileges
* Resource consumption

### 86. Database Security Controls

### 88. Human Approval Controls

The preview should show:

* Agent
* Risk

* Controls
* Residual risk
* Proposed decision
* Expiration

Where possible, risk-management changes should be reversible.

### 92. Agent Execution Limits

### 94. Recursive Delegation Controls

### 95. Agent-to-Agent Risk Authentication

### 103. Concurrency Controls

The number of simultaneous risk-management operations should be controlled.

* Escalated

**Agentic AI risk controls must continuously connect risk identification, authority, technical enforcement, monitoring, and response so that an agent's actual behavior remains aligned with its approved risk posture.**

The control relationship is:

```text id="r6q2xm"

 |
 v

 |
 v
Control
 |
 v
Enforcement
 |
 v

 |
 v

 |
 v
Response
 |
 v
Residual Risk
 |
 v
Reassessment
```

**Risk management must not remain a static governance document. It must operate as a continuously enforced security control system around autonomous authority.**
