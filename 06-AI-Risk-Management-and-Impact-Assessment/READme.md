## Overview

Chapter 06 provides a structured foundation for understanding and applying **AI Governance and AI Risk Management** from a cybersecurity, GRC, compliance, and enterprise-risk perspective.

The chapter explains how organizations can govern AI throughout its lifecycle—from identifying and classifying AI use cases, understanding AI-specific risks, establishing governance requirements, implementing controls, performing risk assessments, determining residual risk, and making formal risk decisions.

The chapter is designed to help a cybersecurity/GRC professional move beyond simply understanding AI technology and instead understand **how AI is governed, assessed, controlled, monitored, and integrated into enterprise GRC**.

---

## Learning Objectives

By completing this chapter, you should be able to:

* Explain the purpose and principles of AI governance.
* Understand the AI lifecycle and associated governance requirements.
* Identify major categories of AI risk.
* Distinguish AI risk from traditional cybersecurity and enterprise risk.
* Perform structured AI risk identification.
* Develop AI risk scenarios.
* Assess inherent and residual risk.
* Evaluate AI controls and their effectiveness.
* Apply risk appetite to AI governance decisions.
* Define AI risk treatment and acceptance processes.
* Establish AI governance roles and responsibilities.
* Integrate AI risk into enterprise GRC.
* Establish AI inventories and risk registers.
* Define AI monitoring and reassessment requirements.
* Communicate AI risks to technical teams, management, and executives.
* Apply a senior-level AI GRC approach during professional interviews.

The chapter begins by establishing what AI governance means and why organizations need a structured governance model for AI.

* Accountability.

## 5. AI Risk Scenarios

Rather than using vague statements such as:

> "AI security risk."

A mature GRC approach develops specific scenarios such as:

> **"An attacker exploits prompt-injection weaknesses in an externally accessible AI agent to cause unauthorized access to sensitive customer information."**

This makes the risk:

* Assessable.
* Actionable.
* Assignable.
* Measurable.
* Traceable to controls.

# Senior GRC Perspective

A key purpose of this chapter is to develop the ability to think beyond individual controls.

A technical professional may ask:

> "Does the AI model have adequate security?"

A GRC professional should additionally ask:

* Who owns the risk?
* What is the business purpose?
* What could go wrong?
* What is the inherent risk?
* What controls exist?
* Are those controls effective?
* What residual risk remains?
* Is it within appetite?
* Who can accept the risk?
* What evidence supports the decision?
* How will the risk be monitored?
* What triggers reassessment?

This is the transition from **technical security thinking to enterprise GRC thinking**.

# Interview Anchor

The core AI GRC sequence to remember is:

> **Inventory → Context → Risk → Score → Controls → Validate → Residual → Appetite → Decision → Monitor → Reassess**

A strong senior-level statement is:

> **"I don't treat AI risk assessment as a one-time questionnaire. I embed it into the AI lifecycle—from intake and risk tiering through assessment, control validation, residual-risk evaluation, approval, continuous monitoring and reassessment."**

# Key Takeaways

After completing Chapter 06, you should understand that:

1. **AI governance is broader than AI security.**
2. **AI risk must be assessed within its business context.**
3. **AI risk assessment should be proportional to the use case's risk.**
4. **Inherent risk should be understood before evaluating controls.**
5. **Controls must be assessed for both design and operating effectiveness.**
6. **Residual risk is the risk remaining after controls.**
7. **Residual risk must be compared against risk appetite.**
8. **Risk ownership belongs with the accountable business/risk owner.**
9. **GRC provides methodology, oversight, challenge, and governance—not business ownership.**
10. **AI approval is not the end of the risk lifecycle.**
11. **AI risk must be continuously monitored.**
12. **Material changes and incidents should trigger reassessment.**
13. **AI governance should integrate with existing enterprise GRC processes.**
14. **Every material AI risk should have traceability from risk through control, evidence, treatment, and decision.**
15. **The ultimate objective is informed, accountable, and risk-based AI adoption.**
