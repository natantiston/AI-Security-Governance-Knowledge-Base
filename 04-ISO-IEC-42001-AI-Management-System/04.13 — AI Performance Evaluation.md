# 04.13 — AI Performance Evaluation

## 1. Introduction to AI Performance Evaluation

AI Performance Evaluation is the systematic process of determining whether an AI system and the associated AI Management System processes are achieving their intended objectives and operating according to established requirements.

Performance evaluation provides evidence that AI governance is functioning in practice rather than existing only as documented policies and procedures.

It may consider:

* AI system performance.
* AI objectives.
* AI risks and opportunities.
* Control effectiveness.
* Operational performance.
* Stakeholder outcomes.
* Compliance.
* Resource effectiveness.
* Management-system effectiveness.

Performance evaluation should be proportionate to the nature, scale, complexity and significance of the AI system.

---

## 2. Purpose of AI Performance Evaluation

The purpose of performance evaluation is to provide reliable information for determining whether intended results are being achieved.

It can help the organization determine:

* Whether AI objectives are being achieved.
* Whether AI systems perform as intended.
* Whether controls are effective.
* Whether risks remain within acceptable boundaries.
* Whether processes are operating consistently.
* Whether resources are adequate.
* Whether improvement is required.

Performance evaluation therefore supports evidence-based management decisions.

---

## 3. AI Performance Evaluation Within the AIMS

Performance evaluation forms part of the management cycle of the AI Management System.

A simplified relationship is:

```text id="h7x3qm"
AI Objectives
      ↓
Operational Activities
      ↓
Performance Measurement
      ↓
Analysis
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Management Decision
      ↓
Improvement
```

This connects strategic AI objectives with actual operational results.

---

## 4. AI Performance Evaluation and ISO/IEC 42001

Within an AI Management System, performance evaluation provides a structured mechanism for determining whether the system's requirements and objectives are being fulfilled.

It supports the organization's ability to:

* Monitor AIMS performance.
* Evaluate AI-related results.
* Assess management-system effectiveness.
* Identify deficiencies.
* Support management review.
* Drive continual improvement.

Performance evaluation should therefore be integrated into the wider AIMS rather than treated as an isolated reporting exercise.

---

## 5. AI Performance Criteria

Performance evaluation requires appropriate criteria against which results can be assessed.

Criteria may include:

* AI objectives.
* Policies.
* Performance targets.
* Service requirements.
* Risk thresholds.
* Control requirements.
* Regulatory obligations.
* Contractual requirements.
* Stakeholder expectations.

Without defined criteria, measured information may be difficult to interpret consistently.

---

## 6. AI Performance Indicators

Performance indicators provide measurable or observable information about AI performance.

Examples include:

* Accuracy.
* Precision.
* Recall.
* Error rate.
* Availability.
* Response time.
* Processing volume.
* Incident frequency.
* Override rate.
* Complaint rate.

Indicators should be selected according to the AI system's purpose and relevant objectives.

---

## 7. AI Key Performance Indicators

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) can be used to evaluate whether AI systems are delivering intended business outcomes.

Examples include:

* Processing efficiency.
* Cost reduction.
* Productivity improvement.
* Service response time.
* Customer experience.
* Decision turnaround time.
* Automation rate.

KPIs should not be considered in isolation from risk and control indicators.

---

## 8. AI Key Risk Indicators

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) can provide information about emerging or increasing AI-related risks.

Examples include:

* Increasing model errors.
* Increasing override rates.
* Rising complaints.
* Increased security incidents.
* Data-quality degradation.
* Unexpected model behavior.
* Increased policy exceptions.

KPIs show whether desired outcomes are being achieved, while KRIs can indicate whether risk conditions are changing.

---

## 9. AI Control Performance Indicators

Control performance indicators can help determine whether AI controls are operating as intended.

Examples include:

* Percentage of required reviews completed.
* Percentage of systems with current assessments.
* Number of unauthorized changes.
* Percentage of access reviews completed.
* Number of overdue corrective actions.
* Percentage of required training completed.

Control indicators provide evidence about the effectiveness of the governance environment surrounding AI.

---

## 10. AI Objective Performance

AI objectives should have appropriate mechanisms for determining whether they are being achieved.

For example:

```text id="c2q8vx"
AI Objective
     ↓
Target
     ↓
Indicator
     ↓
Measurement
     ↓
Evaluation
     ↓
Achievement Status
```

This provides a direct connection between organizational objectives and measurable results.

---

## 11. AI Performance Baselines

A baseline provides a reference point against which future performance can be compared.

Baselines may represent:

* Initial system performance.
* Historical performance.
* Approved benchmark.
* Industry benchmark.
* Pre-AI process performance.

Baseline information can help determine whether performance has improved, degraded or remained stable.

---

## 12. AI Performance Targets

Performance targets establish desired levels of achievement.

Examples include:

* Minimum accuracy.
* Maximum error rate.
* Maximum response time.
* Minimum availability.
* Maximum incident frequency.

Targets should be realistic, measurable where appropriate and aligned with organizational objectives.

---

## 13. AI Performance Thresholds

Thresholds define levels at which additional action may be required.

For example:

```text id="m5v9qp"
Target Range
      ↓
Normal Operation

Warning Threshold
      ↓
Investigation

Critical Threshold
      ↓
Escalation / Corrective Action
```

Thresholds should reflect the consequences associated with poor AI performance.

---

## 14. AI Performance Measurement

Measurement involves collecting information about actual AI performance.

Measurements may be:

* Quantitative.
* Qualitative.
* Automated.
* Manual.
* Continuous.
* Periodic.

The measurement approach should be appropriate to the system and the decision that the information will support.

---

## 15. AI Measurement Accuracy

Performance measurements should be sufficiently reliable for their intended purpose.

Potential measurement problems include:

* Incomplete data.
* Incorrect calculations.
* Poor sampling.
* Inconsistent definitions.
* Unreliable data sources.

Measurement methods should therefore be appropriately controlled.

---

## 16. AI Measurement Frequency

Measurement frequency should reflect the rate at which performance can change and the consequences of delayed detection.

Measurement may occur:

* Continuously.
* In real time.
* Daily.
* Weekly.
* Monthly.
* Quarterly.
* At defined lifecycle events.

Higher-risk AI systems may require more frequent monitoring and evaluation.

---

## 17. AI Performance Data Sources

Performance information may originate from multiple sources.

Sources may include:

* AI system logs.
* Monitoring platforms.
* Operational databases.
* User feedback.
* Incident-management systems.
* Security systems.
* Audit results.
* Assessment records.

Multiple sources may provide a more complete picture of AI performance.

---

## 18. AI Performance Data Quality

Performance evaluation depends on reliable data.

Relevant quality dimensions may include:

* Accuracy.
* Completeness.
* Consistency.
* Timeliness.
* Relevance.
* Integrity.

Poor-quality measurement data can result in incorrect conclusions about AI performance.

---

## 19. AI Performance Measurement Scope

The organization should determine what aspects of performance require measurement.

Scope may include:

* Technical performance.
* Business performance.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Fairness.
* Reliability.
* Availability.
* User experience.
* Governance performance.

Measurement scope should reflect the AI system's purpose and risk profile.

---

## 20. AI Technical Performance

Technical performance may include characteristics such as:

* Accuracy.
* Precision.
* Recall.
* Latency.
* Throughput.
* Availability.
* Reliability.
* Resource utilization.

Technical measures should be appropriate to the AI technology and intended application.

---

## 21. AI Business Performance

Business performance considers whether AI contributes to intended organizational outcomes.

Examples include:

* Increased productivity.
* Reduced processing time.
* Improved service quality.
* Reduced operational cost.
* Improved decision support.

Business performance should be evaluated alongside risk and control outcomes.

---

## 22. AI Security Performance

Security performance evaluation may examine whether security controls continue to protect the AI environment.

Indicators may include:

* Security incidents.
* Unauthorized access attempts.
* Vulnerabilities.
* Security alerts.
* Access-control exceptions.
* Model-security events.

Security performance should connect with the organization's broader cybersecurity monitoring processes.

---

## 23. AI Privacy Performance

Where personal information is involved, performance evaluation may include privacy-related measures.

Examples include:

* Privacy incidents.
* Unauthorized disclosures.
* Data-subject requests.
* Retention exceptions.
* Privacy assessment findings.

Privacy performance should be evaluated against applicable privacy requirements and organizational controls.

---

## 24. AI Fairness Performance

Where fairness is relevant, performance evaluation may examine whether outcomes differ materially across relevant groups.

Possible measures include:

* Error-rate differences.
* Outcome differences.
* Approval-rate differences.
* False-positive differences.
* False-negative differences.

The selected measures should reflect the AI system's purpose and applicable fairness considerations.

---

## 25. AI Reliability Performance

Reliability evaluation examines whether an AI system performs consistently under expected operating conditions.

It may consider:

* Failure frequency.
* Unexpected behavior.
* Service interruptions.
* Output consistency.
* Recovery performance.

Reliability expectations should reflect system criticality.

---

## 26. AI Availability Performance

Availability measures whether the AI system is accessible when required.

Metrics may include:

* Uptime.
* Downtime.
* Service interruptions.
* Mean time to recovery.
* Availability against service-level targets.

Availability should be evaluated against business requirements.

---

## 27. AI User Performance

User interaction can provide important information about AI effectiveness.

Evaluation may consider:

* User satisfaction.
* Adoption.
* Error rates.
* Training effectiveness.
* User complaints.
* Override behavior.

User performance information can reveal weaknesses that technical measurements alone may not identify.

---

## 28. AI Human Oversight Performance

Where human oversight is required, the organization should evaluate whether it is functioning effectively.

Measures may include:

* Review completion.
* Override rates.
* Review quality.
* Escalation frequency.
* Reviewer response time.
* Reviewer competence.

The objective is to determine whether human oversight provides meaningful control.

---

## 29. AI Output Quality

AI output quality should be evaluated according to the intended use of the system.

Quality may involve:

* Accuracy.
* Relevance.
* Completeness.
* Consistency.
* Timeliness.
* Appropriateness.

Output-quality requirements should reflect the consequences of incorrect or unsuitable outputs.

---

## 30. AI Performance Evaluation Evidence

Performance evaluation should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Evidence may include:

* Performance reports.
* System metrics.
* Monitoring records.
* Testing results.
* Audit findings.
* Incident records.
* User feedback.
* Management reports.

Reliable evidence enables management to make informed decisions about AI performance.
