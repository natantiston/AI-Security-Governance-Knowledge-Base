# 04.11 — Operational Planning and Control

## 1. Introduction to Operational Planning and Control

Operational planning and control establishes how the organization translates the AIMS requirements, AI objectives, risk treatments, policies and controls into controlled operational activities.

The purpose is to ensure that AI-related processes are:

* Planned.
* Defined.
* Authorized.
* Implemented.
* Controlled.
* Monitored.
* Evaluated.
* Updated when necessary.

Operational planning connects strategic AI governance with day-to-day execution.

---

## 2. Purpose of Operational Planning

Operational planning provides a structured basis for determining how AI-related activities will be performed.

It helps the organization determine:

* What needs to be performed.
* Who is responsible.
* What resources are required.
* What controls apply.
* What risks must be addressed.
* What evidence must be retained.
* How performance will be evaluated.

Effective planning reduces the possibility that AI activities will operate outside established governance requirements.

---

## 3. Operational Control Within the AIMS

Operational control means establishing mechanisms that keep AI-related activities within approved boundaries.

Controls may address:

* AI system development.
* AI acquisition.
* AI deployment.
* AI operation.
* AI monitoring.
* AI modification.
* AI retirement.
* Third-party AI services.

Operational controls should reflect the organization's AI risks and objectives.

## 7. Operational Planning and Risk Treatment

Risk treatment decisions should be translated into operational requirements.

For example:

**Risk:** AI-generated decisions may be incorrect.

**Treatment:** Mandatory human review.

**Operational control:** Personnel must review specified AI outputs before the decision is finalized.

This demonstrates how risk management becomes an operational activity.
