# 04.11 — Operational Planning and Control

## 1. Introduction to Operational Planning and Control

Operational planning and control establishes how the organization translates the AIMS requirements, AI objectives, risk treatments, policies and controls into controlled operational activities.

The purpose is to ensure that AI-related processes are:

* Planned.
* Defined.
* Authorized.
* Implemented.
* Controlled.
* Monitored.
* Evaluated.
* Updated when necessary.

Operational planning connects strategic AI governance with day-to-day execution.

---

## 2. Purpose of Operational Planning

Operational planning provides a structured basis for determining how AI-related activities will be performed.

It helps the organization determine:

* What needs to be performed.
* Who is responsible.
* What resources are required.
* What controls apply.
* What risks must be addressed.
* What evidence must be retained.
* How performance will be evaluated.

Effective planning reduces the possibility that AI activities will operate outside established governance requirements.

---

## 3. Operational Control Within the AIMS

Operational control means establishing mechanisms that keep AI-related activities within approved boundaries.

Controls may address:

* AI system development.
* AI acquisition.
* AI deployment.
* AI operation.
* AI monitoring.
* AI modification.
* AI retirement.
* Third-party AI services.

Operational controls should reflect the organization's AI risks and objectives.

---

## 4. Operational Planning and the AI Lifecycle

Operational planning should consider relevant stages of the AI lifecycle.

```text
Planning
   ↓
Design
   ↓
Development / Acquisition
   ↓
Testing
   ↓
Deployment
   ↓
Operation
   ↓
Monitoring
   ↓
Change
   ↓
Retirement
```

The controls required at each stage may differ according to the AI system and its risk.

---

## 5. Operational Planning and AI Objectives

Operational activities should support established AI objectives.

For example:

```text
AI Objective
    ↓
Operational Requirement
    ↓
Activity
    ↓
Control
    ↓
Performance Indicator
    ↓
Evaluation
```

This allows management to connect operational execution with strategic AI governance.

---

## 6. Operational Planning and AI Risk

Operational planning should incorporate identified AI risks.

Planning may determine:

* Applicable risks.
* Risk owners.
* Risk treatments.
* Required controls.
* Monitoring requirements.
* Escalation conditions.

Risk information should be available to personnel responsible for operational activities.

---

## 7. Operational Planning and Risk Treatment

Risk treatment decisions should be translated into operational requirements.

For example:

**Risk:** AI-generated decisions may be incorrect.

**Treatment:** Mandatory human review.

**Operational control:** Personnel must review specified AI outputs before the decision is finalized.

This demonstrates how risk management becomes an operational activity.

---

## 8. Operational Planning and Controls

Operational planning should identify the controls necessary to achieve intended outcomes.

Controls may include:

* Access controls.
* Human oversight.
* Data validation.
* Model testing.
* Approval workflows.
* Monitoring.
* Logging.
* Change management.
* Incident management.

Controls should be implemented in a manner appropriate to the organization's risk environment.

---

## 9. Operational Requirements

Operational requirements should be sufficiently clear for responsible personnel.

They may specify:

* Required actions.
* Prohibited actions.
* Approval requirements.
* Review requirements.
* Evidence requirements.
* Escalation requirements.
* Performance expectations.

Clear requirements reduce ambiguity during operational execution.

---

## 10. Operational Responsibility

Operational activities should have clearly assigned responsibilities.

Responsibilities may include:

* AI system owner.
* Business process owner.
* Technical owner.
* AI governance function.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Legal.
* Compliance.
* End users.

Responsibility should be aligned with authority and competence.

---

## 11. Operational Authority

Personnel responsible for AI operations should have appropriate authority to perform their assigned duties.

For example, an AI system operator may need authority to:

* Suspend a system.
* Escalate an incident.
* Reject an unsafe output.
* Initiate a change request.
* Request technical support.

Responsibility without appropriate authority can weaken operational control.

---

## 12. Operational Planning Inputs

Operational planning may consider information from:

* AI policies.
* AI objectives.
* Risk assessments.
* Impact assessments.
* Legal requirements.
* Regulatory requirements.
* Stakeholder expectations.
* AI system specifications.
* Supplier requirements.
* Previous incidents.
* Audit findings.

These inputs help ensure that operational activities reflect the organization's actual requirements.

---

## 13. Operational Planning Outputs

Planning may produce:

* Procedures.
* Work instructions.
* Control requirements.
* Operating schedules.
* Resource requirements.
* Responsibilities.
* Monitoring requirements.
* Records.
* Approval criteria.

Outputs should be communicated to relevant personnel.

---

## 14. AI Operational Procedures

Operational procedures describe how recurring activities should be performed.

Examples include:

* AI system onboarding.
* AI deployment.
* AI monitoring.
* AI incident response.
* AI change management.
* AI access review.
* AI performance evaluation.

Procedures should be consistent with applicable AIMS requirements.

---

## 15. AI Operational Work Instructions

Work instructions may provide detailed guidance for specific operational activities.

For example, a work instruction may describe how an administrator should:

1. Validate an approved change.
2. Confirm testing completion.
3. Verify authorization.
4. Deploy the change.
5. Record the result.
6. Escalate unexpected behavior.

Detailed instructions can be particularly useful for high-risk operational activities.

---

## 16. Operational Planning for AI Acquisition

Organizations acquiring AI technologies should plan appropriate governance activities before deployment.

Considerations may include:

* Business purpose.
* AI risk.
* Supplier assessment.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Data requirements.
* Contractual requirements.
* Performance expectations.
* Monitoring.

Acquisition should not bypass established AIMS processes.

---

## 17. Operational Planning for AI Development

Where AI is developed internally, operational planning may address:

* Development methodology.
* Data management.
* Testing.
* Validation.
* Security.
* Documentation.
* Approval.
* Deployment.

Development activities should remain aligned with applicable AI governance requirements.

---

## 18. Operational Planning for AI Deployment

Deployment should be controlled according to defined requirements.

Deployment planning may include:

* Approval.
* Testing.
* Configuration.
* Access.
* Monitoring.
* User readiness.
* Rollback arrangements.
* Communication.

The organization should avoid deploying AI systems without appropriate operational readiness.

---

## 19. Operational Readiness

Before an AI system enters operation, relevant readiness criteria may be evaluated.

These may include:

* Required assessments completed.
* Risks addressed.
* Controls implemented.
* Responsible personnel identified.
* Users trained.
* Monitoring established.
* Documentation available.
* Incident procedures established.

Readiness criteria should reflect system significance and risk.

---

## 20. Operational Acceptance

Formal acceptance may be appropriate before an AI system becomes operational.

Acceptance may consider:

* Business requirements.
* Risk treatment.
* Performance.
* Testing.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Compliance.
* Operational support.

The acceptance authority should be appropriate to the system's significance.

---

## 21. Operational Planning for AI Use

Once deployed, AI use should remain within approved operating parameters.

These may define:

* Intended purpose.
* Authorized users.
* Permitted data.
* Approved environments.
* Usage limitations.
* Human oversight.
* Escalation requirements.

Operational use outside these parameters may introduce unmanaged risk.

---

## 22. AI Usage Controls

Usage controls may prevent inappropriate application of AI.

Examples include:

* Access restrictions.
* Approved-use lists.
* Data restrictions.
* User permissions.
* Human-review requirements.
* Transaction limits.
* System boundaries.

The appropriate combination depends on the AI system and its risks.

---

## 23. AI Human Oversight in Operations

Where human oversight is required, operational procedures should define how it is performed.

They may specify:

* Who performs the review.
* What must be reviewed.
* When review is required.
* What happens when output is rejected.
* How exceptions are handled.
* What evidence is retained.

Human oversight should be operationally actionable rather than merely stated as a principle.

---

## 24. AI Output Review

AI outputs may require review when incorrect or inappropriate outputs could create significant consequences.

Review activities may consider:

* Accuracy.
* Relevance.
* Completeness.
* Plausibility.
* Context.
* Potential bias.
* Security concerns.

The level of review should reflect the consequences associated with the AI output.

---

## 25. AI Operational Monitoring

Operational planning should establish appropriate monitoring activities.

Monitoring may address:

* Availability.
* Performance.
* Accuracy.
* Data quality.
* Model behavior.
* Security.
* Usage.
* Incidents.

Monitoring requirements should be defined before or during operational deployment where practical.

---

## 26. AI Performance Thresholds

Where meaningful, operational controls may define thresholds that trigger action.

For example:

```text
Performance Within Threshold
        ↓
Continue Operation

Performance Outside Threshold
        ↓
Investigation
        ↓
Corrective Action / Escalation
```

Thresholds should be based on appropriate business, technical and risk considerations.

---

## 27. AI Operational Alerts

AI systems may generate alerts when predefined conditions occur.

Examples include:

* Performance degradation.
* Unexpected output.
* Security event.
* Excessive usage.
* Data-quality issue.
* Service failure.

Alert conditions should be connected to defined response procedures.

---

## 28. AI Operational Escalation

Operational personnel should understand when issues must be escalated.

Escalation may be required when:

* Risk exceeds defined tolerance.
* Harm may occur.
* Security is affected.
* Privacy may be affected.
* System behavior becomes abnormal.
* Operational controls fail.

Clear escalation requirements help prevent unmanaged AI risks.

---

## 29. AI Operational Incident Management

Operational incidents should be managed according to established incident-management processes.

Activities may include:

* Detection.
* Initial assessment.
* Containment.
* Escalation.
* Investigation.
* Recovery.
* Documentation.
* Lessons learned.

AI incidents may also require coordination with cybersecurity, privacy, legal or business-continuity processes.

---

## 30. AI Operational Records

Operational activities should generate appropriate evidence.

Examples include:

* Deployment records.
* Approval records.
* Monitoring results.
* Change records.
* Incident records.
* Access reviews.
* Performance reports.
* Exception records.

Records provide evidence that operational controls are being performed.
