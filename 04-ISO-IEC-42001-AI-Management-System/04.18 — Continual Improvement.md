# 04.18 — Continual Improvement

## 1. Introduction to Continual Improvement

Continual improvement is the systematic effort to enhance the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the AI Management System (AIMS) over time.

An effective AIMS should not remain static after policies, processes and controls have been established. Changes in AI technology, organizational objectives, risks, regulations, stakeholder expectations and operational experience may create a need for ongoing improvement.

Continual improvement connects performance evaluation, management decisions, corrective action, lessons learned and organizational change.

A simplified model is:

```text
AIMS Operation
      ↓
Monitoring & Measurement
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Findings / Lessons / Opportunities
      ↓
Improvement Decisions
      ↓
Implementation
      ↓
Effectiveness Evaluation
      ↓
Improved AIMS
```

---

## 2. Purpose of Continual Improvement

The purpose of continual improvement is to ensure that the AIMS remains capable of supporting organizational objectives while responding to changing circumstances.

Improvement may seek to:

* Increase effectiveness.
* Reduce AI-related risks.
* Improve governance.
* Strengthen controls.
* Improve operational performance.
* Address nonconformities.
* Improve stakeholder outcomes.
* Respond to emerging requirements.
* Increase organizational capability.

Continual improvement should be purposeful rather than change for its own sake.

---

## 3. Continual Improvement as a Management-System Principle

A management system is expected to evolve as the organization and its environment evolve.

For an AIMS, improvement may be driven by:

* Internal performance.
* Audit findings.
* Management review.
* AI incidents.
* Risk assessments.
* Impact assessments.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Regulatory changes.
* Technology developments.
* Lessons learned.

These sources provide evidence that can be used to determine where improvement is required.

---

## 4. Improvement Versus Correction

Continual improvement should be distinguished from simply correcting an immediate problem.

| Correction                      | Continual Improvement                |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Addresses an existing problem   | Enhances the management system       |
| Often immediate                 | May be planned over time             |
| Focuses on restoring conformity | Focuses on improving capability      |
| May be localized                | May be systemic or organization-wide |

For example, correcting a missing AI risk assessment restores compliance with an established process. Redesigning the governance workflow so that risk assessments are automatically triggered may represent a broader improvement.

---

## 5. Improvement Versus Corrective Action

Corrective action addresses the cause of a nonconformity.

Continual improvement has a broader scope and may occur even when there is no nonconformity.

For example:

```text
Nonconformity
     ↓
Corrective Action
     ↓
Problem Resolved

Performance Opportunity
     ↓
Improvement Initiative
     ↓
AIMS Enhanced
```

An organization can therefore improve a process even when the existing process is technically conforming.

## 31. Improvement Prioritization

Not every improvement opportunity can or should be implemented immediately. Improvement initiatives should be prioritized according to organizational needs, risk and expected value.

Prioritization may consider:

* AI risk.
* Stakeholder impact.
* Regulatory significance.
* Strategic importance.
* Operational benefit.
* Resource requirements.
* Implementation complexity.
* Expected improvement.

A risk-based approach helps direct limited resources toward the areas where improvement will provide the greatest benefit.

---

## 32. Improvement Objectives

Significant improvement initiatives should have clearly defined objectives.

An improvement objective should describe:

* What needs to improve.
* Why the improvement is required.
* Expected outcome.
* Relevant success criteria.
* Responsible owner.
* Target timeframe.

For example:

> Reduce the time required to complete AI risk assessments while maintaining the required level of governance assurance.

This provides a basis for evaluating whether the improvement achieved its intended result.

---

## 33. Improvement Plans

Improvement initiatives may be managed through formal improvement plans.

A plan may identify:

| Element                 | Description                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Improvement opportunity | What should be improved              |
| Reason                  | Why improvement is required          |
| Objective               | Desired result                       |
| Owner                   | Responsible person                   |
| Actions                 | Activities required                  |
| Resources               | Required support                     |
| Target date             | Expected completion                  |
| Success measure         | How effectiveness will be determined |

The level of planning should be proportionate to the size and significance of the improvement.

---

## 34. Improvement Ownership

Each significant improvement initiative should have clear ownership.

The owner should have:

* Appropriate authority.
* Sufficient resources.
* Required competence.
* Access to relevant stakeholders.
* Accountability for delivery.

Ownership should remain clear even when multiple teams participate in implementation.

An improvement should be evaluated to determine whether it achieved its intended outcome.

Questions may include:

* Did the targeted problem improve?
* Did performance change?
* Did risk decrease?
* Did stakeholder outcomes improve?
* Did the new process work as intended?
* Were unintended consequences introduced?

Improvement should therefore be evaluated rather than assumed to be successful.

## 83. Improvement and Risk-Based Governance

AIMS improvement should support proportionality.

Higher-risk AI systems may require:

* Stronger controls.
* More frequent monitoring.
* Greater oversight.
* More extensive documentation.

Lower-risk systems may require simpler governance.

Continual improvement should therefore seek not only stronger controls but also more appropriate controls.

Efficiency improvements should preserve necessary governance safeguards.

## 91. Improvement and AIMS Effectiveness

Continual improvement should ultimately strengthen the effectiveness of the AI Management System.

Effectiveness can be considered in terms of whether the AIMS is achieving its intended outcomes, including:

* Supporting AI governance objectives.
* Managing relevant AI risks.
* Meeting applicable requirements.
* Maintaining appropriate controls.
* Supporting responsible AI practices.
* Responding effectively to change.

Improvement should therefore be connected to measurable organizational outcomes.

## 92. Improvement and AIMS Suitability

An AIMS may remain operationally effective while becoming less suitable for the organization's changing environment.

Suitability considers whether the AIMS continues to fit:

* Organizational strategy.
* AI use cases.
* Organizational structure.
* Risk profile.
* Stakeholder expectations.
* External requirements.

Continual improvement helps maintain this alignment.

---

## 93. Improvement and AIMS Adequacy

Adequacy concerns whether the AIMS provides sufficient capability to address organizational needs.

An organization may identify an adequacy gap when:

* New AI risks emerge.
* AI adoption expands.
* Existing controls become insufficient.
* New regulatory requirements arise.
* Organizational responsibilities change.

Improvement initiatives can address these capability gaps.

## 95. Improvement and the PDCA Cycle

Continual improvement can be understood through the Plan-Do-Check-Act model.

### Plan

Determine objectives, risks, resources and actions.

### Do

Implement the planned changes.

### Check

Measure and evaluate results.

### Act

Take action based on findings and improve the system.

```text id="x6q2rv"
PLAN
  ↓
DO
  ↓
CHECK
  ↓
ACT
  ↓
PLAN
```

The cycle creates a repeatable mechanism for ongoing AIMS improvement.

## 103. Improvement and Organizational Adoption

* Apply new controls.
