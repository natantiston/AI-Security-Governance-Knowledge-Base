# 04.16 — Management Review

## 1. Introduction to Management Review

Management review is a formal governance activity through which top management evaluates the continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the AI Management System (AIMS).

The review provides an opportunity for leadership to examine whether the AIMS continues to support:

* Organizational objectives.
* AI governance requirements.
* AI risks and opportunities.
* Stakeholder needs.
* Applicable obligations.
* Continual improvement.

Management review should be based on relevant evidence rather than informal discussion alone.

---

## 2. Purpose of Management Review

The primary purpose of management review is to enable top management to evaluate the overall performance and direction of the AIMS.

Management may use the review to determine:

* Whether the AIMS remains appropriate.
* Whether objectives are being achieved.
* Whether risks are adequately managed.
* Whether controls remain effective.
* Whether resources are sufficient.
* Whether changes are required.

Management review therefore connects operational evidence with strategic governance decisions.

---

## 3. Top Management Responsibility

Management review is a leadership responsibility.

Top management should provide appropriate direction concerning:

* AI governance.
* Resources.
* Risk management.
* AI objectives.
* Performance expectations.
* Improvement priorities.

Management review should not be treated solely as an administrative exercise performed by the AI governance team.

---

## 4. Management Review and the AIMS

Management review forms part of the AIMS performance-evaluation and improvement cycle.

A simplified relationship is:

```text id="h6m2vr"
AIMS Operation
      ↓
Monitoring & Measurement
      ↓
Internal Audit
      ↓
Risk & Performance Information
      ↓
Management Review
      ↓
Management Decisions
      ↓
Actions / Improvement
      ↓
AIMS
```

This allows leadership to determine whether the management system continues to operate as intended.

---

## 5. Management Review Frequency

Management review should occur at planned intervals.

The appropriate frequency may depend on:

* Organizational size.
* AI system criticality.
* Risk profile.
* Number of AI systems.
* Regulatory environment.
* Significant changes.
* Previous management-review results.

Higher-risk AI environments may require more frequent management attention.

---

## 6. Planned Management Review

Management reviews should be planned rather than conducted only when a problem occurs.

Planning should consider:

* Review timing.
* Required participants.
* Inputs.
* Performance information.
* Audit results.
* Risk information.
* Decisions required.
* Follow-up from previous reviews.

A planned approach improves consistency and accountability.

---

## 7. Management Review Participants

Participants should include personnel capable of making or influencing relevant decisions.

Participants may include:

* Top management.
* AI governance leadership.
* AI system owners.
* Risk management.
* Information security.
* Privacy.
* Compliance.
* Legal.
* Internal audit representatives where appropriate.
* Relevant business leaders.

Participation should reflect the organization's governance structure.

---

## 8. Management Review Inputs

Management review should consider relevant information about the performance and effectiveness of the AIMS.

Inputs may include:

* Previous management-review actions.
* Changes in internal and external context.
* AI objectives.
* Risk and opportunity information.
* Monitoring and measurement results.
* Internal-audit results.
* Incidents and nonconformities.
* Corrective actions.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Resource requirements.
* Improvement opportunities.

The specific inputs should reflect the organization's circumstances and applicable requirements.

---

## 9. Status of Previous Management Review Actions

A management review should consider whether actions from previous reviews have been completed effectively.

Management may examine:

* Completed actions.
* Outstanding actions.
* Overdue actions.
* Action effectiveness.
* Residual issues.

Recurring unresolved actions may indicate weaknesses in governance or resource allocation.

---

## 10. Changes in Internal Context

Management should consider changes within the organization that may affect the AIMS.

Examples include:

* Organizational restructuring.
* New AI initiatives.
* Changes in AI governance responsibilities.
* New technologies.
* Changes in business strategy.
* Changes in workforce capability.
* Changes in risk appetite.

Internal changes may require adjustments to the AIMS.

---

## 11. Changes in External Context

External developments may also affect the AIMS.

Examples include:

* New laws and regulations.
* Regulatory guidance.
* Emerging AI standards.
* Changes in technology.
* New threats.
* Market developments.
* Changes in stakeholder expectations.
* Industry practices.

Management should consider whether external changes create new risks or opportunities.

---

## 12. Changes in AI Technology

Rapid technological developments can affect AI governance requirements.

Examples include:

* New foundation models.
* Generative AI.
* Autonomous AI agents.
* New deployment architectures.
* New AI development tools.
* New security techniques.

Technology changes may require reassessment of existing governance arrangements.

---

## 13. Changes in AI Portfolio

The organization's AI portfolio may change over time.

Changes may include:

* New AI systems.
* Retired AI systems.
* Expanded use cases.
* New business units using AI.
* Increased AI dependency.
* Changes in AI system criticality.

Management should ensure that the AIMS remains appropriate for the current AI portfolio.

---

## 14. Changes in AI Risk

AI risks may change as systems, technologies and operating conditions evolve.

Management may review:

* New risks.
* Increased risks.
* Reduced risks.
* Residual risks.
* Emerging risks.
* Changes in risk treatment.

Risk information should support management decisions about priorities and resources.

---

## 15. AI Risk Trends

Management review can examine trends rather than individual risk events.

Examples include:

* Increasing AI incidents.
* Increasing model-performance issues.
* Increasing third-party dependencies.
* Increasing privacy concerns.
* Increasing security threats.

Trend analysis can help management identify areas requiring strategic attention.

---

## 16. Changes in AI Opportunities

The AIMS should consider opportunities as well as risks.

Opportunities may include:

* Improved automation.
* Better decision support.
* Improved service quality.
* Increased operational efficiency.
* New products or services.
* Improved governance capabilities.

Management review can help determine whether the organization is positioned to take advantage of appropriate AI opportunities.

---

## 17. AI Objectives Review

Management should evaluate the status of established AI objectives.

Review questions may include:

* Are objectives being achieved?
* Are indicators showing expected results?
* Are objectives still relevant?
* Are resources sufficient?
* Are targets realistic?
* Are corrective actions necessary?

Objectives should remain aligned with organizational priorities.

---

## 18. AI Performance Review

Management should review relevant AI performance information.

Performance information may include:

* Accuracy.
* Availability.
* Reliability.
* Security.
* User satisfaction.
* Operational efficiency.
* Compliance.
* Control performance.

Performance should be interpreted in the context of organizational objectives and AI risks.

---

## 19. Monitoring and Measurement Results

Monitoring and measurement results provide important inputs to management review.

Management may examine:

* Trends.
* Threshold breaches.
* Significant deviations.
* Performance changes.
* Emerging issues.
* Monitoring gaps.

The objective is to determine whether performance remains acceptable and whether action is required.

---

## 20. Internal Audit Results

Internal-audit results should provide management with an independent perspective on the AIMS.

Management may review:

* Audit findings.
* Nonconformities.
* Recurring findings.
* Corrective actions.
* Audit trends.
* Areas of strong performance.

Audit results can highlight weaknesses that routine operational monitoring may not identify.

---

## 21. AI Incidents

Significant AI incidents should be considered during management review.

Examples include:

* Security incidents.
* Privacy incidents.
* Significant model failures.
* Harmful outputs.
* Control failures.
* Major service disruptions.

Management should consider both individual incidents and broader incident trends.

---

## 22. Nonconformities

Management review should consider relevant nonconformities identified through:

* Internal audit.
* External assessment.
* Monitoring.
* Operational activities.
* Compliance reviews.
* Incident investigations.

The review should consider whether nonconformities indicate isolated issues or systemic weaknesses.

---

## 23. Corrective Action Status

Management should review significant corrective actions and their effectiveness.

Information may include:

* Open actions.
* Closed actions.
* Overdue actions.
* Recurring issues.
* Effectiveness results.

Where corrective actions are ineffective, management may need to provide additional direction or resources.

---

## 24. Stakeholder Feedback

Stakeholder feedback can provide important evidence for management review.

Sources may include:

* Customers.
* Employees.
* AI users.
* Business units.
* Regulators.
* Partners.
* Other affected stakeholders.

Feedback may reveal issues that are not visible through technical metrics alone.

---

## 25. AI User Feedback

AI users can provide information about:

* Usability.
* Performance.
* Reliability.
* Trust.
* Unexpected behavior.
* Operational difficulties.

Management should consider whether recurring user feedback indicates broader AIMS issues.

---

## 26. Resource Adequacy

Management review should consider whether sufficient resources are available to operate and improve the AIMS.

Resources may include:

* Personnel.
* Skills.
* Technology.
* Infrastructure.
* Budget.
* Training.
* External expertise.

Insufficient resources can prevent the organization from achieving AI objectives.

---

## 27. AI Competence Requirements

Management should consider whether the organization has sufficient competence to manage its AI environment.

Gaps may involve:

* AI technical expertise.
* AI governance.
* Risk management.
* Cybersecurity.
* Privacy.
* Legal and regulatory knowledge.
* Audit capability.

Competence requirements should evolve with the organization's AI adoption.

---

## 28. AI Governance Capability

Management review may evaluate whether governance structures remain capable of managing the organization's AI portfolio.

Considerations may include:

* Governance roles.
* Decision authority.
* Escalation mechanisms.
* Cross-functional coordination.
* Oversight capacity.
* Management reporting.

Governance should remain proportionate to organizational complexity and AI risk.

---

## 29. Management Review Decisions

Management review should result in appropriate decisions and actions where necessary.

Decisions may concern:

* AIMS improvements.
* AI objectives.
* Risk treatment.
* Controls.
* Resources.
* Processes.
* Governance structures.
* AI system changes.

A management review that produces no meaningful evaluation or decisions may provide limited governance value.

---

## 30. Management Review Outputs

Management-review outputs should provide evidence of management's evaluation and resulting decisions.

Outputs may include:

* Improvement actions.
* Resource decisions.
* Changes to objectives.
* Changes to governance.
* Risk-treatment decisions.
* Control improvements.
* Assigned responsibilities.
* Target dates.

Outputs should be appropriately documented and communicated to relevant stakeholders.
