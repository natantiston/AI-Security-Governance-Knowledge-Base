# 04.14 — AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis

## 1. Introduction to AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis

AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis establishes the mechanisms through which an organization observes, measures and analyzes the performance and behavior of its AI systems and related AI Management System processes.

Monitoring provides ongoing or periodic observation. Measurement produces defined quantitative or qualitative information. Analysis interprets that information to identify patterns, deviations, trends and areas requiring management attention.

Together, these activities help the organization determine whether AI systems and associated controls remain aligned with:

* AI objectives.
* Organizational requirements.
* Defined performance criteria.
* Risk tolerances.
* Applicable obligations.
* Stakeholder expectations.

---

## 2. Purpose of AI Monitoring

AI monitoring provides visibility into the ongoing behavior and condition of AI systems.

Monitoring can help identify:

* Performance degradation.
* Unexpected behavior.
* Security events.
* Data-quality problems.
* Control failures.
* Operational anomalies.
* Emerging risks.

Monitoring should be proportionate to the significance and characteristics of the AI system.

---

## 3. Purpose of AI Measurement

Measurement converts observations into defined information that can be evaluated against established criteria.

Measurements may include:

* Accuracy.
* Error rates.
* Availability.
* Response time.
* Incident frequency.
* Control compliance.
* User satisfaction.
* Resource utilization.

Measurement methods should be appropriate to what the organization is attempting to determine.

---

## 4. Purpose of AI Analysis

Analysis transforms collected information into meaningful conclusions.

Analysis may identify:

* Trends.
* Variances.
* Correlations.
* Root causes.
* Emerging problems.
* Performance changes.
* Control weaknesses.

Analysis should provide information that supports appropriate management decisions.

---

## 5. Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis Relationship

The three activities are closely connected:

```text id="m7q4cx"
Monitoring
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Measurement
    ↓
Data Collection
    ↓
Analysis
    ↓
Evaluation
    ↓
Management Action
```

Monitoring without analysis may produce large quantities of information without meaningful insight.

---

## 6. AI Monitoring Scope

The organization should determine what aspects of an AI system require monitoring.

Potential monitoring areas include:

* Technical performance.
* Data quality.
* Model behavior.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Availability.
* Reliability.
* Human oversight.
* User behavior.
* Compliance.
* Business outcomes.

The scope should reflect the AI system's intended purpose and risk profile.

---

## 7. AI Monitoring Objectives

Monitoring activities should have clearly defined objectives.

Objectives may include:

* Detecting performance degradation.
* Detecting security events.
* Confirming control operation.
* Identifying unusual behavior.
* Measuring service quality.
* Supporting compliance.
* Detecting emerging risks.

Clear objectives prevent monitoring from becoming an unstructured collection of metrics.

---

## 8. AI Monitoring Criteria

Monitoring should use defined criteria to determine what constitutes normal or abnormal behavior.

Criteria may include:

* Performance targets.
* Risk thresholds.
* Control requirements.
* Service-level agreements.
* Alert thresholds.
* Regulatory requirements.
* Internal policies.

Defined criteria enable consistent interpretation of monitoring results.

---

## 9. AI Monitoring Frequency

Monitoring frequency should reflect how quickly conditions can change and the potential consequences of delayed detection.

Monitoring may be:

* Continuous.
* Real-time.
* Event-driven.
* Daily.
* Weekly.
* Monthly.
* Periodic.

Critical AI systems may require more frequent or continuous monitoring.

---

## 10. AI Continuous Monitoring

Continuous monitoring provides ongoing visibility into selected AI system characteristics.

It may be appropriate for:

* High-volume systems.
* Security-sensitive systems.
* Safety-relevant applications.
* Systems with rapidly changing data.
* Critical business processes.

Continuous monitoring requires appropriate technical and operational capabilities.

---

## 11. AI Event-Driven Monitoring

Event-driven monitoring initiates observation or analysis when a defined event occurs.

Triggers may include:

* Model changes.
* Significant incidents.
* Data changes.
* Performance threshold breaches.
* Security alerts.
* Regulatory changes.
* Major system modifications.

Event-driven monitoring can complement routine monitoring activities.

---

## 12. AI Periodic Monitoring

Periodic monitoring evaluates AI systems at predefined intervals.

Activities may include:

* Monthly performance reviews.
* Quarterly control reviews.
* Periodic model validation.
* Scheduled compliance checks.
* Regular stakeholder feedback reviews.

Periodic monitoring can be useful where continuous monitoring is unnecessary or impractical.

---

## 13. AI Monitoring Indicators

Indicators provide measurable or observable signals about AI behavior.

Examples include:

* Error rate.
* Accuracy.
* Availability.
* Latency.
* Incident frequency.
* Override rate.
* Complaint rate.
* Data-quality score.

Indicators should be selected based on the information required for effective decision-making.

---

## 14. AI Performance Indicators

Performance indicators measure whether AI systems are achieving expected technical or business outcomes.

Examples include:

* Model accuracy.
* Processing time.
* Throughput.
* Service availability.
* Productivity.
* User adoption.

Performance indicators should be linked to relevant AI objectives.

---

## 15. AI Risk Indicators

Risk indicators provide information about changing exposure to AI-related risks.

Examples include:

* Increasing error rates.
* Rising security alerts.
* Increasing privacy incidents.
* Increased model overrides.
* Growing numbers of policy exceptions.
* Declining data quality.

Risk indicators should be connected to the organization's risk-management processes.

---

## 16. AI Control Indicators

Control indicators measure whether defined governance and operational controls are functioning.

Examples include:

* Access reviews completed.
* Required assessments completed.
* Model changes approved.
* Training completed.
* Monitoring alerts investigated.
* Corrective actions closed.

Control indicators can provide evidence of governance effectiveness.

---

## 17. AI Leading Indicators

Leading indicators provide signals about conditions that may precede future problems.

Examples include:

* Increasing data-quality exceptions.
* Declining training completion.
* Increasing manual overrides.
* Growing unresolved alerts.
* Increasing system changes.

Leading indicators can help the organization respond before significant impacts occur.

---

## 18. AI Lagging Indicators

Lagging indicators measure outcomes that have already occurred.

Examples include:

* AI incidents.
* Complaints.
* Service failures.
* Security breaches.
* Confirmed model failures.

Lagging indicators provide evidence about actual outcomes but may identify problems only after they have occurred.

---

## 19. AI Monitoring Data Sources

Monitoring information may come from:

* AI system logs.
* Application logs.
* Model-monitoring platforms.
* Security tools.
* Data-quality systems.
* Incident-management systems.
* User feedback.
* Business systems.
* Audit records.

Multiple sources may be necessary to obtain a complete view.

---

## 20. AI Monitoring Data Collection

Data collection should be designed to obtain information that is:

* Relevant.
* Reliable.
* Timely.
* Complete.
* Consistent.
* Protected.

Collection mechanisms should be appropriate to the sensitivity and operational importance of the information.

---

## 21. AI Monitoring Data Quality

Monitoring conclusions depend on the quality of monitoring data.

Data-quality considerations include:

* Accuracy.
* Completeness.
* Consistency.
* Timeliness.
* Integrity.
* Representativeness.

Poor monitoring data can create false alerts or conceal genuine problems.

---

## 22. AI Monitoring Data Integrity

Monitoring data should be protected against unauthorized modification or loss.

Controls may include:

* Access restrictions.
* Logging.
* Integrity checks.
* Secure storage.
* Version control.
* Data validation.

Integrity is particularly important where monitoring information supports compliance, audit or management decisions.

---

## 23. AI Monitoring Data Retention

Monitoring records should be retained according to applicable organizational, legal, regulatory and operational requirements.

Retention considerations may include:

* Business requirements.
* Investigation needs.
* Audit requirements.
* Regulatory obligations.
* Security requirements.
* Privacy requirements.

Retention should be balanced against data-minimization and protection requirements.

---

A monitoring architecture may integrate multiple information sources:

---

## 25. AI Monitoring Telemetry

Telemetry provides information about system behavior and operating conditions.

Telemetry may include:

* Requests.
* Responses.
* Processing time.
* Errors.
* Resource usage.
* Model outputs.
* System events.

Telemetry collection should be designed with appropriate security and privacy controls.

---

## 26. AI Logging

Logs provide historical records of relevant system and operational events.

AI logging may capture:

* System events.
* Administrative changes.
* Model deployments.
* Configuration changes.
* Errors.
* Access events.
* Significant operational actions.

Logging requirements should reflect the AI system's risk and operational needs.

---

## 27. AI Monitoring Alerts

Alerts notify responsible personnel when predefined conditions occur.

Alerts may be triggered by:

* Threshold breaches.
* Unusual behavior.
* Security events.
* Availability problems.
* Data-quality problems.
* Model-performance degradation.

Alerts should be prioritized to reduce unnecessary noise.

---

## 28. AI Alert Thresholds

Thresholds determine when an observed condition becomes significant enough to require attention.

Thresholds may be based on:

* Historical performance.
* Approved targets.
* Risk appetite.
* Service requirements.
* Regulatory requirements.

Thresholds should be reviewed when system conditions change.

---

## 29. AI Alert Prioritization

Not every alert has the same significance.

Alerts may be classified according to:

* Severity.
* Urgency.
* Potential impact.
* Likelihood.
* Affected systems.
* Number of stakeholders.

Prioritization helps ensure that critical conditions receive appropriate attention.

---

## 30. AI Monitoring Response

Monitoring should be connected to defined response mechanisms.

A basic model is:

```text id="r4v9tz"
Condition Detected
       ↓
Alert
       ↓
Triage
       ↓
Investigation
       ↓
Decision
       ↓
Response / Escalation
       ↓
Verification
```

A monitoring system is more effective when detection is connected to an established response process.
