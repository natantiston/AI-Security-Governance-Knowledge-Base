# 04.14 — AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis

## 1. Introduction to AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis

AI Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis establishes the mechanisms through which an organization observes, measures and analyzes the performance and behavior of its AI systems and related AI Management System processes.

Monitoring provides ongoing or periodic observation. Measurement produces defined quantitative or qualitative information. Analysis interprets that information to identify patterns, deviations, trends and areas requiring management attention.

Together, these activities help the organization determine whether AI systems and associated controls remain aligned with:

* AI objectives.
* Organizational requirements.
* Defined performance criteria.
* Risk tolerances.
* Applicable obligations.
* Stakeholder expectations.

---

## 2. Purpose of AI Monitoring

AI monitoring provides visibility into the ongoing behavior and condition of AI systems.

Monitoring can help identify:

* Performance degradation.
* Unexpected behavior.
* Security events.
* Data-quality problems.
* Control failures.
* Operational anomalies.
* Emerging risks.

Monitoring should be proportionate to the significance and characteristics of the AI system.

---

## 3. Purpose of AI Measurement

Measurement converts observations into defined information that can be evaluated against established criteria.

Measurements may include:

* Accuracy.
* Error rates.
* Availability.
* Response time.
* Incident frequency.
* Control compliance.
* User satisfaction.
* Resource utilization.

Measurement methods should be appropriate to what the organization is attempting to determine.

---

## 4. Purpose of AI Analysis

Analysis transforms collected information into meaningful conclusions.

Analysis may identify:

* Trends.
* Variances.
* Correlations.
* Root causes.
* Emerging problems.
* Performance changes.
* Control weaknesses.

Analysis should provide information that supports appropriate management decisions.

---

## 5. Monitoring, Measurement and Analysis Relationship

The three activities are closely connected:

```text id="m7q4cx"

    ↓
```

Monitoring without analysis may produce large quantities of information without meaningful insight.

## 31. AI Monitoring Baselines

A baseline establishes an expected reference condition against which future observations can be compared.

Effective monitoring requires an understanding of what normal system behavior looks like.

* Historical observations.
* Approved performance targets.
* Statistical ranges.
* Test results.

## 33. AI Anomaly Detection

Anomaly detection identifies observations that differ significantly from expected behavior.

Anomalies may involve:

* Unusual inputs.
* Unexpected outputs.
* Sudden performance changes.
* Abnormal resource consumption.
* Unusual user activity.
* Unexpected model behavior.

An anomaly does not necessarily indicate an incident, but it may warrant investigation.

---

## 34. AI Monitoring False Positives

A false positive occurs when monitoring identifies an apparent problem that does not represent a genuine issue.

Excessive false positives can:

* Increase operational workload.
* Cause alert fatigue.
* Reduce confidence in monitoring.
* Delay attention to important events.

Monitoring rules should therefore be periodically tuned.

---

## 35. AI Monitoring False Negatives

A false negative occurs when a genuine problem is not detected by the monitoring process.

False negatives can be particularly significant when monitoring protects against high-impact events.

The organization should evaluate whether monitoring mechanisms are sufficiently capable of detecting relevant conditions.

---

## 36. AI Alert Fatigue

Alert fatigue occurs when personnel receive excessive numbers of alerts, reducing their ability to identify significant events.

Potential causes include:

* Poor thresholds.
* Duplicate alerts.
* Excessive low-value notifications.
* Poor prioritization.

Alert management should focus attention on conditions that require meaningful action.

---

## 37. AI Monitoring Escalation

Escalation may occur when:

* Thresholds are exceeded.
* An event persists.
* Multiple indicators change simultaneously.
* Potential impact is significant.
* Required response authority exceeds operational responsibility.

Escalation criteria should be clearly defined.
