# 04.09 — AI Awareness and Communication

## 1. Introduction to AI Awareness and Communication

AI awareness and communication are essential components of an effective AI Management System (AIMS).

An organization may have well-designed AI policies, controls and governance structures, but these mechanisms may not achieve their intended outcomes if relevant personnel and stakeholders do not understand:

* The organization's AI objectives.
* AI-related policies.
* Their responsibilities.
* Applicable requirements.
* AI risks.
* Acceptable and unacceptable AI use.
* Reporting and escalation mechanisms.

AI awareness helps people understand what is expected of them, while AI communication ensures that relevant information reaches the appropriate stakeholders at the appropriate time.

---

## 2. Purpose of AI Awareness

AI awareness aims to ensure that relevant personnel understand the organization's expectations regarding AI.

Awareness may help personnel understand:

* The organization's AI policy.
* AI governance principles.
* Relevant responsibilities.
* AI risks.
* Security requirements.
* Privacy requirements.
* Responsible AI expectations.
* Incident-reporting procedures.

Awareness should be appropriate to the person's role and relationship with AI.

---

## 3. Purpose of AI Communication

AI communication enables the organization to exchange information about AI internally and externally.

Communication may support:

* Governance.
* Decision-making.
* Risk management.
* Transparency.
* Coordination.
* Regulatory compliance.
* Stakeholder engagement.
* Incident response.

Communication should be timely, accurate, understandable and appropriate to its audience.

---

## 4. Relationship Between Awareness and Communication

Awareness and communication are closely related but serve different purposes.

**Awareness** focuses on whether people understand relevant AI information.

**Communication** focuses on how AI-related information is exchanged between the organization and its stakeholders.

For example:

```text id="xj5j2w"
AI Policy
    ↓
Communication
    ↓
Employee Receives Information
    ↓
Awareness
    ↓
Employee Understands Requirements
    ↓
Appropriate Behavior
```

Both are necessary to support effective AI governance.

---

## 5. AI Awareness as an AIMS Capability

Awareness should be treated as an organizational capability rather than a one-time information campaign.

An effective awareness capability may include:

* Defined audiences.
* Awareness requirements.
* Communication channels.
* Training materials.
* Awareness activities.
* Effectiveness measurement.
* Periodic updates.

The approach should evolve as AI use and organizational requirements change.

---

## 6. AI Awareness Scope

AI awareness may apply to:

* Employees.
* Contractors.
* Management.
* AI developers.
* AI users.
* System owners.
* Governance personnel.
* Security personnel.
* Suppliers.
* Relevant external stakeholders.

The required level of awareness should depend on each person's responsibilities and exposure to AI.

---

## 7. General AI Awareness

General AI awareness can establish a common organizational understanding of AI.

Topics may include:

* What AI is.
* How the organization uses AI.
* Benefits and limitations of AI.
* AI-related risks.
* Responsible AI expectations.
* Approved AI tools.
* Prohibited uses.
* Reporting procedures.

General awareness provides a common foundation across the organization.

---

## 8. Role-Based AI Awareness

Awareness should be adapted to the responsibilities of different roles.

For example:

| Role            | Relevant Awareness             |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Board           | AI strategy and material risks |
| Executives      | Governance and accountability  |
| Employees       | Acceptable AI use              |
| Developers      | Secure AI practices            |
| Security Teams  | AI-specific threats            |
| Privacy Teams   | AI data and privacy risks      |
| Business Owners | AI lifecycle responsibilities  |
| Internal Audit  | AIMS governance and controls   |

Role-based awareness improves relevance and retention.

---

## 9. Management AI Awareness

Management should understand the organization's AI governance responsibilities.

Management awareness may cover:

* AI strategy.
* AI objectives.
* Significant AI risks.
* Regulatory obligations.
* Resource requirements.
* Performance.
* Major incidents.
* Stakeholder concerns.

Management awareness supports informed oversight and decision-making.

---

## 10. Employee AI Awareness

Employees who use or interact with AI should understand organizational expectations.

Awareness may address:

* Approved AI applications.
* Data restrictions.
* Confidential information.
* Verification of AI-generated content.
* Human oversight.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Incident reporting.

Employees should understand both the benefits and limitations of AI.

---

## 11. AI Acceptable Use Awareness

Personnel should understand what constitutes acceptable AI use.

Examples may include:

* Approved business applications.
* Approved AI tools.
* Appropriate data handling.
* Required human review.
* Authorized automation.

Acceptable-use requirements should be clearly communicated.

---

## 12. AI Prohibited Use Awareness

Personnel should also understand prohibited or restricted AI activities.

Examples may include:

* Entering restricted information into unauthorized AI services.
* Using AI for unauthorized decisions.
* Circumventing security controls.
* Using unapproved AI systems.
* Misrepresenting AI-generated content where disclosure is required.

Prohibited-use requirements should be clear and accessible.

---

## 13. AI Risk Awareness

Personnel should understand relevant AI risks.

Risks may include:

* Incorrect outputs.
* Bias.
* Privacy violations.
* Security threats.
* Data leakage.
* Hallucinations.
* Inappropriate automation.
* Lack of human oversight.
* Regulatory non-compliance.

Risk awareness should be proportional to the person's role.

---

## 14. AI Security Awareness

Security awareness should address AI-specific security considerations.

Topics may include:

* Prompt injection.
* Sensitive-data exposure.
* Unauthorized AI tools.
* Credential protection.
* Malicious AI-generated content.
* AI system vulnerabilities.
* Access control.

Security awareness should complement existing cybersecurity awareness programs.

---

## 15. AI Privacy Awareness

Personnel should understand how AI activities may affect personal information.

Awareness may address:

* Appropriate data use.
* Personal information.
* Data minimization.
* Confidentiality.
* Privacy requirements.
* Unauthorized data disclosure.

Privacy awareness should be aligned with applicable organizational requirements.

---

## 16. AI Data Awareness

Personnel should understand appropriate handling of data used by or generated through AI systems.

Topics may include:

* Data classification.
* Data quality.
* Data ownership.
* Data provenance.
* Data retention.
* Data access.
* Sensitive information.

Data awareness supports reliable and responsible AI use.

---

## 17. AI Output Verification Awareness

Users should understand that AI-generated information may require verification.

Awareness may emphasize that AI outputs can contain:

* Errors.
* Incomplete information.
* Incorrect assumptions.
* Fabricated information.
* Outdated information.

The level of verification should reflect the importance and risk of the intended use.

---

## 18. Human Oversight Awareness

Personnel involved in AI-enabled decisions should understand when human oversight is required.

Awareness may include:

* When human review is mandatory.
* When intervention is appropriate.
* How to challenge AI outputs.
* When to escalate concerns.
* How to stop or override an AI process where authorized.

Human oversight should be meaningful rather than merely procedural.

---

## 19. AI Incident Awareness

Personnel should know how to identify and report AI-related incidents.

Examples include:

* Harmful AI output.
* Security compromise.
* Privacy breach.
* Unauthorized AI use.
* Unexpected system behavior.
* Material bias.
* AI service failure.

Reporting channels should be clear and accessible.

---

## 20. AI Policy Awareness

Personnel whose activities are affected by AI policies should understand relevant requirements.

Awareness should communicate:

* Policy objectives.
* Key requirements.
* Responsibilities.
* Restrictions.
* Exceptions.
* Reporting obligations.

Policies should not rely solely on publication; relevant personnel should understand their practical implications.

---

## 21. AI Governance Awareness

Governance awareness helps personnel understand how AI decisions are governed.

Topics may include:

* AI approval processes.
* Roles and responsibilities.
* Governance committees.
* Risk escalation.
* AI inventory requirements.
* Assessment requirements.
* Decision authority.

Governance awareness supports consistent implementation.

---

## 22. AI Accountability Awareness

Personnel should understand who is accountable for AI-related decisions and activities.

Awareness may clarify:

* System ownership.
* Business accountability.
* Technical responsibility.
* Risk ownership.
* Approval authority.
* Escalation responsibilities.

Clear accountability reduces uncertainty when issues arise.

---

## 23. AI Regulatory Awareness

Relevant personnel should be aware of applicable AI-related legal and regulatory requirements.

Awareness may cover:

* Applicable laws.
* Regulatory obligations.
* Industry requirements.
* Contractual requirements.
* Organizational commitments.

Detailed legal interpretation may remain the responsibility of qualified legal or compliance personnel.

---

## 24. AI Ethical Awareness

AI awareness may include the organization's expectations regarding responsible and ethical AI use.

Topics may include:

* Fairness.
* Transparency.
* Accountability.
* Human oversight.
* Respect for affected individuals.
* Avoidance of harmful uses.

Ethical expectations should be connected to practical organizational behavior.

---

## 25. AI Stakeholder Awareness

Relevant external stakeholders may also require appropriate information about AI.

Stakeholders may include:

* Customers.
* Partners.
* Suppliers.
* Regulators.
* Service providers.
* Individuals affected by AI systems.

The nature and extent of communication should reflect stakeholder needs and applicable requirements.

---

## 26. AI Awareness and Organizational Culture

Awareness contributes to an organizational culture in which responsible AI behavior becomes part of normal operations.

A mature culture encourages personnel to:

* Ask questions.
* Report concerns.
* Challenge inappropriate AI use.
* Follow governance processes.
* Escalate risks.
* Consider stakeholder impact.

Awareness should therefore support behavior, not simply information retention.

---

## 27. AI Communication Principles

Effective AI communication should generally be:

* Accurate.
* Timely.
* Relevant.
* Understandable.
* Consistent.
* Appropriate to the audience.
* Secure where necessary.

Communication should provide enough information to support appropriate action without unnecessarily exposing sensitive information.

Internal communication should use established organizational channels where practical.

---

## 30. AI External Communication

External communication may be appropriate when stakeholders require information about AI activities.

Examples include:

* Customer disclosures.

## 31. AI Communication Planning

AI communication should be planned according to organizational needs and stakeholder requirements.

Communication planning may define:

* What information must be communicated.
* Who should receive it.
* When it should be communicated.
* Who is responsible for communicating it.
* Which communication channel should be used.
* What level of detail is appropriate.
* Whether the information requires approval.

A communication plan helps ensure that important AI information is not communicated inconsistently or only after problems occur.

---

## 32. AI Communication Responsibilities

Responsibilities for AI communication should be clearly established.

Different responsibilities may include:

* Preparing communication.
* Reviewing information.
* Approving communication.
* Distributing communication.
* Responding to questions.
* Maintaining communication records.

For sensitive or high-impact communications, appropriate approval authority should be defined.

---

## 33. AI Communication Channels

Organizations may use multiple channels to communicate AI-related information.

Examples include:

* Email.
* Intranet.
* Internal knowledge bases.
* Training platforms.
* Meetings.
* Workshops.
* Governance committees.
* Dashboards.
* Management reports.
* Public websites.
* Customer portals.

The channel should be appropriate to the audience and sensitivity of the information.

---

## 34. AI Communication Frequency

Communication frequency should reflect the nature of the information.

Possible frequencies include:

* Continuous.
* Event-driven.
* Daily.
* Weekly.
* Monthly.
* Quarterly.
* Annually.

For example, a major AI incident may require immediate communication, while general AI awareness information may be communicated periodically.

---

## 35. AI Event-Driven Communication

Certain events should trigger specific communications.

Triggers may include:

* Major AI incidents.
* Significant system changes.
* New regulatory requirements.
* Policy changes.
* Security events.
* Privacy events.
* Material AI performance changes.

Event-driven communication should follow predefined escalation and notification procedures.

---

## 36. AI Communication Escalation

Communication should be escalated when an issue exceeds established thresholds.

Escalation may be required for:

* High-risk AI incidents.
* Significant privacy events.
* Material security incidents.
* Regulatory concerns.
* Significant AI system failures.
* Serious stakeholder impact.

Escalation paths should identify who must be informed and within what timeframe where applicable.

---

## 37. AI Communication Approval

Certain communications may require formal review and approval.

Examples include:

* Regulatory communications.
* Public statements.
* Customer notifications.
* Significant incident communications.
* Material policy changes.

Approval helps reduce the risk of inaccurate, incomplete or unauthorized information being released.

---

## 38. AI Communication Accuracy

AI-related communications should be factually accurate.

Organizations should take reasonable measures to avoid:

* Misleading statements.
* Unsupported claims.
* Incorrect AI performance information.
* Incomplete risk descriptions.
* Misrepresentation of AI capabilities.

Accuracy is particularly important where communication may influence stakeholder decisions.

---

## 39. AI Communication Consistency

AI information communicated through different channels should remain consistent.

For example:

```text id="6c4f8a"
AI Policy
     ↓
Employee Training
     ↓
Intranet Guidance
     ↓
Management Communication
```

Material inconsistencies can create confusion and weaken governance.

---

## 40. AI Communication Timeliness

Information should be communicated within a timeframe appropriate to its importance.

Timeliness is particularly important for:

* Security incidents.
* Privacy incidents.
* Regulatory changes.
* Critical AI failures.
* Emergency changes.
* Material governance decisions.

Delayed communication can increase organizational risk.

---

## 41. AI Communication Relevance

Communication should contain information relevant to its intended audience.

For example:

```text id="0r1b2m"
Board
→ Strategic Risk

Developer
→ Technical Requirements

Employee
→ Acceptable Use

Customer
→ Relevant AI Use and Impact
```

Relevant communication improves understanding and reduces information overload.

---

## 42. AI Communication Accessibility

AI-related information should be accessible to the people who need it.

Accessibility may consider:

* Language.
* Technical knowledge.
* Disability requirements.
* Geographic location.
* Work environment.
* Availability of communication channels.

Information should be presented in a form that the intended audience can reasonably understand and use.

---

## 43. AI Communication Clarity

AI communication should avoid unnecessary technical complexity.

Where technical concepts are required, they should be explained in terms appropriate to the audience.

For example, executive communication may focus on:

* Risk.
* Business impact.
* Decision requirements.
* Strategic implications.

Technical teams may require more detailed information about system behavior and controls.

---

## 44. AI Communication Confidentiality

AI communication may contain sensitive information.

Examples include:

* AI architecture.
* Security vulnerabilities.
* Confidential data.
* Customer information.
* Proprietary models.
* Business strategies.

Communication mechanisms should protect information according to its classification.

---

## 45. AI Communication Integrity

AI-related communication should be protected against unauthorized alteration.

Integrity controls may include:

* Access restrictions.
* Approval processes.
* Version control.
* Controlled repositories.
* Change tracking.

Stakeholders should be able to trust that important AI information has not been improperly modified.

---

## 46. AI Communication Availability

Relevant AI information should be available when needed.

Examples include:

* Policies.
* Procedures.
* Incident contacts.
* AI governance guidance.
* System documentation.
* Escalation instructions.

Availability requirements should reflect the operational importance of the information.

---

## 47. AI Communication Records

Certain communications may need to be retained as evidence.

Records may include:

* Governance decisions.
* Regulatory communications.
* Customer notifications.
* Incident notifications.
* Policy announcements.
* Training communications.

Retention requirements should reflect legal, regulatory, contractual and organizational needs.

## 49. AI Communication Feedback

Communication should allow stakeholders to provide feedback.

Feedback mechanisms may include:

* Surveys.

* Help desks.
* Workshops.
* Governance meetings.
* Feedback forms.

Feedback can identify misunderstandings and communication gaps.

## 50. AI Awareness Feedback

Personnel should have opportunities to identify areas where AI guidance is unclear.

For example, employees may ask:

* Which AI tools are approved?
* What information can be entered into an AI system?
* When is human review required?
* How should an AI incident be reported?

Recurring questions can identify areas requiring improved awareness materials.

---

## 51. AI Awareness Measurement

Organizations may measure whether awareness activities are reaching their intended audiences.

Possible indicators include:

* Awareness training completion.
* Knowledge assessment results.
* Participation rates.
* Policy acknowledgment.
* Awareness survey results.
* Incident-reporting trends.

Measurement should focus on useful evidence rather than activity counts alone.

---

## 52. AI Awareness Effectiveness

Awareness effectiveness should evaluate whether personnel actually understand and apply relevant requirements.

For example:

```text id="3h2f6q"
Training Completed
       ↓
Knowledge Tested
       ↓
Behavior Observed
       ↓
Incidents / Errors Evaluated
       ↓
Effectiveness Determined
```

Training completion by itself does not necessarily demonstrate effective awareness.

---

## 53. AI Awareness Testing

Knowledge assessments may be used to evaluate awareness.

Testing may include:

* Quizzes.
* Scenario-based questions.
* Simulations.
* Practical exercises.
* Policy assessments.

Testing should be appropriate to the role and level of risk.

---

## 54. AI Awareness Scenarios

Scenario-based awareness can help personnel understand how AI requirements apply to real situations.

For example:

> An employee wants to use a public generative AI service to summarize a document containing confidential customer information.

An awareness program should enable the employee to recognize:

* The information sensitivity.
* The potential risk.
* Whether the tool is approved.
* What alternative process should be used.
* Where to seek guidance.

Scenario-based learning can be more effective than policy statements alone.

---

## 55. AI Communication During Incidents

AI incident communication should be coordinated with incident management processes.

Potential communications may include:

* Initial notification.
* Incident escalation.
* Status updates.
* Stakeholder notifications.
* Regulatory notifications where required.
* Resolution communication.
* Lessons learned.

Communication requirements should be established before major incidents occur.

---

## 56. AI Incident Communication Roles

Incident communication should establish who is responsible for:

* Technical updates.
* Management escalation.
* Regulatory communication.
* Customer communication.
* Public communication.
* Internal employee communication.

Responsibilities should be clearly defined to avoid conflicting messages.

---

Major AI incidents may require crisis communication.

Examples include:

* Significant AI service failure.
* Major security compromise.
* Significant privacy event.
* Material harm caused by AI.
* Widespread incorrect AI output.

Crisis communication should be coordinated with the organization's broader crisis-management processes.

---

Potential information may include:

* Incident information.
* Risk assessments.
* Compliance evidence.
* System information.
* Corrective actions.

Regulatory communication should be managed by appropriately authorized personnel.

Communication may address:

* AI use.
* Relevant limitations.
* Human support.
* Important decisions.

* Appropriate disclosures.

The level of information should reflect the nature and impact of the AI system.

---

## 60. AI Supplier Communication

Suppliers may need to receive AI governance requirements.

* AI policies.

Supplier communication should be aligned with contractual and governance requirements.
