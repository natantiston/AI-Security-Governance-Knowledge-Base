# 04.15 — Internal Audit of the AIMS

## 1. Introduction to Internal Audit of the AIMS

Internal audit of the AI Management System (AIMS) provides an independent and systematic mechanism for evaluating whether the organization's AI management arrangements are appropriately implemented and maintained.

An internal audit can examine whether the AIMS:

* Conforms to established organizational requirements.
* Conforms to applicable ISO/IEC 42001 requirements.
* Is effectively implemented.
* Is maintained as intended.
* Supports achievement of AI objectives.
* Addresses identified AI risks and opportunities.
* Produces appropriate evidence.

Internal audit provides management with assurance about the effectiveness of the AIMS.

---

## 2. Purpose of AIMS Internal Audit

The purpose of an AIMS internal audit is to obtain objective evidence about the effectiveness and conformity of the AI Management System.

An audit may determine whether:

* Required processes have been established.
* Responsibilities have been assigned.
* Required controls are implemented.
* Documented requirements are followed.
* Evidence demonstrates effective operation.
* Identified deficiencies are addressed.

The audit should provide conclusions that management can use for improvement and decision-making.

---

## 3. Internal Audit as an Assurance Activity

Internal audit is an assurance mechanism within the management system.

It provides an independent perspective on whether established arrangements are operating as intended.

A simplified relationship is:

```text id="p8v3km"
AIMS Requirements
       ↓
Implementation
       ↓
Operation
       ↓
Audit Evidence
       ↓
Audit Evaluation
       ↓
Audit Findings
       ↓
Management Action
```

This helps distinguish internal audit from routine operational monitoring.

---

---

## 5. Internal Audit Scope

An audit scope defines the boundaries and subject matter of the audit.

The scope may specify:

* Organizational units.
* AI systems.
* AI processes.
* Locations.
* Technologies.
* Management-system processes.
* Applicable controls.
* Time period.

The scope should be appropriate to the audit objective.

---

## 6. AIMS Audit Objectives

Audit objectives define what the audit is intended to determine.

Objectives may include:

* Assessing conformity with ISO/IEC 42001.
* Evaluating implementation of AI policies.
* Assessing control effectiveness.
* Evaluating risk-management processes.
* Reviewing AI impact-assessment practices.
* Verifying corrective-action effectiveness.

Clear objectives provide direction for audit planning and execution.

---

## 7. AIMS Audit Criteria

Audit criteria provide the requirements against which evidence is evaluated.

Criteria may include:

* ISO/IEC 42001 requirements.
* Organizational AI policies.
* Internal procedures.
* AI governance standards.
* Risk criteria.
* Contractual requirements.
* Applicable legal or regulatory requirements.

Audit findings should be based on comparison between objective evidence and defined criteria.

---

## 8. Audit Evidence

Audit evidence consists of information that supports audit conclusions.

Evidence may include:

* Policies.
* Procedures.
* Records.
* System configurations.
* Logs.
* Risk assessments.
* AI impact assessments.
* Interviews.
* Observations.
* Test results.
* Management records.

Evidence should be relevant, reliable and sufficient for the audit objective.

---

## 9. Objective Evidence

Audit conclusions should be based on objective evidence rather than assumptions or unsupported opinions.

Objective evidence may demonstrate that:

* A process exists.
* A control is implemented.
* A required activity occurred.
* A decision was approved.
* A corrective action was completed.

Statements made during interviews may require supporting evidence where appropriate.

---

## 10. Audit Independence

Internal audit activities should be conducted with an appropriate degree of independence.

Auditors should avoid auditing activities where they have direct responsibility for the subject matter being evaluated, where practical.

Independence helps reduce conflicts of interest and strengthens the credibility of audit conclusions.

---

## 11. Auditor Objectivity

Auditors should approach the audit objectively and without predetermined conclusions.

Objectivity requires:

* Evidence-based evaluation.
* Consistent application of criteria.
* Professional judgment.
* Appropriate challenge.
* Avoidance of personal bias.

Auditors should report both conformity and nonconformity based on evidence.

---

## 12. Auditor Competence

AIMS auditors should possess appropriate knowledge and skills for the audit being performed.

Competence may include knowledge of:

* AI governance.
* ISO/IEC 42001.
* Management systems.
* Risk management.
* AI technologies.
* Cybersecurity.
* Privacy.
* Internal controls.
* Audit techniques.

Specialist expertise may be required for technically complex AI systems.

---

## 13. Auditor Training

Auditors may require training to maintain their competence.

Training may cover:

* AIMS requirements.
* Audit methodology.
* AI risks.
* AI lifecycle concepts.
* Evidence evaluation.
* Interview techniques.
* Reporting.
* Corrective-action verification.

Training should reflect the organization's audit requirements.

---

## 14. Audit Program

An audit program establishes the planned internal-audit activities over a defined period.

The program may consider:

* Audit frequency.
* Audit scope.
* Audit objectives.
* Audit criteria.
* Auditors.
* Resources.
* Priority areas.

The audit program should reflect the importance and risk of the activities being audited.

---

## 15. Risk-Based Audit Planning

AIMS internal-audit planning should consider the significance and risk associated with AI activities.

Higher-priority areas may include:

* High-impact AI systems.
* Significant AI risks.
* Critical business processes.
* Previous nonconformities.
* Repeated control failures.
* Major system changes.
* Significant incidents.

Risk-based planning helps direct audit resources where they provide the greatest assurance value.

---

## 16. Audit Frequency

Audit frequency should be determined according to organizational needs and relevant requirements.

Factors may include:

* Risk level.
* AI system criticality.
* Previous audit results.
* Significant changes.
* Incident history.
* Control performance.
* Organizational complexity.

High-risk areas may require more frequent auditing.

---

## 17. Audit Scheduling

Audit schedules should consider operational and organizational constraints.

Scheduling may account for:

* Availability of personnel.
* System criticality.
* Planned changes.
* Business cycles.
* Previous audit results.
* Required specialist resources.

Audits should be planned sufficiently in advance to obtain appropriate evidence.

---

## 18. Audit Team Selection

Audit teams should be selected according to the audit scope and required competence.

Considerations may include:

* Technical expertise.
* AI governance knowledge.
* Audit experience.
* Independence.
* Industry knowledge.
* Language or communication requirements.

A multidisciplinary team may be appropriate for complex AI environments.

---

## 19. Audit Resources

An effective audit requires appropriate resources.

Resources may include:

* Qualified auditors.
* Time.
* Audit tools.
* Documentation.
* System access.
* Technical specialists.
* Secure evidence storage.

Resource requirements should correspond to audit complexity.

---

## 20. Audit Preparation

Preparation establishes the foundation for effective audit execution.

Preparation may include:

* Reviewing previous audit results.
* Reviewing applicable requirements.
* Understanding the AI environment.
* Identifying relevant AI systems.
* Reviewing risk assessments.
* Preparing audit questions.
* Establishing sampling approaches.

Adequate preparation improves audit efficiency and evidence quality.

---

## 21. Audit Working Papers

Audit working papers document the work performed and evidence considered.

They may include:

* Audit plans.
* Checklists.
* Interview notes.
* Sampling records.
* Evidence references.
* Test results.
* Findings.
* Conclusions.

Working papers should be appropriately protected and retained.

---

## 22. Audit Checklist

A checklist can help auditors ensure that important areas are consistently considered.

It may cover:

* Context.
* Leadership.
* Planning.
* Risk and opportunity.
* Objectives.
* Resources.
* Operations.
* Impact assessment.
* Performance evaluation.
* Controls.
* Corrective action.
* Continual improvement.

A checklist should support professional judgment rather than replace it.

---

## 23. Audit Opening Meeting

An opening meeting establishes a common understanding of the audit.

Topics may include:

* Audit objectives.
* Scope.
* Criteria.
* Schedule.
* Audit methods.
* Roles.
* Communication arrangements.
* Evidence-access requirements.

The meeting helps establish transparency between auditors and auditees.

---

## 24. Audit Interviews

Interviews can provide information about how the AIMS operates in practice.

Auditors may interview:

* AI system owners.
* Process owners.
* Risk managers.
* Security personnel.
* Privacy personnel.
* Developers.
* Operators.
* Senior management.

Interview information should be evaluated against objective evidence where appropriate.

---

## 25. Audit Observation

Auditors may observe processes and activities to determine whether actual practices correspond with documented requirements.

Observation may include:

* Reviewing operational processes.
* Observing human oversight.
* Examining system-management activities.
* Observing control execution.

Observation provides evidence about actual implementation.

---

## 26. Audit Sampling

Auditors may use sampling when reviewing large populations of records or activities.

Sampling may involve:

* AI systems.
* Risk assessments.
* Change records.
* Access reviews.
* Training records.
* Incidents.
* Corrective actions.

Sampling methodology should be appropriate to the audit objective and population.

---

## 27. Audit Testing

Testing evaluates whether selected requirements or controls operate as intended.

Testing may include:

* Document inspection.
* Configuration review.
* Record verification.
* Re-performance.
* Observation.
* Interviews.
* Technical testing where appropriate.

Testing should produce sufficient evidence to support the audit conclusion.

---

## 28. Audit Traceability

Audit conclusions should be traceable to evidence and applicable criteria.

A strong audit trail may follow:

```text id="q3m8vx"
Audit Criterion
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Finding
      ↓
Conclusion
```

Traceability improves the defensibility and credibility of audit results.

---

## 29. Audit Findings

An audit finding represents the result of evaluating evidence against audit criteria.

Findings may identify:

* Conformity.
* Nonconformity.
* Observations.
* Opportunities for improvement.

Findings should be clearly supported by evidence.

---

## 30. Audit Conclusions

Audit conclusions summarize the overall results of the audit.

A conclusion may address:

* Conformity.
* Effectiveness.
* Significant weaknesses.
* Risks requiring attention.
* Opportunities for improvement.

Conclusions should reflect the evidence obtained within the defined audit scope.
