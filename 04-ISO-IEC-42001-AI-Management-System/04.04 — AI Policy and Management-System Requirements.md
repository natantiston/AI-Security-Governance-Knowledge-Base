# 04.04 — AI Policy and Management-System Requirements

## 1. Introduction to AI Policy and Management-System Requirements

An AI Management System (AIMS) requires a structured set of policies, principles, requirements, and management practices that establish how the organization governs the development, provision, deployment, use, monitoring, and continual improvement of AI systems.

AI policy provides the organizational direction for AI governance. Management-system requirements translate that direction into structured expectations that can be implemented, monitored, evaluated, and improved.

The relationship can be represented as:

```text
Organizational Strategy
        ↓
AI Governance Direction
        ↓
AI Policy
        ↓
Management-System Requirements
        ↓
Processes and Controls
        ↓
Operational Implementation
        ↓
Monitoring and Evaluation
        ↓
Continual Improvement
```

A policy should therefore not exist as an isolated document. It should provide the foundation for the broader management system.

---

## 2. Purpose of an AI Policy

An AI policy establishes the organization's high-level position regarding the responsible management and use of AI.

It may communicate the organization's commitment to:

* Responsible AI.
* Appropriate AI governance.
* Risk-based decision-making.
* Security.
* Privacy.
* Transparency.
* Accountability.
* Regulatory compliance.
* Human oversight.
* Continual improvement.

The policy provides a common direction for business units, technical teams, governance functions, and other stakeholders.

---

## 3. AI Policy as a Management Direction

An effective policy communicates management's expectations.

It should answer fundamental questions such as:

* Why does the organization govern AI?
* What AI activities are within scope?
* What principles apply?
* Who is accountable?
* What expectations apply to employees and third parties?
* How are significant AI risks managed?
* How are AI governance requirements maintained?

A policy should provide direction without attempting to document every operational procedure.

---

## 4. AI Policy and Organizational Context

The AI policy should reflect the organization's context.

Relevant factors may include:

* Organizational size.
* Industry.
* Geographic presence.
* Regulatory environment.
* AI use cases.
* Business objectives.
* Risk profile.
* Technology environment.
* Stakeholder expectations.
* AI maturity.

For example, an organization developing safety-critical AI systems may require substantially different governance emphasis from an organization using AI primarily for internal productivity.

## 6. AI Policy and Risk Management

AI policy should establish a risk-based approach.

This means the organization should not necessarily apply identical governance requirements to every AI system.

Risk considerations may include:

* Business criticality.
* Impact on individuals.
* Data sensitivity.
* Security exposure.
* Regulatory requirements.
* Degree of automation.
* Decision-making authority.
* External stakeholder impact.
* Potential harm.

Higher-risk AI systems may require stronger governance and oversight.

Scope may cover:

* AI systems developed internally.

* AI-enabled SaaS platforms.
* Generative AI tools.
* Machine-learning models.

* AI used for internal processes.
* AI services provided to customers.

The scope should be sufficiently clear to prevent uncertainty about which activities fall under the AIMS.

## 12. AI Policy

The policy establishes high-level mandatory organizational requirements.

It may state that:

* AI systems must be appropriately governed.
* Significant AI risks must be assessed.
* AI systems must have accountable owners.
* Applicable requirements must be identified.
* Appropriate controls must be implemented.
* AI performance must be monitored.
* Significant incidents must be escalated.

The policy should establish expectations without becoming an operational procedure manual.

Examples include:

* AI security standard.
* AI data governance standard.
* AI model management standard.
* AI supplier standard.
* AI testing standard.
* AI logging standard.

Standards provide greater specificity than policy.

## 14. AI Procedures

Procedures describe how requirements are executed.

Examples include procedures for:

* AI risk assessment.
* AI impact assessment.
* AI system approval.
* AI incident management.
* AI model change management.
* AI supplier assessment.
* AI retirement.

Procedures should provide sufficiently clear steps for consistent execution.

## 15. AI Guidelines

Guidelines provide recommended practices where strict mandatory requirements may not be appropriate.

Examples include:

* Recommended prompt-security practices.
* Recommended documentation practices.
* Recommended human-review practices.
* Recommended model-testing practices.

Guidelines should be clearly distinguished from mandatory requirements.

## 16. AI Policy and Mandatory Language

Policy language should distinguish between mandatory and recommended requirements.

Common mandatory terms include:

* Must.
* Shall.
* Required.
* Prohibited.
* Mandatory.

Recommended language may include:

* Should.
* Recommended.
* May.
* Where appropriate.

Ambiguous language can create inconsistent interpretation.

* Using AI systems outside approved purposes.
* Bypassing security controls.
* Misrepresenting AI-generated information.

Prohibited activities should be clearly defined and appropriately governed.

Control types may include:

### Preventive

### Detective

Designed to identify problems.

### Corrective

### Compensating

Designed to reduce risk when the primary control cannot operate as intended.

A mature AIMS may use multiple control types.
