# 04.10 — AI Management-System Documentation

## 1. Introduction to AI Management-System Documentation

Documentation is a fundamental component of an effective AI Management System (AIMS).

An AIMS requires documented information to establish, operate, maintain and demonstrate the effectiveness of AI governance and management processes.

Documentation provides evidence of:

* Organizational requirements.
* AI governance arrangements.
* Roles and responsibilities.
* AI risks.
* AI objectives.
* Processes and controls.
* Decisions.
* Assessments.
* Monitoring activities.
* Corrective actions.
* Continual improvement.

Documentation should support the practical operation of the AIMS rather than become an administrative exercise.

---

## 2. Purpose of AIMS Documentation

AIMS documentation serves several purposes.

It can:

* Define how AI management activities are performed.
* Establish organizational expectations.
* Provide evidence of implementation.
* Support accountability.
* Preserve organizational knowledge.
* Facilitate communication.
* Support audits.
* Demonstrate conformity.
* Enable continual improvement.

The amount and detail of documentation should be appropriate to the organization's circumstances.

---

## 3. Documented Information

An organization should distinguish between information that needs to be maintained as part of the AIMS and information that needs to be retained as evidence of activities performed.

Examples include:

**Maintained information**

* Policies.
* Procedures.
* Methodologies.
* Guidelines.
* Frameworks.
* Process descriptions.

**Retained evidence**

* Completed assessments.
* Audit results.
* Management-review records.
* Training records.
* Incident records.
* Corrective-action records.

Both forms contribute to effective AIMS operation.

---

## 4. Documentation as an AIMS Control Mechanism

Documentation can function as a governance mechanism by establishing a common reference point.

For example:

```text id="6j3x7p"

    ↓

    ↓
Defined Process
    ↓
Assigned Responsibility
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Evidence
```

This creates traceability between organizational expectations and actual AI management activities.

## 31. AI Documentation Ownership

Every significant AIMS document should have an identifiable owner.

The document owner may be responsible for:

* Maintaining the document.
* Ensuring accuracy.
* Coordinating reviews.
* Incorporating approved changes.
* Managing the document lifecycle.
* Ensuring appropriate availability.
* Coordinating retirement when the document is no longer required.

Ownership prevents important documentation from becoming unmanaged or outdated.

---

## 32. AI Documentation Approval

Documents that establish organizational requirements should be appropriately reviewed and approved before becoming effective.

Approval may apply to:

* AI policies.
* Standards.
* Procedures.
* Governance frameworks.
* Risk methodologies.
* Assessment methodologies.
* Control requirements.

Approval authority should correspond to the significance of the document.

---

## 33. AI Documentation Review

Documentation should be reviewed periodically and when significant changes occur.

Review triggers may include:

* Regulatory changes.
* Organizational changes.
* New AI technologies.
* New AI risks.
* Significant incidents.
* Audit findings.
* Changes to AI systems.
* Changes to business processes.

The purpose of review is to ensure that documentation remains suitable and effective.

| Document Type              | Potential Review Approach                      |

Specific frequencies should be defined by the organization where appropriate.

* ISO/IEC 42001 requirements.
* Organizational policies.
* Legal and regulatory obligations.
* Contractual commitments.

## 62. Determining the Appropriate Level of Documentation

* AI system risk.
* System complexity.
* Potential impact.
* Regulatory significance.
* Number of affected stakeholders.
* Business criticality.
* Degree of automation.
* Use of third-party AI services.

Higher-risk AI activities generally require stronger documentation and evidence.

## 63. Risk-Based Documentation

A risk-based approach can help determine documentation requirements.

For example:

```text
Low-Risk AI
    ↓
Basic Documentation

Moderate-Risk AI
    ↓
Detailed Governance Documentation

High-Risk AI
    ↓
Enhanced Assessment + Decision Records + Evidence
```

This approach helps avoid both under-documentation and unnecessary administrative burden.

## 64. AI Documentation and Proportionality

Proportionality helps maintain an efficient AIMS.

## 147. AI Documentation Principles

A practical set of principles for AIMS documentation includes:

1. **Purposeful** — document information because it serves a governance, operational, legal or evidentiary purpose.
2. **Accurate** — documentation should reflect reality.
3. **Current** — outdated information should be identified and controlled.
4. **Traceable** — important decisions and evidence should be connected.
5. **Accessible** — authorized personnel should be able to find required information.
6. **Protected** — sensitive information should be appropriately secured.
7. **Proportionate** — documentation should reflect risk and organizational complexity.
8. **Controlled** — documents should have appropriate ownership and lifecycle management.
