# 04.19 — Management Review

## 1. Introduction to Management Review

Management review is a structured governance activity through which top management evaluates the continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the AI Management System (AIMS).

It provides an opportunity for leadership to examine whether the AIMS is:

* Achieving its intended objectives.
* Managing relevant AI risks.
* Meeting applicable requirements.
* Responding to changes in the organization and its environment.
* Supporting responsible AI governance.
* Receiving adequate resources.
* Improving over time.

Management review connects operational evidence with strategic decision-making.

A simplified relationship is:

```text
AIMS Performance
      ↓
Monitoring & Evaluation
      ↓
Management Review
      ↓
Management Decisions
      ↓
Actions / Resources / Improvements
      ↓
Improved AIMS Performance
```

---

## 2. Purpose of Management Review

The primary purpose of management review is to provide top management with a structured mechanism for evaluating the AIMS and determining whether changes or actions are required.

Management review helps leadership determine:

* Whether the AIMS remains suitable.
* Whether the AIMS remains adequate.
* Whether the AIMS remains effective.
* Whether objectives are being achieved.
* Whether significant risks are appropriately managed.
* Whether improvement opportunities should be pursued.

It is therefore more than a status meeting or presentation of cybersecurity or AI metrics.

---

## 3. Management Review as a Leadership Activity

Management review demonstrates that AI governance is a management responsibility rather than solely a technical or compliance function.

Leadership should use management review to:

* Challenge assumptions.
* Evaluate evidence.
* Make decisions.
* Resolve significant issues.
* Allocate resources.
* Establish priorities.
* Direct improvement.

This reinforces accountability for the AIMS at the appropriate management level.

---

## 4. Management Review and the AIMS

Management review provides a connection between day-to-day operation of the AIMS and organizational leadership.

The relationship can be represented as:

```text
AI Governance Activities
        ↓
Operational Evidence
        ↓
Performance Information
        ↓
Management Review
        ↓
Leadership Decisions
        ↓
AIMS Direction
```

This allows management to evaluate whether the AIMS continues to support organizational objectives.

---

## 5. Management Review and Strategic Alignment

The AIMS should remain aligned with the organization's strategic direction.

Management review may therefore consider changes in:

* Business strategy.
* AI strategy.
* Organizational priorities.
* AI adoption.
* Operating models.
* Technology strategy.
* Risk appetite.

A significant change in organizational strategy may require corresponding changes to AI governance.

---

## 6. Management Review and Organizational Context

Management should consider whether changes in the internal or external context affect the AIMS.

Internal changes may include:

* Organizational restructuring.
* New AI initiatives.
* Changes in leadership.
* New business processes.
* Changes in resources.
* Changes in technology.

External changes may include:

* Regulatory developments.
* New AI technologies.
* Emerging threats.
* Market developments.
* Stakeholder expectations.
* Industry practices.

Changes in context can create new risks or improvement opportunities.

---

## 7. Management Review and AI Governance Objectives

Management review should consider whether established AIMS objectives remain appropriate and whether they are being achieved.

Management may evaluate:

* Objective status.
* Performance against targets.
* Deviations.
* Reasons for underperformance.
* Resource constraints.
* Required corrective actions.

Where objectives are consistently missed, management should determine whether the issue concerns execution, resources, measurement or the objectives themselves.

---

## 8. Management Review and Performance

Management review should be supported by meaningful performance information.

Performance information may include:

* AI risk indicators.
* Control performance.
* Audit findings.
* Nonconformities.
* Incident trends.
* Assessment completion.
* Corrective-action status.
* Stakeholder feedback.

Performance information should enable management to make informed decisions rather than simply receive reports.

---

## 9. Management Review and Evidence

Management review should be evidence-based.

Relevant evidence may include:

* Performance data.
* Audit results.
* Risk assessments.
* AI impact assessments.
* Incident reports.
* Monitoring results.
* Corrective-action records.
* Stakeholder feedback.
* Regulatory developments.

Reliable evidence enables management to distinguish between perceived performance and actual AIMS performance.

---

## 10. Management Review Inputs

Management review should consider relevant information about the AIMS.

Typical inputs may include:

* Status of previous management-review actions.
* Changes in internal and external issues.
* Changes in stakeholder requirements.
* AIMS performance.
* Achievement of objectives.
* Nonconformities and corrective actions.
* Monitoring and measurement results.
* Audit results.
* Feedback from relevant stakeholders.
* Risk and opportunity information.
* Improvement opportunities.
* Resource adequacy.

The precise inputs should reflect the organization's context and AIMS requirements.

---

## 11. Status of Previous Management Review Actions

Management should review whether actions from previous reviews were completed as intended.

This can include:

```text
Previous Decision
      ↓
Assigned Action
      ↓
Implementation Status
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Effectiveness
```

An action should not be considered successful merely because it was marked as completed.

---

## 12. Changes Affecting the AIMS

Management review should identify significant changes that may affect the AIMS.

Examples include:

* New AI applications.
* Changes to existing AI systems.
* New regulations.
* New organizational structures.
* Changes in suppliers.
* New cybersecurity threats.
* Changes in stakeholder expectations.

These changes may require updates to governance arrangements, controls or resources.

---

## 13. Stakeholder Requirements

Management should consider whether relevant stakeholder requirements have changed.

Stakeholders may include:

* Customers.
* Employees.
* Regulators.
* Business partners.
* Suppliers.
* Users.
* Individuals affected by AI systems.
* Senior leadership.

Changes in stakeholder expectations may create new governance requirements or improvement opportunities.

---

## 14. AI Risk Information

AI risk information is an important management-review input.

Management may evaluate:

* Significant AI risks.
* Changes in risk levels.
* Emerging risks.
* Risk treatment status.
* Residual risk.
* Risk acceptance.
* Control effectiveness.

The objective is to determine whether the organization's risk posture remains appropriate.

---

## 15. AI Impact Information

Where relevant, management may also consider information concerning AI impacts.

This can include:

* Stakeholder impacts.
* Human impacts.
* Social impacts.
* Privacy considerations.
* Security implications.
* Fairness-related concerns.
* Changes in intended use.

Management should consider whether identified impacts require changes to governance or controls.

---

## 16. Audit Results

Audit results provide independent or structured evidence regarding AIMS performance.

Management review may consider:

* Internal audit results.
* External audit results.
* Certification assessments.
* Follow-up findings.
* Recurring findings.
* Significant observations.

The focus should be on understanding what the findings mean for the overall effectiveness of the AIMS.

---

## 17. Nonconformities and Corrective Actions

Management should consider significant nonconformities and the status of corrective actions.

Relevant questions include:

* Are significant findings being resolved?
* Are corrective actions effective?
* Are findings recurring?
* Are actions overdue?
* Do findings indicate systemic weaknesses?

Recurring or significant findings may require management intervention.

---

## 18. Monitoring and Measurement Results

Monitoring and measurement provide quantitative and qualitative evidence of AIMS performance.

Management may review:

* Key performance indicators.
* Key risk indicators.
* Control performance.
* Compliance metrics.
* AI monitoring results.
* Incident trends.

Metrics should be interpreted in context rather than considered in isolation.

---

## 19. Stakeholder Feedback

Stakeholder feedback can provide valuable information about how AI governance operates in practice.

Feedback may reveal:

* Governance concerns.
* Usability problems.
* Transparency issues.
* Process weaknesses.
* Communication gaps.
* Emerging expectations.

Management should consider whether recurring feedback indicates a broader improvement opportunity.

---

## 20. Resource Adequacy

Management review should consider whether the AIMS has adequate resources to operate effectively.

Resources may include:

* Personnel.
* Competence.
* Budget.
* Technology.
* Governance tools.
* Training.
* External expertise.

Resource constraints that materially affect AIMS performance should be visible to management.

---

## 21. Continual Improvement Inputs

Management review should consider opportunities to improve the AIMS.

Potential improvement areas include:

* Governance processes.
* Risk management.
* Controls.
* AI lifecycle management.
* Monitoring.
* Documentation.
* Training.
* Supplier management.
* Automation.

Management review can therefore serve as a major input into the continual-improvement process.

---

## 22. Management Review Frequency

Management review should occur at planned intervals appropriate to the organization's circumstances.

The frequency should consider:

* Organizational size.
* AI portfolio.
* Risk profile.
* Regulatory environment.
* Rate of change.
* AIMS maturity.

Higher-risk or rapidly changing environments may require more frequent management attention.

---

## 23. Management Review Participants

Management review should involve appropriate leadership and subject-matter participants.

Depending on organizational structure, participants may include:

* Top management.
* AI governance leadership.
* Risk management.
* Information security.
* Privacy.
* Legal.
* Compliance.
* Business leadership.
* Technology leadership.

Participants should have sufficient authority and knowledge to contribute to meaningful decisions.

---

## 24. Management Review Preparation

Effective management review requires preparation.

Preparation may include:

* Collecting performance information.
* Validating metrics.
* Reviewing previous actions.
* Identifying significant issues.
* Preparing decision items.
* Highlighting emerging risks.

Good preparation allows management to focus on decisions rather than spending the entire review validating basic information.

---

## 25. Management Review Agenda

A structured agenda can help ensure important topics are addressed.

A practical agenda may include:

```text
1. Previous Actions
2. Context Changes
3. AIMS Performance
4. Objectives
5. Risk & Opportunity
6. Audit Results
7. Nonconformities
8. Corrective Actions
9. Stakeholder Feedback
10. Resource Adequacy
11. Improvement Opportunities
12. Management Decisions
```

The agenda should be adapted to organizational needs.

---

## 26. Management Review as a Decision Forum

Management review should not become a passive reporting exercise.

Its value comes from management decisions regarding:

* Priorities.
* Resources.
* Risk treatment.
* Governance changes.
* Corrective actions.
* Improvement initiatives.

The output should demonstrate that leadership actively evaluated the AIMS.

---

## 27. Management Review and Accountability

Management review reinforces accountability by connecting performance information with responsible functions.

For significant issues, management may identify:

* Responsible owner.
* Required action.
* Target date.
* Required resources.
* Expected outcome.

This ensures that management decisions are translated into accountable actions.

---

## 28. Management Review and Risk Appetite

Management review provides an opportunity to consider whether current AI risks remain within organizational risk appetite.

For example:

```text
AI Risk
   ↓
Residual Risk
   ↓
Risk Appetite
   ↓
Within Appetite?
   ├── Yes → Continue Monitoring
   └── No  → Additional Treatment / Decision
```

This helps connect operational AI risk management with executive-level risk decisions.

---

## 29. Management Review and Strategic Decisions

Management review may identify issues requiring strategic decisions.

Examples include:

* Whether to expand AI governance capabilities.
* Whether to invest in governance technology.
* Whether to restrict certain AI use cases.
* Whether additional expertise is required.
* Whether supplier arrangements should change.

These decisions demonstrate the strategic role of AIMS governance.

---

## 30. Management Review and Organizational Direction

Effective management review provides leadership with a structured mechanism to determine whether the AIMS continues to support the organization's objectives while managing AI-related risks and responding to changes in its operating environment.
