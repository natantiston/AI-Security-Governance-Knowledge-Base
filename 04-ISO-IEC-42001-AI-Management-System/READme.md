# Chapter 04 — ISO/IEC 42001 Artificial Intelligence Management System

## Overview

Chapter 04 provides a comprehensive foundation for understanding **ISO/IEC 42001**, the international management-system standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an **Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS)**.

The chapter approaches ISO/IEC 42001 from a **GRC, cybersecurity, risk-management, governance, and implementation perspective**.

The objective is not simply to understand the requirements of the standard, but to understand how an organization can translate those requirements into an operational AI governance capability.

---

## Chapter Objectives

By completing Chapter 04, the learner should be able to:

* Explain the purpose and structure of ISO/IEC 42001.
* Understand the concept of an Artificial Intelligence Management System.
* Explain the relationship between AI governance, AI risk management, cybersecurity, privacy, and enterprise GRC.
* Understand organizational context and AIMS scope.
* Identify relevant interested parties and their requirements.
* Understand leadership responsibilities and AI governance structures.
* Establish AI policies and objectives.
* Develop an AI inventory.
* Perform AI risk assessment and treatment.
* Understand AI impact assessment.
* Understand Annex A controls.
* Develop and maintain a Statement of Applicability.
* Establish operational AI governance processes.
* Understand monitoring, measurement, internal audit, and management review.
* Implement continual improvement.
* Prepare an organization for ISO/IEC 42001 certification.
* Understand certification readiness versus documentation readiness.
* Maintain the AIMS after certification.

# Core Concepts Covered in Chapter 04

## Artificial Intelligence Management System

An **AIMS** is the management-system structure through which an organization governs its AI-related activities, risks, impacts, controls, performance, and continual improvement.

---

## AI Governance

AI governance establishes:

* Who makes AI decisions.
* Who owns AI risks.
* Who approves AI systems.
* Who operates controls.
* Who provides oversight.
* Who provides independent assurance.

---

## AI Risk Management

AI risk management establishes a systematic approach to:

```text
Identify
   ↓
Analyze
   ↓
Evaluate
   ↓
Treat
   ↓
Monitor
   ↓
Review
```

---

## AI Impact Assessment

Impact assessment examines the potential consequences of AI systems on relevant individuals, groups, organizations, society, and other stakeholders.

---

## Annex A

Annex A provides a structured set of AI-related controls that organizations can evaluate as part of their AIMS.

The controls should be considered in relation to:

* Organizational context.
* AI risks.
* AI impacts.
* Applicable requirements.
* Business objectives.

---

## Statement of Applicability

The **Statement of Applicability (SoA)** documents the organization's decisions regarding control applicability.

The fundamental question is:

> **Which controls are applicable to our organization, and why?**

---

## Objective Evidence

AIMS implementation should be supported by objective evidence.

Examples include:

* Policies.
* Risk assessments.
* Impact assessments.
* Approval records.
* Control records.
* Monitoring results.
* Training records.
* Audit reports.
* Management-review records.
* Corrective-action records.

---

## Certification Readiness

Certification readiness requires more than having documentation.

A useful distinction is:

```text
Documentation
      ≠
Implementation
      ≠
Operational Effectiveness
      ≠
Certification Readiness
```

Certification readiness requires the organization to demonstrate that the AIMS is established, implemented, maintained, monitored, evaluated, and continually improved.

# Key Implementation Principle

The central lesson of Chapter 04 is:

> **ISO/IEC 42001 should not be implemented as a documentation exercise. It should establish a functioning management system that enables an organization to identify, assess, govern, monitor, and continually improve how it manages AI.**

The objective is to move from:

```text
Compliance Documentation
        ↓
Control Implementation
        ↓
Operational Governance
        ↓
Measured Effectiveness
        ↓
Continual Improvement
```

# Certification Readiness Model

An organization should be able to answer five fundamental questions:

### 1. What AI do we have?

**AI inventory and AIMS scope**

### 2. What can go wrong?

**AI risk and impact assessment**

### 3. What are we doing about it?

**Risk treatment and controls**

### 4. How do we know it works?

**Evidence, monitoring, testing and assurance**

### 5. How do we improve?

**Internal audit, management review and continual improvement**

This provides a practical mental model for understanding the entire chapter.
