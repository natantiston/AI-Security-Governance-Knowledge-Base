# 18.03 — AI Security KRIs

## Part 1 — AI Security KRI Foundations, Risk Measurement, Governance and Interpretation

### 1. AI Security KRI

An **AI Security Key Risk Indicator (KRI)** is a metric selected because it provides meaningful information about changes in an AI security risk condition.

A KRI is intended to help management identify:

- increasing risk exposure;
- deteriorating security conditions;
- emerging risk;
- concentration;
- control weakness;
- risk accumulation;
- conditions approaching defined tolerance levels.

A KRI is therefore primarily **risk-oriented**, rather than performance-oriented.

### 2. KRI Objective

The objective of an AI security KRI is to provide timely and decision-useful information about a risk condition.

A KRI should help answer questions such as:

- Is a material AI security risk increasing?
- Is exposure approaching a defined tolerance?
- Is a risk becoming concentrated?
- Is a control weakness increasing residual exposure?
- Does management need to reassess the risk?

### 3. KRI vs KPI

A **KPI** measures performance against an objective.

A **KRI** provides information about risk exposure or changing risk conditions.

For example:

```text id="kri-kpi01"
KPI
Security assessments completed
        |
        v
Measures performance

KRI
Critical AI systems without current assessment
        |
        v
Indicates risk exposure
```

The same underlying data can sometimes support both a KPI and a KRI, but the interpretation and governance purpose are different.

### 4. KRI vs Metric

A metric is any quantitative or qualitative measurement.

A KRI is a metric that has been deliberately selected because it provides meaningful information about risk.

Therefore:

**Every KRI is a metric, but not every metric is a KRI.**

### 5. KRI vs Control

A KRI is not itself a security control.

A control reduces, detects, transfers, or otherwise manages risk.

A KRI provides information about the condition of the risk or the effectiveness of relevant risk management.

```text id="kri-control01"
RISK
  |
  +------> CONTROL
  |          |
  |          v
  |      Risk Treatment
  |
  +------> KRI
             |
             v
        Risk Visibility
```

### 6. KRI vs Control Objective

A control objective defines the intended condition that controls should achieve.

A KRI measures or indicates a relevant risk condition.

For example:

- **Control objective:** Critical AI systems should have appropriate security monitoring.
- **KRI:** Percentage of critical AI systems without required security monitoring.

The KRI provides visibility into the risk condition; it does not establish the control objective.

### 7. KRI vs Risk Appetite

Risk appetite defines the level and types of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.

A KRI provides information that may help management determine whether risk remains within the approved appetite.

A KRI should not automatically be treated as the risk appetite itself.

### 8. KRI vs Risk Tolerance

Risk tolerance defines an acceptable range of variation around an objective, risk condition, or appetite boundary.

A KRI can be associated with a tolerance where appropriate.

The relationship should be explicitly documented.

### 9. KRI vs Threshold

A KRI threshold is a defined measurement boundary that may trigger:

- investigation;
- escalation;
- management review;
- risk reassessment;
- corrective action.

A threshold is therefore a governance mechanism associated with the KRI.

### 10. KRI Scope

KRI scope defines the population, systems, risks, environments, or business services covered by the measurement.

Scope should identify whether the KRI applies to:

- all AI systems;
- critical AI systems;
- specific models;
- specific business services;
- specific suppliers;
- specific environments;
- defined risk categories.

### 11. KRI Risk Domain

Each KRI should identify the risk domain it measures or informs.

Possible domains include:

- AI cybersecurity;
- model security;
- data security;
- identity and access;
- AI supply-chain security;
- privacy;
- resilience;
- operational security;
- third-party risk;
- AI safety.

### 12. KRI Risk Statement

A KRI should be linked to a defined risk statement.

A risk statement should describe:

- the risk event;
- relevant cause or condition;
- potential consequence.

This prevents KRIs from becoming disconnected measurements without a defined risk purpose.

### 13. KRI Risk Owner

Each material KRI should have an identified risk owner or accountable governance owner.

The owner should understand:

- what risk the KRI represents;
- how the KRI is calculated;
- how it should be interpreted;
- what action may be required.

### 14. KRI Data Owner

The KRI data owner is responsible for the reliability and availability of the underlying measurement data.

The data owner may be different from the risk owner.

### 15. KRI Definition

The KRI definition should specify precisely what the measurement represents.

A definition should avoid ambiguous terms such as:

- significant;
- adequate;
- sufficient;
- material;

unless those terms have defined organizational criteria.

### 16. KRI Formula

Where quantitative calculation is used, the formula should be documented.

For example:

```text id="kri-formula01"
KRI =
Number of critical AI systems
without required security control
----------------------------------
Total number of critical AI systems
```

The formula should be consistently applied across reporting periods.

### 17. KRI Numerator

The numerator identifies the measured risk population or condition being counted.

The numerator definition should be precise enough to permit reproducible calculation.

### 18. KRI Denominator

The denominator establishes the population against which the numerator is evaluated.

Changes to the denominator can materially change KRI interpretation even when the numerator remains unchanged.

### 19. KRI Population

The KRI population defines the complete set of records, assets, systems, events, or conditions from which the measurement is derived.

The population should be documented and appropriately governed.

### 20. KRI Inclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria determine which records or conditions enter the KRI calculation.

Criteria should be:

- documented;
- consistently applied;
- reproducible;
- appropriate to the defined risk.

### 21. KRI Exclusion Criteria

Exclusions should be explicitly documented where they could materially affect interpretation.

Uncontrolled exclusions can create artificial reductions in reported risk.

### 22. KRI Measurement Period

The measurement period identifies the time interval represented by the KRI.

Examples include:

- daily;
- weekly;
- monthly;
- quarterly;
- annual;
- rolling 30-day period.

The measurement period should reflect the risk's behavior and decision requirements.

### 23. KRI Measurement Frequency

Measurement frequency defines how often the KRI is calculated.

High-volatility risks may require more frequent measurement than relatively stable strategic risks.

### 24. KRI Reporting Frequency

Reporting frequency determines how often the KRI is presented to its intended audience.

Measurement and reporting frequency do not necessarily need to be identical.

### 25. KRI Baseline

A KRI baseline provides a reference condition against which changes in risk can be interpreted.

Possible baselines include:

- historical performance;
- approved risk condition;
- established operating state;
- defined reference population.

### 26. KRI Risk Trend

KRI trend represents the direction and persistence of risk movement over time.

A single observation may not establish a meaningful trend.

### 27. KRI Risk Directionality

The direction of risk should be explicitly defined.

For example:

- higher value = higher risk;
- lower value = higher risk;
- movement away from target = increasing risk.

Directionality must not be assumed.

### 28. KRI Risk Velocity

Risk velocity describes how quickly a risk condition changes.

A rapidly increasing KRI may require more urgent management attention than a slowly changing KRI even when both reach the same absolute value.

### 29. KRI Risk Acceleration

Risk acceleration describes an increasing rate of change in the risk condition.

For example:

```text id="kri-velocity01"
Risk Level
   |
   |                 /
   |              ./
   |           ./
   |        ./
   |     ./
   |___.________________ Time
```

Acceleration may provide an earlier warning than absolute risk level alone.

### 30. KRI Risk Persistence

Risk persistence measures whether an elevated condition continues across multiple measurement periods.

Persistent elevation may indicate a structural condition rather than an isolated event.

### 31. KRI Risk Recurrence

Risk recurrence identifies repeated reappearance of a risk condition after apparent resolution.

Recurrence can indicate:

- ineffective remediation;
- control weakness;
- systemic causes;
- changing threat conditions.

### 32. KRI Risk Volatility

Risk volatility describes the degree to which the KRI changes over time.

High volatility may reflect:

- unstable controls;
- changing threat conditions;
- operational variability;
- measurement instability.

### 33. KRI Risk Stability

A stable KRI indicates limited movement over the defined observation period.

Stability should not automatically be interpreted as low risk.

A stable high-risk condition remains high risk.

### 34. KRI Risk Concentration

Risk concentration occurs when significant exposure is clustered within a limited number of:

- models;
- applications;
- suppliers;
- platforms;
- business units;
- geographic regions;
- datasets.

### 35. KRI Risk Aggregation

KRI aggregation combines risk measurements across multiple populations.

Aggregation can support enterprise-level visibility but may conceal localized or concentrated risk.

### 36. KRI Risk Disaggregation

Disaggregation allows management to examine risk by dimensions such as:

- AI system;
- model;
- business service;
- supplier;
- geography;
- environment;
- risk category.

Disaggregation is particularly important where aggregate values may conceal material exposure.

### 37. KRI Risk Segmentation

Risk segmentation divides the measured population into meaningful groups.

Possible segments include:

- critical vs non-critical AI;
- production vs development;
- internal vs external models;
- regulated vs non-regulated use cases;
- high-risk vs lower-risk systems.

### 38. KRI Criticality

KRI criticality reflects the importance of the risk information to organizational decision-making.

A KRI may be classified as critical where failure to monitor it could materially impair risk governance.

### 39. KRI Materiality

Materiality concerns whether a KRI condition could materially affect:

- security;
- operations;
- customers;
- regulatory exposure;
- financial position;
- resilience;
- organizational objectives.

Materiality criteria should be defined by the governance framework.

### 40. KRI Risk Threshold

A risk threshold establishes a measurement boundary associated with a defined management response.

Thresholds should be documented and approved according to the organization's governance process.

A threshold established internally is not automatically a statutory or regulatory threshold.

### 41. KRI Early-Warning Threshold

An early-warning threshold may indicate that risk is approaching a level requiring increased management attention.

It may support preventive intervention before a higher-risk condition develops.

### 42. KRI Escalation Threshold

An escalation threshold identifies a condition that requires escalation under defined governance procedures.

Escalation criteria should specify:

- recipient;
- timeframe;
- required information;
- potential action.

### 43. KRI Critical Threshold

A critical threshold represents a condition requiring heightened governance attention because the measured risk has reached a materially concerning state according to the organization's approved criteria.

The designation is internal unless established by an applicable external requirement.

### 44. KRI Threshold Calibration

Thresholds should be calibrated using appropriate evidence such as:

- historical risk levels;
- incident experience;
- control performance;
- risk appetite;
- operational conditions;
- threat intelligence.

Threshold calibration should be periodically reviewed.

### 45. KRI Threshold Breach

A threshold breach should trigger appropriate analysis to determine:

- whether the measurement is valid;
- whether the condition is genuine;
- whether the risk is material;
- whether escalation is required;
- whether corrective action is appropriate.

A threshold breach does not automatically establish that a security incident or legal violation has occurred.

### 46. KRI Risk Context

KRI values should be interpreted within relevant context.

Context may include:

- AI system criticality;
- business dependency;
- threat environment;
- control environment;
- incident activity;
- supplier conditions;
- recent architectural changes.

### 47. KRI Data Quality

KRI reliability depends on the quality of its underlying data.

Data quality should consider:

- accuracy;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- timeliness;
- integrity;
- provenance.

### 48. KRI Evidence

Material KRI results should be supported by evidence sufficient to establish how the result was produced.

Evidence may include:

- source records;
- calculation logs;
- data lineage;
- system records;
- validation results;
- approvals.

### 49. KRI Assurance

KRI assurance evaluates whether the measurement is sufficiently reliable for its intended governance purpose.

Assurance may examine:

- methodology;
- source data;
- calculations;
- controls;
- reporting;
- interpretation.

KRI assurance is distinct from the KRI itself.

### 50. Core Principle

**AI security KRIs should provide reliable, contextual, and decision-useful visibility into changing risk conditions. A KRI should be explicitly connected to a defined risk, measured using controlled and reproducible methodology, interpreted according to its directionality and context, and governed through appropriate thresholds and escalation mechanisms. A KRI is an indicator of risk, not the risk itself, and remaining within a KRI threshold does not by itself establish that AI security risk has been eliminated or that all applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, or governance requirements have been satisfied.**

Internal `AI-SM-KRI-*` identifiers remain knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

## Part 2 — AI Security KRI Controls, Risk Measurement, Data Governance, Thresholds and Assessment

### 51. AI Security KRI Policy

The organization should establish a KRI policy defining:

- KRI governance;
- risk ownership;
- measurement methodology;
- data requirements;
- threshold management;
- reporting;
- escalation;
- validation;
- assurance.

The policy should distinguish KRI governance from the underlying risk-management process.

### 52. AI Security KRI Inventory

A KRI inventory should identify material AI security KRIs and document:

- KRI identifier;
- associated risk;
- risk owner;
- formula;
- data source;
- population;
- threshold;
- reporting frequency;
- escalation criteria.

### 53. AI Security KRI Register

The KRI register should provide a controlled repository of approved KRI definitions and methodologies.

It should prevent different teams from using materially different definitions for the same risk indicator.

### 54. AI Security KRI Risk Mapping

Each material KRI should map to one or more documented risks.

```text id="krimap01"
AI SECURITY RISK
       |
       v
   RISK OWNER
       |
       v
      KRI
       |
       v
MEASUREMENT RESULT
       |
       v
GOVERNANCE ACTION
```

The mapping should identify the relationship between the risk and its indicator rather than implying that the KRI completely represents the risk.

### 55. AI Security KRI Control Mapping

Where a KRI is affected by one or more controls, the relationship between:

- risk;
- control;
- KRI

should be documented.

A KRI may indicate deterioration associated with a control without proving that the control itself caused the deterioration.

### 56. AI Security KRI Governance Ownership

Governance ownership should establish responsibility for:

- KRI definition;
- methodology;
- interpretation;
- threshold management;
- escalation;
- reporting.

### 57. AI Security KRI Risk Ownership

The risk owner should be responsible for understanding and managing the underlying risk.

KRI ownership and risk ownership may be assigned to different individuals where appropriate.

### 58. AI Security KRI Data Ownership

Data ownership should establish responsibility for the quality and availability of source information used to calculate the KRI.

### 59. AI Security KRI Approval

Material KRIs should be appropriately approved before being used for formal governance reporting.

Approval should confirm that the KRI is relevant to the intended risk.

### 60. AI Security KRI Methodology Control

The methodology should document:

- formula;
- population;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- data sources;
- measurement period;
- calculation rules;
- interpretation rules.

### 61. AI Security KRI Formula Control

KRI formulas should be controlled through appropriate change-management procedures.

Unauthorized changes can materially alter reported risk.

### 62. AI Security KRI Population Control

The population underlying a KRI should be clearly defined and periodically validated.

Changes in the population should be identifiable where they can materially affect the result.

### 63. AI Security KRI Inclusion Control

Inclusion criteria should be consistently applied across reporting periods.

### 64. AI Security KRI Exclusion Control

Exclusions should be documented and justified where they could materially affect the measured risk.

### 65. AI Security KRI Denominator Control

The denominator should be validated because changes in the measured population can materially change the KRI.

For example:

```text id="kriden01"
100 critical AI systems
10 without required control
        |
        v
KRI = 10%

Population expands to
200 critical AI systems
20 without required control
        |
        v
KRI remains 10%
```

The percentage remained unchanged, but the absolute exposure doubled.

### 66. AI Security KRI Source-System Control

Approved source systems should be identified for each material KRI.

Source selection should consider:

- reliability;
- completeness;
- integrity;
- availability;
- timeliness.

### 67. AI Security KRI Data Extraction

Extraction procedures should preserve the relationship between source data and the KRI calculation.

### 68. AI Security KRI Data Transformation

Transformations should be documented and controlled.

Material transformations should be reproducible.

### 69. AI Security KRI Data Reconciliation

KRI inputs should be reconciled against authoritative sources where appropriate.

### 70. AI Security KRI Data Completeness

Completeness controls should identify missing records or populations that could materially understate risk.

### 71. AI Security KRI Data Accuracy

Accuracy controls should identify incorrect, inconsistent, or stale source values.

### 72. AI Security KRI Data Timeliness

KRI data should be sufficiently current for the intended risk-management decision.

### 73. AI Security KRI Data Consistency

Definitions and classifications used to calculate a KRI should remain consistent unless an approved methodology change has occurred.

### 74. AI Security KRI Data Provenance

The origin of material KRI data should be traceable where provenance is necessary to establish measurement reliability.

### 75. AI Security KRI Data Lineage

Data lineage should connect the KRI result to:

- source;
- transformation;
- calculation;
- output.

### 76. AI Security KRI Calculation Validation

Calculation logic should be validated against the approved KRI methodology.

### 77. AI Security KRI Automated Calculation

Automated calculation can improve:

- consistency;
- timeliness;
- repeatability;
- scalability.

Automation should remain subject to governance and validation.

### 78. AI Security KRI Manual Calculation

Manual calculation may be appropriate for limited or specialized KRIs.

Material manual calculations should have controls against:

- transcription error;
- omission;
- unauthorized modification;
- inconsistent methodology.

### 79. AI Security KRI Reproducibility

A material KRI should be reproducible using the defined methodology and appropriate retained evidence.

### 80. AI Security KRI Calculation Reconciliation

Material KRI calculations should be reconciled against independent or authoritative information where appropriate.

### 81. AI Security KRI Baseline Governance

KRI baselines should have documented:

- definition;
- calculation method;
- effective period;
- source;
- owner.

### 82. AI Security KRI Baseline Review

Baselines should be reviewed when material changes occur in:

- AI architecture;
- business operations;
- threat conditions;
- control environment;
- measured population.

### 83. AI Security KRI Threshold Governance

KRI thresholds should be defined according to the organization's risk-management framework.

Thresholds may include:

- early-warning;
- escalation;
- critical.

### 84. AI Security KRI Threshold Approval

Material thresholds should be approved by the appropriate governance authority.

### 85. AI Security KRI Early-Warning Threshold

An early-warning threshold should identify conditions that may require increased monitoring or preventive action.

It should provide sufficient time for management intervention where the risk permits.

### 86. AI Security KRI Escalation Threshold

An escalation threshold should define when the KRI requires management attention according to established governance procedures.

### 87. AI Security KRI Critical Threshold

A critical threshold should identify a materially elevated risk condition according to approved organizational criteria.

An internally defined critical threshold is not automatically a regulatory threshold.

### 88. AI Security KRI Threshold Calibration

Thresholds should be calibrated using relevant evidence such as:

- historical risk;
- incidents;
- threat activity;
- control performance;
- risk appetite;
- business criticality.

### 89. AI Security KRI Threshold Testing

Thresholds should be tested to determine whether they generate:

- excessive false alarms;
- delayed escalation;
- insufficient sensitivity;
- inappropriate management attention.

### 90. AI Security KRI Threshold Tuning

Thresholds may be tuned when evidence demonstrates that their current configuration does not provide appropriate decision support.

Changes should be documented.

### 91. AI Security KRI Risk Appetite Mapping

Where applicable, KRI thresholds should be mapped to approved risk appetite or tolerance statements.

The mapping should distinguish internal governance criteria from externally imposed requirements.

### 92. AI Security KRI Tolerance Mapping

Where risk tolerance is formally defined, KRI values should be mapped to relevant tolerance boundaries.

### 93. AI Security KRI Risk Capacity

Risk capacity represents the organization's ability to absorb adverse consequences.

A KRI should not be interpreted solely against risk appetite without considering relevant capacity where material.

### 94. AI Security KRI Residual Risk

KRI reporting should support understanding of residual risk after applicable controls and treatments have been considered.

### 95. AI Security KRI Inherent Risk

Where appropriate, organizations may distinguish indicators associated with inherent risk from those associated with residual risk.

### 96. AI Security KRI Risk Treatment Monitoring

KRIs should be used, where appropriate, to monitor whether risk-treatment activities are changing the underlying risk condition.

### 97. AI Security KRI Control Effectiveness Relationship

A KRI may provide evidence that a control environment is associated with changing risk exposure.

It should not automatically be treated as a direct measure of control effectiveness.

### 98. AI Security KRI Risk Reduction Measurement

Where measurable, KRI analysis may determine whether risk exposure is declining following treatment.

Risk reduction should be supported by evidence rather than inferred solely from increased control activity.

### 99. AI Security KRI Leading Indicator Design

Leading KRIs should identify conditions that may precede material risk deterioration.

Examples include:

- critical AI systems without current security assessments;
- critical dependencies without tested alternatives;
- unresolved high-risk vulnerabilities;
- increasing privileged-access exceptions.

### 100. AI Security KRI Lagging Indicator Design

Lagging KRIs measure risk outcomes that have already occurred.

Examples include:

- material security incidents;
- recurring compromises;
- confirmed unauthorized model changes;
- prolonged critical-service disruption.

### 101. AI Security KRI Leading-Lagging Relationship

Leading and lagging KRIs should be considered together where this provides better understanding of risk development.

### 102. AI Security KRI Outcome Relationship

Outcome-oriented KRIs should be distinguished from activity-oriented measurements.

For example, the number of security assessments completed is an activity measurement; the number of critical AI systems remaining without required assurance may be a risk indicator.

### 103. AI Security KRI Risk Coverage

The KRI framework should provide coverage of material AI security risk categories.

Coverage should consider:

- cybersecurity;
- model security;
- data security;
- identity;
- supply chain;
- resilience;
- third-party risk.

### 104. AI Security KRI Coverage Gap

A coverage gap exists where a material risk lacks an appropriate indicator or reliable measurement mechanism.

Coverage gaps should be documented and assessed.

### 105. AI Security KRI Measurement Blind Spot

A measurement blind spot exists where management cannot reliably observe a material risk condition.

Possible causes include:

- incomplete inventories;
- insufficient telemetry;
- unavailable supplier data;
- poor data quality;
- undocumented AI usage.

### 106. AI Security KRI Risk Visibility

Risk visibility reflects how reliably management can observe relevant changes in risk exposure.

High visibility does not necessarily mean low risk.

### 107. AI Security KRI Risk Signal

A KRI signal is an observed change that may indicate a change in risk condition.

A signal requires interpretation before management treats it as evidence of material deterioration.

### 108. AI Security KRI Signal Quality

Signal quality should consider:

- sensitivity;
- specificity;
- timeliness;
- reliability;
- contextual relevance.

### 109. AI Security KRI False Positive

A false positive occurs when a KRI indicates a risk condition that is not actually present according to the defined measurement criteria.

High false-positive rates can reduce management confidence and create alert fatigue.

### 110. AI Security KRI False Negative

A false negative occurs when the KRI fails to identify a material risk condition that exists.

False negatives may be more difficult to identify because the missing signal may remain invisible.

### 111. AI Security KRI Sensitivity

Sensitivity represents the ability of a KRI to identify relevant changes in the risk condition.

Higher sensitivity may increase false positives.

### 112. AI Security KRI Specificity

Specificity represents the ability of a KRI to distinguish relevant risk conditions from non-material variation.

### 113. AI Security KRI Timeliness

A KRI should provide information early enough to support the decision it is intended to inform.

### 114. AI Security KRI Detection Latency

Detection latency is the time between a material change in risk condition and the point at which the KRI identifies that change.

### 115. AI Security KRI Escalation Latency

Escalation latency is the time between a defined escalation condition and management notification or escalation.

### 116. AI Security KRI Action Latency

Action latency is the time between recognition of a material KRI condition and initiation of the relevant management response.

These measures should not be conflated.

### 117. AI Security KRI Risk Trend Analysis

KRI trends should be analyzed across sufficient historical periods to distinguish:

- isolated variation;
- persistent deterioration;
- improvement;
- cyclical behavior;
- structural change.

### 118. AI Security KRI Trend Break

A material break in a historical KRI trend should be investigated where it could indicate:

- changing risk;
- control degradation;
- threat change;
- population change;
- methodology change.

### 119. AI Security KRI Risk Acceleration Analysis

Increasing rates of deterioration may justify earlier management attention even when the absolute KRI remains below a critical threshold.

### 120. AI Security KRI Persistence Analysis

Persistent elevated risk should be distinguished from short-duration excursions.

### 121. AI Security KRI Recurrence Analysis

Repeated return of a risk condition should be assessed for:

- ineffective treatment;
- recurring control failure;
- systemic causes;
- changing threat conditions.

### 122. AI Security KRI Concentration Analysis

KRI analysis should identify whether risk is concentrated within a small number of:

- critical models;
- suppliers;
- platforms;
- business services;
- geographic locations.

### 123. AI Security KRI Correlation Analysis

Related KRIs may be analyzed together to identify potentially systemic conditions.

Correlation should not automatically be interpreted as causation.

### 124. AI Security KRI Risk Escalation

Material KRI conditions should be escalated according to documented governance criteria.

Escalation should consider:

- severity;
- persistence;
- velocity;
- concentration;
- criticality;
- business impact;
- uncertainty.

### 125. Core Control Principle

**AI security KRI governance should ensure that material risks are measured through controlled, traceable, appropriately sensitive indicators with defined ownership, thresholds, data requirements, and escalation mechanisms. KRI design should distinguish risk exposure from control activity, leading conditions from realized outcomes, and genuine risk deterioration from measurement or population changes. Thresholds and escalation rules should be treated as governance mechanisms unless a specific external requirement establishes a corresponding legal or regulatory obligation.**

## Part 3 — Continuous AI Security KRI Monitoring, Risk Analysis, Assurance and Resilience

### 126. AI Security KRI Continuous Monitoring

Material AI security KRIs should be monitored at a frequency appropriate to the volatility, criticality, and potential impact of the associated risk.

Continuous monitoring does not require every KRI to be measured in real time.

### 127. AI Security KRI Monitoring Frequency

Monitoring frequency should reflect how rapidly the underlying risk can change.

High-velocity risks may require frequent monitoring, while strategic risks may be appropriately measured periodically.

### 128. AI Security KRI Measurement Cadence

The measurement cadence should define when KRI data is:

- collected;
- processed;
- calculated;
- validated;
- reviewed;
- reported.

### 129. AI Security KRI Reporting Cadence

Reporting cadence should correspond to the governance decision supported by the KRI.

A KRI may be calculated continuously while being reported to a governance committee on a periodic basis.

### 130. AI Security KRI Real-Time Monitoring

Real-time KRI measurement may be appropriate where rapid risk changes can materially affect security decisions.

Real-time measurement does not automatically make a KRI more reliable or more useful.

### 131. AI Security KRI Near-Real-Time Monitoring

Near-real-time monitoring may provide sufficient timeliness while reducing unnecessary processing and infrastructure requirements.

### 132. AI Security KRI Periodic Monitoring

Periodic monitoring may be appropriate for relatively stable risks, strategic risk indicators, and measurements dependent on periodic assessment processes.

### 133. AI Security KRI Baseline Monitoring

KRI results should be compared with an approved baseline where meaningful.

The baseline should remain traceable to its underlying methodology and effective period.

### 134. AI Security KRI Baseline Drift

A baseline may become less representative because of:

- changes in the AI portfolio;
- architectural changes;
- business growth;
- threat changes;
- control improvements;
- changes in the measured population.

Baseline changes should therefore be governed.

### 135. AI Security KRI Threshold Breach Analysis

A threshold breach should be analyzed to determine whether it reflects:

- genuine risk deterioration;
- data-quality problems;
- population changes;
- methodology changes;
- temporary variation.

### 136. AI Security KRI Threshold Persistence

Persistent threshold breaches may provide stronger evidence of sustained risk than isolated excursions.

Persistence should be evaluated using defined criteria.

### 137. AI Security KRI Threshold Recurrence

Repeated threshold breaches should be examined for:

- ineffective remediation;
- recurring control weakness;
- systemic conditions;
- inappropriate threshold calibration;
- changing threat conditions.

### 138. AI Security KRI Risk Variance Analysis

Material variance should be analyzed against:

- baseline;
- target condition;
- risk appetite;
- tolerance;
- historical behavior.

### 139. AI Security KRI Trend Analysis

Trend analysis should evaluate the direction and persistence of risk over time.

A single KRI observation should not automatically be interpreted as a trend.

### 140. AI Security KRI Trend Significance

Trend significance should consider:

- magnitude;
- duration;
- volatility;
- persistence;
- population;
- context.

### 141. AI Security KRI Trend Break

A material change in an established trend should be investigated where it may indicate:

- changing threat conditions;
- control deterioration;
- architectural change;
- supplier change;
- measurement change.

### 142. AI Security KRI Risk Velocity Analysis

Risk velocity should be monitored where rapid deterioration could materially reduce the time available for management intervention.

### 143. AI Security KRI Risk Acceleration Analysis

Acceleration in risk growth may provide an earlier warning than absolute KRI levels.

Acceleration should be interpreted together with the underlying risk context.
