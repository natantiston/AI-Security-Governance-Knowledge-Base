# 18.11 — AI Incident Metrics

## Part 1 — AI Incident Metric Foundations, Measurement Principles, Governance and Interpretation

### 1. Definition of AI Incident Metrics

AI incident metrics are measurements used to quantify, characterize, analyze, and monitor incidents involving AI systems.

AI incident metrics may measure:

* Number of AI incidents
* Incident frequency
* Incident severity
* Incident impact
* Incident detection time
* Incident response time
* Incident containment time
* Incident recovery time
* Incident recurrence
* Incident concentration
* Incident root-cause categories
* Incident control failures
* Incident remediation
* Regulatory relevance
* Supplier involvement
* Model involvement
* Data involvement

AI incident metrics provide structured evidence about observed incident conditions.

**AI incident metric ≠ AI incident.**

An incident is an event or condition classified according to the organization's incident-management framework.

A metric is a measurement derived from incident information.

---

The purpose of AI incident metrics is to provide measurable information about AI incident activity, response performance, resilience, and recurring weaknesses.

### 3. AI Incident Metric vs KPI

An AI incident metric becomes a KPI only when the organization formally designates it as a key indicator of performance against an important objective.

For example:

* Incident metric — average time to contain AI incidents
* KPI — percentage of material AI incidents contained within the approved response objective

The first is a measurement.

The second is formally designated as a key performance indicator.

**AI incident metric ≠ KPI automatically.**

---

### 4. AI Incident Metric vs KRI

An incident metric measures observed incident conditions or incident-management performance.

A KRI indicates a change in risk exposure.

For example:

* AI incident count measures observed incidents.
* AI incident recurrence may indicate elevated risk.
* AI incident concentration may indicate dependency risk.
* Increasing material incidents may contribute to risk reassessment.

However, incident frequency itself is not automatically a KRI.

**Incident metric ≠ KRI automatically.**

---

### 5. AI Incident Metric vs Control

An incident metric measures an observed condition.

A control is designed to prevent, detect, contain, respond to, or reduce risk.

Examples:

* Incident metric — number of prompt-injection incidents
* Control — prompt-injection detection
* Incident metric — mean time to containment
* Control — incident-response procedure
* Incident metric — recurrence rate
* Control — corrective-action process

**Incident metric ≠ Control.**

---

### 6. AI Incident Metric vs Control Objective

A control objective defines what a control is intended to accomplish.

For example:

> AI security incidents should be detected and contained within defined organizational response requirements.

Potential metrics may include:

* Mean time to detect
* Mean time to contain
* Percentage contained within target
* Percentage of incidents with documented response

The control objective defines the desired condition.

The metric measures evidence related to that condition.

**Control objective ≠ Incident metric.**

---

### 7. AI Incident Metric vs Target

An incident metric represents an observed measurement.

A target represents an intended performance level.

For example:

> Observed mean time to contain: 4.8 hours.

versus:

> Internal response objective: material AI incidents should be contained within the approved target period.

The target is a governance decision applied to the measurement.

**Incident metric ≠ Target.**

---

### 8. AI Incident Metric vs Threshold

Examples include:

### 15. AI Incident Metric Formula

Incident metrics should use documented formulas appropriate to their intended purpose.

For example:

\[
Incident\ Frequency =
\frac{Number\ of\ AI\ Incidents}
{Defined\ Measurement\ Population}
\]

For incident rate:

\[
AI\ Incident\ Rate =
\frac{AI\ Incidents}
{Defined\ Exposure\ Population}
\times K
\]

where \(K\) is a defined scaling factor.

For recurrence:

\[
Incident\ Recurrence\ Rate =
\frac{Recurring\ AI\ Incidents}
{Applicable\ AI\ Incidents}
\times 100
\]

The formula should remain aligned with the underlying incident definition.

### 50. Core Principle

AI incident metrics provide structured measurements of **AI incident frequency, severity, impact, detection, response, recovery, recurrence, concentration, and remediation within a defined incident-management population**.

They do not independently establish the organization's complete AI security posture, resilience, compliance status, or risk exposure.

The fundamental distinctions are:

**AI incident metric ≠ AI incident.**

**Incident metric ≠ KPI automatically.**

**Incident metric ≠ KRI automatically.**

**Incident metric ≠ Control.**

**Incident metric ≠ Control objective.**

**Incident metric ≠ Target.**

**Incident metric ≠ Threshold.**

**Incident count ≠ Risk exposure automatically.**

**More incidents detected ≠ Worse security automatically.**

**Fewer reported incidents ≠ Better security automatically.**

**Incident severity ≠ Incident impact automatically.**

**Incident impact ≠ Root cause.**

**Incident category ≠ Root cause.**

**Incident detection ≠ Incident response.**

**Incident response ≠ Incident recovery.**

**Incident recovery ≠ Risk elimination.**

**Incident recurrence ≠ Root cause automatically.**

**Incident metric ≠ Complete incident-management effectiveness.**

**Incident data quality ≠ Incident-management effectiveness.**

**Evidence ≠ Proof of causation automatically.**

**Correlation ≠ Causation.**

**Historical baseline ≠ Expected risk level automatically.**

**Internal threshold ≠ Legal threshold.**

**Internal escalation trigger ≠ Regulatory notification obligation.**

**Incident classification ≠ Legal classification automatically.**

**Metric assurance ≠ Complete incident assurance.**

**Automation ≠ Accountability.**

**Maturity ≠ Compliance.**

Effective AI incident measurement therefore requires **controlled incident definitions, consistent classification, reliable data, appropriate denominators, severity and impact context, model and version traceability, evidence integrity, validated calculations, meaningful trend analysis, appropriate assurance, and explicit recognition of measurement limitations**.

### 210. Continuous AI Incident Metric Assurance Principle

Continuous AI incident metric assurance should determine whether incident measurements remain **complete enough for their intended purpose, accurately classified, appropriately calculated, traceable to evidence, sufficiently current, contextually interpretable, and supported by proportionate assurance**.

The fundamental distinctions remain:

**Continuous monitoring ≠ Continuous assurance.**

**Incident metric ≠ Incident.**

**Incident count ≠ Risk exposure.**

**More incidents detected ≠ Worse security automatically.**

**Fewer incidents reported ≠ Better security automatically.**

**Incident category ≠ Root cause.**

**Trigger ≠ Root cause.**

**Contributing factor ≠ Root cause.**

**Severity ≠ Impact.**

**Impact ≠ Causation.**

**Detection ≠ Response.**

**Response ≠ Recovery.**

**Recovery ≠ Risk elimination.**

**Recurrence ≠ Root cause automatically.**

**Correlation ≠ Causation.**

**Trend ≠ Root cause.**

**Outlier ≠ Systemic failure automatically.**

**Threat intelligence ≠ Proof of causation.**

**Supplier incident information ≠ Independent assurance.**

**Measurement confidence ≠ Complete incident visibility.**

**Trusted measurement state ≠ Complete incident assurance.**

**Internal escalation trigger ≠ Regulatory notification obligation.**

**Internal incident threshold ≠ Statutory threshold.**

**Automation ≠ Accountability.**

**Independent validation ≠ Complete incident-management assurance.**

Effective continuous incident measurement therefore requires **reliable incident records, consistent taxonomy, controlled severity and impact classification, complete-enough measurement populations, model and version traceability, data-quality controls, validated calculations, historical comparability, meaningful trend and concentration analysis, explicit uncertainty, proportionate assurance, and clear separation between observed incident facts, analytical interpretation, risk assessment, and governance decisions**.

### 400. Core Enterprise AI Incident Metric Assurance Principle

AI incident metrics should operate as an **enterprise governance capability for measuring, analyzing, interpreting, monitoring, and improving AI incident conditions across defined AI systems and business services while preserving incident classification integrity, severity and impact context, model and version traceability, evidence integrity, calculation reliability, accountability, human oversight, resilience, and explicit assurance boundaries**.

The fundamental enterprise principles are:

**AI incident metric ≠ AI incident.**

**Incident count ≠ Risk exposure.**

**More incidents detected ≠ Worse security automatically.**

**Fewer incidents reported ≠ Better security automatically.**

**Incident category ≠ Root cause.**

**Trigger ≠ Root cause.**

**Contributing factor ≠ Root cause.**

**Severity ≠ Impact.**

**Impact ≠ Causation.**

**Detection ≠ Response.**

**Response ≠ Recovery.**

**Recovery ≠ Risk elimination.**

**Recurrence ≠ Root cause automatically.**

**Correlation ≠ Causation.**

**Trend ≠ Root cause.**

**Outlier ≠ Systemic failure automatically.**

**Threat intelligence ≠ Proof of causation.**

**Supplier-reported incident information ≠ Independent assurance.**

**Fourth-party visibility ≠ Fourth-party assurance.**

**Coverage ≠ Effectiveness.**

**Activity ≠ Outcome.**

**Remediation completion ≠ Risk elimination automatically.**

**Measurement failure ≠ Incident reduction.**

**Composite incident score ≠ Complete incident-risk assessment.**

**Measurement confidence ≠ Complete incident visibility.**

**Trusted measurement state ≠ Complete incident assurance.**

**Maturity ≠ Compliance.**

**Internal incident threshold ≠ Legal or regulatory threshold.**

**Internal escalation trigger ≠ Regulatory notification obligation.**

**Automation ≠ Accountability.**

**AI-generated root-cause hypothesis ≠ Confirmed root cause.**

**Independent metric validation ≠ Complete incident-management assurance.**

An enterprise AI incident measurement capability is trustworthy only when it maintains **controlled incident definitions, reliable and sufficiently complete incident populations, consistent classification, appropriate severity and impact assessment, model and version identity, validated calculations, historical comparability, evidence traceability, meaningful trend and concentration analysis, explicit uncertainty, proportionate independent assurance, controlled remediation, human accountability, and clear recognition of measurement limitations**.
