# 18.14 — AI Risk Trend Analysis

## Part 1 — AI Risk Trend Analysis Foundations, Measurement Principles, Governance and Interpretation

### 1. Definition of AI Risk Trend Analysis

AI risk trend analysis is the systematic examination of how AI-related risk indicators, exposures, control conditions, incidents, vulnerabilities, dependencies, and other relevant risk signals change over time.

The purpose is to distinguish:

* Stable risk conditions
* Improving conditions
* Deteriorating conditions
* Accelerating risk
* Persistent risk
* Recurring risk
* Emerging risk
* Structural change
* Temporary fluctuation
* Measurement change

Trend analysis should evaluate changes within their appropriate context rather than treating every movement in a metric as a change in underlying risk.

**Metric movement ≠ Risk movement automatically.**

---

### 2. Purpose of AI Risk Trend Analysis

The purpose of AI risk trend analysis is to provide decision-makers with a temporal view of AI risk.

Trend analysis can support:

* AI risk management
* Security governance
* Model governance
* Privacy governance
* Safety governance
* Control monitoring
* Incident management
* Supplier risk management
* Risk appetite monitoring
* Executive reporting
* Board reporting
* Remediation prioritization
* Scenario planning
* Assurance

A single measurement provides a point-in-time observation.

A trend provides information about change across time.

---

### 3. AI Risk Trend vs AI Risk Metric

An AI risk metric measures a defined characteristic of AI risk.

AI risk trend analysis examines how that metric changes over time.

For example:

```text id="airisktrend18d14-01"
AI Risk Metric
      |
      v
Point-in-Time Measurement
      |
      v
Repeated Measurements
      |
      v
Trend Analysis
      |
      v
Risk Interpretation
```

The metric provides the observations.

The trend analysis evaluates their temporal behavior.

**Risk metric ≠ Risk trend.**

---

### 4. AI Risk Trend vs KPI

A KPI measures progress toward an organizational objective.

An AI risk trend evaluates changes in risk-related conditions.

For example:

> KPI: Percentage of AI systems completing required governance assessments.

versus:

> Risk trend: Change in the number and severity of unresolved high-risk AI exposures over time.

A KPI may contribute evidence to risk analysis, but it does not automatically represent risk.

**KPI trend ≠ Risk trend automatically.**

---

### 5. AI Risk Trend vs KRI

A KRI is an indicator designed to provide information about risk exposure or changing risk conditions.

Risk trend analysis can use KRIs as primary inputs.

```text id="airisktrend18d14-02"
Risk
 |
 +--> KRI 1 ----\
 |
 +--> KRI 2 -----+--> Trend Analysis
 |
 +--> KRI 3 ----/
```

The KRI provides the measurement signal.

Trend analysis evaluates its behavior over time.

**KRI ≠ Risk itself.**

---

### 6. AI Risk Trend vs Control Effectiveness

Control effectiveness evaluates whether a control operates as intended.

Risk trend analysis evaluates how risk-related conditions change.

A declining control-effectiveness metric may contribute to increasing risk, but the relationship should be evaluated rather than assumed.

**Control deterioration ≠ Risk increase automatically.**

---

### 7. AI Risk Trend vs Risk Appetite

Risk appetite expresses the level and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or retain under its governance framework.

A trend shows how measured risk conditions are changing.

```text id="airisktrend18d14-03"
Risk Appetite
      |
      v
Risk Tolerance
      |
      v
Measured Risk
      |
      v
Risk Trend
```

A risk trend should be interpreted against approved appetite and tolerance where applicable.

**Risk trend ≠ Risk appetite.**

---

### 8. AI Risk Trend vs Risk Threshold

A threshold establishes an internal boundary for governance attention or action.

A trend describes movement over time.

A risk can deteriorate substantially without crossing a threshold.

Conversely, a threshold can be crossed because of a temporary event.

**Threshold breach ≠ Sustained risk trend automatically.**

### 12. AI Risk Trend Owner

The risk owner is accountable for managing the relevant risk.

Responsibilities may include:

* Reviewing risk trends
* Assessing material changes
* Approving treatment
* Monitoring residual risk
* Escalating material conditions
* Accepting risk where authorized

The risk owner is not necessarily the owner of the underlying data.

### 13. AI Risk Trend Data Owner

The data owner is responsible for the quality and appropriate management of data used in trend analysis.

Responsibilities may include:

* Data quality
* Data availability
* Data lineage

* Retention
* Reconciliation

Risk ownership and data ownership may belong to different functions.

### 14. AI Risk Trend Definition

A risk trend should have an explicit definition.

The definition should specify:

* Risk being analyzed
* Measurement indicator
* Population
* Time period
* Measurement frequency
* Directionality
* Baseline
* Interpretation method
* Relevant context

For example:

> Change in the number of material unresolved AI security risks across the applicable production AI population over successive monthly measurement periods.

---

### 15. AI Risk Trend Formula

A simple trend calculation may compare measurements across periods.

For example:

```text id="airisktrend18d14-04"
Risk Change (%) =
(Current Risk Value - Previous Risk Value)
----------------------------------------- × 100
Previous Risk Value
```

For time-series analysis, additional methods may include:

* Moving averages
* Rate of change
* Trend slopes
* Rolling statistics
* Control charts
* Regression analysis
* Time-series models

The selected methodology should be documented.

---

### 16. AI Risk Trend Numerator

Where a risk trend is based on a ratio or rate, the numerator represents the relevant risk condition.

Examples include:

* Number of material unresolved risks
* Number of high-severity vulnerabilities
* Number of critical control failures
* Number of AI incidents
* Number of affected AI systems

The numerator should be consistently defined.

---

### 17. AI Risk Trend Denominator

The denominator represents the population against which the risk condition is measured.

Examples include:

* Total AI systems
* Total production models
* Total applicable control instances
* Total suppliers
* Total assessed AI systems

Denominator changes can materially affect apparent trends.

**Denominator change ≠ Risk change automatically.**

### 18. AI Risk Trend Measurement Population

The population should represent the AI environment relevant to the risk being analyzed.

Population sources may include:

* AI inventory

* Application inventory
* Business-service inventory
* Supplier inventory

Population completeness is fundamental to meaningful trend analysis.

### 19. AI Risk Trend Inclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria define which risk observations enter the analysis.

* Critical suppliers

### 20. AI Risk Trend Exclusion Criteria

Exclusions should be documented.

### 24. AI Risk Trend Baseline

A baseline establishes the reference state against which risk changes are evaluated.

Possible baselines include:

* Initial approved state
* Historical stable period
* Previous reporting period
* Approved risk baseline
* Post-remediation state
* Model-version baseline

Baseline selection should be documented.

**Baseline ≠ Target.**

### 25. AI Risk Trend Target

A target represents a desired risk condition under the organization's governance framework.

Examples include:

* Maximum unresolved critical-risk population
* Desired risk-reduction level
* Maximum acceptable exposure rate

Targets should be risk-based and approved.

A target is not itself evidence that risk has been eliminated.

### 27. AI Risk Trend Tolerance

Tolerance represents an approved degree of variation around an expected risk condition.

Tolerance may consider:

* Risk appetite
* Risk capacity
* Business impact
* Measurement uncertainty
* Temporary conditions

Internal tolerance should not be interpreted as permission to disregard mandatory requirements.

---

### 28. AI Risk Trend Variance

Variance represents the difference between the observed risk condition and a reference point.

For example:

```text id="airisktrend18d14-06"
Baseline Risk = 100
Current Risk  = 125

Variance = +25
```

Variance should be interpreted in context.

A numerical increase may reflect:

* Actual risk increase
* Better detection
* Population growth
* Classification change
* Methodology change

### 29. AI Risk Trend Direction

Direction describes whether a measured risk condition is increasing, decreasing, or stable.

Possible states include:

* Increasing
* Decreasing
* Stable
* Mixed
* Indeterminate

Direction should account for the semantics of the metric.

For some metrics, higher values indicate higher risk.

For others, higher values may indicate stronger controls and lower risk.

### 30. AI Risk Trend Stability

Stability describes whether risk measurements remain relatively consistent over time.

A risk series may show:

* Stable exposure
* Gradual deterioration
* Gradual improvement
* High volatility
* Periodic fluctuation
* Structural change

**Stable risk measurement ≠ Low risk.**

### 43. AI Risk Trend Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency concerns whether the available evidence is adequate to support the trend conclusion.

For example:

> A rise in reported AI incidents may demonstrate increased incident reporting.

It does not automatically demonstrate:

> AI security risk increased by the same amount.

The conclusion should not exceed the evidence.

### 50. Core Principle

AI risk trend analysis should determine **how relevant AI risk conditions change over time while preserving the distinction between actual risk movement, measurement changes, population changes, methodological changes, detection changes, and contextual changes**.

The fundamental principles are:

**Risk metric ≠ Risk trend.**

**KPI ≠ Risk automatically.**

**KRI ≠ Risk itself.**

**Metric movement ≠ Risk movement automatically.**

**Trend ≠ Risk Cause.**

**Trend ≠ Root Cause.**

**Correlation ≠ Causation.**

**Acceleration ≠ Root Cause.**

**Outlier ≠ Systemic Risk automatically.**

**Threshold breach ≠ Sustained Risk Trend automatically.**

**Risk increase ≠ Control failure automatically.**

**Risk decrease ≠ Control effectiveness automatically.**

**More detected events ≠ More underlying risk automatically.**

**Fewer reported events ≠ Lower underlying risk automatically.**

**Population growth ≠ Risk deterioration automatically.**

**Methodology change ≠ Risk change automatically.**

**Better visibility ≠ Higher risk automatically.**

**Data quality ≠ Risk assurance.**

**Evidence existence ≠ Evidence sufficiency.**

**Evidence integrity ≠ Evidence correctness.**

**Measurement confidence ≠ Absence of Risk.**

**Trend monitoring ≠ Continuous assurance.**

**Internal threshold ≠ Legal or Regulatory Threshold.**

**Internal tolerance ≠ Permission to Disregard Mandatory Requirements.**

**Internal escalation trigger ≠ Regulatory Notification Obligation.**

**Automation ≠ Accountability.**

**Trend assurance ≠ Complete AI Risk Assurance.**

A reliable AI risk trend therefore requires **consistent definitions, appropriate populations, reliable data, controlled methodology, historical comparability, contextual interpretation, evidence traceability, explicit uncertainty, and governance processes capable of converting meaningful risk changes into appropriate decisions**.
