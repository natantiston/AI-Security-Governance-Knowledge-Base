# 18.02 — AI Security KPIs

## Part 1 — AI Security KPI Foundations, Measurement Principles, Governance, Design and Interpretation

### 1. AI Security KPI

An AI security Key Performance Indicator (KPI) is a defined measurement used to evaluate performance against an established AI security objective.

A KPI should provide information that supports governance, operational management, control improvement, or strategic decision-making.

### 2. KPI Objective

The objective of an AI security KPI is to translate an AI security objective into a measurable indicator that can be consistently evaluated over time.

A KPI should answer a defined management question rather than simply report an available data point.

### 3. KPI vs Metric

A metric is a quantitative or qualitative measurement.

A KPI is a metric selected because it has specific significance for evaluating performance against an objective.

Therefore:

**Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI.**

### 4. KPI vs KRI

A KPI primarily measures performance against an objective.

A Key Risk Indicator (KRI) primarily provides an indicator of changing or elevated risk.

The two may use related data but serve different governance purposes.

### 5. KPI vs Control

A KPI measures performance.

A control is a mechanism designed to manage risk or achieve an objective.

A KPI can measure whether a control appears to be operating effectively, but the KPI itself is not necessarily the control.

### 6. KPI vs Control Objective

A control objective describes the desired condition a control should achieve.

A KPI provides evidence about performance against that objective.

### 7. KPI vs Target

A target defines the desired value or performance level for a KPI.

For example:

```text id="f5m8rx"
OBJECTIVE
   |
   v
KPI
   |
   v
TARGET
   |
   v
ACTUAL RESULT
   |
   v
VARIANCE
```

### 8. KPI vs Threshold

A target represents a desired performance level.

A threshold represents a boundary at which attention, escalation, or another action may be required.

A target and threshold should not automatically be treated as equivalent.

### 9. KPI Scope

AI security KPIs may measure performance across:

- AI systems;
- models;
- datasets;
- applications;
- agents;
- infrastructure;
- security controls;
- governance processes;
- suppliers;
- incident management;
- resilience.

### 10. KPI Measurement Domain

KPI domains may include:

- security;
- governance;
- risk;
- compliance;
- resilience;
- monitoring;
- incident management;
- model assurance;
- supplier security.

### 11. KPI Governance

AI security KPIs should operate within defined governance covering:

- ownership;
- definition;
- data source;
- calculation;
- frequency;
- target;
- interpretation;
- reporting;
- review.

### 12. KPI Owner

Each material KPI should have an identified owner responsible for:

- definition;
- data quality;
- calculation;
- reporting;
- interpretation;
- remediation where appropriate.

### 13. KPI Data Owner

The KPI owner and data owner may be different.

The data owner is responsible for the relevant source data, while the KPI owner is responsible for the measurement and its governance.

### 14. KPI Definition

A KPI definition should clearly establish:

- what is measured;
- why it is measured;
- how it is calculated;
- which population is included;
- which population is excluded;
- measurement frequency;
- data source;
- interpretation.

### 15. KPI Formula

Where a KPI is quantitative, its formula should be explicitly defined.

For example:

```text id="k3r7mx"
KPI = Numerator / Denominator × 100
```

The numerator and denominator must be defined consistently.

### 16. KPI Numerator

The numerator represents the quantity being counted or measured within the defined KPI calculation.

### 17. KPI Denominator

The denominator defines the reference population or total against which the numerator is evaluated.

An incorrectly defined denominator can materially distort the KPI.

### 18. KPI Population

The KPI population defines the systems, events, controls, users, models, or processes included in the measurement.

### 19. KPI Inclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria define what is counted within the KPI population.

Criteria should be explicit and consistently applied.

### 20. KPI Exclusion Criteria

Exclusion criteria define what is intentionally excluded from measurement.

Exclusions should have documented rationale where they materially affect interpretation.

### 21. KPI Measurement Period

The measurement period defines the time interval represented by the KPI.

Examples include:

- hourly;
- daily;
- weekly;
- monthly;
- quarterly;
- annually.

### 22. KPI Measurement Frequency

Measurement frequency defines how often KPI values are calculated or refreshed.

Frequency should be appropriate to:

- risk;
- operational volatility;
- decision requirements;
- data availability.

### 23. KPI Reporting Frequency

Reporting frequency defines how often KPI results are communicated to stakeholders.

Measurement frequency and reporting frequency do not have to be identical.

### 24. KPI Baseline

A KPI baseline is a reference measurement against which future performance may be compared.

A baseline may represent:

- historical performance;
- approved target state;
- pre-change condition;
- industry reference where appropriately comparable.

### 25. KPI Target

A KPI target defines the desired performance level.

Targets should have a documented rationale.

### 26. KPI Threshold

A KPI threshold defines a boundary at which additional management attention or action may be appropriate.

A threshold should not automatically be represented as a legal or regulatory requirement.

### 27. KPI Tolerance

Tolerance defines the acceptable range around a target or expected KPI value.

### 28. KPI Variance

Variance represents the difference between actual performance and the relevant target, baseline, or reference value.

### 29. KPI Trend

A KPI trend represents the direction of movement in the measurement over time.

A single KPI value may be less informative than a sustained trend.

### 30. KPI Stability

KPI stability describes the degree to which KPI values remain relatively consistent over time.

Stable performance does not necessarily mean effective security.

### 31. KPI Volatility

KPI volatility represents the degree of variation in KPI results over time.

High volatility may require contextual analysis before conclusions are drawn.

### 32. KPI Directionality

Directionality defines whether higher or lower KPI values generally represent better performance.

For example:

- higher control coverage may indicate stronger performance;
- lower unresolved critical findings may indicate stronger performance.

Directionality should be explicitly documented.

### 33. KPI Interpretation

KPI interpretation converts the measured value into meaningful management information.

Interpretation should consider:

- context;
- population;
- timeframe;
- target;
- baseline;
- material changes.

### 34. KPI Context

A KPI should be interpreted in the context of:

- AI system criticality;
- business function;
- threat environment;
- regulatory environment;
- architecture;
- operational changes.

### 35. KPI Comparability

KPI values should only be compared when their:

- definitions;
- populations;
- formulas;
- measurement periods;
- data sources

are sufficiently comparable.

### 36. KPI Consistency

KPI calculations should be applied consistently across measurement periods unless a documented methodology change occurs.

### 37. KPI Methodology

KPI methodology defines how data is collected, transformed, calculated, validated, and interpreted.

### 38. KPI Methodology Change

Changes to KPI methodology should be documented because they may affect comparability with historical results.

### 39. KPI Restatement

Where a material calculation error or methodology issue is identified, historical KPI values may require restatement.

Restatement should be governed and documented.

### 40. KPI Data Quality

KPI data quality should consider:

- accuracy;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- timeliness;
- validity;
- uniqueness where relevant.

### 41. KPI Data Lineage

Data lineage should identify the path from source data to reported KPI.

```text id="r8m4qx"
SOURCE DATA
    |
    v
COLLECTION
    |
    v
TRANSFORMATION
    |
    v
CALCULATION
    |
    v
VALIDATION
    |
    v
KPI
    |
    v
REPORTING
```

### 42. KPI Data Provenance

Data provenance identifies the origin and relevant processing history of information used to calculate a KPI.

### 43. KPI Evidence

KPI evidence is the underlying information supporting the reported KPI value.

Evidence should be sufficiently reliable to support the intended management conclusion.

### 44. KPI Evidence Sufficiency

The existence of a KPI value does not automatically demonstrate that sufficient evidence exists to support the value.

### 45. KPI Evidence Integrity

KPI evidence should be protected against inappropriate:

- alteration;
- deletion;
- manipulation;
- substitution.

### 46. KPI Calculation Integrity

KPI calculations should produce results consistent with the approved methodology.

Calculation logic should be appropriately controlled.

### 47. KPI Validation

KPI validation should confirm, as appropriate, that:

- source data is relevant;
- formulas are correct;
- calculations operate as intended;
- reported values are accurate.

### 48. KPI Assurance

KPI assurance provides confidence that a KPI is appropriately defined, calculated, reported, and supported by evidence.

KPI assurance is distinct from simply producing the KPI.

### 49. KPI Decision Usefulness

A KPI should provide information that can support a meaningful decision.

A KPI that is technically accurate but does not influence any management decision may have limited governance value.

### 50. Core Principle

**An AI security KPI should be an intentionally designed measurement that connects an AI security objective to reliable evidence and meaningful management action. KPI definitions, formulas, populations, targets, thresholds, ownership, and interpretation should be governed so that reported performance remains consistent, comparable, and decision-useful.**

Internal `AI-SM-KPI-*` identifiers used throughout this topic represent knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers and do not represent statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

## Part 2 — AI Security KPI Controls, Measurement Design, Data Governance, Validation and Reporting

### 51. AI Security KPI Policy

The organization should establish a KPI policy defining:

- KPI governance;
- ownership;
- measurement standards;
- calculation methods;
- data requirements;
- reporting frequency;
- target setting;
- validation;
- assurance;
- change management.

### 52. AI Security KPI Inventory

A KPI inventory should identify material AI security KPIs and record:

- KPI identifier;
- objective;
- owner;
- formula;
- data source;
- population;
- target;
- threshold;
- reporting frequency;
- status.

### 53. AI Security KPI Register

The KPI register should provide a controlled source for KPI definitions and methodology.

It should prevent different teams from using inconsistent definitions for the same KPI.

### 54. AI Security KPI Objective Mapping

Each material KPI should map to a defined:

- security objective;
- governance objective;
- control objective;
- risk objective;
- resilience objective.

### 55. AI Security KPI Risk Mapping

KPIs should be mapped to the risks they help management understand.

A KPI does not necessarily measure the risk directly.

### 56. AI Security KPI Control Mapping

Where a KPI evaluates control performance, the relationship between the KPI and the relevant control should be documented.

### 57. AI Security KPI Governance Ownership

KPI ownership should define responsibility for:

- methodology;
- data quality;
- calculation;
- reporting;
- interpretation;
- corrective action.

### 58. AI Security KPI Approval

Material KPIs should be approved by the appropriate governance authority before being used for formal reporting.

### 59. AI Security KPI Methodology Control

KPI methodology should document:

- formula;
- data sources;
- population;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- measurement period;
- calculation logic.

### 60. AI Security KPI Formula Control

Formulas should be controlled to prevent unauthorized or undocumented changes.

### 61. AI Security KPI Population Control

The population used to calculate a KPI should be clearly defined and consistently applied.

### 62. AI Security KPI Inclusion Control

Inclusion criteria should be applied consistently across reporting periods.

### 63. AI Security KPI Exclusion Control

Exclusions should be documented where they could materially affect the reported result.

### 64. AI Security KPI Denominator Control

Denominators should be validated because changes in the denominator can materially change KPI interpretation even when the numerator remains constant.

### 65. AI Security KPI Data Source Control

Approved source systems should be identified for each material KPI.

### 66. AI Security KPI Source Reliability

Source systems should be assessed for:

- availability;
- accuracy;
- completeness;
- integrity;
- timeliness.

### 67. AI Security KPI Data Extraction

Data extraction procedures should be controlled to preserve consistency between source data and KPI calculations.

### 68. AI Security KPI Data Transformation

Transformations applied before calculation should be documented and appropriately controlled.

### 69. AI Security KPI Data Reconciliation

KPI data should be reconciled with authoritative source information where appropriate.

### 70. AI Security KPI Data Completeness

Completeness checks should identify missing data that could materially affect the KPI.

### 71. AI Security KPI Data Accuracy

Accuracy checks should identify incorrect or inconsistent source values.

### 72. AI Security KPI Data Timeliness

Data should be sufficiently current for the intended KPI decision or reporting purpose.

### 73. AI Security KPI Data Consistency

Data definitions should remain consistent across relevant reporting periods and systems.

### 74. AI Security KPI Data Validation

Material KPI datasets should be validated before calculation where appropriate.

### 75. AI Security KPI Calculation Validation

Calculation logic should be tested to confirm that the implemented formula matches the approved methodology.

### 76. AI Security KPI Automated Calculation

Automation may be used to calculate KPIs where it improves:

- consistency;
- timeliness;
- scalability;
- reproducibility.

Automated calculation remains subject to governance.

### 77. AI Security KPI Manual Calculation

Manual calculation may be used where appropriate, but material manual processes should have adequate controls against:

- calculation error;
- omission;
- unauthorized modification.

### 78. AI Security KPI Calculation Reconciliation

Reported KPI values should be reconciled against independently available source or calculation evidence where appropriate.

### 79. AI Security KPI Calculation Reproducibility

A material KPI should be reproducible from the defined methodology and retained source information.

### 80. AI Security KPI Historical Comparability

Historical KPI results should remain comparable when:

- definitions are unchanged;
- populations are consistent;
- formulas remain stable.

Material methodology changes should be documented.

### 81. AI Security KPI Methodology Versioning

KPI methodology should be version controlled so that historical values can be associated with the methodology applicable at the time.

### 82. AI Security KPI Change Management

Changes to material KPI definitions should be subject to appropriate:

- review;
- approval;
- testing;
- documentation;
- communication.

### 83. AI Security KPI Target Governance

Targets should have documented:

- rationale;
- owner;
- effective period;
- measurement basis.

### 84. AI Security KPI Target Setting

Targets may be based on:

- risk appetite;
- control objectives;
- historical performance;
- strategic objectives;
- operational requirements.

### 85. AI Security KPI Target Review

Targets should be periodically reviewed to determine whether they remain relevant.

### 86. AI Security KPI Threshold Governance

Thresholds should identify when management attention or predefined action may be required.

### 87. AI Security KPI Threshold Calibration

Thresholds should be calibrated using:

- historical performance;
- risk analysis;
- incidents;
- control effectiveness;
- business context.

### 88. AI Security KPI Tolerance Governance

Tolerance ranges should be defined where management requires an acceptable performance range.

### 89. AI Security KPI Variance Analysis

Variance analysis should identify differences between:

- actual;
- target;
- baseline;
- tolerance.

### 90. AI Security KPI Trend Analysis

Trend analysis should identify sustained movement rather than relying exclusively on individual observations.

### 91. AI Security KPI Leading Indicator

A leading KPI provides information about conditions that may influence future security performance.

Examples may include:

- percentage of critical AI systems with tested monitoring;
- percentage of overdue remediation actions;
- percentage of models with current security assessments.

### 92. AI Security KPI Lagging Indicator

A lagging KPI measures outcomes that have already occurred.

Examples may include:

- resolved security incidents;
- confirmed control failures;
- completed remediation;
- security-event response performance.

### 93. AI Security KPI Leading-Lagging Balance

A mature KPI framework should avoid relying exclusively on either leading or lagging measurements.

### 94. AI Security KPI Outcome Measurement

Outcome KPIs measure results achieved by security and governance activities.

### 95. AI Security KPI Activity Measurement

Activity KPIs measure actions performed.

High activity does not automatically establish effective security.

### 96. AI Security KPI Effectiveness Measurement

Effectiveness KPIs should evaluate whether intended security outcomes are being achieved.

### 97. AI Security KPI Efficiency Measurement

Efficiency KPIs may measure resources or time required to achieve defined security outcomes.

Efficiency should not be optimized at the expense of security effectiveness.

### 98. AI Security KPI Coverage Measurement

Coverage KPIs may measure the proportion of relevant AI assets or controls covered by defined security activities.

### 99. AI Security KPI Compliance Measurement

Compliance KPIs may measure adherence to defined:

- policies;
- procedures;
- control requirements;
- contractual requirements;
- applicable regulatory requirements.

Compliance metrics should distinguish internal policy compliance from legal compliance.

### 100. AI Security KPI Resilience Measurement

Resilience KPIs may measure:

- recovery capability;
- monitoring continuity;
- tested fallback;
- recovery-test success;
- critical dependency coverage.

### 101. AI Security KPI Control-Effectiveness Measurement

Control-effectiveness KPIs should provide evidence about whether controls operate as intended.

### 102. AI Security KPI Risk-Reduction Measurement

Where measurable, KPIs may evaluate whether security activities reduce identified risk.

Risk reduction should not be inferred solely from increased control activity.

### 103. AI Security KPI Security-Coverage Measurement

Security coverage may measure the proportion of relevant AI assets with required:

- security controls;
- monitoring;
- assessments;
- testing.

### 104. AI Security KPI Assessment Coverage

Assessment coverage may measure the proportion of applicable AI systems receiving required security assessments.

### 105. AI Security KPI Security-Testing Coverage

Security-testing coverage may measure the proportion of relevant AI systems, models, or components subject to defined security testing.

### 106. AI Security KPI Vulnerability-Remediation Performance

A KPI may measure remediation performance using:

- remediation time;
- overdue findings;
- critical findings resolved;
- recurrence.

### 107. AI Security KPI Monitoring Coverage

A KPI may measure the proportion of material AI assets with required monitoring coverage.

### 108. AI Security KPI Telemetry Availability

A KPI may measure the percentage of required telemetry available during the reporting period.

### 109. AI Security KPI Detection Performance

Detection KPIs may measure:

- detection latency;
- detection coverage;
- false-positive rate;
- false-negative rate;
- validated detection rate.

### 110. AI Security KPI Incident Response Performance

Incident-related KPIs may measure:

- time to detect;
- time to triage;
- time to contain;
- time to recover;
- recurrence.

### 111. AI Security KPI Model-Security Performance

Model-security KPIs may measure:

- security-testing coverage;
- model-artifact integrity;
- unauthorized model changes;
- model security findings;
- security assessment completion.

### 112. AI Security KPI Data-Security Performance

Data-security KPIs may measure:

- sensitive-data exposure;
- data-access control coverage;

### 113. AI Security KPI Agent-Security Performance

Agent-security KPIs may measure:

- authorized tool-use coverage;

- agent security-testing coverage.

### 114. AI Security KPI API-Security Performance

API-security KPIs may measure:

- authenticated request coverage;
- authorization failures;

- abnormal API activity;

### 115. AI Security KPI Identity Performance

Identity-related KPIs may measure:

- privileged-access review completion;

### 116. AI Security KPI Configuration-Security Performance

Configuration KPIs may measure:

- secure-configuration compliance;

- remediation time.

### 117. AI Security KPI Supplier-Security Performance

Supplier KPIs may measure:

- vendor-assessment completion;
- critical supplier coverage;
- unresolved supplier findings;
- supplier incident response;
- security-evidence availability.

### 118. AI Security KPI Fourth-Party Performance

Where material, fourth-party KPIs may measure:

- critical dependency visibility;

- unresolved fourth-party findings.

### 119. AI Security KPI Security-Awareness Performance

Awareness KPIs may measure:

- training completion;
- role-specific training;
- simulation performance;
- security-policy acknowledgment.

Completion alone should not be interpreted as evidence of effective security behavior.

### 120. AI Security KPI Exception Performance

Exception KPIs may measure:

- open exceptions;
- overdue exceptions;
- expired exceptions;
- exceptions without compensating controls.

### 121. AI Security KPI Remediation Performance

Remediation KPIs may measure:

- overdue actions;
- average remediation time;
- critical findings resolved;
- recurring findings.

### 122. AI Security KPI Recurrence Performance

Recurrence KPIs may identify whether previously addressed security issues reappear.

### 123. AI Security KPI Management Reporting

KPI reporting should provide appropriate context, including:

- target;
- actual;
- trend;
- variance;
- material exceptions;
- data limitations.

### 124. AI Security KPI Escalation

KPI conditions exceeding defined governance thresholds should trigger appropriate management review or escalation.

### 125. Core Control Principle

**AI security KPI governance should ensure that measurements are deliberately designed, consistently calculated, supported by reliable data, connected to defined objectives, and interpreted in context. KPI frameworks should balance leading, lagging, activity, outcome, effectiveness, efficiency, coverage, and resilience measures rather than treating any single category as sufficient evidence of security performance.**

## Part 3 — Continuous AI Security KPI Monitoring, Analysis, Assurance and Decision Support

### 126. AI Security KPI Continuous Monitoring

Material AI security KPIs should be monitored continuously or at an appropriate defined frequency according to their:

- risk significance;
- volatility;
- decision importance;
- reporting requirement;
- operational dependency.

Continuous monitoring does not mean that every KPI must be recalculated in real time.

### 127. AI Security KPI Monitoring Frequency

Monitoring frequency should be aligned with the speed at which a KPI can materially change.

High-volatility operational KPIs may require frequent measurement, while strategic KPIs may be appropriate for periodic measurement.

### 128. AI Security KPI Measurement Cadence

A defined measurement cadence should specify when KPI data is:

- collected;
- calculated;
- validated;
- reviewed;
- reported.

### 129. AI Security KPI Reporting Cadence

Reporting frequency should reflect the governance decision supported by the KPI.

Operational security KPIs may require more frequent reporting than board-level strategic KPIs.

### 130. AI Security KPI Real-Time Measurement

Real-time KPI measurement may be appropriate where immediate changes can materially affect security decisions.

Real-time measurement should not automatically be interpreted as superior measurement.

### 131. AI Security KPI Near-Real-Time Measurement

Near-real-time measurement may provide sufficiently current information while reducing processing and infrastructure requirements.

### 132. AI Security KPI Periodic Measurement

Periodic measurement may be appropriate for:

- maturity;
- governance;
- assurance;
- strategic performance;
- long-term trends.

### 133. AI Security KPI Baseline Monitoring

KPI performance should be compared with an approved baseline where meaningful.

The baseline should have a documented definition and effective period.

### 134. AI Security KPI Baseline Drift

A baseline may become less representative because of:

- architectural change;
- business growth;
- model changes;
- threat changes;
- control changes;
- changes in operating conditions.

Baseline changes should therefore be governed rather than assumed.

### 135. AI Security KPI Target Drift

Targets may become inappropriate when:

- risk changes;
- business objectives change;
- regulatory expectations change;
- technology changes;
- control maturity improves.

Target changes should be documented rather than silently applied.

### 136. AI Security KPI Threshold Breach

A threshold breach should be evaluated to determine:

- whether it is genuine;
- whether data quality contributed;
- whether the breach is material;
- whether escalation is required;
- whether corrective action is necessary.

A threshold breach is not automatically evidence of a security incident.

### 137. AI Security KPI Threshold Persistence

Persistent threshold breaches may provide stronger management evidence than isolated deviations.

Persistence should be evaluated using a defined methodology.

### 138. AI Security KPI Threshold Recurrence

Repeated threshold breaches should be analyzed for:

- systemic control weakness;
- recurring operational conditions;
- insufficient target calibration;
- inadequate remediation;
- measurement problems.

### 139. AI Security KPI Variance Investigation

Material variance should be investigated where it could affect security decisions.

Investigation should distinguish:

- actual performance change;
- measurement error;
- population change;
- methodology change;
- environmental change.

### 140. AI Security KPI Trend Detection

Trend analysis should identify sustained directional movement rather than relying only on period-to-period comparison.

### 141. AI Security KPI Trend Significance

A trend should be interpreted in relation to:

- magnitude;
- duration;
- volatility;
- baseline;
- target;
- risk context.

### 142. AI Security KPI Trend Break

A significant change in an established KPI trend should be investigated where it may indicate:

- control degradation;
- improvement;
- environmental change;
- measurement change;
- data-quality issue.

### 143. AI Security KPI Volatility Analysis

High KPI volatility should be evaluated to determine whether it represents:

- genuine operational variability;
- unstable controls;
- unstable measurement;
- insufficient data;
- external events.

### 144. AI Security KPI Seasonality

Seasonal effects should be considered when interpreting KPIs affected by:

- workload;
- business cycles;
- deployment cycles;
- reporting periods;
- predictable operational patterns.

### 145. AI Security KPI Outlier Analysis

Outliers should be investigated where they could materially affect management interpretation.

An outlier should not automatically be removed from the KPI population.

### 146. AI Security KPI Statistical Context

Where appropriate, statistical techniques may support:

- trend detection;
- anomaly identification;
- confidence assessment;
- variance analysis;
- forecasting.

Statistical significance does not automatically establish business or security significance.

### 147. AI Security KPI Confidence Assessment

Management reporting may include confidence information where KPI uncertainty is material.

Confidence should reflect the quality and limitations of the underlying measurement.

### 148. AI Security KPI Measurement Uncertainty

Measurement uncertainty may arise from:

- incomplete data;
- estimation;
- sampling;
- inconsistent source systems;
- classification uncertainty;
- methodological limitations.

Material uncertainty should be disclosed.

### 149. AI Security KPI Data Quality Exception

Data-quality failures affecting a KPI should be recorded and assessed.

The organization should distinguish between:

- a poor KPI result;
- an unreliable KPI result.

### 150. AI Security KPI Data Completeness Monitoring

Completeness should be monitored over time to identify deterioration in source-system coverage.

### 151. AI Security KPI Data Integrity Monitoring

Integrity monitoring should identify unauthorized or unexplained changes to KPI source data and calculation inputs.

### 152. AI Security KPI Data Lineage Monitoring

Changes to KPI data lineage should be identified where source systems, transformations, or calculation pipelines change.

### 153. AI Security KPI Calculation Monitoring

Automated KPI calculations should be monitored for:

- execution failure;
- unexpected output;
- processing errors;
- missing data;
- unauthorized logic changes.

### 154. AI Security KPI Pipeline Monitoring

KPI data pipelines should be monitored where interruption or degradation could materially affect reporting.

### 155. AI Security KPI Pipeline Failure

Pipeline failure should be distinguished from poor security performance.

The KPI should not be interpreted as representative when its underlying data-generation process has materially failed.

### 156. AI Security KPI Restatement Control

A KPI should be restated where a material error makes the previously reported value misleading, subject to the organization's approved methodology.

### 157. AI Security KPI Restatement Documentation

Restatements should document:

- original value;
- corrected value;
- reason;
- affected periods;
- methodology;
- approval;
- reporting impact.

### 158. AI Security KPI Historical Integrity

Historical KPI records should be preserved so that management can distinguish:

- original reporting;
- corrected reporting;
- methodology changes;
- target changes.

### 159. AI Security KPI Methodology Break

A methodology change may create a break in the historical series.

The organization should identify the break rather than presenting incomparable results as a continuous trend.

### 160. AI Security KPI Comparable-Series Reconstruction

Where feasible, historical data may be recalculated using a new methodology to support meaningful comparison.

Any reconstructed series should be clearly identified.

### 161. AI Security KPI Benchmarking

Benchmarking may compare KPI performance against:

- internal business units;
- historical performance;
- approved external benchmarks;
- peer organizations where reliable comparable data exists.

Benchmarking requires methodological comparability.

### 162. AI Security KPI Peer Comparison

Peer comparison should account for differences in:

- organizational scale;
- AI portfolio;
- risk exposure;
- regulatory environment;
- architecture;
- operating model.

### 163. AI Security KPI Normalization

Normalization may be necessary when comparing organizations or business units with materially different populations.

Examples include:

- incidents per model;
- findings per assessed system;
- remediation days per critical finding.

### 164. AI Security KPI Aggregation

KPIs may be aggregated across:

- models;
- applications;
- business units;
- geographic regions;
- suppliers;
- environments.

Aggregation should not conceal material localized deterioration.

### 165. AI Security KPI Disaggregation

Material KPIs should be capable of being disaggregated where aggregate values could conceal:

- high-risk systems;
- critical models;
- vulnerable populations;
- individual business-unit deterioration;
- supplier-specific exposure.

### 166. AI Security KPI Segmentation

KPI segmentation may use dimensions such as:

- AI system criticality;
- model type;
- deployment environment;
- business service;
- supplier;
- geography;
- risk classification.

### 167. AI Security KPI Materiality Segmentation

Material systems should receive appropriate measurement attention even when their population is small.
