# 18.05 — AI Governance Metrics

## Part 1 — AI Governance Metrics Foundations, Measurement Principles, Governance and Interpretation

### 1. AI Governance Metric
An AI governance metric is a defined quantitative or qualitative measure used to assess an aspect of AI governance, such as governance coverage, accountability, policy implementation, risk oversight, control operation, compliance management, incident governance, or assurance. A governance metric provides information about governance conditions but does not itself establish that governance is effective.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-001`

### 2. Governance Metric Objective
A governance metric should have a defined objective explaining what governance condition it is intended to measure and why that information is relevant. Without a defined objective, metrics can become reporting activities without meaningful decision value.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-002`

### 3. Governance Metric vs KPI
A governance metric is a broad measurement concept, while a KPI is a selected indicator considered particularly significant for evaluating progress toward an objective. Not every governance metric should become a KPI.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-003`

### 4. Governance Metric vs KRI
A governance metric may measure governance activity, coverage, quality, or effectiveness, whereas a KRI specifically provides an indicator of risk exposure or changing risk conditions. A governance metric may support KRI interpretation without being a KRI itself.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-004`

### 5. Governance Metric vs Control
A metric measures or describes a condition; a control is an action, mechanism, or process intended to prevent, detect, reduce, or manage risk. Measuring policy compliance is not the same as operating the control that establishes or enforces the policy.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-005`

### 6. Governance Metric vs Control Objective
A control objective describes the intended outcome of a control framework or control activity. A governance metric provides evidence or an indicator that can help determine whether the objective is being approached or achieved.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-006`

### 7. Governance Metric vs Target
A metric represents an observed or calculated condition. A target represents a desired future state or performance level. A metric of 92% and a target of 95% are conceptually different pieces of information.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-007`

### 8. Governance Metric vs Threshold
A threshold establishes a boundary that may require investigation, escalation, or action. A metric measures a condition against which that threshold may be evaluated.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-008`

### 9. Governance Metric vs Standard
A standard establishes defined requirements, specifications, or practices within a particular framework or context. A metric may measure conformity with a standard but does not itself become the standard.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-009`

### 10. Governance Metric Scope
The scope of a governance metric identifies the organizational population, AI systems, models, business processes, policies, controls, suppliers, geographic areas, or governance domains included in measurement.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-010`

### 11. Governance Measurement Domain
Governance measurement domains may include:

- AI governance structure;
- accountability;
- policy management;
- AI inventory;
- risk management;
- control management;
- compliance management;
- privacy governance;
- security governance;
- model governance;
- data governance;
- third-party governance;
- incident governance;
- assurance;
- training and awareness;
- exception management; and
- continuous improvement.

A metric should identify the domain it represents to avoid ambiguous interpretation.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-011`

### 12. Governance Metric Taxonomy
A governance metric taxonomy provides a controlled classification of metrics. The taxonomy should support consistent categorization, ownership, aggregation, reporting, and interpretation across AI governance functions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-012`

### 13. Governance Metric Owner
Each material governance metric should have an accountable owner responsible for its definition, relevance, interpretation, maintenance, and appropriate use.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-013`

### 14. Governance Metric Data Owner
The data owner is responsible for the relevant source information used to produce the metric. The data owner and metric owner may be the same person or function, but the responsibilities should remain clearly defined.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-014`

### 15. Governance Metric Definition
A metric definition should specify exactly what is being measured. A useful definition should address the subject, population, measurement method, timeframe, unit, and intended interpretation.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-015`

### 16. Governance Metric Formula
Where a metric is quantitative, the formula should be documented sufficiently to permit consistent calculation. Formula documentation should identify relevant variables, transformations, assumptions, and calculation logic.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-016`

### 17. Governance Metric Numerator
For ratio- or percentage-based metrics, the numerator represents the counted population or measured quantity contributing to the result. The numerator should be defined consistently and supported by appropriate source data.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-017`

### 18. Governance Metric Denominator
For ratio- or percentage-based metrics, the denominator establishes the population against which the numerator is evaluated. Changes to the denominator can materially alter the result even when the numerator remains unchanged.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-018`

### 19. Governance Metric Population
The measurement population identifies the complete set of entities, events, controls, systems, assessments, personnel, suppliers, or other objects to which the metric applies.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-019`

### 20. Governance Metric Inclusion Criteria
Inclusion criteria define which records or entities are counted in the measurement population. Criteria should be objective, documented, consistently applied, and appropriate to the metric objective.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-020`

### 21. Governance Metric Exclusion Criteria
Exclusion criteria define which records or entities are deliberately omitted. Material exclusions should be documented because exclusions can materially influence the resulting measurement.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-021`

### 22. Governance Metric Measurement Period
The measurement period establishes the timeframe represented by the metric. It may be a point-in-time measurement, reporting period, rolling period, or another defined interval.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-022`

### 23. Governance Metric Measurement Frequency
Measurement frequency specifies how often the underlying metric is calculated or evaluated. Frequency should reflect the decision purpose, risk dynamics, data availability, and operational requirements.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-023`

### 24. Governance Metric Reporting Frequency
Reporting frequency specifies how often the metric is communicated to its intended audience. Measurement and reporting frequencies do not necessarily need to be identical.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-024`

### 25. Governance Metric Baseline
A baseline provides a reference point against which subsequent governance performance or conditions can be compared. Baselines should identify their timeframe, population, methodology, and relevant assumptions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-025`

### 26. Governance Metric Target
A target defines a desired governance condition or performance level. Targets should have an identified owner, rationale, timeframe, and approval authority where appropriate.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-026`

### 27. Governance Metric Threshold
A threshold establishes a boundary at which a defined governance response may be initiated. Internal thresholds should be explicitly distinguished from externally imposed legal, regulatory, contractual, or framework requirements.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-027`

### 28. Governance Metric Tolerance
Tolerance defines the acceptable degree of variation around a target or expected condition. Tolerance should not be confused with legal permission to deviate from an applicable requirement.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-028`

### 29. Governance Metric Variance
Variance represents the difference between an observed governance metric and a defined target, baseline, threshold, or reference point. Variance should be interpreted in context rather than treated automatically as evidence of governance failure.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-029`

### 30. Governance Metric Trend
A trend represents the direction and pattern of a metric over time. Trend analysis can identify sustained improvement, deterioration, stability, volatility, or structural change.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-030`

### 31. Governance Metric Stability
Stability describes the degree to which a metric remains within an expected range over time. Stability does not necessarily mean that the underlying governance condition is effective.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-031`

### 32. Governance Metric Volatility
Volatility represents the degree of variation in a metric over time. High volatility may result from genuine governance changes, changing populations, measurement problems, operational events, or methodology changes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-032`

### 33. Governance Metric Directionality
Directionality identifies whether higher or lower values generally represent improvement, deterioration, or another condition. Directionality should be documented because interpretation is not always intuitive.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-033`

### 34. Governance Metric Interpretation
Metric interpretation converts a measured result into meaningful governance information. Interpretation should consider context, scope, methodology, uncertainty, materiality, and relevant changes rather than relying solely on the numerical result.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-034`

### 35. Governance Metric Context
Context may include organizational size, AI deployment volume, business criticality, risk exposure, regulatory environment, technology architecture, supplier dependencies, incidents, and changes in governance requirements.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-035`

### 36. Governance Metric Comparability
Metrics should be compared across periods, organizational units, or AI systems only when their populations, definitions, methodologies, and measurement conditions are sufficiently comparable.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-036`

### 37. Governance Metric Consistency
Consistent definitions, formulas, populations, and calculation practices support reliable longitudinal governance measurement. Necessary methodology changes should be documented rather than hidden.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-037`

### 38. Governance Metric Methodology
The methodology defines how governance information is collected, transformed, calculated, validated, and interpreted. Methodology should be documented according to the materiality and complexity of the metric.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-038`

### 39. Governance Metric Methodology Change
A methodology change can affect the meaning or comparability of historical measurements. Material methodology changes should therefore be documented, approved where appropriate, version-controlled, and communicated to relevant users.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-039`

### 40. Governance Metric Restatement
Restatement occurs when previously reported metrics are recalculated because material errors, data problems, population changes, or methodology issues are identified. Restatement should preserve appropriate historical context.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-040`

### 41. Governance Metric Data Quality
Data quality concerns whether the information used to calculate a metric is sufficiently accurate, complete, timely, consistent, valid, and fit for its intended purpose.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-041`

### 42. Governance Metric Data Lineage
Data lineage identifies the movement and transformation of information from source systems through processing and calculation to the reported governance metric.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-042`

### 43. Governance Metric Data Provenance
Data provenance records the origin and relevant history of measurement information. Provenance supports traceability but does not automatically establish that the source information is accurate or complete.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-043`

### 44. Governance Metric Evidence
Evidence supporting a governance metric may include policy records, inventories, risk assessments, control records, audit results, training records, assessment results, system data, incident records, approvals, exceptions, and other relevant documentation.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-044`

### 45. Governance Metric Evidence Sufficiency
Evidence sufficiency concerns whether the available evidence is adequate to support the intended interpretation of the metric. A large volume of evidence does not necessarily constitute sufficient evidence.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-045`

### 46. Governance Metric Evidence Integrity
Evidence integrity concerns whether measurement evidence has remained complete, accurate, attributable, and protected against unauthorized alteration. Evidence integrity is distinct from evidence existence.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-046`

### 47. Governance Metric Calculation Integrity
Calculation integrity concerns whether the metric has been calculated according to its approved methodology using the intended population and data. Calculation integrity should be validated where the metric is material.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-047`

### 48. Governance Metric Validation
Validation evaluates whether the metric definition, data, methodology, calculation, and interpretation are appropriate for the intended governance purpose. Validation may be automated, manual, internal, or independent depending on risk and assurance requirements.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-048`

### 49. Governance Metric Assurance
Assurance provides a defined level of confidence regarding a metric, its underlying process, or the governance condition it represents. Assurance scope should be explicitly defined because assurance of metric calculation does not automatically constitute assurance of governance effectiveness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-049`

### 50. Core Principle
AI governance metrics should transform governance information into reliable, interpretable, and decision-useful measurements. A metric should have a defined purpose, scope, owner, population, methodology, data source, calculation method, and interpretation.

The following distinctions are fundamental:

```text
METRIC          ≠ KPI
METRIC          ≠ KRI
METRIC          ≠ CONTROL
METRIC          ≠ CONTROL OBJECTIVE
METRIC          ≠ TARGET
METRIC          ≠ THRESHOLD
METRIC          ≠ ASSURANCE
MEASUREMENT     ≠ GOVERNANCE EFFECTIVENESS
DATA            ≠ EVIDENCE
EVIDENCE        ≠ ASSURANCE
TREND           ≠ CAUSATION
THRESHOLD       ≠ LEGAL REQUIREMENT
INTERNAL TARGET ≠ REGULATORY REQUIREMENT
```

A governance metric can indicate the condition of an AI governance program, but the metric itself does not establish that the organization is secure, compliant, effective, or free from material AI risk.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-050`

**Taxonomy note:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-*` identifiers are internal knowledge-base taxonomy identifiers. They are not statutory provisions, regulatory article numbers, certification requirements, or legal obligations.

## Part 2 — AI Governance Metric Controls, Measurement Design, Data Governance, Validation and Reporting

### 51. Governance Metric Policy
The organization should establish governance requirements for defining, approving, maintaining, reporting, and reviewing AI governance metrics. The policy should establish responsibilities, measurement principles, evidence expectations, change requirements, and escalation mechanisms.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-051`

### 52. Governance Metric Inventory
A controlled inventory should identify material AI governance metrics used across the organization. The inventory should include metric name, purpose, owner, domain, methodology, data source, frequency, audience, status, and relevant dependencies.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-052`

### 53. Governance Metric Register
The metric register should provide the authoritative record for approved governance metrics. It should distinguish active, proposed, suspended, deprecated, and retired metrics where appropriate.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-053`

### 54. Governance Metric Ownership
Each material metric should have clearly assigned ownership for definition, data quality, calculation, interpretation, reporting, and review. Responsibilities should remain clear when metric production involves multiple teams.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-054`

### 55. Governance Metric Approval
Material metrics should undergo appropriate approval before being used for formal governance reporting. Approval should consider the metric objective, methodology, population, data source, calculation, interpretation, and intended decision use.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-055`

### 56. Governance Metric Audience
The intended audience should be documented for each material metric. Different audiences may require different levels of detail, context, aggregation, and reporting frequency.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-056`

### 57. Governance Metric Purpose
Each metric should have a clearly documented purpose describing the governance question it is intended to answer. Metrics without a defined decision or monitoring purpose should be challenged for continued relevance.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-057`

### 58. Governance Metric Scope Control
The scope of each metric should be explicitly documented, including applicable AI systems, models, business units, suppliers, processes, controls, or governance domains.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-058`

### 59. Governance Metric Risk Mapping
Where appropriate, governance metrics should be mapped to relevant AI risks. The mapping should identify how the metric provides information about the risk without implying that measurement itself constitutes risk treatment.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-059`

### 60. Governance Metric Objective Mapping
Metrics should be mapped to relevant governance objectives where such objectives have been formally established. This allows management to determine whether measurement activities remain aligned with intended governance outcomes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-060`

### 61. Governance Metric Control Mapping
Where a metric evaluates a control condition, the relevant control should be identified. The metric should remain conceptually distinct from the control being measured.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-061`

### 62. Governance Metric Requirement Mapping
Where metrics are used to monitor legal, regulatory, contractual, policy, or framework requirements, the applicable requirement should be explicitly identified. Internal governance metrics should not be represented as legal obligations merely because they support compliance monitoring.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-062`

### 63. Governance Metric Data Source
Each material metric should identify its authoritative or designated data source. Source selection should consider reliability, completeness, timeliness, accessibility, provenance, and suitability for the measurement objective.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-063`

### 64. Governance Metric Source Validation
Data sources should be validated to determine whether they provide information appropriate for the metric. A source being authoritative for one purpose does not automatically make every field suitable for another purpose.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-064`

### 65. Governance Metric Source Reliability
The reliability of material data sources should be evaluated based on factors such as data quality, ownership, controls, update frequency, integrity, and historical performance.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-065`

### 66. Governance Metric Data Extraction
Extraction processes should retrieve the intended population and fields from source systems. Extraction logic should be controlled and tested where errors could materially affect the metric.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-066`

### 67. Governance Metric Data Transformation
Transformations applied before calculation should be documented and controlled. Transformation logic should preserve the intended meaning of the underlying data.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-067`

### 68. Governance Metric Data Reconciliation
Material data should be reconciled against authoritative sources or independent reference information where appropriate. Reconciliation should identify unexplained differences rather than merely produce matching totals.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-068`

### 69. Governance Metric Completeness
Completeness controls should determine whether the metric population and required data fields are sufficiently represented. Missing records should be distinguished from records that legitimately do not apply.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-069`

### 70. Governance Metric Accuracy
Accuracy controls should assess whether reported information correctly represents the underlying source information and approved calculation methodology.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-070`

### 71. Governance Metric Timeliness
Timeliness measures whether required information is available within the period necessary for the intended governance decision. A technically accurate metric can still be ineffective if it arrives too late.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-071`

### 72. Governance Metric Consistency Control
The same metric should use consistent definitions and calculation methods across reporting periods unless an approved methodology change has occurred.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-072`

### 73. Governance Metric Validity
Validity concerns whether the data and calculation satisfy defined rules and constraints. Invalid records should be detected and handled according to documented procedures.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-073`

### 74. Governance Metric Integrity
Metric integrity requires protection against unauthorized alteration of source data, calculation logic, configuration, and reported results.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-074`

### 75. Governance Metric Data Lineage Control
Material metrics should maintain sufficient lineage to identify the source, transformation, calculation, and reporting stages used to produce the result.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-075`

### 76. Governance Metric Data Provenance Control
Provenance should identify the origin and relevant history of data used in material measurements. Provenance information should itself be protected from inappropriate alteration.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-076`

### 77. Governance Metric Formula Control
Approved formulas should be documented and protected against unauthorized changes. Formula changes should follow appropriate change-management procedures.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-077`

### 78. Governance Metric Population Control
The measurement population should be defined using controlled criteria. Changes to population logic should be documented because they can create apparent performance changes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-078`

### 79. Governance Metric Inclusion Control
Inclusion criteria should be implemented consistently across measurement cycles. Exceptions should be documented and reviewed where they could materially affect the result.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-079`

### 80. Governance Metric Exclusion Control
Exclusions should have a documented rationale and, where material, appropriate approval. Deliberate exclusion of difficult or unfavorable records can distort governance measurement.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-080`

### 81. Governance Metric Denominator Control
Denominators should be controlled and monitored for material changes. A changing denominator can alter a percentage even when the numerator remains unchanged.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-081`

### 82. Governance Metric Calculation Control
Calculation processes should apply the approved formula to the intended population using the approved data sources and transformation logic.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-082`

### 83. Automated Governance Metric Calculation
Automated calculation may reduce manual error and improve repeatability. Automated processes should nevertheless be tested, monitored, access-controlled, and periodically validated.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-083`

### 84. Manual Governance Metric Calculation
Where manual calculations are necessary, documented procedures, controlled spreadsheets or tools, review mechanisms, and appropriate segregation should be applied according to metric materiality.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-084`

### 85. Governance Metric Reproducibility
Authorized personnel should be able to reproduce material metrics using documented methodology, appropriate source information, and relevant calculation logic.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-085`

### 86. Governance Metric Independent Recalculation
Material metrics may be independently recalculated to verify calculation accuracy. Independent recalculation provides evidence about the calculation process but does not automatically establish governance effectiveness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-086`

### 87. Governance Metric Validation Testing
Validation testing should evaluate whether metric calculations produce expected results under defined test conditions, including normal cases, boundary conditions, missing data, duplicate records, and invalid inputs where relevant.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-087`

### 88. Governance Metric Regression Testing
When calculation logic changes, regression testing should confirm that existing metric behavior remains consistent except where changes are intentional and documented.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-088`

### 89. Governance Metric Methodology Versioning
Material methodology changes should be version-controlled. Historical results should retain sufficient metadata to determine which methodology was used.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-089`

### 90. Governance Metric Change Management
Changes to definitions, formulas, populations, sources, thresholds, targets, or reporting logic should be governed according to their potential impact.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-090`

### 91. Governance Metric Historical Comparability
Historical comparisons should account for changes in methodology, population, data source, governance structure, AI deployment, or measurement conditions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-091`

### 92. Governance Metric Baseline Governance
Baselines should have documented ownership, timeframe, population, methodology, and rationale. Baselines should be reviewed when material changes invalidate their usefulness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-092`

### 93. Governance Metric Target Governance
Targets should be approved by an appropriate authority and supported by a defined rationale. Targets should reflect organizational objectives rather than being selected solely because they are easy to achieve.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-093`

### 94. Governance Metric Threshold Governance
Thresholds should have documented definitions, owners, rationale, response expectations, and review requirements. Internal thresholds should be distinguished from externally mandated limits.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-094`

### 95. Governance Metric Threshold Calibration
Thresholds should be calibrated using relevant historical data, risk context, operational experience, materiality, and decision requirements where appropriate.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-095`

### 96. Governance Metric Threshold Testing
Thresholds should be tested to determine whether they produce meaningful signals and appropriate governance responses. Excessively sensitive thresholds can generate noise, while excessively permissive thresholds can delay attention.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-096`

### 97. Governance Metric Early-Warning Threshold
An early-warning threshold may identify emerging governance deterioration before a critical condition is reached. Early-warning thresholds are normally internal governance mechanisms unless an external requirement establishes a specific threshold.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-097`

### 98. Governance Metric Escalation Threshold
An escalation threshold defines a condition that should initiate a specified governance response. The response should identify responsible personnel and expected timing.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-098`

### 99. Governance Metric Critical Threshold
A critical threshold identifies a condition requiring urgent management attention according to organizational governance arrangements. Critical status should not automatically be interpreted as a legal or regulatory classification.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-099`

### 100. Governance Metric Threshold Breach
A threshold breach occurs when a measured condition crosses a defined boundary. The breach should be investigated in context because it may result from genuine governance deterioration, data problems, population changes, or methodology changes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-100`

### 101. Governance Metric Target Variance
Target variance should identify the difference between actual performance and the approved target. Variance should be analyzed for significance rather than treated automatically as a governance failure.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-101`

### 102. Governance Metric Trend Analysis
Trend analysis should evaluate movement over time rather than relying on a single measurement. Trend interpretation should account for changes in population, methodology, AI deployment, and governance conditions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-102`

### 103. Governance Metric Persistence
Persistence measures whether a governance condition remains present over multiple measurement periods. Persistent deterioration may warrant greater management attention than an isolated observation.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-103`

### 104. Governance Metric Recurrence
Recurrence identifies repeated occurrence of a governance condition after previous remediation or closure. Recurrence analysis may indicate ineffective remediation, systemic causes, or changing environmental conditions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-104`

### 105. Governance Metric Volatility Analysis
Volatility should be analyzed to distinguish genuine governance variability from measurement instability, data-quality problems, population changes, or methodology changes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-105`

### 106. Governance Metric Leading Indicator
Leading governance metrics provide information about activities or conditions that may influence future governance outcomes. Examples may include assessment completion, control implementation, policy review, training coverage, or remediation progress.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-106`

### 107. Governance Metric Lagging Indicator
Lagging governance metrics measure outcomes or conditions that have already occurred, such as incidents, overdue remediation, control failures, audit findings, or policy exceptions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-107`

### 108. Leading-Lagging Balance
A governance measurement framework should consider both leading and lagging indicators. Leading indicators may provide earlier signals, while lagging indicators provide evidence of realized outcomes.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-108`

### 109. Governance Coverage Metric
Coverage metrics measure the proportion of the defined population subject to a governance activity, such as inventory coverage, risk assessment coverage, policy applicability, control coverage, or supplier assessment coverage.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-109`

### 110. Governance Compliance Metric
Compliance metrics may measure adherence to defined legal, regulatory, contractual, policy, or internal requirements. The metric definition should clearly identify the requirement against which compliance is measured.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-110`

### 111. Governance Effectiveness Metric
Effectiveness metrics seek to determine whether a governance activity produces its intended outcome. Completion or participation alone does not necessarily demonstrate effectiveness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-111`

### 112. Governance Efficiency Metric
Efficiency metrics examine the resources, time, effort, or cost required to perform governance activities. Efficiency should not be optimized at the expense of governance effectiveness or risk management.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-112`

### 113. Governance Activity Metric
Activity metrics measure whether governance activities occurred, such as policy reviews, risk assessments, control tests, committee meetings, training sessions, or supplier assessments.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-113`

### 114. Governance Outcome Metric
Outcome metrics measure the result of governance activities rather than merely their execution. Outcome metrics may provide stronger insight into whether governance objectives are being achieved.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-114`

### 115. Governance Control-Effectiveness Metric
Control-effectiveness metrics may evaluate whether controls operate as intended or achieve defined outcomes. A control effectiveness metric should distinguish design effectiveness, operating effectiveness, and outcome effectiveness where relevant.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-115`

### 116. AI Inventory Governance Metric
Inventory metrics may measure the percentage of identified AI systems, models, agents, applications, datasets, APIs, or suppliers recorded within the approved governance inventory.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-116`

### 117. AI Risk Assessment Governance Metric
Risk-assessment metrics may measure the proportion of applicable AI systems assessed according to defined risk-management procedures. The metric should identify the applicable population and assessment criteria.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-117`

### 118. AI Policy Governance Metric
Policy metrics may measure policy coverage, review status, approval status, acknowledgement, applicability, exceptions, or implementation. Policy existence should not automatically be interpreted as effective implementation.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-118`

### 119. AI Control Governance Metric
Control metrics may measure control implementation, testing, effectiveness, exceptions, remediation, or coverage. The metric should identify which aspect of the control lifecycle is being measured.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-119`

### 120. AI Exception Governance Metric
Exception metrics may measure the number, age, severity, business impact, ownership, approval status, and remediation status of governance exceptions.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-120`

### 121. AI Remediation Governance Metric
Remediation metrics may measure overdue actions, remediation aging, closure rates, recurrence, risk reduction, or remediation effectiveness. Closure alone should not automatically be treated as evidence that the original risk has been eliminated.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-121`

### 122. AI Assurance Governance Metric
Assurance metrics may measure assessment coverage, testing completion, findings, unresolved assurance issues, evidence sufficiency, remediation, or assurance-cycle timeliness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-122`

### 123. AI Training Governance Metric
Training metrics may measure applicable population coverage, completion, currency, assessment results, role-specific training, or overdue training. Completion should not automatically demonstrate competence or behavioral effectiveness.

**Control ID:** `AI-SM-GOVMET-123`

### 124. Governance Metric Reporting
Governance metrics should be reported at an appropriate level of aggregation and frequency for their intended audience. Reporting should include sufficient context to prevent misleading interpretation and should identify material limitations where relevant.

### 125. Core Control Principle
AI governance metrics should be governed as controlled measurement mechanisms rather than isolated numbers.

Effective measurement requires:

- defined objectives;
- controlled metric definitions;
- accountable ownership;
- appropriate populations;
- reliable data sources;
- controlled formulas;
- documented inclusion and exclusion criteria;
- validated calculations;
- methodology versioning;
- controlled targets and thresholds;
- leading and lagging indicators;
- appropriate data lineage and provenance;
- evidence supporting material results;
- controlled reporting and escalation.

The measurement process should distinguish governance activity from governance outcome and measurement from assurance.

## Part 3 — Continuous AI Governance Metric Monitoring, Analysis, Assurance and Decision Support

### 126. Continuous Governance Metric Monitoring
Continuous governance metric monitoring evaluates relevant governance measurements on an ongoing or appropriately frequent basis. The objective is to identify material changes, deterioration, anomalies, recurring conditions, and emerging governance gaps.

The organization should monitor whether expected metric refreshes occur within defined timeframes. Missed refreshes should be distinguished from stable measurements because absence of new data does not necessarily indicate stable governance conditions.

### 129. Governance Metric Staleness Detection
Staleness detection identifies measurements that have not been updated within their expected reporting or measurement period. Stale information should be identified to prevent users from interpreting outdated measurements as current conditions.

### 133. Governance Metric Data-Pipeline Monitoring
